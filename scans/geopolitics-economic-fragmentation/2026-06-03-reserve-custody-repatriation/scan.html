Custody, Not Just Composition: How Gold Repatriation Is Fragmenting the Reserve System

Central banks buying record gold reads as a familiar dollar hedge. The sharper shift is in custody and settlement: reserves are migrating out of the dollar-centric vault network, with gold repatriated from New York and London and now exceeding US Treasuries in official holdings, exposing reserve managers, the Treasury market and bullion infrastructure to 2028.

The consensus on official reserves is familiar: central banks are buying record gold as a hedge against a weaponised dollar. Beneath it runs a less-obvious shift, increasingly about custody and settlement rather than allocation. Reserves are migrating out of the dollar-centric vault-and-clearing network: gold is being repatriated from New York and London into domestic vaults, and the value of official gold has overtaken central banks' holdings of US Treasuries. The composition change is visible; the plumbing change is quieter and more durable. The question is no longer only what share of reserves sits in dollars, but where reserves physically sit and through whose infrastructure they clear.

Signal Identification

This is a structural shift in the reserve system's architecture — part composition inflection, part custody-and-settlement reordering. The consensus tracks the currency mix; the signal is that reserve managers are relocating where assets are held and how they clear, treating dollar custody itself as a political and sanctions risk. Custody, not just composition, is the live variable.

Time horizon: Near-term to 5 years (underway now; 2026-2028 inflection as the gold-over-Treasuries crossover and repatriation institutionalise; structural reset later in the decade) Plausibility band: Medium–High Geographic / Jurisdictional Scope: Global. Incumbent custody hubs: the New York Fed and London. Leading edge: France and Germany on repatriation; China, Poland, Turkey and India on accumulation. Sectors exposed: Central banks and reserve managers; sovereign wealth funds; the US Treasury market and dollar-clearing infrastructure; bullion banks, vaulting and custody providers; gold miners and refiners; global macro investors.

What's Changing

The composition crossover is now in the primary data. Bullion reached 27% of global central-bank reserve assets at end-2025, up from 20% a year earlier, overtaking US Treasuries, which fell to 22% from 25%, while dollar assets stayed the largest single share at 42% (European Central Bank, 02/06/2026). By value, foreign central banks' gold reached roughly $4 trillion, edging past their $3.9 trillion of US Treasuries — the first such crossover since 1996 (MINING.COM, 02/06/2026). The dollar's share of allocated reserves drifted to 56.77% in Q4 2025 as the residual 'other currencies' category, rising since 2021, more than doubled (IMF COFER, 27/03/2026), and central banks bought a further 244 tonnes in Q1 2026 with unreported buying still elevated (World Gold Council, 29/04/2026).

The quieter shift is in custody. Between July 2025 and January 2026 the Banque de France sold 129 tonnes of older bullion at the New York Fed and bought equivalent higher-standard bars in Europe, so that all 2,437 tonnes of French gold now sit in Paris — repatriation without shipping a bar or overtly withdrawing custody (IBTimes, 08/04/2026). In Germany, lawmakers renewed calls to bring home the 1,236 tonnes — about 37% of reserves — still at the New York Fed, which one association head called "no longer safe in the Fed's vaults" (Kitco News, 26/01/2026). Reserve managers, OMFIF argues, are entering a new phase of diversification focused on concentration risk and "the future composition of safe assets" (OMFIF, 29/05/2026).

Disruption Pathway

The pathway runs in three stages. Through 2026-2027, the composition crossover and quiet repatriations normalise: more reserve managers bring gold onshore and upgrade legacy bars, treating accessible, sanction-proof holdings as a baseline. Across 2027-2028, custody fragmentation institutionalises — domestic vaulting expands and the access-versus-custody distinction hardens into policy. Later in the decade, a more multipolar custody-and-settlement map emerges, in which dollar clearing is one route among several.

Stress concentrates at three points: the incumbent custody hubs — the New York Fed alone holds about 6,300 tonnes for more than 30 central banks (Kitco News, 26/01/2026); the marginal official-sector bid for Treasuries, now losing reserve share; and the bullion-market plumbing that standardisation and repatriation are reshaping. Two adaptations follow. Operationally, reserve managers build domestic vaulting and upgrade to internationally tradable bars, as France did. At policy level, the official sector reframes gold as a crisis-accessible, sanction-resistant asset and explores settlement outside dollar infrastructure — a shift OMFIF ties to a structural reassessment of safety and liquidity (OMFIF, 29/05/2026).

Why This Matters

For central banks, finance ministries, the Treasury market and bullion banks, the signal reprices the system's anchor. If reserve managers treat dollar custody as a sanctions risk and Treasuries as losing reserve share, the official-sector bid that long absorbed US issuance weakens at the margin, even as the dollar keeps its dominant currency role. Reserve managers should treat custody location as a strategic decision, not back-office logistics; debt managers should price a thinner official-sector bid into issuance; macro investors should fold the crossover and repatriation trend into long-horizon reserve-currency theses. As ECB President Lagarde put it, "Geopolitical tensions continue to drive strong central bank demand for gold" (European Central Bank, 02/06/2026).

Decision-action posture for this signal: Prepare — the reordering is underway but plays out over years, so the move is to model a thinner official-sector Treasury bid, track custody relocations and position for a more multipolar settlement map rather than assume the status quo holds.

Counter-Argument

The strongest objection is that this is mostly a price effect, not reallocation. Gold's reserve share rose largely because its price soared, not because central banks dumped Treasuries; the dollar still anchors 42% of reserve assets and 56.77% of allocated currency reserves (IMF COFER, 27/03/2026), net buying actually eased to 850 tonnes in 2025, and Turkey sold or loaned 130 tonnes early in 2026 (MINING.COM, 02/06/2026). France itself called its repatriation "not politically motivated" (IBTimes, 08/04/2026). On this reading the custody moves are technical and idiosyncratic.

Even granting the price effect, the custody relocations and the symbolic crossover change reserve managers' mental model and the politics of dollar custody. Once a central bank treats overseas custody as a vulnerability — as Germany's debate and France's full repatriation show — the logic of bringing reserves home and diversifying settlement rarely reverses quickly, regardless of what gold's price does next.

Implications

This is a durable reordering of reserve custody and settlement, not only a swing in the currency mix. The inflection window runs from now to roughly 2028, when repatriation and the gold-over-Treasuries crossover institutionalise. Gold, domestic vaulting and non-dollar settlement rails gain; the dollar-custody hubs and the marginal Treasury bid lose ground. The dollar's dominance as a transaction currency is not in question; what is shifting is where reserves physically sit and whose infrastructure they depend on — a slower, stickier change than the headline allocation numbers convey.

Early Indicators to Monitor

  • More central banks announcing repatriation or domestic-vaulting programmes after France and the German debate.
  • New York Fed and Bank of England custody holdings declining as foreign central banks withdraw or upgrade bars.
  • 2026 World Gold Council and OMFIF surveys showing higher domestic-storage and gold-allocation intentions.
  • Gold's share of official reserve assets rising further in subsequent ECB and IMF data releases.
  • Gold-backed or non-dollar cross-border settlement pilots advancing among major reserve holders.

Disconfirming Signals

  • A sustained gold-price correction reversing the share crossover with Treasuries.
  • Central banks resuming net Treasury accumulation and stabilising the dollar's reserve share in COFER.
  • Repatriation remaining a one-off, technical exercise with no new sovereign movers after France.
  • Major reserve holders reaffirming dollar custody as convenient and liquid, as the Bundesbank has.

Strategic Questions

  • Should reserve managers relocate custody home now, or keep New York and London for their liquidity and convenience?
  • At what point should debt managers price official-sector reserve-demand erosion into Treasury issuance?
  • Which hedge wins: physical repatriation, further gold accumulation, or building non-dollar settlement rails?
