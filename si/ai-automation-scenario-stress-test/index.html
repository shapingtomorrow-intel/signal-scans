Which AI Moves Hold in Every Future to 2030

27 October 2026 · Built on the Decision Intelligence briefing AI Agents Are Outrunning the Controls and its System Map · Horizons: 2030 and 2040 Uses the briefing’s scenarios and options, with evidence from the briefing and the Change Tracker; no other sources. Grades and indicator readings are drafted by analysis.
Scanned and filtered by AI. Stress-tested and judged by humans.

Scenarios are not forecasts. Their job is to show how differently the next few years could turn out, so that a plan can be built to survive any of them, and then to be watched, so that the plan changes when the evidence does.

This page does both. It takes the nine options in the briefing and runs each one through four plausible futures to 2030, plus a wild card to 2040. A move that works in every future is robust and can be committed to now. A move that works well in some futures and poorly in others is a bet, held ready with a clear trigger. A move that makes things worse in any future is fragile and should not be relied on. It then scores leading indicators on the matrix’s two axes, shows how that reading has moved since September, and turns the reading into investment priorities.

1. Four futures to 2030, and a wild card to 2040

The two axes are the system’s two biggest uncertainties, the same two the System Map identifies: whether the brakes on agents work, which decides whether their risk falls on deployers or on those affected, and how far borrowed money drives the build-out, which decides whether it is paid for with debt or from earnings.

Earnings · deployers carry risk

Licensed Agents

Agents work under licence. Deployers must show what each agent can reach, insurers price that evidence, and the build-out grows at the pace earnings allow.

By 2040: Agents are registered, insured and supervised much as vehicles and their drivers are.

Earnings · others carry risk

The Unwritten Bill

The largest firms, paying from earnings, deploy agents everywhere. Rules lag, and harm from agents lands on customers, suppliers and the public and is settled years later in court.

By 2040: Courts, not regulators, have written the rules for agents, case by case.

Borrowed money · deployers carry risk

The Slow Payback

Agents are tightly controlled and adoption is careful, but the data centres were built with borrowed money on the assumption of fast adoption, and returns arrive late.

By 2040: The build-out has been refinanced, and cheap computing serves a careful, accountable agent economy.

Borrowed money · others carry risk

The Leveraged Race

The build-out runs on borrowed money and agents are deployed fast with loose controls. Incidents are frequent, and a fall in valuations would hit the debt and the agents at once.

By 2040: A correction, and the incidents around it, have forced the rules the race avoided.

Wild card to 2040

Agents need a licence

Governments require AI agents acting outside their owner’s systems to carry a registered identity and a licence, and insurers will cover only licensed agents.

Built from the briefing’s Potential Surprise to 2040: AI agents need a licence to act, as drivers do (2029 to 2035, Low-Medium).

What the stress test shows
  1. There is a robust core, and it matches the briefing’s four Implications. Limiting and watching agent access, funding AI in stages against measured returns, and keeping the junior intake while redesigning the work hold up in all four futures.
  2. Each area has one fragile move, and they are the moves that cost least today. Relying on vendor assurances, spending to plan and cutting graduate intake each backfire in at least one future: where deployers are held to account, or where borrowed money meets late returns.
  3. Each bet turns on one axis. Pausing wide-access agents and building an academy pay off where deployers carry the risk; buying AI as you use it pays off where the build-out runs on borrowed money. The signposts in section 6 settle them.

3. What it means for the plan

Commit now

Robust: works in every future.

  • Limit each agent’s access, monitor it live and keep the evidence (Implications 1 and 2).
  • Fund AI in stages against measured returns, and test commitments for a correction (Implication 3).
  • Keep the graduate intake and redesign junior roles around checking AI (Implication 4).

Prepare, and act on a trigger

Bets: strong in some futures, weak in others.

  • Pausing wide-access agents until an independent test: release it if agent risk settles on the deployers.
  • Buying AI as you use it: released now, because the build-out has read on the borrowed-money side at every reading; keep it while that lasts.
  • An academy pairing juniors with agents: release it on the same trigger as the pause.

Do not rely on

Fragile: backfires in at least one future.

  • Relying on current controls and vendor assurances.
  • Spending to plan on suppliers’ return claims.
  • Cutting the graduate intake and letting AI absorb junior work.

4. Which future is gaining momentum, and how it has moved since September

Each future is a corner of the matrix, so the question “which future is gaining momentum” becomes two questions the evidence can answer: does the risk of agents fall on deployers or on those affected, and is the build-out paid for with borrowed money or from earnings? Twelve leading indicators answer them, six on each axis. Scored at each reading, they give a reading on each axis and a point on the matrix; the trail of points shows where the evidence is heading.

The pale band (the no-lead zone) marks readings within 15 points of an axis, where no future can lead. Hover over a point for its reading.

Build-out paid from: borrowed money, at every reading so far−29 in October. Debt and private credit carry the build-out; firms reporting value and Meta’s share sale have pulled the reading towards the centre.
Agent risk: close to the centre−7 in October. Government gates, the first enforcement and insurers’ terms push the risk onto deployers; unnoticed incidents, lagging controls and delayed rules push it onto those affected.

What would settle itA US rule making review a condition of release, or enforcement after an agent incident, moves the reading towards the deployers; another incident that goes unnoticed and unpunished moves it towards those affected. Firms reporting measured value, or more builders selling shares, move it towards earnings.
How the reading is scored
  1. Each indicator is marked Moving (+1), Pending (+½), Not yet (0) or Against (−1), on the evidence published by the date of the reading.
  2. Each carries a weight: 3 if it would settle its axis on its own, 2 if it is strong evidence, 1 if it supports.
  3. An axis reading is the weighted total for that axis, scaled from −100 (deployers, or borrowed money) to +100 (those affected, or earnings).
  4. A future leads when the reading sits in its corner, at least 15 points from both axes, for two readings running.
  5. A shift is a move of 10 points or more on either axis between readings. It is flagged and reviewed in the next quarterly briefing.
  6. A signpost reached (section 6) overrides the score and triggers its decision directly.

7. How the monitoring runs over time

Every weekSignal Scans attach new evidence to each indicator and signpost, including weak signals that do not yet fit any future.
Every monthThe Change Tracker rescores each indicator, the two axis readings are recalculated, and the trail on the matrix gains a point.
Every quarterThe Decision Intelligence briefing reviews any flagged shift, and redraws the scenarios, the System Map and this stress test.
When a signpost is reachedAction Triggers fire, releasing the bet prepared for it and naming the decision it changes.

8. Running it with your own plan

The options above are the briefing’s. The real value comes from replacing them with the organisation’s own plans and asking the same question of each: does it hold in every future?

BeforeThe organisation shares its AI, security, spending and workforce plans for the next few years; we grade each against the scenarios and the evidence base.
Session, 2 hours20 minutes in the four futures; 40 minutes in which each group lives in one future and answers “it is 2030: what do we wish we had done in 2026?”; 40 minutes testing the plan against the matrix; 20 minutes agreeing the robust core and the trigger for each bet.
AfterThe agreed matrix, indicators and signposts join the programme, and the monitoring cycle above keeps them current.
