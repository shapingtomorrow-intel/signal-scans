Where Politics and Power Could Hold Up the AI Build‑Out

Produced using Shaping Tomorrow’s six-stage process for converting external change into decision-ready intelligence. Scanned and filtered by AI. Stress-tested and judged by humans.

In brief

Politics now sets the clock for the AI build-out as much as engineering does. Of the seventeen places on this web, seven carry a high chance that a new data centre is late, costs more than planned or is stopped, and the chance is rising in twelve. The binding constraint differs by place: grid waits in Northern Europe, voters and permits in the US Midwest, who pays for power across the US and Australia, and conflict in the Gulf.

For insurers the main risk is delay, not cancellation. A site that cannot connect on time moves its tenant’s start date, and the strain passes down to contractors, equipment suppliers and their lenders and insurers through 2027. Twelve dated decisions in the next twelve months will move these colours.

An illustrative example for discussion, built only from public sources dated up to 6 November 2026. TexasHigh chance of delay
▲ rising since mid-2026		No new large data centre can switch on in the ERCOT grid until the state’s audit ends, and ERCOT must file it by 10 December (ERCOT, September 2026). Requests total about 474 GW, roughly 90% from data centres, and ERCOT says its large-load study will miss its April 2027 date (Utility Dive, August 2026). Since 21 September the state environmental regulator’s data-centre permits are paused too (Bracewell, September 2026), and the governor-elect has called for a moratorium on all approvals (Texas Tribune, November 2026).10 December 2026, ERCOT’s audit; 19 January 2027, the new governor takes office.
OhioHigh chance of delay
▲ rising since mid-2026		On 3 November every one of Ohio’s 21 local data-centre measures went against development, including bans or size caps in 11 places (our count from Ballotpedia’s tables) (Ballotpedia, November 2026). New sales-tax breaks for data centres are frozen (WOSU, May 2026), and a proposed constitutional ban on sites over 25 MW is cleared to collect signatures (Ballotpedia, November 2026).2 November 2027, a possible statewide vote on a ban.
United Arab EmiratesHigh chance of delay
▲ rising since mid-2026		Physical risk has overtaken chip access. Since July the UAE has had easier US export-control status, but licence-free access for G42 and Core42 expires on 6 April 2027 unless they become US companies (Federal Register, July 2026). After Iranian attacks the main AI campus is being redesigned as a network of sites, with unknown effect on cost and timing (Reuters, September 2026), and AWS says it cannot restore one of its UAE zones (The National, September 2026). Political-violence cover now costs 2% to 10% of the limit a year, and a single policy tops out at $100m to $300m (EnterpriseAM, October 2026).6 April 2027, G42 and Core42’s licence-free chip access ends unless renewed.
DenmarkHigh chance of delay
▲ rising since mid-2026		Energinet paused new large connections in March with about 60 GW of demand queued (Energinet, March 2026). In October it said full grid access looks difficult for the lowest-priority applicants, which include large consumption projects (Energinet, October 2026).November 2026, Energinet’s answers to the lowest-priority applicants.
Dublin, IrelandHigh chance of delay
▶ steady		EirGrid considers Greater Dublin fully constrained (EirGrid, May 2026), and data centres already use 23% of Ireland’s metered electricity (CSO, July 2026). New connections must bring their own generation (William Fry, December 2025), and that policy faces a High Court challenge (Irish Times, April 2026), listed for May 2027 (William Fry, June 2026).May 2027, the High Court hearing.
NetherlandsHigh chance of delay
▶ steady		Amsterdam now answers new applications above 5 MVA with “no, unless” and expects no new large connections until about 2032 (Gemeente Amsterdam, July 2026). A new high-voltage station takes about ten years to build (Dutch government, October 2026), a court has found the grid near Schiphol genuinely full (NL Times, April 2026), and parliament has called for a halt to large-scale data-centre building (NL Times, March 2026).The cabinet’s decision on further national rules (no date set).
Frankfurt, GermanyHigh chance of delay
▶ steady		New applicants in the Rhine-Main region will probably have to plan for a connection in the early or mid 2030s (ZfK, October 2026), and Frankfurt’s grid operator shares out new capacity above 3.5 MW pro rata (ingenieur.de, October 2026). Nationally, a draft law would delay data centres’ 100% renewables duty from 2027 to 2030, but the Bundestag has not yet passed it (CMS, July 2026).1 January 2027, the renewables duty applies under current law unless the amendment passes.
VirginiaPotential delay
▲ rising since mid-2026		The state regulator has ruled that data centres must pay upfront for the lines and substations that connect them, and Microsoft has filed notice of appeal (Utility Dive, September 2026; Virginia Mercury, August 2026). Loudoun County votes on 17 November on a pause of up to 12 months on some applications (Loudoun County, October 2026). Dominion has about 25,000 MW of large-load requests (WTVR CBS 6, October 2026).17 November 2026, Loudoun County’s vote on a pause.
GeorgiaPotential delay
▲ rising since mid-2026		On 3 November voters gave Democrats a majority on the utility regulator, which approves power contracts for data centres (Ballotpedia News, November 2026). About 100 projects needing nearly 20 GW are in the pipeline, and an incoming commissioner backs a pause of at least a year while rules are written (Bisnow, October 2026).The first data-centre power contracts the new majority reviews.
ArizonaPotential delay
▲ rising since mid-2026		The state regulator must vote by 31 December on the main utility’s rates, including what large loads pay (Arizona Corporation Commission, July 2026). The Attorney General has called for a pause on development; the governor refused, but lawmakers paused new tax credits for three years (KJZZ, August 2026; KJZZ, September 2026). Water used to generate data centres’ power could rise up to 400% (KJZZ, October 2026).31 December 2026, the regulator’s vote on large-load rates.
Saudi ArabiaPotential delay
▲ rising since mid-2026		No strike on a Saudi data centre has been confirmed, but the region’s risk applies (RBC Capital Markets, July 2026; Al Jazeera, October 2026). Humain plans a 1.7 GW build in 2027 (EnterpriseAM, September 2026) and is turning to outside investment as the state curbs spending; Saudi Arabia lacks the UAE’s export-control status (Fortune, September 2026). The OECD warns that disruption around Hormuz could delay or halt Gulf projects (AGBI, June 2026).End of 2026, Humain’s first 250 MW due online.
Johor, MalaysiaPotential delay
▲ rising since mid-2026		States without enough water can no longer host data centres (The Edge Malaysia, October 2026), and operators get no cheap power or water (The Sun, October 2026). There is no blanket moratorium (W.Media, October 2026). US rules now require licences for advanced chips sent to China-headquartered firms outside China, with Malaysia named (Reuters, June 2026).How the water test is applied to projects approved but not yet started.
AustraliaPotential delay
▲ rising since mid-2026		National Cabinet has backed mandatory standards that would make large data centres net generators and have them pay their full connection costs and for extra water infrastructure (Clayton Utz, August 2026). AEMO counts 225 data centres with 67 GW of capacity in development (AEMO, August 2026), but more than 40% of projects since 2025 have dropped out or slipped back in the queue (iTnews, August 2026).Early 2027, federal legislation for the standards.
Norway and FinlandPotential delay
▲ rising since mid-2026		Finland moved data-centre power into the standard tax class from 1 July 2026 (Finnish Government, October 2025), then dropped the support meant to replace it (Xinhua, September 2026), and plans to end first-come, first-served grid connections from 1 January 2027 (TechRadar, October 2026). In Norway, connections already approved could take data-centre use to 25 TWh, and parliament has debated a halt (Reuters, October 2026).1 January 2027, Finland’s new connection rules are due to start.
Aragón, SpainPotential delay
▲ rising since mid-2026		A draft decree would require at least 80% hour-by-hour renewable matching (MITECO, August 2026), and the minister hopes to approve it before the 29 November general election (Renewable Energy Magazine, October 2026). Aragón gets the most new grid capacity of any region in the revised plan, mostly for data centres (MITECO, October 2026), so the rules, not the grid, are the open question.29 November 2026, Spain’s general election.
Great BritainPotential delay
▶ steady		The demand connection queue more than tripled in less than a year, mostly from data centres, and Ofgem proposes an upfront fee that speculative projects would lose (Ofgem, July 2026). Against that, the Budget lets exceptionally strategic projects run on-site power for a time (Pinsent Masons, October 2026), and AI Growth Zones get power discounts and priority access from April 2027 (DSIT, November 2025). Risk is falling for favoured sites and rising for the rest.April 2027, AI Growth Zone discounts due to start.
SingaporeRelatively open
▶ steady		Capacity is scarce but the rules are clear. In August 200 MW was allocated to four operators (Singapore EDB, August 2026), and the need for another call will be reviewed in 18 to 24 months (W.Media, August 2026). Once a project has its allocation, little stands in its way.No dated decision expected in the next twelve months.

2. The dates that will move the colours

Twelve decisions between now and November 2027, each with a date set by a regulator, a court, a legislature or a voter. Most fall in the next six months, before the January renewals have bedded in.
