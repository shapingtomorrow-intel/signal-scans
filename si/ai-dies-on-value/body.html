Why This Matters Now

Two cycles ago the question was whether AI could do the work. For a narrow and identifiable set of tasks it is settled. What kills AI programmes in 2026 is not a capability ceiling but the discovery, at renewal, that nobody measured anything.

Three things happened between June and August. The lab behind the most cited measurement of AI's effect on skilled work walked it back, and says the design no longer works. National statistical offices in the United States and the United Kingdom decomposed what adopters actually do, and both found narrower use than headline rates imply. And the state, a buyer that runs no business cases, moved from encouraging adoption to setting targets and writing procurement preferences into statute.

The one thing to take into the next meeting: the binding constraint has moved from the contract layer to the evidence layer, and the early warning is the first AI renewal approved without a measured before-and-after.

Executive Synthesis

Can anyone here show what actually changed after an AI tool went in, or only that the people using it are happy with it?

The group behind this field's most-cited productivity measurement has stopped running the experiment, and reports that people overestimate AI's effect on their own time by 40 percentage points.

A second constraint has hardened beneath the contract, insurance and compute layers named in the last edition, and it decides which projects survive a budget round: organisations renewing AI spending find the evidence they hold is testimony rather than measurement.

METR's 2025 randomised trial found that When developers are allowed to use AI tools, they take 19% longer to complete issues, while those developers believed they had been sped up (METR, July 2025). In February 2026 the same group restated it as Our early 2025 study found the use of AI causes tasks to take 19% longer, with a confidence interval between +2% and +39%., reported a follow-up interval that now includes zero, and abandoned the design, because developers who will not work without AI select themselves out of the control arm (METR, February 2026).

Self-report fills the vacuum, and it errs in a measured direction. METR's survey work warns that people misjudge the effect on their time spent on tasks by 40 percentage points on average (METR, May 2026), and the same holds at the top, where a study of nearly 750 executives finds that We document a productivity paradox, in which perceived productivity gains are larger than measured productivity gains (NBER, March 2026).

The field's most rigorous instrument has been retired, and what fills the vacuum is self-report.

Even the denominator is unstable. The Federal Reserve's review of US adoption measurement notes that Business survey data from the Census Bureau show that about 18 percent of firms have adopted AI as of year-end 2025., while other instruments put it at 41% of individuals and 78% of the labour force at adopting firms, a spread driven by question wording (Federal Reserve, April 2026). For a board, being behind on those numbers is a claim about wording, not evidence.

Three consequences follow. What is bought is often not what the category name implies, with Gartner estimates only about 130 of the thousands of agentic AI vendors are real. and more than 40% of agentic projects forecast for cancellation by end-2027 (Gartner, June 2025). Where AI works it works narrowly, with 57% of users integrate AI in three or fewer business functions, most commonly Sales and Marketing (52%), Strategy and Business Development (45%), and IT (41%). among adopting US firms (US Census Bureau, April 2026). The automation that is measurably scaling is physical, and was never sold as AI.

Underneath all three sits a buyer that does not apply the value test. China's State Council has set a 70% AI agent penetration target for 2027, and Korea has written a preference for AI products into procurement law. Where the state is the demand floor, the value test does not arise, and a strategy function should plan for a two-speed world.

Where this analysis could be wrong

The load-bearing assumption is that missing measurement means missing value. It may mean only missing measurement. Randomised field experiments at a cross-border online retailer, at production scale with hard sales outcomes, found that We find that GenAI adoption significantly increases sales, with treatment effects ranging from 0% to 16.3% (Toulouse School of Economics, October 2025): the design this briefing says almost nobody runs, and the answer was positive. On agents, the Remote Labor Index records 15.8% of real paid projects completed at client-acceptable quality, against 2.5% at launch: a fast-moving line this briefing may be reading as structural. What settles it is whether any organisation you deal with can produce a controlled before-and-after on a live deployment. If several can within twelve months, the measurement problem was a lag, not a void.

The decisions that cannot be deferred this cycle

  1. Stop accepting self-report as evidence of value. Renewals rest on a number the field's own researchers show is biased by roughly 40 percentage points in a known direction.
  2. Re-contract the agent layer on demonstrated task completion. The category label carries no information, the benchmarks that verify it have been found compromised, and cancellation rates are forecast above 40%.
  3. Separate the automation portfolio from the AI portfolio. The two sit on different evidence bases, time constants and budget lines; merging them hides the part that is working.

Each is developed below, with a decision posture, in the four Strategic Implications.

Audience Snapshots

Four lenses on the same evidence base, each with the question this cycle puts to that audience.

Boards and chief executives

What would it take for this board to know whether its AI spending worked?

The shift: The question has moved from whether to invest to whether anyone can demonstrate a return.

The question to brief: Of the AI commitments renewed in the last twelve months, how many rested on a controlled comparison rather than on users who felt faster?

Technology and operations leaders

How many of the systems in the AI portfolio meet the standards-body definition of an agent?

The shift: Agent reliability is improving far more slowly than capability, and the benchmarks that verify it have been found compromised.

The question to brief: Which vendors can demonstrate autonomous completion of a whole task on your own data, and which only a benchmark score?

Risk, legal and procurement

What does the contract entitle you to if the system is not what the category name implies?

The shift: Regulators have moved from principles to enforcement on AI claims, and insurers are writing absolute AI exclusions into standard lines.

The question to brief: Do your AI contracts define acceptance against a measured task-completion rate, and price the exit if it is not met?

Policymakers and investors reading diffusion as competitiveness

Is a national adoption rate a measure of advantage, or a measure of activity?

The shift: Seven jurisdictions have set adoption targets, procurement preferences or subsidised compute, while auditors find no measurement behind the spending.

The question to brief: If penetration reaches its target and measured productivity does not move, what would the policy response be, and who would notice first?

1. AI's benefits are self-reported and largely unmeasured

Immediate

The evidence base for AI productivity got weaker between June 2025 and August 2026, not stronger. The one thing measured consistently is the gap between what people believe AI did for them and what it did.

Figure 1: One economy, four adoption rates, all from reputable instruments

US figures as reviewed by the Federal Reserve, April 2026. The UK bar is from the Office for National Statistics, July 2026, shown for scale on a different population; it is not part of the Federal Reserve comparison.

  • The field's most rigorous result has been qualified by its own authors. METR restated that Our early 2025 study found the use of AI causes tasks to take 19% longer, with a confidence interval between +2% and +39%., reported a follow-up estimate of minus 18% on an interval from minus 38 to plus 9, and is abandoning the randomised design because developers self-select out of the control arm (METR, February 2026). The original finding: When developers are allowed to use AI tools, they take 19% longer to complete issues, while believing the opposite (METR, July 2025).
  • What replaced it is self-report with a health warning attached. A survey of 349 technical workers reports a median threefold speed increase, published alongside the authors' own finding that people misjudge the effect on their time spent on tasks by 40 percentage points on average (METR, May 2026).
  • The same gap appears in the executive suite: We document a productivity paradox, in which perceived productivity gains are larger than measured productivity gains, across nearly 750 corporate executives (NBER, March 2026).
  • The denominator will not hold still either. Business survey data from the Census Bureau show that about 18 percent of firms have adopted AI as of year-end 2025., against 41% of individuals and 78% of the labour force at adopting firms on other instruments (Federal Reserve, April 2026).
  • The most-quoted number in the debate is the weakest. The claim that 95% of organizations are getting zero return (MIT NANDA, July 2025) rests on 52 interviews and a paper survey of another 153 people, scores as failures firms that never ran a pilot, and requires a sustained profit-and-loss impact within six months to count as success (80,000 Hours, April 2026). The briefing carries it as an artefact of the debate, not as evidence within it.

Counter-argument

Measurement is possible, is being done, and produces positive answers. Randomised field experiments across seven generative-AI workflows at a cross-border online retailer, covering millions of users and products with prices held constant, found that We find that GenAI adoption significantly increases sales, with treatment effects ranging from 0% to 16.3% (Toulouse School of Economics, October 2025). That is a hard commercial outcome at scale, and the design this theme complains nobody runs. The honest reading is narrower than the theme's headline: the evidence is not absent, it is scarce and expensive, and the range from zero to 16.3% across workflows inside one firm is itself the point. One of the seven tested negative. An organisation that measures will find some deployments working and some not, a more useful position than faith or scepticism.

2. Most products sold as AI agents are not agents

Immediate

There is now an authoritative definition of an agent, which turns a matter of taste into a matter of fact. The buyer's natural response to a mislabelled category is to ask for benchmark evidence, and that is where a procurement irritation becomes a governance problem: the verification layer is compromised at the moment buyers start relying on it.

  • The definition exists and is public. Agentic AI refers to artificial intelligence systems that function as autonomous agents capable of independently making decisions, learning from interactions, and adapting to changing environments. (NIST, July 2026). Independent decision-making and adaptation are the test, and most products in the category do neither.
  • The market estimate is stark: Gartner estimates only about 130 of the thousands of agentic AI vendors are real., with over 40% of agentic projects forecast to be cancelled by end-2027 on escalating cost, unclear value or inadequate risk controls (Gartner, June 2025). A year on it became category-specific buyer guidance: agent washing further obscures those differences by relabeling conventional automation as agentic, increasing the risk of misaligned investments and long-term lock-in. (Gartner, May 2026).
  • The verification route is compromised. An audit of agent benchmarks reports that We audit 2,385 traces across 15 agent benchmarks and find evidence of exposures and reward hacking in 67.0% of Frontier Science traces and 66.7% of AutoLab tasks., with measured score inflation between 0.45 and 1.00 (arXiv preprint, July 2026). A preprint rather than a refereed paper, and treated directionally, but the direction matters.
  • Capability and reliability have come apart, the mechanism behind the cycle's title. Evaluating 14 agentic models across two complementary benchmarks, we find that recent capability gains have only yielded small improvements in reliability. (arXiv preprint, February 2026). Delegating consequential action depends on the second quantity, which is not moving.
  • The pattern has regulatory history. The UK advertising regulator reviewed around 16,000 ads using AI as a marketing term in a single quarter, finding most claims for established consumer products were general descriptions rather than substantiable statements (Advertising Standards Authority, November 2024), and the US Federal Trade Commission has enforced the principle that existing consumer-protection law applies to AI claims without exemption (Federal Trade Commission, September 2024).

Counter-argument

The category is not fake. On the Remote Labor Index, which grades whole real paid projects by whether a client would accept the work, the best system now completes 15.8% against 2.5% at launch, and the authors note that The frontier has more than quadrupled in under eight months, a concrete signal of how quickly economically capable AI agents are advancing. (Center for AI Safety, July 2026). Roughly 84% of real economic tasks still fail, which supports the theme; but a fourfold rise in eight months is not a plateau, and a procurement policy written to today's failure rate will be wrong within a year. The claim defended here is the narrow one, that most products sold in the category are mislabelled, not the broader claim that the category does not work.

3. AI works on a narrower set of tasks than the pitch suggests

Immediate

Two national statistical offices have now measured this from opposite directions and agree: adoption is broadening across firms while staying flat in depth inside them. The pitch is general capability. The measurement is a narrow band.

  • US national data decomposes it to the business function: Among adopting firms, the scope of use remains limited: 57% of users integrate AI in three or fewer business functions, most commonly Sales and Marketing (52%), Strategy and Business Development (45%), and IT (41%)., with 65% of worker use confined to three or fewer tasks, led by writing, document analysis and information search (US Census Bureau, April 2026).
  • UK national data measures the same thing on a different axis and finds it flat: since September 2023 the average number of AI technologies used per business has increased only modestly, rising from around 1.4 to 1.6 (Figure 2)., while headline adoption among businesses with ten or more employees nearly tripled from around 12% to around 35% (Office for National Statistics, July 2026). Breadth is rising; depth is not.
  • Where deployment is real, regulated and countable, it is concentrated in one speciality: A total of 950 AI/ML devices were authorized by the FDA, of which 723 were radiology devices (76%)., and only 29% of those authorisations incorporated clinical testing (JAMA Network Open, November 2025).
  • The productivity effect is concentrated too. Across 5,179 customer support agents, Access to the tool increases productivity, as measured by issues resolved per hour, by 14% on average, including a 34% improvement for novice and low-skilled workers but with minimal impact on experienced and highly skilled workers. (NBER, April 2023). Alongside METR, the gradient is the same and the sign flips at the experienced end.
  • Model-side usage data points at the same band: Computer and Mathematical occupations account for roughly 30% of measured usage, far above their 4% share of US employment, with explanations, documents and guidance the commonest outputs (Anthropic, June 2026). First-party vendor telemetry for one model, so directional, but it converges with the supply-side finding.
  • Two mechanisms explain the boundary. Agents degrade non-linearly as tasks lengthen, because Large language model (LLM) agents perform strongly on short- and mid-horizon tasks, but often break down on long-horizon tasks that require extended, interdependent action sequences. (arXiv preprint, April 2026), and tasks demanding accountability for a consequential output leave a visible failure trail: a running census of court and tribunal decisions on AI-hallucinated material in legal filings now records 1,922 decisions across 49 jurisdictions (AI Hallucination Cases Database, August 2026).

Counter-argument

Narrow is not the same as small. The ILO and NASK index finds a quarter of global employment sits in occupations with some generative-AI task exposure, rising to 34% in high-income countries, while noting that Full job automation, however, remains limited, since many tasks, though done more efficiently, continue to require human involvement. (International Labour Organization, May 2025). A band that touches a quarter of global employment is an enormous economic surface. There is also a timing objection: the Census and ONS data describe 2025 and early 2026 deployment, and a snapshot of a fast-moving diffusion curve is not a structural law. The defensible version is not that AI will remain narrow, but that it is narrow now, measurably so, and that a business case written against general capability is being tested against something the evidence does not yet describe.

Decision link: Strategic Implications 1 and 3.

4. Physical automation is scaling while office pilots stall

Near-Term

Two automation stories are running at once. One is measured, capital-intensive, sits in operations budgets and has a decades-old evidence base. The other is the AI programme portfolio. The claim is not that physical automation is booming everywhere, but that returns are counted in one domain and asserted in the other.

Figure 2: Where automation is counted, and where it is asserted

Density and installation figures from the International Federation of Robotics, April and September 2025 to May 2026. The right-hand panel juxtaposes two kinds of evidence deliberately, to show the asymmetry in measurement rather than to compare quantities.

  • The base is large and still growing. The total number of industrial robots in operational use worldwide was 4,664,000 units in 2024, on 542,000 installations, the fourth consecutive year above half a million (International Federation of Robotics, September 2025).
  • The concentration is national. China´s manufacturing industry already has an operational stock of around 2 million units, roughly 4.5 times Japan's, taking 54% of 2024 installations, with domestic suppliers rising from 30% in 2020 to 57% in 2024 (International Federation of Robotics, May 2026).
  • Density tells a more disciplined story. The Republic of Korea records the world´s highest robot density with 1,220 robots per 10,000 employees, growing by 7% on average annually since 2019., ahead of Singapore, Germany, Japan and the United States, while China sits 22nd at 166 (International Federation of Robotics, April 2026). Volume leadership and intensity leadership are not the same thing.
  • The Chinese state is directing embodied systems into production rather than pilots. A joint ministerial instrument sets out to 带动形成万台级规模落地能力, that is, to build a ten-thousand-unit-scale deployment capability, alongside more than a hundred high-value application scenarios in real production settings (MIIT and SASAC, June 2026). It is a target document, so the figure is an objective rather than an outcome.
  • The adjacent domain is moving too. In the 2026 MHI and Deloitte annual industry report, robotics and automation was rated significant or greater in disruptive impact by 39% rating its impact as significant or greater, up 16 percentage points year on year (MHI and Deloitte, April 2026). A practitioner perception survey, so directional, but the size of the single-year jump is the signal.

Weak signals to watch

  • Emerging Humanoid systems moving from demonstration to line. Roland Berger models a 300 billion dollar humanoid market at original-equipment level by 2035, while warning that Hardware maturity does not mean market readiness. The broader ecosystem still trails by three to five years, held back by immature supply chains and fragmented regulatory frameworks, while the software-to-hardware gap persists. (Roland Berger, April 2026). Consultancy modelling, carried directionally.
  • Watch Whether the sectoral rotation in North American orders persists. Robot orders held at 9,055 units in the first quarter of 2026 while automotive original-equipment orders fell 35.1% and life sciences rose 54.1% (Association for Advancing Automation, May 2026). A flat headline concealing a collapse in the anchor sector is a composition change that shows up in capital plans a year later.
  • Watch Whether Europe's automation supply base stabilises. Germany's trade federation forecasts national robotics and automation revenue falling a further 5% in 2026 to 14.1 billion euros, after a 7% decline the year before (VDMA Robotik und Automation, February 2026). The country third in the world for robot density is watching its own automation industry shrink two years running.

Counter-argument

The German and North American figures above suggest the real story is not automation versus AI but Asia versus Europe and North America, with China's volume growth substituting domestic supply for imports rather than expanding the market. There is a measurement objection too: robot installations count units shipped, not value realised, and a national target to build ten-thousand-unit deployment capability is the kind of unaudited adoption claim this briefing criticises when a software vendor makes it. Physical automation has thirty years of consistently collected data and a unit of account everyone agrees on; enterprise AI has neither. That asymmetry, not the growth rate, is what should change how the two are appraised.

5. Governments are mandating adoption, not waiting for business cases

Longer-Range

While private programmes are being killed on unproven value, a different class of buyer has removed the value test altogether. Seven jurisdictions now set AI deployment by decree, statute or subsidy rather than by appraisal. This is a five-year strategic variable rather than a this-quarter one, which is why it is rated Longer-Range.

  • The hardest instrument is Chinese and numerical. The State Council's guideline states the objective that 到2027年，率先实现人工智能与6大重点领域广泛深度融合，新一代智能终端、智能体等应用普及率超70%, that is, by 2027 broad and deep integration of AI across six key domains, with penetration of new-generation intelligent terminals and AI agents exceeding 70%, rising above 90% by 2030 (State Council of the People's Republic of China, August 2025).
  • Japan's statutory plan is qualitative but no less directive: the Cabinet decision commits the country to an AI transformation in which あらゆる組織で、ＡＩを前提として意思決定や業務の進め方を根底から見直す「ＡＩトランスフォーメーション（ＡＸ）」を進めていく, that is, every organisation rethinks its decision-making and working practices on the premise of AI, framed as a means of strengthening national power (Cabinet Office, Government of Japan, July 2026).
  • Korea has made it a purchasing duty. Reporting on the enforcement decree notes that AI 기본법 제16조 제3항은 국가기관 등이 제품·서비스를 구매하거나 용역을 발주할 때 AI 제품·서비스를 우선 고려하도록 규정하고 있습니다., that is, Article 16(3) of the AI Framework Act requires state agencies to give priority to AI products and services when purchasing or commissioning work (Lawtimes, May 2026). A trade publication rather than the statute portal, which refuses automated retrieval, so treated accordingly.
  • The European Union has moved from governing risk to prosecuting diffusion. Of the Apply AI Strategy the Commission says: It encourages an AI first policy, so more companies consider AI as a part of the solution to tackle challenges, while taking into careful consideration the benefits and the risks of the technology., with around a billion euros mobilised behind uptake (European Commission, October 2025).
  • The liberal-market versions buy diffusion rather than mandate it. The United Kingdom designated five AI Growth Zones, generating £28.2 billion in investment, creating more than 15,000 jobs, and providing £5 million of targeted funding for each zone to drive adoption at the local level (Department for Science, Innovation and Technology, January 2026), and India reports that More than 38 thousand GPUs for common compute facility have been onboarded through the AI compute portal, which are being provided to Indian start-ups and academia at an affordable rate. (Press Information Bureau, India, March 2026).
  • Dubai has set the clock for its private sector rather than for itself, providing under a two-year agentic-AI transition programme that As part of the two-year transformation programme, specialised training tracks will be introduced for all business councils affiliated with the Dubai Chamber of Commerce. (Dubai Media Office, May 2026).

Counter-argument

If states are diffusing AI without a value test, they are accumulating the same unproven-value problem that is killing corporate projects, only later, at public expense, and without a cancellation mechanism. The US Government Accountability Office found that Agency officials from DOD, DHS, GSA, and VA told us they did not systematically document lessons from their AI acquisition efforts because their departmental policies and guidance do not require them to do so., while reported federal AI use more than doubled between 2023 and 2024 (US Government Accountability Office, April 2026). That is a review of selected agencies, so it establishes an evidence gap rather than a failure of the deployments. State mandates do not mean governments know something the market does not; they are running the same experiment with a longer feedback loop and a weaker stopping rule. The first national audit of whether a penetration target produced value will matter for this file.

Strategic Implications

Four decisions turn this cycle's evidence into the next planning round. Each names a move, a date and a posture.

SI 1: Require a measured before and after on every AI renewal above a defined threshold

Any renewal above a stated value should carry a comparison against a period, team or workflow without the tool, reported in the unit the business already uses. Without one, an organisation cannot tell which of its deployments is producing value. That is a rule, not an evaluation programme. Deployments that cannot support the comparison should be named as acts of faith and sized accordingly, a legitimate position honestly held.

Action: the executive to set a renewal threshold above which a controlled or quasi-controlled before-and-after is mandatory, and to apply it to every AI renewal falling due from 31 October 2026.

Decide Draws on Themes 1 and 3

SI 2: Re-contract the agent layer on demonstrated task completion rather than on category language

Acceptance should turn on a measured completion rate on the organisation's own representative tasks, agreed before purchase, with a defined remedy if it is not met. A benchmark score cannot do that work. It is also the cheapest protection against the forecast cancellation rate, converting a cancellation into a contractual exit rather than a write-off.

Action: procurement and legal to add a demonstrated task-completion acceptance clause, with an exit remedy, to the standard AI contract template, applying to all new agentic purchases from 31 December 2026.

Decide Draws on Themes 2 and 1

SI 3: Appraise the physical and process automation portfolio separately from the AI portfolio

Different evidence bases, time constants and budget lines mean that a single digital or AI programme hides the part with a measurable return. Separation shows where the returns sit, protects the automation case from the credibility damage that will follow the first visible AI write-down, and surfaces the geographic asymmetry: a contracting supply base in Europe against rising intensity in Asia.

Action: finance and operations to report physical and process automation as a distinct investment line, with its own measured return, in the plan submitted for the financial year beginning after 31 March 2027.

Prepare Draws on Themes 4 and 3

SI 4: Track state adoption mandates as a demand and market-access variable, not as policy background

A state mandate is at once a demand floor independent of any proven return, a compliance variable that may arrive before the internal case is ready, and a reason to distrust diffusion rates read as competitive position. The watch should be specific: hard numerical targets, statutory procurement preferences, and any audit body asking whether adoption produced value.

Action: strategy to add a state-mandate column to the quarterly vendor and market map, covering China, Korea, Japan, the European Union, the United Kingdom, India and the United Arab Emirates, reporting quarterly through 31 December 2027.

Monitor Draws on Themes 5 and 4

The audited build

Controlled evaluation becomes normal practice, as the Toulouse experiments show it can, and buyers rather than governments set the pace. Spending concentrates where returns are demonstrable, and the agent category splits between a few vendors that can prove task completion and a long tail that cannot. The risk is over-correction: measurement favours what is easy to measure, so deployments with long or diffuse payoffs get cut alongside those that never worked.

Early indicators: a replacement for METR's abandoned design from any credible group; national statistical offices adding outcome as well as adoption questions; the first large enterprise publishing a controlled AI result against its own financials.

Directed and measured

States keep setting the pace but bring auditors with them. Public-sector deployment at scale becomes the reference dataset the private sector never built, and audit-office reports on penetration targets are the best evidence a corporate buyer has. Procurement preferences give domestic vendors a demand floor, so competitive position turns partly on jurisdiction, and policy documents become a primary source for capability assessment, not where most organisations look.

Early indicators: an audit body publishing outcome rather than process findings on a national AI programme; Korea or China publishing measured penetration against target; procurement preferences appearing in trade negotiations.

The quiet write-down

No credible measurement arrives and the market decides on sentiment. Spending continues on faith for another budget cycle or two, then stops abruptly when a visible failure gives permission. The correction cannot then separate deployments that worked from those that did not, so both are cut. Those that suffer least are the organisations that kept records, not the ones that chose best. Current evidence points here, which is why the first Strategic Implication is a measurement rule.

Early indicators: agentic cancellation rates tracking toward the forecast 40%; insurers widening absolute AI exclusions; AI spending reported as a cost rather than an investment line in annual reports.

Adoption by decree

Penetration targets are met on paper while measured productivity does not move, and no mechanism requires anyone to investigate the gap. Diffusion statistics become a competitiveness narrative detached from output, and firms in mandate jurisdictions carry costs they did not choose. The tell is an absence: no audit, no published outcome data, and a widening gap between reported adoption and productivity in the national accounts. An international organisation ends up running two operating models, one appraised and one complied with.

Early indicators: penetration targets restated or redefined rather than missed; audit findings on process without outcome, as in the GAO review; adoption rates rising while measured productivity growth stays flat.

What We Are Not Planning For

Capability

A frontier capability discontinuity that makes the measurement question moot

A system that is obviously and generally more productive would need no controlled trial, and this briefing's argument dissolves. The possibility is real but excluded, because no action taken today is improved by assuming it. This cycle's evidence points the other way, with reliability improving far more slowly than capability.

↺ Reinstated if a single system produces an unambiguous, externally verified step change on whole real economic tasks rather than on benchmarks.

Agents

General-purpose agents clearing the majority of real paid work inside this horizon

The Remote Labor Index has moved from 2.5% to 15.8% in under eight months, and a straight line would cross a majority within this horizon. Benchmarks rarely extrapolate that way, the remaining tasks are harder than those already cleared, and reliability rather than capability binds. Theme 2 treats the trajectory as a serious counter-argument, not a planning assumption.

↺ Reinstated if the index passes 40%, or if a comparable measure on client-accepted work shows the same slope in a second independent benchmark.

Policy

A coordinated retreat from state adoption targets

Mandates could be quietly abandoned if measured returns disappoint, as some emissions targets have been re-dated. Nothing in this cycle suggests it: the movement runs the other way, with new instruments in Japan and Korea this year and the European Union deferring compliance obligations rather than adoption ambitions. Planning for a retreat would discount a demand floor that exists.

↺ Reinstated if any of the seven jurisdictions in SI 4 formally revises a numerical adoption target downward or repeals a procurement preference.

Questions For The Next Strategy Meeting

Five questions this cycle's evidence should put on the agenda.

  1. Could the organisation produce a controlled before-and-after for its three largest AI deployments this quarter? If not, on what basis were they renewed?
  2. Which deployments would we find hardest to withdraw for a control arm, and is that attachment evidence of value or of habit?
  3. Among US firms that adopt AI, 57% use it in three or fewer functions. Which three are ours, and was that the plan or the outcome?
  4. Does our capital process appraise physical automation and enterprise AI on the same standard of evidence, and should it, when only one has an agreed unit of account and thirty years of data?
  5. In the markets where five governments now set adoption targets or procurement preferences, does that create demand to position for, an obligation to prepare for, or a distortion to discount?
