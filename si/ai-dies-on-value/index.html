Key opportunities

  • Organisations that measure what AI changed can tell sooner which programmes deserve renewal. Controlled experiments at an online retailer found sales effects from 0% to 16.3% across workflows, showing which ones paid (Toulouse School of Economics, October 2025). See Theme 1 →

Why This Matters Now

For a narrow and identifiable set of tasks, AI works. What kills AI programmes now is not a capability ceiling but the discovery, at renewal, that nobody measured anything.

Between June and August the lab behind the most-cited measurement of AI's effect on skilled work walked it back. Statistics offices in the US and UK found narrower use than headline rates imply. And states, buyers that run no business cases, moved from encouraging adoption to setting targets.

The one thing to take into the next meeting: the constraint has moved from contracts to evidence, and the warning sign is the first AI renewal approved without a measured before-and-after.

Executive Synthesis

Can anyone here show what actually changed after an AI tool went in, or only that the people using it are happy with it?

The group behind this field's most-cited productivity measurement has stopped running the experiment, and reports that people overestimate AI's effect on their own time by 40 percentage points.

AI programmes are now cut for want of evidence, not capability. In the most-cited trial of AI's effect on skilled work, developers took 19% longer with AI while believing they were faster (METR, July 2025); that design is now being abandoned, because developers who will not work without AI leave the control group (METR, February 2026). Self-report fills the gap, and people misjudge AI's effect on their time by 40 percentage points on average (METR, May 2026).

Some of this is settled: among US firms that use AI, 57% use it in three or fewer business functions (US Census Bureau, April 2026), and Gartner estimates that only about 130 of the thousands of agentic AI vendors are real (Gartner, June 2025). Three things are not. Will a trial design that works at scale replace the abandoned one? Will agents become reliable enough to delegate to, when recent capability gains have brought only small improvements in reliability (arXiv preprint, February 2026)? And will states keep setting adoption by target, as China has with above 70% penetration of AI agents by 2027 (State Council of the People's Republic of China, August 2025)?

Looking ahead to 2030, executives in the US, UK, Germany and Australia forecast that AI will boost their firms' productivity by 1.4 percent over the next three years (Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta, March 2026), Gartner expects 33% of enterprise software applications to include agentic AI by 2028 (Gartner, June 2025), the International Federation of Robotics expects annual robot installations to pass 700,000 by 2028 (International Federation of Robotics, September 2025), and China's State Council targets penetration of AI agents above 90% by 2030 (State Council of the People's Republic of China, August 2025). None of this is certain, and each is a place where readers can still choose their position: section 10 sets out the options.

Where this analysis could be wrong

The weakest assumption is that missing measurement means missing value; it may mean only missing measurement. Randomised experiments at a cross-border online retailer found sales effects ranging from 0% to 16.3% (Toulouse School of Economics, October 2025), the design this briefing says almost nobody runs, with a positive answer. If several organisations can show a controlled before-and-after on a live deployment within twelve months, the measurement problem was a lag, not a void.

The decisions that cannot be deferred this cycle

  1. Stop accepting self-report as evidence of value. It is biased by roughly 40 percentage points in a known direction.
  2. Re-contract the agent layer on demonstrated task completion. The label carries no information and the benchmarks are compromised.
  3. Separate the automation portfolio from the AI portfolio. Merging them hides the part that is working.

Each is developed below, with a decision posture, in the four Strategic Implications, and with alternatives in the Options.

1. AI's benefits are self-reported and largely unmeasured

Immediate

Weight: high · Who decides: Each organisation's executive and finance teams · Room to act: High: any organisation can measure its own deployments

The evidence base for AI productivity got weaker between June 2025 and August 2026, not stronger. The one thing measured consistently is the gap between what people believe AI did for them and what it did.

Figure 1: One economy, four adoption rates, all from reputable instruments

US figures as reviewed by the Federal Reserve, April 2026. The UK bar is from the Office for National Statistics, July 2026, shown for scale on a different population; it is not part of the Federal Reserve comparison.

  • The field's most rigorous result has been qualified by its own authors. METR restated that Our early 2025 study found the use of AI causes tasks to take 19% longer, with a confidence interval between +2% and +39%. Its follow-up interval now includes zero, and it is abandoning the randomised design because developers self-select out of the control arm (METR, February 2026). The original finding: When developers are allowed to use AI tools, they take 19% longer to complete issues, while developers believed the opposite (METR, July 2025).
  • Self-report fills the gap, with a known bias. A survey of 349 technical workers reports a median threefold speed increase, alongside the authors' finding that people misjudge the effect on their time spent on tasks by 40 percentage points on average (METR, May 2026). Among nearly 750 executives, We document a productivity paradox, in which perceived productivity gains are larger than measured productivity gains (NBER, March 2026).
  • Even the denominator will not hold still: Business survey data from the Census Bureau show that about 18 percent of firms have adopted AI as of year-end 2025. Other instruments put adoption at 41% of individuals and 78% of the labour force at adopting firms (Federal Reserve, April 2026).
  • The most-quoted number is the weakest. The claim that 95% of organizations are getting zero return (MIT NANDA, July 2025) rests on 52 interviews and a paper survey of another 153 people, and scores as failures firms that never ran a pilot (80,000 Hours, April 2026).

Outlook to 2030

Executives in the US, UK, Germany and Australia forecast that AI will boost their firms' productivity by 1.4 percent over the next three years, while more than 80 percent of firms report no impact so far (Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta, March 2026). On this briefing's reading, by 2030 the gap will be settled firm by firm, by those that measure.

What it means: Start measuring now, so the comparison exists when the forecast falls due.

Counter-argument

Measurement is possible and can come back positive. Randomised experiments across seven generative-AI workflows at a cross-border online retailer found: We find that GenAI adoption significantly increases sales, with treatment effects ranging from 0% to 16.3% (Toulouse School of Economics, October 2025). The evidence is scarce rather than absent, and an organisation that measures will find some deployments working and some not.

On the Causal Loop Diagram: R1 Testimony renews itself and B2 Measuring closes the gap; which wins decides whether renewals rest on evidence.

2. Most products sold as AI agents are not agents

Immediate

Weight: high · Who decides: Buyers, through contracts; standards bodies and consumer regulators · Room to act: High for buyers, through acceptance terms

There is now an authoritative definition of an agent, which turns a matter of taste into a matter of fact. Buyers who turn to benchmark evidence instead meet a verification layer that is compromised just as they start relying on it.

  • The definition is public: Agentic AI refers to artificial intelligence systems that function as autonomous agents capable of independently making decisions, learning from interactions, and adapting to changing environments. (NIST, July 2026). Regulators already police AI claims: the UK advertising regulator reviewed around 16,000 ads using AI as a marketing term in one quarter (Advertising Standards Authority, November 2024), and the US Federal Trade Commission allows no AI exemption (Federal Trade Commission, September 2024).
  • The market estimate is stark: Gartner estimates only about 130 of the thousands of agentic AI vendors are real. Over 40% of agentic projects are forecast to be cancelled by end-2027 (Gartner, June 2025), and a year on Gartner warns that agent washing further obscures those differences by relabeling conventional automation as agentic, increasing the risk of misaligned investments and long-term lock-in. (Gartner, May 2026)
  • The verification route is compromised: We audit 2,385 traces across 15 agent benchmarks and find evidence of exposures and reward hacking in 67.0% of Frontier Science traces and 66.7% of AutoLab tasks. (arXiv preprint, July 2026). A preprint, carried directionally.
  • Capability and reliability have come apart: Evaluating 14 agentic models across two complementary benchmarks, we find that recent capability gains have only yielded small improvements in reliability. (arXiv preprint, February 2026). Delegating consequential action depends on the second, which is not moving.

Outlook to 2030

Gartner forecasts that 33% of enterprise software applications will include agentic AI by 2028, up from less than 1% in 2024 (Gartner, June 2025). On this briefing's reading, by 2030 agents will sit inside software organisations already license, so the question becomes what the contract makes them prove.

What it means: Write completion-based acceptance into the standard contract now.

Counter-argument

The category is not fake. On the Remote Labor Index the best agent now completes 15.8% of real paid projects, against 2.5% at launch, and The frontier has more than quadrupled in under eight months, a concrete signal of how quickly economically capable AI agents are advancing. (Center for AI Safety, July 2026). The claim defended here is the narrow one: most products sold in the category are mislabelled.

On the Causal Loop Diagram: mislabelled agents, compromised benchmarks and slow gains in reliability all feed B1 The write-down.

Decision link: Strategic Implications 2 and 1.

3. AI works on a narrower set of tasks than the pitch suggests

Immediate

Weight: medium · Who decides: Each organisation, through where it deploys AI · Room to act: Moderate: firms choose the tasks, not what the technology can do

Two national statistical offices have now measured this from opposite directions and agree: adoption is broadening across firms while staying flat in depth inside them. The pitch is general capability; the measurement is a narrow band.

  • US data decomposes it to the business function: Among adopting firms, the scope of use remains limited: 57% of users integrate AI in three or fewer business functions, most commonly Sales and Marketing (52%), Strategy and Business Development (45%), and IT (41%). Worker use is confined to three or fewer tasks in 65% of cases (US Census Bureau, April 2026).
  • UK data finds the same on another axis: the average number of AI technologies used per business has increased only modestly, rising from around 1.4 to 1.6 (Figure 2). Headline adoption among businesses with ten or more employees nearly tripled, from around 12% to around 35% (Office for National Statistics, July 2026).
  • The gains are concentrated too. Across 5,179 customer support agents, Access to the tool increases productivity, as measured by issues resolved per hour, by 14% on average, including a 34% improvement for novice and low-skilled workers but with minimal impact on experienced and highly skilled workers. (NBER, April 2023).
  • Two mechanisms set the boundary: Large language model (LLM) agents perform strongly on short- and mid-horizon tasks, but often break down on long-horizon tasks that require extended, interdependent action sequences. (arXiv preprint, April 2026). And tasks that carry accountability leave a trail: a running census records 1,922 court and tribunal decisions on AI-hallucinated material in legal filings across 49 jurisdictions (AI Hallucination Cases Database, August 2026).

Outlook to 2030

Census data show business AI use hovering between 17% and 20%, with between 20% and 23% expecting to use it within six months (US Census Bureau, May 2026). On this briefing's reading, adoption will broaden faster than it deepens to 2030.

What it means: Budget AI by function, against the tasks where it is measured to work.

Counter-argument

Narrow is not small. The ILO and NASK index finds a quarter of global employment in occupations with some generative-AI exposure, while noting that Full job automation, however, remains limited, since many tasks, though done more efficiently, continue to require human involvement. (International Labour Organization, May 2025). The defensible claim is that AI is narrow now, measurably, so a business case written against general capability rests on something the evidence does not describe.

On the Causal Loop Diagram: B3 Failures keep use narrow: visible failures on long or high-stakes tasks hold use to a few functions.

Decision link: Strategic Implications 1 and 3.

4. Physical automation is scaling while office pilots stall

Near-Term

Weight: medium · Who decides: Operations and capital committees; national industrial policy · Room to act: Moderate: firms choose their own automation, not the supply base

Two automation stories are running at once. One is measured, capital-intensive and sits in operations budgets; the other is the AI programme portfolio. Returns are counted in the first and asserted in the second.

Figure 2: Where automation is counted, and where it is asserted

Density and installation figures from the International Federation of Robotics, April and September 2025 to May 2026. The right-hand panel juxtaposes two kinds of evidence deliberately, to show the asymmetry in measurement rather than to compare quantities.

  • The base is large and still growing: The total number of industrial robots in operational use worldwide was 4,664,000 units in 2024, on 542,000 installations, the fourth consecutive year above half a million (International Federation of Robotics, September 2025).
  • The concentration is national: China´s manufacturing industry already has an operational stock of around 2 million units, roughly 4.5 times Japan's, and China took 54% of 2024 installations (International Federation of Robotics, May 2026).
  • Density tells a more disciplined story: The Republic of Korea records the world´s highest robot density with 1,220 robots per 10,000 employees, growing by 7% on average annually since 2019. China sits 22nd at 166 (International Federation of Robotics, April 2026). The Chinese state now aims to 带动形成万台级规模落地能力, a ten-thousand-unit deployment capability in real production settings (MIIT and SASAC, June 2026).

Weak signals to watch

  • Watch Whether Europe's automation supply base stabilises. Germany's trade federation forecasts robotics and automation revenue falling a further 5% in 2026 to 14.1 billion euros, after a 7% decline the year before (VDMA Robotik und Automation, February 2026).

Outlook to 2030

The International Federation of Robotics expects annual robot installations to pass 700,000 by 2028 (International Federation of Robotics, September 2025). On this briefing's reading, physical automation stays the part of the portfolio with an agreed unit of account through 2030, even as Europe's own supply base shrinks.

What it means: Appraise automation on its own measured record, and check where the equipment will come from.

Counter-argument

The German figures suggest the real story may be Asia against Europe rather than automation against AI, and robot installations count units shipped, not value realised. But physical automation has thirty years of consistent data and an agreed unit of account; enterprise AI has neither, and that asymmetry is what should change how the two are appraised.

On the Causal Loop Diagram: R2 Counted returns, at risk from an AI write-down through a shared budget (leverage point 3).

5. Governments are mandating adoption, not waiting for business cases

Longer-Range

Weight: low · Who decides: Governments in China, Japan, Korea, the EU, the UK, India and the UAE · Room to act: Low over mandates; moderate over where to compete

While private programmes die on unproven value, another class of buyer has removed the value test. Seven jurisdictions now set AI deployment by decree, statute or subsidy rather than by appraisal.

  • The hardest instrument is Chinese and numerical: the State Council aims that 到2027年，率先实现人工智能与6大重点领域广泛深度融合，新一代智能终端、智能体等应用普及率超70%, that is, penetration of AI agents and new intelligent devices above 70% by 2027, rising above 90% by 2030 (State Council of the People's Republic of China, August 2025).
  • Japan and Korea have made adoption national practice and a purchasing duty. Japan's Cabinet plan directs every organisation to rethink how it decides and works on the premise of AI (Cabinet Office, Government of Japan, July 2026), and Korea's AI Framework Act requires state agencies to give priority to AI products and services when buying (Lawtimes, May 2026).
  • Europe and the UK fund diffusion: the Commission's Apply AI Strategy encourages an AI first policy, with around a billion euros behind uptake (European Commission, October 2025), and the UK's five AI Growth Zones report £28.2 billion of investment and £5 million of adoption funding per zone (Department for Science, Innovation and Technology, January 2026).
  • India and Dubai subsidise and set clocks: more than 38 thousand GPUs have been onboarded to India's common compute facility at an affordable rate (Press Information Bureau, India, March 2026), and Dubai has given its private sector a two-year transition to agentic AI (Dubai Media Office, May 2026).

Outlook to 2030

China targets penetration of AI agents and new intelligent devices above 90% by 2030 (State Council of the People's Republic of China, August 2025), and Dubai has given its private sector two years to move to agentic AI (Dubai Media Office, May 2026). On this briefing's reading, by 2030 several large markets will have mandated adoption with no published test of whether it paid.

What it means: Treat mandates as demand and compliance facts, and watch for the first audit of one.

Counter-argument

If states diffuse AI without a value test, they accumulate the same unproven-value problem, later and at public expense. The US Government Accountability Office found that Agency officials from DOD, DHS, GSA, and VA told us they did not systematically document lessons from their AI acquisition efforts because their departmental policies and guidance do not require them to do so. (US Government Accountability Office, April 2026), while federal AI use more than doubled.

On the Causal Loop Diagram: state targets add spending outside the renewal loops; B4 Audits check mandates is dormant.

Strategic Implications

Implication 1: Require a measured before and after on every AI renewal above a defined threshold

Any renewal above a set value should carry a comparison against a period, team or workflow without the tool, in a unit the business already uses. Deployments that cannot support one should be named as acts of faith and sized accordingly.

Action: the executive to set a renewal threshold above which a controlled before-and-after is mandatory, for every AI renewal due from 31 October 2026.

Decide Draws on Themes 1 and 3 On the Causal Loop Diagram: leverage point 1, controlled tests, which restores the comparison that R1 removes.

Implication 2: Re-contract the agent layer on demonstrated task completion rather than on category language

Acceptance should turn on a completion rate measured on the organisation's own tasks, agreed before purchase, with a remedy if it is missed. That turns a forecast cancellation into a contractual exit rather than a write-off.

Action: procurement and legal to add a task-completion acceptance clause, with an exit, to the standard AI contract from 31 December 2026.

Decide Draws on Themes 2 and 1 On the Causal Loop Diagram: leverage point 2, acceptance on benchmark scores, which lets relabelled agents through.

Implication 3: Appraise the physical and process automation portfolio separately from the AI portfolio

Different evidence, time constants and budget lines mean one combined programme hides the part with a measurable return. Separating them shows where returns sit and protects automation from the first visible AI write-down.

Action: finance and operations to report automation as its own investment line, with its own measured return, from the plan for the year after 31 March 2027.

Prepare Draws on Themes 4 and 3 On the Causal Loop Diagram: leverage point 3, where an AI write-down can reach working automation through a shared budget.

Implication 4: Track state adoption mandates as a demand and market-access variable, not as policy background

A state mandate is at once a demand floor, a compliance variable that may arrive before the internal case is ready, and a reason to distrust diffusion rates read as competitive position. Watch numerical targets, procurement preferences and any audit of whether adoption paid.

Action: strategy to add a state-mandate column to the quarterly vendor map for the seven jurisdictions in Theme 5, through 31 December 2027.

The audited build

↗ Shift from todayFrom a randomised trial design abandoned in February 2026 (METR, February 2026) to controlled before-and-after tests as a normal condition of renewing AI spending.

In 2030 buyers, not governments, set the pace, and they pay for what they can measure. Controlled comparisons are routine at renewal, so spending concentrates where returns show in the organisation's own numbers, and the agent market splits between the few vendors that can prove task completion and the rest. The risk is over-correction: deployments with long or diffuse payoffs are cut alongside those that never worked.

Early indicators: a credible replacement for METR's abandoned design; a large enterprise publishing a controlled AI result against its own financials.

Directed and measured

↗ Shift from todayFrom four large US agencies that do not systematically document lessons from AI purchases (US Government Accountability Office, April 2026) to audit offices publishing the measured outcomes of national AI programmes.

In 2030 states still set the pace, but auditors travel with the mandates. Public deployment at scale has become the reference dataset the private sector never built, and audit-office reports are the best evidence a corporate buyer can find. Procurement preferences give domestic vendors a floor of demand, so competitive position turns partly on jurisdiction.

Early indicators: an audit body publishing outcome findings on a national AI programme; Korea or China publishing measured penetration against target.

The quiet write-down

↗ Shift from todayFrom over 40% of agentic AI projects forecast for cancellation by the end of 2027 (Gartner, June 2025) to cuts that fall on working and failing deployments alike.

In 2030 the market decides on sentiment. Spending ran on faith for two or three budget cycles, then stopped abruptly when a visible failure gave permission. Because almost nothing was measured, the correction cut deployments that worked along with those that did not, and the organisations that kept records suffered least. The evidence in this briefing points here today.

Early indicators: agentic cancellations tracking the forecast; insurers widening absolute AI exclusions.

Adoption by decree

↗ Shift from todayFrom China's target of above 70% penetration of AI agents by 2027 (State Council of the People's Republic of China, August 2025) to targets met on paper while measured productivity stays flat.

In 2030 penetration targets have been met on paper, measured productivity has not moved, and no rule requires anyone to explain the gap. Diffusion statistics have become a competitiveness story detached from output, and an international organisation runs two operating models, one appraised and one complied with.

Early indicators: penetration targets restated rather than missed; adoption rising while measured productivity stays flat.

Potential Surprises to 2040

Weak signal

Workers who have never used AI become too scarce to measure AI against

Window: 2030 to 2036 · Probability band: Low-Medium (20 to 40%)

Why it would surprise: Plans to measure AI's return assume a group without it can always be found.

The most-cited trial of AI's effect on skilled work is being abandoned, because developers who will not work without AI leave the control group (METR, February 2026).

Signposts to watch:

  • By June 2027: a second research group reports that it cannot recruit an AI-free comparison group for a workplace trial.
  • By December 2028: an auditor or regulator accepts a pre-AI historical baseline in place of a live comparison group when judging an AI programme.

What it touches today: Whether to hold back a team without the tool at roll-out.

Emerging

Insurers, not benchmarks, decide which AI agents are fit to deploy

Window: 2030 to 2037 · Probability band: Low-Medium (20 to 40%)

Why it would surprise: Buyers expect vendors and benchmarks, not insurers, to certify agents.

Carriers are writing absolute AI exclusions into cyber, errors-and-omissions and general liability lines (Risk Management Magazine, August 2026), while an audit of agent benchmarks found exposures and reward hacking in 67.0% of Frontier Science traces (arXiv preprint, July 2026).

Signposts to watch:

  • By December 2027: an insurer offers cover for an agentic AI system only if it passes the insurer's own completion test.
  • By June 2029: a public procurement rule requires an independent or insurer-set completion test before an AI agent is accepted.

What it touches today: AI contract acceptance terms and insurance renewals.

Weak signal

Courts, not regulators, set the rules for AI in professional work

Window: 2030 to 2036 · Probability band: Low-Medium (20 to 40%)

Why it would surprise: Plans expect statute, not case law, to set the rules.

A running census of court and tribunal decisions on AI-hallucinated material in legal filings now records 1,922 decisions across 49 jurisdictions (AI Hallucination Cases Database, August 2026).

Signposts to watch:

  • By December 2027: a court awards damages against a professional firm for loss caused by AI-generated material it did not check.
  • By December 2028: a professional regulator makes checking AI output a written condition of practice, citing court decisions rather than legislation.

What it touches today: Review of AI-drafted work and indemnity cover.

What We Are Not Planning For

Capability

A frontier capability discontinuity that makes the measurement question moot

A system obviously and generally more productive would need no controlled trial, and this briefing's argument would dissolve. It is excluded because no action taken today is improved by assuming it, and reliability is improving far more slowly than capability.

↺ Reinstated if a single system produces an unambiguous, externally verified step change on whole real economic tasks rather than on benchmarks.

Agents

General-purpose agents clearing the majority of real paid work inside this horizon

The Remote Labor Index rose from 2.5% to 15.8% in under eight months, but benchmarks rarely extrapolate in straight lines and the remaining tasks are harder. Theme 2 treats the trajectory as a counter-argument, not a planning assumption.

↺ Reinstated if the index passes 40%, or if a comparable measure on client-accepted work shows the same slope in a second independent benchmark.

Policy

A coordinated retreat from state adoption targets

Mandates could be quietly dropped if returns disappoint, but this cycle's movement runs the other way, with new instruments in Japan and Korea. Planning for a retreat would discount a demand floor that exists.

↺ Reinstated if any of the seven jurisdictions in Implication 4 formally revises a numerical adoption target downward or repeals a procurement preference.

Questions For The Next Strategy Meeting

Five questions this cycle's evidence should put on the agenda.

  1. Could the organisation produce a controlled before-and-after for its three largest AI deployments this quarter? If not, on what basis were they renewed?
  2. Which deployments would we find hardest to withdraw for a control arm, and is that attachment evidence of value or of habit?
  3. Among US firms that adopt AI, 57% use it in three or fewer functions. Which three are ours, and was that the plan or the outcome?
  4. Does our capital process appraise physical automation and enterprise AI on the same standard of evidence, and should it, when only one has an agreed unit of account and thirty years of data?
  5. Which of our current activities looks least wise in these futures?
