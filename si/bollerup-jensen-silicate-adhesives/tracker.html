Change Tracker · Bollerup Jensen Group · 22 July 2026

The formaldehyde cliff lands; the silicate opening moves to inorganic ground

With the EU formaldehyde limit halving on 6 August 2026, the shift beyond petrochemical binders is real but selective: wood-panel makers are choosing fossil MDI and lignin over silicate, while sodium-silicate chemistry gains its clearest ground in low-carbon construction, fire protection and foundry binders.

Edition 2 · previous cycle: 'Formaldehyde cliff hardens; wood-panel substitution chooses pMDI and lignin, not silicate' (30 May 2026)

The consensus read on sustainable adhesives is that tightening chemistry rules open a wide substitution window, with silicate binders among the chemistries expected to gain. This cycle's evidence is sharper. The EU formaldehyde limit halves to 0.062 mg/m3 on 6 August 2026, yet the wood-panel industry is visibly choosing fossil no-added-formaldehyde MDI and lignin, not silicate. Since the 30 May cycle the broad REACH revision has been shelved for targeted rules, softening the wider tailwind, while a soda-ash and energy squeeze has landed on European inorganic producers. The question for Bollerup Jensen: press silicate where it wins on inorganic performance, or contest a panel market already closing?

The Commodity Squeeze

The worst pairing: the formaldehyde cliff is met on cost, not performance, and the sustainable shift stays diffuse. Panel and coating buyers pick the cheapest compliant route, reformulated fossil MDI or lignin; no low-carbon or fire specification pays a performance premium; and silicate stays a low-margin commodity just as the soda-ash and energy squeeze compresses margins. Bollerup Jensen defends volume on price in a shrinking, undifferentiated pool.

Indicators: no second silicate-binder Technical Assessment lands; PFAS derogations widen or the restriction slips; tenders specify on price; Nordic power prices climb toward continental levels.

Right Chemistry, Wrong Clock

Silicate is the right answer, but demand forms too slowly to pay off this horizon. The inorganic case in load-bearing geopolymer, fire protection and foundry cores is proven and Bollerup Jensen holds the chemistry, yet standards translate slowly, procurement lags, and adoption stays at pilot scale. The opportunity is real but sits beyond the cycle, and the wait has to be funded.

Indicators: silicate-activated approvals stay isolated; embodied-carbon declarations roll out but procurement does not yet price them; foundry inorganic-binder use stays at pilot; coating reformulation is announced, not specified.

The Fast Bypass

Demand moves fast and specification-led, but around bio-organic and reformulated fossil chemistries, not silicate. Lignin and soy binders reach cost parity, PFAS reformulation flows to organic systems that are not silicate, and the quick specification wave in panels and coatings is written for chemistries Bollerup Jensen does not supply. A real shift arrives, and silicate is not on the ticket.

Indicators: a third major panel line converts to lignin or soy; a lignin binder reaches cost parity; a coatings major launches a non-silicate organic line; retailers specify bio-bonded panels.

Specified In

The best pairing: the sustainable shift concentrates in inorganic-performance markets and moves at specification speed. Silicate-activated geopolymer binders cross into load-bearing use, PFAS and fire-safety rules pull surface treatments toward mineral systems, and foundries commit to inorganic cores, fast enough to write silicate into specifications. Bollerup Jensen's Nordic, low-carbon silicate is specified in as a performance input, not priced as a commodity.

Indicators: a second silicate-binder Technical Assessment in another EU state; a fire-protection certificate for a silicate-bonded assembly; a European foundry commits to an inorganic-only roadmap; a coatings major adopts an inorganic silicate line.

Pattern narrative

The panel window closes to non-silicate routesEvolving Risk

Three signals point the same way. The 0.062 mg/m3 formaldehyde limit lands on 6 August 2026 with no grace period, a deadline Fraunhofer IBP underlines for the largest EU market (Fraunhofer IBP, May 2026). The panel industry's answer is not silicate: MEDITE's compliant range runs on no-added-formaldehyde MDI, Egger has launched a formaldehyde-free bonded board (Wood & Panel, June 2026), and Koskisen and Stora Enso are widening their lignin-bonded Zero range (Wood & Panel, May 2026). The panel federation puts the resin-cost step at 15% to 25%. The wood-panel opening that framed the last cycle is narrowing as fossil and bio-organic routes take the contracts.

Regulation narrows and the cost base tightensTransformation Driver

The regulatory picture has shifted since May. Brussels has shelved the broad REACH revision for targeted restrictions, pruning the wider tailwind. Two rules still bite: the advancing PFAS restriction pushes fluorinated coatings toward reformulation, and the new EU building-carbon method deepens the low-carbon binder pull. Against this, a cost squeeze has appeared: Solvay is cutting Torrelavega soda ash to 420 kilotons (from 600 kt previously) and Europe's energy-intensive producers are pressing Brussels on power prices (Cefic, February 2026). Nordic electricity remains Bollerup Jensen's quiet advantage to protect.

Silicate wins on inorganic groundEmerging Opportunity

Where silicate competes on inorganic performance, momentum is clearer. Hoffmann Green's silicate-activated 0% clinker cement now carries a load-bearing Assessment, and Ecocem has taken a low-carbon binder to a full-scale residential build (World Cement, July 2026). Surface treatments and foundry chemistry add to it: coatings are reformulating toward mineral systems, and foundry-binder suppliers are positioning inorganic silicate cores (Cast Metals Federation, February 2026). These are the fronts where a Danish silicate maker sells performance, not price.

Signals gaining momentum

  1. The formaldehyde cliff lands. On 6 August 2026 the E05 limit applies with no grace period, forcing every panel maker's binder choice within weeks.
  2. Feedstock and energy squeeze. Solvay's soda-ash cut and industry power-cost demands signal a tightening European cost base that rewards low-energy Nordic production.
  3. PFAS restriction advances. The SEAC draft opinion moves the universal ban forward, pulling fluorinated coating and sealant chemistries toward inorganic swaps.
  4. Silicate crosses into load-bearing use. Certified silicate-activated cement and full-scale low-carbon builds extend the specification template silicate systems can lean on.

Wild Cards to Watch

Silicate-activated chemistry lands a flagship EU specification

Disruptive OpportunityPotential impact: High

Surprise characteristics: A national procurement or fire-safety standard writes silicate-activated binder or coating into specification language.

Early warning indicators: A second silicate-binder Technical Assessment in another EU state; a notified body certifies a silicate-bonded assembly.

The Hoffmann Green load-bearing Assessment is the template a silicate adhesive or coating line would lean on; a second national approval in the next two quarters would turn adjacent proof into a specification silicate producers can sell into (World Cement, January 2026).

Bio-organic binders reach cost parity and take the panel share

Disruptive RiskPotential impact: Very High

Surprise characteristics: A lignin or soy binder reaches fossil-resin cost parity and captures the whole formaldehyde-free panel share, closing silicate out of wood panels.

Early warning indicators: A third major panel line converting to lignin or soy; a tier-1 retailer specifying bio-bonded panels.

Koskisen and Stora Enso's Zero expansion shows the bio-organic route already scaling (Wood & Panel, May 2026); a cost-parity breakthrough would settle the panel question against silicate for a decade.

PFAS restriction turns surface treatments into the biggest silicate pull

Disruptive OpportunityPotential impact: High

Surprise characteristics: PFAS reformulation and a coatings major's inorganic pivot make water-glass surface treatments, not construction, the largest new silicate demand.

Early warning indicators: A coatings major announcing an inorganic silicate line; a fire-protection certificate for a silicate-bonded coating.

Coatings are already reformulating toward mineral systems (European Coatings, June 2026); if the PFAS ban lands hard, silicate surface treatments could scale faster than the construction route the last cycle emphasised.

Implications

This cycle reads the sustainable-binder shift as real but selective, and the wood-panel adhesive window as closing to non-silicate routes. Worth monitoring: the next lignin or MDI conversion by an EU panel major, and whether the Nordic power advantage holds as the energy squeeze spreads. Warrants preparation: a Bollerup Jensen position that leads with silicate where it wins on inorganic performance, in low-carbon construction binders, fire protection and foundry cores, rather than contesting panel adhesives head-on (Bollerup Jensen, evergreen). A decision point opens if a second silicate-binder Technical Assessment or a PFAS-driven coating reformulation lands next quarter. The EU is backing the move beyond petrochemicals, a shift in which the EU bioeconomy was estimated at €2.7 trillion in 2023 (Council of the European Union, March 2026).
