Executive Synthesis

Which decisions on your desk this week assume the formaldehyde cliff will pull demand to silicate?

The cycle 2 evidence says it has not, and the bigger opportunity is structural geopolymer cement, not panel adhesives.

The formaldehyde cliff arrived on schedule, and the wood-panel industry chose pMDI and lignin. Six weeks after cycle 1 framed sodium silicate as positioned for the EU low-carbon construction demand pull, the evidence base has partially refuted that thesis on its primary substitution surface. MEDITE SMARTPLY moved its full MDF range to No-Added-Formaldehyde MDI resin on 13 April 2026 (MEDITE SMARTPLY, April 2026), Koskisen and Stora Enso extended the lignin-based NeoLigno Zero family into flooring and construction board (Panels and Furniture Asia, May 2026), and the European Panel Federation's own cost estimate, 15 to 25 percent on resin cost, names UF, MUF and NAF reformulation, not silicate, as the compliance route (Notifix, April 2026). The substitution window the April cycle expected to open for silicate panel adhesives has closed before it opened.

Taken together, the sources suggest the spine of this cycle is no longer the panel-adhesive opening but the structural geopolymer threshold. Hoffmann Green's H-UKR alkali-activated cement received a French ATec Technical Assessment for all superstructure applications in January 2026, the first 0 percent clinker cement certified for load-bearing construction (World Cement, January 2026). The European Commission convened a high-level cement-industry policy dialogue on 25 March 2026 advancing a low-carbon concrete label, revised CPR standards with lifecycle climate impact disclosure, and a EUR 100bn Industrial Decarbonisation Bank (European Commission DG CLIMA, March 2026). And from 8 January 2026, Declarations of Performance under CPR 2024/3110 must carry mandatory GWP disclosure for concrete and cement (European Commission DG GROW, December 2025). Sodium silicate is the activator chemistry beneath the dominant geopolymer formulations; the demand pull that did not arrive in panel adhesives is forming in cement.

Three further forces are now dominating leadership attention. First, the compliance tailwind that cycle 1 framed as a structural margin support has been pruned: the Council adopted the Omnibus I package on 24 February 2026, narrowing CSRD scope to firms above 1,000 employees and EUR 450m turnover (Council of the European Union, February 2026), and CBAM Phase 2 has deferred chemicals scope to a 2027 evaluation report (European Commission DG TAXUD, December 2025). Second, the foundry inorganic transition continues: the EU's LIFE GREEN CASTING project documents 80 to 95 percent VOC reduction targets across six ferrous foundries (European Commission LIFE Programme, structural anchor), and ASK Chemicals' INOTEC Generation 6 is mature enough to be cost-competitive with phenolic and furan in aluminium core production. Third, a new and unexpected adjacency has opened in passive fire protection: the UK Building Safety Regulator's 25 March 2026 consultation on Approved Document B and the April 2026 ASFP guidance both push specification toward non-combustible silicate-bonded assemblies.

The board-level insight that may surprise leadership: the cycle 1 ownership-window theme has dropped out of this cycle's spine, not because the M&A pressure has eased but because the strategic-redirection question now precedes the ownership question. Whether BJ continues independent, recapitalises, or sells, the answer is conditional on which of three doors it walks through, cement activator supply, foundry binder input, or fire-protection and structural silicate bonding. The panel-adhesive door has been quietly closed by other people's product launches.

Where this analysis could be wrong

The synthesis assumes the structural geopolymer adjacency is the spine of BJ's next 24 months. If a second 0 percent clinker cement system fails its national technical assessment in 2026, or if the EU Industrial Decarbonisation Bank's first cement-sector allocation goes entirely to electrified clinker capture rather than alkali-activated alternatives, the geopolymer demand pull would reset to a 5-to-10-year horizon and the cycle 1 four-theme architecture would re-assert itself. The falsifying signal would be a Cemex, Heidelberg or Holcim public withdrawal from an announced alkali-activated pilot, paired with one further OPC capacity reinvestment in Northern Europe.

The decisions that cannot be deferred this cycle

  1. Stop scoping the wood-panel adhesive opening as an active commercial target. The substitution share has been claimed by pMDI and lignin; further sales engineering into panel adhesives extends a closed window.
  2. Authorise a formulator-engagement programme into alkali-activated cement and structural retrofit binders. The Hoffmann Green ATec, the Commission's 25 March cement dialogue and the CPR GWP declaration together open a 12-to-18-month specification window.
  3. Commission a soda ash and energy-cost stress test on FY27 margin. The Solvay Torrelavega cut, EU power prices 2.5x US levels, and the AccelerateEU State aid framework all signal cost-side pressure that compresses any redirection programme's runway.

Each is developed below, with a decision posture, in the four Strategic Implications.

1. The Cliff Without the Pull: Wood-Panel Substitution Chooses pMDI and Lignin, Not Silicate

Immediate

The formaldehyde cliff that the April cycle framed as a silicate-adhesive opening has arrived on schedule, and the wood-panel sector has chosen two non-silicate compliance routes. From 6 August 2026, REACH Annex XVII Entry 77 halves the EU formaldehyde-emission limit for wood panels from E1 (0.124 mg/m3) to 0.062 mg/m3, with no grace period and no indoor-panel loophole. The substitution share has not gone to alkaline silicate adhesives; it has gone to reformulated UF, MUF and No-Added-Formaldehyde MDI on the synthetic side, and to lignin on the bio-based side. For Bollerup Jensen, the strategic implication is direct: the panel-adhesive opening must be retired as an active commercial target, and the silicate-adhesive narrative redirected to the structural, foundry and fire-protection adjacencies treated in the next five themes.

  • The cliff is binding and the exemption scope is narrow. REACH Annex XVII Entry 77 imposes 0.062 and 0.080 mg/m3 limits from 6 August 2026; exemptions are confined to outdoor-only articles, industrial non-public exposure, medical devices, PPE, biocides and food contact, with no loophole for indoor wood panels (Analytical Group, March 2026).
  • The dominant panel-major compliance route is fossil pMDI, not silicate. MEDITE SMARTPLY moved its full MDF range to "No Added Formaldehyde MDI resin, which already meets the new REACH limit" on 13 April 2026 (MEDITE SMARTPLY, April 2026).
  • The bio-based substitution route is going to lignin, not silicate. Koskisen and Stora Enso extended the lignin-based NeoLigno Zero family with a new flooring and construction board in May 2026, claiming "formaldehyde-free panels can work in large-scale industrial production while meeting high performance expectations" (Panels and Furniture Asia, May 2026).
  • The European Panel Federation's own cost estimate names UF/MUF/NAF, not silicate, as the compliance route, and quantifies the cost shock: "the added cost for resins will range between 15% and 25%, depending on the type of panel and the pressing technology" (Notifix, April 2026).

Counter-argument

The window may not be fully closed: ECHA's measurement methodology, published in June 2025, only stabilised compliance testing for E05 in mid-2025 (EUWID Wood Products and Panels, June 2025), meaning some smaller panel producers may yet face capital-allocation choices in late 2026 where a silicate-adhesive option could enter as a fallback. On the available evidence, however, the formaldehyde-resin chain is consolidating around incumbents, with Bondalti acquiring 77.23 percent of Ercros by 13 March 2026 (EUWID Wood Products and Panels, March 2026), suggesting the substitution share is being claimed faster than a silicate-adhesive entrant can win specification.

One thing that matters: The substitution share is being claimed by other people's chemistries; the panel-adhesive door is closed before BJ entered it. Decision link: Strategic Implication SI 1.

2. The Structural Geopolymer Threshold: Silicate-Activated Binders Cross into Load-Bearing Construction

Near-Term

A larger and more durable demand pull for sodium silicate has opened in cement, not in wood panels. The first French ATec Technical Assessment for a 0 percent clinker alkali-activated cement covering all superstructure applications was issued to Hoffmann Green in January 2026, the regulatory milestone the geopolymer sector has needed for two decades. The European Commission convened a high-level cement-industry dialogue on 25 March 2026 advancing a low-carbon concrete label, revised CPR product standards and a EUR 100bn Industrial Decarbonisation Bank. From 8 January 2026, Declarations of Performance under CPR 2024/3110 carry mandatory GWP disclosure for cement and concrete. Sodium silicate is the activator chemistry beneath the dominant geopolymer formulations, and the Northern European cement majors are visibly rebalancing their portfolios. For BJ, the redirection question is whether to position as a specification-grade activator supplier into the European alkali-activated cement chain before larger silicate majors lock in the formulator contracts.

  • The first 0 percent clinker cement crossed the load-bearing certification threshold in January 2026. Hoffmann Green received a French ATec for its H-UKR alkali-activated geopolymer cement covering all superstructure applications, a world first for a clinker-free cement (World Cement, January 2026).
  • The EU's cement-sector policy machinery is consolidating around low-carbon binders. The Commission's 25 March 2026 high-level cement dialogue advanced a low-carbon concrete label, revised CPR product standards with mandatory lifecycle climate impact disclosure, and a EUR 100bn Industrial Decarbonisation Bank (European Commission DG CLIMA, March 2026).
  • The specifier-level GWP comparison is now mandatory and favours alkali-activated chemistry. From 8 January 2026, CPR Declarations of Performance must include GWP for concrete and cement, forcing specifier-level low-clinker comparisons (Ecochain, structural anchor); the CPR Working Plan 2026-2029 was published 15 December 2025 (European Commission DG GROW, December 2025).
  • Northern European cement majors are rebalancing portfolios. Heidelberg Materials announced 19 March 2026 it will concentrate Swedish clinker at Slite from 2027, scaling down Skoevde (Heidelberg Materials AG, March 2026), a Nordic structural shift directly adjacent to BJ's Danish position.

Counter-argument

The geopolymer threshold is real but the demand-pull velocity into Nordic markets is unproven. A single national technical assessment is not yet a binding EN standard, and the Commission's cement-policy programme could allocate the Industrial Decarbonisation Bank's first round to electrified clinker capture rather than alkali-activated alternatives, which would compress the alkali-activator demand pull to a 5-to-10-year horizon. The EOTA route documents an ETA pathway for inorganic-matrix systems (EOTA, structural anchor), but pathway is not yet purchase order, and BJ's commercial readiness to translate technical capability into formulator specification is the binding constraint.

One thing that matters: The first 0 percent clinker superstructure ATec converts geopolymer from a research story into a load-bearing specification opportunity, and silicate is the activator. Decision link: Strategic Implication SI 2.

3. The Foundry Inorganic Transition: From Pilot to Specification

Near-Term

The foundry-binder rotation that cycle 1 identified continues to harden into specification. The EU LIFE GREEN CASTING project frames the institutional driver as 80 to 95 percent reduction targets for VOC, SO2, NOx and PM emissions across six European ferrous foundries. ASK Chemicals' INOTEC Generation 6 sodium-silicate binder system is the technical step that makes inorganic systems cost-competitive with phenolic and furan organics in aluminium core production. Foseco, the third major foundry-chemicals supplier, documents a recent resurgence of sodium silicate binder demand driven by health, safety and environmental legislation. For BJ, the role remains as cycle 1 framed it: be the specified silicate input that the foundry-binder majors formulate around, not a competitor to those majors on finished binder systems. What has changed is timing pressure: the LIFE project closes in 2026 and the formulator-binder competitive set is consolidating.

  • The EU's institutional driver for the foundry inorganic transition is documented and quantified. The LIFE GREEN CASTING project targets 80 to 95 percent reduction in VOC, SO2, NOx and PM emissions and 90 percent reduction in landfilled waste sand through inorganic silicate binder substitution across six European ferrous foundries (European Commission LIFE Programme, structural anchor).
  • The technical readiness step has been taken on the supplier side. ASK Chemicals' Generation 6 INOTEC inorganic sodium silicate binder delivers 82 percent strength retention over 24 hours and reduces promoter addition rates 24 to 31 percent versus Generation 4, the structural performance step that makes inorganic systems cost-competitive with phenolic and furan in aluminium core production (foundry-planet.com, structural anchor).
  • The market-wide pull is visible across the three foundry-chemicals majors. Foseco confirms a recent resurgence of sodium silicate binder demand in steel, iron and non-ferrous foundries, driven by health, safety and environmental legislation (Foseco, structural anchor).

Counter-argument

The foundry-binder transition is structurally real but slow. The aluminium core sub-segment where inorganic systems are cost-competitive is a fraction of the European foundry market, and the iron and steel segments retain phenolic and furan systems where worker-exposure compliance can be managed by extraction. The competing thesis is that BJ's foundry exposure is a steady but non-accelerating margin floor, not a redirection vehicle, and that capital allocated to formulator specification in foundry binders earns less than the same capital allocated to alkali-activated cement engagement.

One thing that matters: The foundry inorganic transition is now specification-grade, not pilot-grade, and the formulator-binder consolidation is the timing pressure. Decision link: Strategic Implication SI 3.

4. The Compliance Tailwind Pruned: Omnibus I, ESG Scope Reduction, CBAM Phase 2 Sequencing

Immediate

Cycle 1 named the converging REACH revision, CBAM Phase 2 and the Energy-Intensive Industries Package as a compliance squeeze on mid-cap European chemicals. Six weeks later, two of those clocks have slowed. The Council formally adopted the Omnibus I package on 24 February 2026, narrowing CSRD scope to firms above 1,000 employees and EUR 450m turnover; CBAM Phase 2 has deferred chemicals scope to a 2027 evaluation report; and the AccelerateEU communication of 22 April 2026 is now framed around resilience and state aid rather than additional emissions disclosure. The mechanical implication for BJ is a reduced compliance overhead on the reporting side, but the strategic implication runs the other way: the compliance overhead was also the regulatory tailwind that pulled specifiers toward low-toxicity binders. With that pull softened, the demand-side case for silicate substitution leans harder on the cement and foundry adjacencies and less on the panel-adhesive cliff.

  • The CSRD scope reduction is now formal and material. The Council adopted Omnibus I on 24 February 2026, narrowing CSRD scope to companies above 1,000 employees and EUR 450m turnover, easing reporting pressure on mid-tier construction and panel producers (Council of the European Union, February 2026).
  • CBAM Phase 2 has explicitly deferred chemicals scope. The definitive period entered force 1 January 2026, with proposed 2028 scope extension covering approximately 180 downstream steel and aluminium articles, and chemicals deferred to a 2027 evaluation report (European Commission DG TAXUD, December 2025).
  • The EU's pivot has reframed industrial policy from disclosure to resilience and state aid. AccelerateEU (22 April 2026) is the Commission's five-pillar response to the renewed energy crisis, including a State aid framework, an ETS Investment Booster of 400 million allowances, and energy market reform (European Commission DG Energy, April 2026).

Counter-argument

The pruning of the disclosure tailwind does not mean the substitution case has gone away. The CPR's mandatory GWP declaration from 8 January 2026 remains binding; the formaldehyde Annex XVII Entry 77 cliff is unchanged; and a future Commission could reinstate scope tightening on a faster timeline if the Industrial Decarbonisation Bank conditions it on lifecycle disclosure. The competing reading is that the compliance tailwind has been re-shaped rather than reduced: less disclosure overhead, more product-level GWP and embodied-carbon comparison, which still favours low-clinker binders and silicate-bonded fire-protection assemblies.

One thing that matters: The CSRD-driven pull toward low-toxicity binders has softened; the demand-side case now leans harder on the cement and structural adjacencies than on the panel-adhesive cliff. Decision link: Strategic Implications SI 1 and SI 4.

5. Energy and Feedstock: Nordic Power, Soda Ash and Silica Sourcing Under European Producer Rebalancing

Near-Term

The cost side of the equation has hardened. Solvay will cut Torrelavega soda ash capacity 30 percent in Q3 2026, removing approximately 180kt of European supply at a moment when sodium silicate spot prices are already rising. Cefic's Q4 2025 Chemical Trends Report quantifies the structural gap: European gas prices at 2.5x US levels through 2025, electricity prices considerably above US, sector competitiveness far below the 2014 to 2019 pre-crisis average. The AccelerateEU package and the AEII coalition's EUR 50 per MWh KPI for the Electrification Action Plan name the cost-side pressure honestly but offer no near-term price relief. For BJ, the implication is direct: any redirection programme into cement, foundry or fire-protection adjacencies operates against a tightening soda ash supply and a structurally higher European energy bill. This is the cost-side risk to monitor.

  • European soda ash supply is tightening at a structurally adverse moment. Solvay will cut Torrelavega (Spain) soda ash production capacity 30 percent (from 600kt to 420kt) in Q3 2026 in response to oversupply and high EU energy and carbon costs (Solvay, February 2026).
  • Sodium silicate spot prices are already rising. European sodium silicate spot prices reached USD 0.63 per kg in February 2026, with a 5.0 percent month-on-month increase driven by higher energy and production costs (IMARC Group, February 2026).
  • The structural European energy disadvantage is quantified by the industry's own body. European gas prices were 2.5x US levels throughout 2025; electricity prices remain considerably above US; chemical sector competitiveness sits far below the pre-crisis 2014 to 2019 average (Cefic, March 2026).
  • The energy-intensive industries coalition has named a binding cost KPI. The AEII coalition (Cefic, Eurofer, CEMBUREAU and others) demands EUR 50 per MWh maximum total industrial electricity cost as KPI for the May 2026 EU Electrification Action Plan, calling current EU energy prices structurally uncompetitive (Cefic Alliance of Energy Intensive Industries, February 2026).

Counter-argument

Tightening soda ash supply can be a margin opportunity, not only a margin risk: with European supply contracting, silicate producers with stable feedstock contracts and energy-pass-through pricing power can hold or grow margin while the spot price rises. The competing reading is that BJ's Northern European location, Danish power grid mix and Nordic gas-substitute electricity make its energy exposure materially better than the Cefic European average, and that a programme of multi-year soda ash contracting now could lock in the cost advantage before further European capacity exits.

One thing that matters: Soda ash tightens and European power prices stay structurally elevated; the FY27 margin envelope for any redirection programme depends on locking feedstock and power early. Decision link: Strategic Implication SI 4.

6. Silicate Adhesives Beyond the Cliff: Industrial Bonding, Fire Protection and Specification Routes for Inorganic Adhesive Chemistry

Near-Term

A new and previously under-weighted adjacency has opened. With the panel-adhesive door closed and the cement door opening, the third route through which silicate-adhesive chemistry reaches specification is the industrial-bonding and passive fire protection chain. The UK Building Safety Regulator's consultation on Approved Document B, launched 25 March 2026 and running to 1 July 2026, tightens external wall fire performance and the BS9414 cladding test framework, addressing Grenfell Phase 2 recommendations directly. The Association for Specialist Fire Protection's revised April 2026 guidance formalises the specifier framework increasingly favouring silicate-bonded, non-combustible Class A2-s1, d0 assemblies. The EOTA EAD 340275-00-0104 establishes the European Technical Assessment route for inorganic-matrix composite strengthening of concrete and masonry. Hoffmann Green's H-P2A, a sodium and potassium silicate plus flash-clay activated geopolymer, is already marketed for industrial adhesives, passive fire protection, insulation and adhesive mortars with peel strength over 25 MPa. The pattern is consistent: silicate chemistry crossing from research into specification in adjacent, more regulated bonding applications than wood panels.

  • The UK regulatory tailwind for non-combustible silicate-bonded assemblies is open and live. The UK Building Safety Regulator launched a 14-week consultation on Approved Document B on 25 March 2026, addressing Grenfell Phase 2 recommendations, tightening external wall fire performance, BS9414 cladding test framework and combustible structural materials above 11m (UK Government Building Safety Regulator, March 2026).
  • The specifier framework is being formalised toward Class A2-s1, d0 silicate-bonded assemblies. The ASFP issued revised passive fire protection guidance in April 2026, formalising the post-Grenfell specifier framework that increasingly favours silicate-bonded, non-combustible assemblies (International Fire and Safety Journal, April 2026).
  • The European Technical Assessment route is open for silicate-bonded structural strengthening. EOTA's EAD 340275-00-0104 establishes the assessment pathway for inorganic-matrix (silicate-bonded) composite strengthening systems for concrete and masonry, the structural-regulatory pathway through which silicate adhesives gain CE-marked specification in retrofit (EOTA, structural anchor).
  • Hoffmann Green has already commercialised silicate adhesive chemistry into BJ's exact niche. H-P2A is marketed as a sodium and potassium silicate plus flash-clay activated geopolymer explicitly for industrial adhesives, passive fire protection, insulation and adhesive mortars, with peel strength over 25 MPa (Hoffmann Green Cement Technologies, structural anchor).

Counter-argument

Bollerup Jensen's own public positioning does not yet reflect this opening: the BJ silicate page remains a generic adhesives and water glass description (Bollerup Jensen Group, structural anchor), with no 2026 product launch in fire protection or structural strengthening. Hoffmann Green's H-P2A and the major adhesive-chemistry incumbents may capture the specification window before BJ can position; the competing reading is that this adjacency is real but BJ's commercial-readiness gap is wide enough that the first 12 months should be invested in technical specification preparation and formulator relationships rather than product launch.

One thing that matters: Fire protection and structural retrofit are the second and third doors silicate chemistry can walk through; both are open and both are being walked through by other people first. Decision link: Strategic Implications SI 2 and SI 3.

Scenario Matrix

The two critical uncertainties shaping Bollerup Jensen's next two to five years are the pace of EU low-carbon construction demand pull, particularly into alkali-activated cement, and the scope and sequencing of CBAM Phase 2 as it touches chemicals. The four scenarios below are planning aids, not forecasts.

Specifier-Led Pull, Chemicals In CBAM

EU low-carbon construction demand pull accelerates: Industrial Decarbonisation Bank funds a 2027 cohort of alkali-activated cement plants, two more national technical assessments follow Hoffmann Green's H-UKR ATec, and the CPR GWP declaration moves from disclosure to procurement preference. CBAM Phase 2's 2027 chemicals evaluation report lands inside scope, removing the soda ash import-cost advantage for non-EU competitors. BJ becomes a margin-accretive specification-grade activator supplier into a growing European demand pool. Energy-cost pressure persists but is offset by AccelerateEU State aid and ETS Investment Booster allocations to energy-intensive chemicals. The redirection programme from panel adhesives to cement activator supply succeeds on schedule.

Early indicators: Industrial Decarbonisation Bank first cement cohort announced 2027; second 0 percent clinker ATec issued by national authority; CBAM Phase 2 2027 evaluation includes chemicals.

Specifier-Led Pull, Chemicals Stays Out

The cement and structural demand pull materialises on schedule but CBAM Phase 2 continues to defer chemicals beyond 2028. Non-EU silicate, soda ash and activator suppliers gain a structural cost advantage against the European producer base. BJ wins specification on technical merit and Northern European logistics but faces price pressure from imports throughout the value chain. The programme succeeds in commercial terms but operating margin is compressed by 200 to 400 basis points relative to scenario 1. The hedging and multi-year contracting decision in SI 4 becomes the binding margin lever.

Early indicators: CBAM 2027 evaluation explicitly defers chemicals to 2030; non-EU silicate import volumes into Northern Europe rise 10 percent year on year; Solvay or second producer announces further soda ash capacity cut.

Slow Pull, Chemicals In CBAM

Low-carbon construction demand pull stalls: the Industrial Decarbonisation Bank's first cohort goes to electrified clinker capture rather than alkali-activated alternatives, the EN standards for alkali-activated cement remain in committee, and procurement specifiers continue to anchor on CEM I and CEM II. CBAM Phase 2 brings chemicals in scope by 2028, removing the import-cost gap. The redirection programme from panel adhesives to cement activator supply does not generate near-term revenue, but BJ holds a structurally improved competitive position once the demand pull eventually arrives. The foundry inorganic adjacency and the UK fire-protection pull carry the next 24 to 36 months.

Early indicators: EU Industrial Decarbonisation Bank first cohort funds CCS, not alkali-activated; no further national ATec issued by mid-2027; CBAM 2027 evaluation brings chemicals in scope.

Slow Pull, Chemicals Stays Out

The worst-credible operating environment: both demand-side and cost-side levers move against BJ. Low-carbon construction demand pull remains a research story through 2028, CBAM defers chemicals, and Solvay's Torrelavega cut is followed by a second European soda ash producer rebalancing. European energy costs stay structurally elevated. The redirection programme runs at a loss for 24 months; the foundry and fire-protection adjacencies provide partial margin support but not enough to offset the cost-side compression. The explicit owner-intent decision flagged in cycle 1 is forced inside this horizon.

Early indicators: No alkali-activated cement plant FID announced in Europe through 2027; second European soda ash producer announces capacity cut; AccelerateEU State aid framework allocations bypass silicate chemistry.

Weak signals beneath the cycle

The themes above tracked the visible, well-evidenced moves of the past three months. These five weaker signals are under the consensus radar but worth seeding into next cycle's scan:

  1. Lithium silicate into EV battery component coatings. Bollerup Jensen is one of few European producers of lithium silicates. The European silicon-anode and solid-state cell value chains are scaling 2026 to 2028 and lithium silicate is plausibly a passivation-coating input. No public European cell-maker has yet specified a lithium-silicate spec; the first sourcing call would be a step-change signal for Bollerup Jensen's lithium silicate line.
  2. Soil stabilisation for offshore wind foundations. Sodium silicate grouting has decades of soil-stabilisation precedent. North Sea offshore wind foundation programmes (Dogger Bank, Hollandse Kust, Energy Island Bornholm) are entering ground-conditioning phases through 2026 to 2028 and silicate-based grouting is one of the candidate chemistries. Worth tracking which tier-1 marine contractors specify what.
  3. Silicate-bonded ceramic 3D printing. Industrial-scale binder-jet printing of refractories and structural ceramics is moving from research into commercial production; sodium silicate is one of the binder chemistries. European players (voxeljet, ExOne under Desktop Metal, EOS) and Japanese (Mimaki, KEYENCE) hold the IP front. Patent-filing density would be the leading indicator.
  4. Defence and NATO hardened-asset fire protection. Post-Ukraine European defence procurement is rebuilding hardened-shelter, ammunition-store and command-bunker capacity. Silicate-bonded passive fire protection has a documented military procurement history; NATO Standardisation Office publications and national defence-procurement notices are where this would first surface.
  5. Carbon-capture mineralisation pathways using sodium silicate. Several European start-ups (Carbon Re, Neustark, Carbonaide) are working CO2 mineralisation routes that involve silicate-bearing reactants. Bollerup Jensen's silicate chemistry sits within the precursor stack. Funding rounds and pilot announcements in this space would surface the demand-side opportunity early.

What We Are Not Planning For

The wood-panel adhesive substitution market

This cycle treats the panel-adhesive opening as closed. The substitution share has been claimed by pMDI and lignin chemistries, the EPF's own resin-cost estimate names UF, MUF and NAF reformulation rather than silicate, and the formaldehyde-resin chain is consolidating (Bondalti and Ercros) rather than exiting. Further commercial scoping into this market for the FY27 plan would extend a closed window. The redirection of effort into cement, foundry and fire-protection adjacencies releases the capital that the panel-adhesive line item was reserving.

Reinstate if: a major European panel producer announces a silicate-based binder commercial launch, or the European Panel Federation issues a revised cost analysis naming silicate as a credible E05 compliance route.

Standalone finished-binder competition against ASK, HA Group or Foseco

This cycle continues to treat finished foundry-binder system competition as out of scope. BJ's role is to be the specified silicate input that the binder majors formulate around, not a competitor to those majors on finished systems. The capital intensity, R&D depth and customer-side qualification cost of finished binder competition exceeds the redirection programme's return horizon, and the partner-of-the-majors path documented in SI 3 captures more of the foundry inorganic transition value at lower risk.

Reinstate if: one of the three foundry-chemicals majors exits a binder line that BJ already supplies into, opening a vertical-integration opportunity, or a Northern European OEM specifies a binder system formulated jointly by BJ and an EU foundry house.

An owner-intent decision in this cycle

Cycle 1 listed the owner-intent decision as one of three decide-now items. This cycle deliberately defers it. The strategic-redirection question now precedes the ownership question, and an owner-intent declaration made before SI 2 and SI 3 produce their first results would be made on incomplete information about which adjacency carries the next 24 months. The M&A cycle in adjacent silicate and binder assets continues, but the foregone optionality from one more cycle of deferral is judged smaller than the information value of the structural geopolymer engagement programme's first results.

Reinstate if: a named European silicate producer or geopolymer formulator changes hands in 2026 or H1 2027, or the cement and structural redirection programme produces a Q4 2026 milestone that materially repositions BJ's strategic optionality.
