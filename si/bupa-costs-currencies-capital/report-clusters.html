Why This Matters Now

Bupa is setting its plan for 2027 now. The half year went well: more customers, higher prices and better margins.

Three things could still move the numbers more than the plan expects. The cost of medical care is still rising much faster than general prices, and in some markets governments decide how far premiums can follow. Currencies, inflation in Türkiye and lower interest income can make reported profit look better or worse than the business really is. And government decisions on tax, refunds to customers and care funding now land directly in the accounts. At the same time, Bupa holds more capital than its own target range, while it buys clinics and hospitals.

The one thing to take into the next meeting: plan each market's medical costs, prices and government decisions as separate lines, so the plan shows where profit growth really comes from.

Executive Synthesis

Which numbers in the 2027 plan on your desk this week assume medical costs slow down?

The evidence here says cost trend stays near 9% while governments decide how far some prices can follow.

Bupa's half year was strong: revenue rose 10% to £9.9bn as customers grew and prices rose, and underlying profit rose 21% at constant exchange rates (Bupa, Aug 2026). The 2027 plan now has to decide how much of that carries forward. Medical costs are still rising far faster than general inflation: Aon expects employer medical plan costs to rise 9% globally in 2027 (Aon, Oct 2026), and WTW put UK healthcare inflation at 10% for 2026 (WTW, Nov 2025). Where governments have a say, prices follow more slowly. Australia's average premium rose 4.41% in April 2026, and a bill to remove the higher rebate for over-65s from 1 April 2027 has cleared a Senate committee (Insurance Business Australia, Oct 2026).

Some of the profit picture is settled: solvency is 189%, above the 140 to 170% working range, and the £900m credit facility is undrawn (Bupa Finance plc half-year report, Aug 2026). Three things are not. How much will currencies flatter or hide trading, when the stronger Australian dollar added £11m at actual rates this half and Türkiye's inflation adjustment cost £17m (Bupa Finance plc half-year report, Aug 2026)? Which government decisions recur, when Mexico's 2027 revenue proposal keeps the limit on insurers' VAT credit (EY Mexico, in Spanish, Sep 2026) and Chile's isapres had repaid only 21.2% of their court-ordered debt by June (Chile's health insurance regulator, in Spanish, Aug 2026)? And what is the surplus capital for, as the Partnered Health purchase is cleared (Australian Competition and Consumer Commission, Oct 2026)?

Looking ahead to 2030, Aon expects Europe's medical cost trend to ease from 8.2% in 2026 to 7.5% in 2027 (Aon, Oct 2026), IQVIA forecasts cancer drug spending to grow 10% a year from 2026 to 2030 (IQVIA, Jul 2026), Commonwealth Bank expects the pound to buy 1.8551 Australian dollars at the end of 2027 (Commonwealth Bank, reported by Pound Sterling Live, Oct 2026), and Chile's isapres have until December 2037 to finish repaying (La Tercera, in Spanish, Aug 2026). None of these is certain, and each is a point where the plan can still choose its position: section 8 sets out the options.

Where this analysis could be wrong

The weakest assumption is that the gap between costs and prices stays open. Bupa's combined operating ratio improved to 94% as prices rose (Bupa, Aug 2026), and Aon's global trend is falling, from 9.8% in 2026 to 9.0% in 2027 (Aon, Oct 2026). If that continues, the 2027 plan could carry more pricing room than this briefing assumes.

The decisions that cannot be deferred this cycle

  1. Set medical cost trend market by market, and show the price-cost gap in each. One group trend hides where prices cannot follow.
  2. Plan at constant exchange rates, with a separate bridge for currency, inflation accounting and interest income. Otherwise the plan cannot show what trading delivers.
  3. Decide what the capital above the working range is for before the next acquisition. Deals are moving faster than the plan for the surplus.

1. Medical costs are still rising faster than regulators will let prices follow

Immediate

Weight: high · Who decides: Market finance and pricing teams, with Australia's Health Minister approving premium changes · Room to act: Moderate: Bupa sets most of its own prices in the UK and Spain; Australian increases need government approval

Medical costs in Bupa's markets are still rising at close to 9 to 10% a year, well above general inflation. Prices are following, but more slowly where governments have a say: in Australia the Health Minister approves premium changes, and support for older members is being cut. The 2027 plan has to assume a gap between costs and prices that differs by market.

Sources: (WTW, Nov 2025); (Aon, Oct 2026); (Aon, Oct 2026); (Insurance Business Australia, Oct 2026).

  • Aon expects employer medical plan costs to rise by an average of 9% globally in 2027, with Europe easing from 8.2% to 7.5% (Aon, Oct 2026), and Asia Pacific rising 9.7% after 11.3% in 2026, still more than three times the rate of inflation (Aon, Oct 2026). WTW put UK healthcare inflation at 10% for 2026, among the highest in Western Europe (WTW, Nov 2025).
  • In Australia the average premium rose 4.41% in April 2026, and a bill to remove the higher rebate for over-65s from 1 April 2027 has cleared a Senate committee (Insurance Business Australia, Oct 2026). Health insurers' after-tax profit fell to $210 million in the March 2026 quarter, from $417 million in December 2025 (Insurance Business Australia, May 2026), and the average out-of-pocket payment for a hospital stay reached $513 (Australian Prudential Regulation Authority, Aug 2026).
  • In the UK, insurance-funded private hospital admissions fell 2.6% in the first quarter of 2026 while self-pay admissions rose 7.7% (Private Healthcare Information Network, Sep 2026). In Spain, Sanitas grew its health insurance income 9.39%, faster than the market (El Español, in Spanish, Jul 2026).

Early indicators

  • Weak signal Governments are cutting support for older members, not just capping prices. Australia's rebate change for over-65s comes on top of the 2026 premium rise (Insurance Business Australia, Oct 2026), while in the UK insured admissions fall as self-pay grows (Private Healthcare Information Network, Sep 2026). Insured books could shrink at the oldest, highest-claiming end.

Outlook to 2030

WTW found that 55% of insurers expect further cost increases, and expect elevated levels to persist for more than three years (WTW, Nov 2025). Department of Health modelling projects about 44,000 fewer adults aged 65 and over holding cover in Australia by 2028-29 if the rebate change goes ahead (Insurance Business Australia, Oct 2026). On this briefing's reading, the 2027 to 2029 plan should carry a medical cost trend of 7.5 to 10% depending on the market, with the least pricing room in Australia.

What it means: Set medical cost trend market by market and show the gap between costs and allowed or achievable prices in each, rather than one group trend.

Counter-argument

Prices have kept up so far. Bupa says customer growth and price increases lifted revenue 10% to £9.9bn (Bupa, Aug 2026), and Aon's global trend is falling, with most European countries reporting flat or lower trends (Aon, Oct 2026). If trend keeps easing, the gap could close faster than this theme assumes.

2. Currencies and interest rates now swing reported profit in either direction

Immediate

Weight: high · Who decides: Group FP&A and Treasury; central banks and currency markets set the rates · Room to act: Low over the rates themselves; high over how the plan separates them from trading

Bupa reports in sterling but earns most of its profit in other currencies, so currency movements, Türkiye's inflation accounting and interest income now move reported profit by tens of millions of pounds a period, sometimes up and sometimes down. In the last two periods they ran in opposite directions. The 2027 plan needs to show them separately from trading.

Sources: (Euronews, in Turkish, Aug 2026); (Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye, in Turkish, Sep 2026).

  • At actual exchange rates, half-year profit was lifted by a further £11m as the Australian dollar strengthened against sterling (Bupa Finance plc half-year report, Aug 2026). A year earlier the opposite happened: full-year statutory profit of £970m was flat at actual rates because currency movements offset growth (Bupa, Mar 2026).
  • Türkiye's inflation accounting produced a net monetary loss of £17m within currency losses of £24m (Bupa Finance plc half-year report, Aug 2026). The central bank holds its policy rate at 37%, and the market expects inflation of 29.4% at the end of 2026 and 21.9% at the end of 2027 (Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye, in Turkish, Sep 2026), well above the central bank's own forecast of 15% for 2027 (Euronews, in Turkish, Aug 2026).
  • Australia's health insurance business grew against a headwind from reduced investment income (Bupa Finance plc half-year report, Aug 2026). The Australian dollar is about 5% higher than at the start of 2026, and markets price only about half a rate rise by the end of the year (Reserve Bank of Australia, Aug 2026). The Bank of England held Bank Rate at 3.75%, while UK short-term rates are expected to peak at around 4.9% by the end of 2027 (Bank of England, Sep 2026).
  • In Latin America, Brazil's policy rate of 13.75% is expected to fall to 12% in 2027 and 10.5% in 2028 (Agência Brasil, in Portuguese, Oct 2026), and Chile's central bank expects inflation to return to 3% in the second quarter of 2027, with its policy rate at 4.5% (Central Bank of Chile, in Spanish, Sep 2026).

Early indicators

  • Weak signal Market inflation expectations in Türkiye are rising, not falling. The end-2027 expectation rose 0.5 points to 21.9% in September (Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye, in Turkish, Sep 2026), so the inflation adjustment may last longer than the central bank's path suggests.

Outlook to 2030

Commonwealth Bank expects the pound to buy 1.8551 Australian dollars at the end of 2027 and 1.8986 through 2028 (Commonwealth Bank, reported by Pound Sterling Live, Oct 2026); Türkiye's central bank forecasts inflation of 9% at the end of 2028 (Euronews, in Turkish, Aug 2026); and Brazil's market expects a policy rate of 10.5% in 2028 (Agência Brasil, in Portuguese, Oct 2026). On this briefing's reading, currency effects in 2027 and 2028 are likely to be smaller than this year's, but inflation accounting in Türkiye and falling interest income in Latin America will keep moving reported profit.

What it means: Plan at constant exchange rates and show a separate bridge for currency translation, inflation accounting and investment income, so the Board sees what trading delivers.

Counter-argument

The effects may be small and cancel out. The £11m currency gain is small next to the 21% growth in underlying profit at constant rates (Bupa, Aug 2026), and Commonwealth Bank's forecast implies only a modest move in the Australian dollar against sterling over the plan years (Commonwealth Bank, reported by Pound Sterling Live, Oct 2026).

3. Government decisions now arrive as lines in the forecast

Near-Term

Weight: medium · Who decides: Tax authorities, health regulators and parliaments in Mexico, Chile, Spain and Australia · Room to act: Low over the decisions; moderate over how early the plan carries them

Government decisions now land directly in Bupa's profit and cash: a tax change in Mexico, court-ordered refunds in Chile, state contract terms in Spain and care funding in Australia. Several recur rather than happen once. Bupa itself names the risk of sudden government policy changes. The plan should carry announced decisions as numbers, with ranges, rather than leave them in the risk register.

  • In Mexico, underlying profit fell because a new tax law denies the recovery of VAT on hospital and medical costs (Bupa Finance plc half-year report, Aug 2026). The 2027 economic package sent to Congress keeps the limit on insurers' VAT credit (EY Mexico, in Spanish, Sep 2026), so the cost recurs in the plan year.
  • In Chile, isapres had deposited 21.2% of the total debt into members' accounts by 30 June 2026, under a law that allows up to 13 years, and they cannot pay out profits or dividends until the debt is paid (Chile's health insurance regulator, in Spanish, Aug 2026).
  • In Spain, a snap general election on 29 November could turn the tenders for the state's civil-servant health schemes into an extension (Redacción Médica, in Spanish, Oct 2026), adding uncertainty to the price the state pays private insurers.
  • In Australia, the government-set price for residential aged care rises 2.55% from 1 October 2026, while award wages went up 4.75% (Inside Ageing, Sep 2026).

Early indicators

  • Weak signal Mexico's lawmakers are turning to premiums themselves. With medical inflation projected at 14.8% this year and insurers losing their VAT credit, Congress is preparing to regulate major medical insurance (El CEO, in Spanish, Aug 2026).

Outlook to 2030

The latest deadline for Chile's repayments is December 2037, although several isapres have chosen to pay early (La Tercera, in Spanish, Aug 2026), and Mexico's revenue law for 2027 still needs approval by Congress (EY Mexico, in Spanish, Sep 2026). On this briefing's reading, by 2030 government decisions on tax, refunds and funding will move profit in at least three of Bupa's markets each year, so they belong in the base plan.

What it means: Carry each announced government decision as an explicit line in the 2027 plan, with a low and a high case, and review the lines when parliaments vote.

Counter-argument

Some of these risks are fading. Chile's isapre system returned to a net profit of 34,091 million pesos in the first quarter, with eight of ten isapres profitable (Chile's health insurance regulator, in Spanish, Jun 2026), and Bupa reports stability in its isapre (Bupa Finance plc half-year report, Aug 2026).

4. Capital above the target range: buy, invest or hold

Near-Term

Weight: medium · Who decides: The Group CFO and Board · Room to act: High: the use of surplus capital is Bupa's own decision

Bupa holds more capital than its own working range calls for, at the same time as it buys clinics and hospitals and issues new debt. The surplus gives room to act, but it also raises the question the plan has to answer: what is it for? Deals take time to pay back, and integration costs arrive first.

Source: (Bupa Finance plc half-year report, Aug 2026).

  • Bupa's solvency coverage ratio of 189% is above the top end of its 140 to 170% working range; leverage fell to 14.9%, and the £900m credit facility was undrawn (Bupa Finance plc half-year report, Aug 2026).
  • The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission has cleared Bupa's purchase of Partnered Health (Australian Competition and Consumer Commission, Oct 2026), which would give Bupa 100 clinics and make it Australia's fifth-largest general practice operator (The Medical Republic, Jun 2026). In the UK, health services profit fell as Bupa invests in King Edward VII's and New Victoria hospitals (Bupa Finance plc half-year report, Aug 2026).
  • Bupa has issued £300m of 6.875% subordinated notes due 2038, partly to refinance existing securities (Slaughter and May, Oct 2026). In Mexico, Fitch says capital remains under pressure after VAT-related losses raised the combined ratio to 102.3% (Fitch Ratings, in Spanish, Oct 2026).

Early indicators

  • Weak signal Insurers are becoming large owners of family doctors. With Partnered Health, insurers would run three in every 20 group general practices in Australia (The Medical Republic, Jun 2026), and Saudi insurers are opening their own clinics (L.E.K. Consulting, Jul 2026). Regulators may start to treat insurer-owned care as a competition question.

Outlook to 2030

Bain finds that large deals take roughly seven months to close and another 24 to 36 months to deliver most of their cost savings (Bain & Company, Jun 2026). In Saudi Arabia, where Bupa has an associate business, the government aims to raise the private share of healthcare delivery from 40% to 65% by 2030, and L.E.K. warns that insurers that hesitate will face inflated asset prices (L.E.K. Consulting, Jul 2026). On this briefing's reading, deals agreed in 2026 will cost money in 2027 and pay back mostly from 2028.

What it means: Set a target solvency ratio for the end of 2027 and an envelope for acquisitions, with integration costs in the 2027 plan and benefits from 2028.

Counter-argument

There are good reasons to hold the surplus. Acquirers underperformed the MSCI World Index by 10.3 percentage points in the third quarter of 2026 (Insurance Business, reporting WTW, Oct 2026), Chile's isapres still owe most of their court-ordered refunds (Chile's health insurance regulator, in Spanish, Aug 2026), and Mexico's capital is under pressure (Fitch Ratings, in Spanish, Oct 2026).

5. Cancer, heart disease and weight-loss drugs are reshaping claims to 2030

Longer-Range

Weight: medium · Who decides: Clinicians, drug regulators and price-setting bodies; Bupa's actuaries set the assumptions · Room to act: Moderate: Bupa chooses what it covers and how it models claims by condition

Drugs and long-term conditions are taking a larger share of claims. Cancer drug costs keep rising fast, heart disease leads claims in Asia Pacific, and weight-loss drugs are both a new cost and, where cheaper copies arrive, a falling one. Multi-year claims assumptions need to be set by condition, not as one trend.

  • Aon finds cardiovascular disease is the leading condition driving claims in Asia Pacific, followed by respiratory diseases and cancer, and that prescription medication has moved into third place among the costliest parts of plans (Aon, Oct 2026). WTW expects drugs to reach nearly 27% of US employer health spending in 2027, from 24.6% in 2025 (WTW, Sep 2026).
  • Bupa Global's claims costs for obesity, diabetes and other metabolic conditions rose 97% since 2021 (Health Insurance & Protection, reporting Bupa Global, Nov 2025).
  • Cheaper copies of semaglutide, the active ingredient in Ozempic and Wegovy, are arriving: Brazil approved its first after the patent expired on 20 March 2026 (Brazil's health regulator Anvisa, in Portuguese, May 2026), and Canada approved a generic for weight management, noting that generics are often 45 to 90% cheaper (Health Canada, Jun 2026). In Australia, a subsidy for Wegovy was recommended but has not yet been listed (Australian Government Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme, Jul 2026).

Early indicators

  • Weak signal Metabolic conditions could overtake cancer as the biggest cost. Bupa Global says obesity and diabetes claims costs could soon become the leading driver of international private medical insurance costs (Health Insurance & Protection, reporting Bupa Global, Nov 2025).

Outlook to 2030

IQVIA forecasts cancer drug spending, $291Bn in 2025, to grow 10% a year from 2026 to 2030 (IQVIA, Jul 2026). On this briefing's reading, by 2030 drugs and long-term conditions will be a larger share of Bupa's claims than today, with weight-loss drug costs rising in markets without cheaper copies and falling in those with them.

What it means: Model claims for the main conditions separately in the 2027 to 2029 plan, and tie weight-loss drug assumptions to the arrival of cheaper copies market by market.

Counter-argument

Weight-loss drugs may lower later claims. England's medicines regulator NICE has made more than 1.2 million people eligible for semaglutide to protect the heart, after a trial showed a 20% reduced risk of a serious cardiovascular event (Pulse, May 2026), and generics will cut prices (Health Canada, Jun 2026).

The emerging-market engine

↗ Shift from todayFrom a £17m inflation adjustment in Türkiye this half year (Bupa Finance plc half-year report, Aug 2026) to high-inflation markets carrying a much larger share of Group profit.

In 2030 Bupa's growth comes mainly from Latin America, Türkiye and its associates in India and Saudi Arabia, where insurers can still price for local inflation. Profit grows fast in local currency but swings in sterling, and inflation accounting is a permanent feature of the results. The finance team's main job is separating real growth from currency and inflation.

Early indicators: Premium growth in high-inflation markets outpaces the core; currency effects grow larger than this year's; new acquisitions in emerging markets.

Growth with a government hand on the price

↗ Shift from todayFrom one tax change in Mexico and court-ordered refunds in Chile (EY Mexico, in Spanish, Sep 2026) to rules on premiums in most of Bupa's high-growth markets.

In 2030 the high-growth markets still grow, but governments there set or cap what insurers can charge, through tax, refund and premium rules. Profit growth lags volume growth, and the plan is shaped by legislative calendars as much as by customers. Bupa's provision businesses, which are paid by many payers, matter more than its insurance margins.

Early indicators: Mexico's Congress regulates major medical premiums; another market orders refunds; price approvals spread beyond Australia.

Priced to cost at home

↗ Shift from todayFrom an approved average premium rise of 4.41% in Australia (Insurance Business Australia, Oct 2026) to prices in the core markets that track medical costs year by year.

In 2030 growth comes from the UK, Australia and Spain, and insurers there can price to medical costs. Margins are steady, the claims mix shifts toward drugs and long-term conditions, and the main planning question is how quickly costs rise by condition. Owning clinics and hospitals helps control costs rather than drive growth.

Early indicators: Approved premium rises in Australia close the gap with costs; UK insured admissions recover; cheaper weight-loss drugs reach core markets.

The regulated utility

↗ Shift from todayFrom a planned cut to the rebate for over-65s in Australia (Insurance Business Australia, Oct 2026) to government-set prices and support across Bupa's core markets.

In 2030 the mature markets still provide most of the profit, but governments set prices, subsidies and contract terms for private cover, much as they regulate a utility. Returns are stable and capped. Surplus capital has fewer uses in insurance, so it flows into provision or is held, and older members are the hardest group to keep.

Early indicators: Further cuts to support for older members; the state sets contract prices for longer periods; insurers' margins converge.

Potential Surprises to 2040

Emerging

Governments withdraw support for older members, and insured books shrink from the top

Window: 2030 to 2036 · Probability band: Low-Medium (20 to 40%)

Why it would surprise: Most plans assume older members stay insured because they value cover most, whatever governments do.

Australia plans to remove the higher rebate for over-65s from April 2027, and Department of Health modelling projects about 44,000 fewer over-65s with cover by 2028-29 (Insurance Business Australia, Oct 2026); in the UK, insured admissions fell while self-pay grew (Private Healthcare Information Network, Sep 2026).

Signposts to watch:

  • By June 2027: Australia's rebate change for over-65s is in force.
  • By December 2028: another of Bupa's markets cuts tax relief or a subsidy for older people's private health cover.

What it touches today: Pricing and retention plans for members over 65, and the age mix assumed in the 2027 to 2029 plan.

Weak signal

Regulators force insurers to separate from the clinics they own

Window: 2031 to 2038 · Probability band: Low (under 20%)

Why it would surprise: Buying clinics and hospitals is treated as a way to control costs, not as a competition risk.

With Partnered Health, insurers would run three in every 20 group general practices in Australia (The Medical Republic, Jun 2026), and Saudi insurers are building their own clinic networks (L.E.K. Consulting, Jul 2026).

Signposts to watch:

  • By December 2027: a competition regulator in one of Bupa's markets opens a study of insurer ownership of doctors, clinics or hospitals.
  • By December 2028: a government restricts how insurers steer members to clinics they own.

What it touches today: The price paid for clinic and hospital acquisitions, and the savings the plan assumes from steering members to them.

Weak signal

Metabolic conditions overtake cancer as the largest claims cost

Window: 2030 to 2035 · Probability band: Low-Medium (20 to 40%)

Why it would surprise: Cancer is assumed to remain the largest and fastest-growing claims cost for the foreseeable future.

Bupa Global's claims costs for obesity, diabetes and metabolic conditions rose 97% since 2021, and it says they could soon become the leading driver of international cover costs (Health Insurance & Protection, reporting Bupa Global, Nov 2025).

Signposts to watch:

  • By December 2027: an international health insurer reports metabolic conditions as its largest claims category.
  • By June 2028: a cheaper copy of semaglutide is approved in Mexico, Chile or Türkiye.

What it touches today: Cover decisions for weight-loss drugs and the condition mix in the 2027 to 2029 claims model.
