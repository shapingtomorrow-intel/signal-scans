Why This Matters Now

Five large programmes are queuing for the same scarce resource at the same time: the Box 3 transition, the new coalition's reform mandate, the European e-invoicing build, the rebuild of fraud defences, and compliance with the EU's AI rules. The resource is not money and it is not headcount. It is the capacity to change systems safely, and it is only now beginning to come free.

That makes capacity allocation the strategic decision of this cycle rather than a planning detail. Whichever of the five is served first is, in effect, the organisation's strategy, whether or not anyone writes it down that way. Around this sits a harder constraint still: trust is recovering slowly, the ceiling on the administration's own use of AI is rising just as AI-enabled fraud scales, and the working environment for staff has hardened to the point where a court has allowed them to be named only by reference number.

The one thing to take into the next meeting: the queue will be resolved either by decision or by default, and a default is the most expensive option available.

Executive Synthesis

Which of the five programmes competing for your change capacity has actually been ranked, and by whom?

Box 3, the reform mandate, the e-invoicing build, the fraud-defence rebuild and AI Act compliance all draw on the same scarce capacity, and nothing in the public record ranks them against each other.

This is the first Decision Intelligence cycle on the Belastingdienst, so it sets a baseline rather than grading a previous reading. The baseline is this: capacity has stopped being a support question and become the strategic one. Five major workstreams are converging on the same constrained change capacity. The Box 3 transition is legislated toward 1 January 2028, with the Wet werkelijk rendement box 3 through the lower house in February (Meijburg & Co, May 2025). The new coalition has committed to a fundamental overhaul of income tax, benefits and social security with its reform agenda due at the end of the year (EY Netherlands, Feb 2026). The European e-invoicing and digital reporting build runs to a 2030 mandate (RTC Suite, Oct 2025). Fraud defences need rebuilding against a materially different attacker. And the EU AI rules fall due inside the window.

Nothing in the public record ranks these five against one another. That is the finding. A queue that is never explicitly ordered is still ordered, by whichever programme has the nearest statutory date or the loudest sponsor, and the result is a strategy nobody chose. The lever available is prioritisation with disclosure: saying out loud which of the five is being served first and what the others will not get, rather than absorbing the conflict inside delivery plans where it becomes visible only as slippage.

Three constraints sit underneath and none of them is inside the organisation's gift. Trust is recovering, but slowly and from a low base: reported trust in government fell from 51% to 44% across a single winter, and among the most highly educated group, historically the most positive, from 60% to 52% (the Sociaal en Cultureel Planbureau, Mar 2025). The Algemene Rekenkamer found that the Belastingdienst and Dienst Toeslagen still do not manage privacy risks well in their use of algorithms, while pointing out that another executive agency runs the same class of tool compliantly (the Algemene Rekenkamer, May 2025). And the working environment has hardened far enough that in January a court accepted that naming tax officials in cases involving the soevereinen movement would expose them to real danger (the Gerechtshof Arnhem-Leeuwarden, Jan 2026).

A queue that is never explicitly ordered is still ordered, by whichever programme has the nearest statutory date or the loudest sponsor. That is a strategy nobody chose.

The cross-border wave is the cycle's clearest opportunity and its clearest squeeze, and it is the same wave. DAC8 took effect on 1 January 2026 and the Dutch implementing bill cleared the lower house in March (KPMG, Mar 2026); the 2026 package carried DAC9 and the latest Pillar Two guidance (KPMG, Dec 2025). Taken together these give the administration its strongest information position in a decade. They also arrive as one more set of overlapping reporting regimes on an administration that is already among the busiest recipients in Europe, which is the case the advisers' own recast submission makes (CFE Tax Advisers Europe, Feb 2026). The uplift and the burden are not separable, and the second is not a reason to decline the first.

The temptation in a cycle like this is to treat the reform mandate as the answer to everything: a window in which the citizen-state interaction could be redesigned rather than merely maintained. The window is real. The capacity to use it is the thing this brief keeps returning to, and the administration's own published plan is more sober about it than the surrounding commentary. That tension is not resolved here. It is the fourth theme, and the evidence there runs against the optimistic reading.

Where this analysis could be wrong

The capacity crunch has been the standing narrative for years, and collection has not failed. An argument that this time the queue is different has to carry that history. The honest form of the claim is narrower: not that the organisation will fail, but that five programmes with external dates cannot all be first, and that the ranking is currently implicit. The thesis weakens if a published plan ranks them explicitly and names what each will not receive.

Three of this cycle's fraud sources are commercially interested and make the same argument. Sumsub, Experian and Sardine all sell fraud prevention and all argue that agentic AI fraud is arriving and that incumbent detection cannot stop it (Sumsub, Nov 2025) (Experian, Jan 2026) (Sardine.ai, Feb 2026). That is one vendor argument repeated three times, not three independent corroborations, and two of the three are forecasts about 2026 written before it began. We have treated the direction as credible and the magnitude as unevidenced.

Parts of the evidence base are thinner than a source count suggests. The Algemene Rekenkamer news item and its underlying audit report are one publication, not two (the Algemene Rekenkamer, May 2025) (the Algemene Rekenkamer, May 2025), and the two Burgerperspectieven instalments are successive editions of one series (the Sociaal en Cultureel Planbureau, Mar 2025) (the Sociaal en Cultureel Planbureau, Oct 2025). Six of the pages cited carry no printed publication date at all, including the European Commission's own AI policy page, so they are recorded as undated rather than assigned a date inferred from a web address. The court ruling is cited from a commercial aggregator rather than the court's own text, which is why it is graded Tier 3 despite being a judgment (the Gerechtshof Arnhem-Leeuwarden, Jan 2026).

The decisions that cannot be deferred this cycle

  1. Rank the five, in writing. The queue is being ordered by statutory proximity rather than by strategic value, which is a choice made by not making one.
  2. Decide whether the reform is delivery or redesign. The answer determines whether the organisation is in the room when the new system is specified.
  3. Treat the AI ceiling as a design constraint, not a delay. The defence against industrialised fraud has to be built inside the limits, because the limits are not going to move first.
  4. Bank the cross-border information uplift before absorbing its burden. DAC8 and DAC9 data have analytical value that is separable from the reporting load they arrive with.

Each is developed below, with a decision posture, in the four Strategic Implications.

Themes

The cycle's signals are organised into four themes, ranked by impact on near-term decisions. Immediate: changes the near-term plan, the structure or the proposition. Near-Term: changes position over the next twelve months. Longer-Range: a multi-year factor to track and revisit each cycle.

1. Trust recovery as the binding constraint

Immediate

Trust is recovering slowly while the operating environment for staff has hardened, and the two move together. Three vectors converge: the unresolved legacy of the childcare-benefits scandal, continuing scrutiny of algorithmic selection, and an anti-institutional movement that has changed how officials can safely work. Any further visible misstep resets the recovery clock at exactly the moment the organisation needs goodwill capital to absorb a large reform mandate. This is the binding constraint on every other theme in this brief, because it governs what the organisation is permitted to do rather than what it is able to do.

Goodwill is the capital a reform mandate is spent from. It fell in both groups across a single winter.

  • Reported trust in government fell from 51% to 44% between summer and autumn 2024 and the following winter. Among the higher-educated group, historically the most positive, it fell from 60% to 52%, closing a margin that had held for years (the Sociaal en Cultureel Planbureau, Mar 2025).
  • By the autumn instalment the framing had moved from confidence to maatschappelijk onbehagen, a settled sense that the country is heading the wrong way, read as the frame for the election run-up rather than as an episodic mood (the Sociaal en Cultureel Planbureau, Oct 2025).
  • The Algemene Rekenkamer found that the Belastingdienst and Dienst Toeslagen still do not manage privacy risks well in their use of algorithms, and framed it as a governance failure rather than a problem with the tools, pointing out that another executive agency runs the same class of instrument compliantly (the Algemene Rekenkamer, May 2025). The underlying audit records a structural staffing shortfall and unreplaced legacy IT as what blocks simplification (the Algemene Rekenkamer, May 2025).
  • In January a court accepted that naming officials in cases involving the soevereinen movement would expose them to doxing and real danger, and allowed the Belastingdienst to withhold staff names. Anonymity is now legally grounded operating practice rather than a contingency (the Gerechtshof Arnhem-Leeuwarden, Jan 2026).
  • The AIVD's annual report, published five days before this brief, describes a compounding rather than episodic threat picture, made persistent by the destabilisation of the wider order (the AIVD, Apr 2026).
  • The administration's own answer to the legitimacy problem is disclosure: an algorithm register published on the argument that saying what each system does is what makes its use defensible (the Belastingdienst algorithm register, May 2023).

Early indicators

  • Weak signal Anonymity as an operating model, not an exception. A court has now blessed a defensive posture. The second-order effect is on recruitment, on the willingness of staff to take enforcement roles, and on how an organisation explains itself publicly when its people cannot be named (the Gerechtshof Arnhem-Leeuwarden, Jan 2026).

Counter-argument

Trust measures are volatile and a single winter is not a trend; the series itself is one publication read twice, not two independent readings. The audit finding is also a year old at the cycle date and may have been acted on without a public statement. The thesis weakens if the next Burgerperspectieven instalment shows the fall reversing, or if the audit office records the privacy findings as resolved.

Decision link: SI 1. Goodwill is the capital a reform mandate is spent from, so its level is a planning input, not a communications matter.

2. Three AI vectors converging at once

Immediate

Three distinct AI vectors arrive at the same moment. Taxpayers have begun treating generative AI as their first-line adviser. Fraudsters are industrialising. And the administration's own ceiling on AI use is rising, under post-scandal scrutiny at home and a new regulatory regime from Europe. The awkwardness is structural rather than accidental: the natural defence against automated attack is more automated detection, which is exactly the move the regulatory regime is narrowing.

  • Around 64% of businesses now consult public AI chatbots before asking their accountant. The survey is UK based and the Dutch direction of travel reads consistent rather than proven (Accountant.nl, Oct 2025).
  • The EU framework makes trustworthy AI the condition of market access rather than merely restricting it, and the obligations on high-risk public-sector systems fall inside this planning window (the European Commission, undated). The Act applies in phases rather than at a single moment, which decides what has already bitten and what can still be prepared for (Kennedys Law, undated).
  • Agentic systems break the framework's assumptions about traceability and human oversight: without an audit trail and a fast way to revoke an agent's authority, lawful operation cannot be demonstrated (AI News, Apr 2026).
  • Fraud's composition is shifting even where its headline rate is flat, from single-step deception toward multi-step and agentic schemes (Sumsub, Nov 2025), with vendors forecasting agentic attack as the leading 2026 threat (Experian, Jan 2026) (Sardine.ai, Feb 2026). All three are commercially interested and are treated here as one argument, not three.
  • Putting tax material through public AI platforms now carries a privilege risk after a US ruling that exchanges with a public AI tool were not protected, which makes workflow choice a legal question rather than a productivity one (Morgan Lewis, Mar 2026).
  • The Dutch regulator's position is that algorithmic risk should be tracked and published as a recurring public picture rather than handled case by case (the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, undated).

Early indicators

  • Weak signal The adviser layer is being disintermediated before the administration is. If most businesses ask a chatbot first, the quality of the answer they receive becomes a compliance variable the administration does not control and cannot see (Accountant.nl, Oct 2025).

Counter-argument

The regulatory ceiling may be lower in the headlines than in practice: phasing and public-interest provisions give more room than a strict reading implies, and the fraud case rests on interested vendors forecasting a year that had barely started. The honest position is that the direction is credible and the magnitude is not evidenced. The thesis weakens if a supervisory instrument gives public-authority enforcement systems explicit room, or if independent, non-vendor data on agentic fraud fails to materialise.

Decision link: SI 3. Build the detection capability that is defensible inside the current constraints rather than one that assumes they ease.

3. Cross-border harmonisation as both opportunity and operational squeeze

Near-Term

The 2026 wave of European instruments gives the administration its strongest information position in a decade and its heaviest concurrent reporting load, and these are the same wave rather than two competing ones. The question this theme puts is not whether to absorb the burden but whether the analytical value of the incoming data is being extracted at all, or whether it is being received, stored and left.

  • DAC8 extends automatic exchange to crypto-assets, which the Commission frames as closing a gap national authorities cannot close alone (the European Commission, undated). The Dutch implementing bill cleared the lower house in March and bites retroactively from the start of 2026, so obligations run ahead of final enactment (KPMG, Mar 2026).
  • The 2026 package completed passage on schedule carrying DAC9 transposition and Pillar Two amendment, making both settled law rather than pending proposals (KPMG, Dec 2025).
  • The Commission's agenda has turned toward simplification, with a package reopening fifteen existing directives (Accountancy Europe, Feb 2026), and the omnibus is consolidation rather than a new front: a review of how existing rules operate together (Grant Thornton, Mar 2026).
  • The advisers' own submissions argue the DAC has fragmented across eight amendments into an incoherent compliance stack that the recast should consolidate (CFE Tax Advisers Europe, Feb 2026), and that simplification is only real if it removes duplication with Pillar Two rather than weakening safeguards (CFE Tax Advisers Europe, Mar 2026).
  • Industry makes the same argument from the other side: overlapping DAC7, CESOP and ViDA obligations have produced a sharp rise in compliance cost (the European Tech Alliance, Feb 2026).
  • Portfolio note, because it bears on who the administration negotiates with: the Commissioner carrying taxation holds it as a secondary responsibility alongside climate and clean growth, not as his portfolio (the European Commission, undated).

Counter-argument

An information uplift is only an uplift if it is used, and nothing in this evidence base demonstrates analytical capacity to exploit DAC8 and DAC9 data rather than merely receive it. It is equally arguable that the simplification agenda will reduce the load before the burden becomes binding. The thesis weakens if the recast lands early and materially, or if the administration publishes a concrete analytical use for the new data flows.

Decision link: SI 4. Separate the value of the data from the cost of receiving it, and fund the first.

4. The reform window meets the capacity that has to deliver it

Longer-Range

A coalition with a reform mandate is, on the face of it, a once-in-a-generation chance to redesign the citizen-state tax interaction toward something embedded and seamless rather than periodic and adversarial. That reading is the optimistic one and this brief does not adopt it. On the evidence assembled here the window is real and the capacity to use it is not yet demonstrated, and the sources closest to the organisation are the most cautious of all.

Only one of the five has no external date. Where a queue is not ranked explicitly, statutory proximity ranks it.

  • The coalition commits to a fundamental overhaul of income tax, benefits and social security with the reform agenda due at the end of 2026, and a new Box 3 regime targeted at 1 January 2028 (EY Netherlands, Feb 2026) (Meijburg & Co, May 2025).
  • The direction of travel internationally is real: comparative data across tax administrations shows prefilling, embedded payment and digital identity scaling (OECD, Nov 2025), and administrations describing themselves as becoming technology-led institutions (the OECD, undated).
  • But the administration's own published plan puts fully automatic taxation after 2035, which is two planning horizons beyond the reform agenda it is said to enable (the Belastingdienst, Dec 2025).
  • And the estate has not yet moved far enough to carry it. Legacy systems stood at 19.3% with several phase-out projects behind their planned progress (ICT Magazine, May 2025).
  • And the mandate itself is weaker than the word suggests. The measures come from a minority coalition that lacks the parliamentary majority to enact them, which is the reading the analysis stresses rather than a caveat added to it (KPMG, Feb 2026).
  • The e-invoicing infrastructure that would operationalise an embedded model is targeted at July 2030, well outside the coalition's own term (RTC Suite, Oct 2025).

Early indicators

  • Weak signal The design table and the delivery table are being allocated now. Whether the administration is treated as the designer of a new operating model or the deliverer of someone else's policy is being settled by where capacity is pointed this year, not by the reform agenda's eventual text (EY Netherlands, Feb 2026).

Counter-argument

The case for the optimistic reading is that windows like this are rare and that waiting for demonstrated capacity guarantees the window closes unused: an organisation that will only commit once its estate is ready will never commit. That argument has force, and it is the reason this theme is graded Longer-Range rather than dismissed. But the three sources closest to the work, the administration's own plan, the state of its legacy estate, and the arithmetic of a minority coalition, all point the same way, and a thesis whose nearest evidence contradicts it should be held lightly. The thesis strengthens if the reform agenda arrives with a funded operating-model workstream and a date earlier than 2035 attached to it.

Decision link: SI 2. Decide whether this is policy delivery or operating-model redesign, because the two need different people and a different budget.

Strategic Implications

Four implications, each carrying a decision posture. Two are decisions this cycle rather than next, because in both cases the default is being chosen now by not choosing.

SI 1: Rank the five competing programmes in writing, and publish what the losers do not get

Box 3 toward 2028 (Meijburg & Co, May 2025), the reform agenda due at year end (EY Netherlands, Feb 2026), the e-invoicing build toward 2030 (RTC Suite, Oct 2025), the fraud-defence rebuild and AI Act compliance all draw on the same constrained change capacity. Four of the five carry an external date and one does not, which means the undated one loses by default whatever its strategic value. The lever is prioritisation with disclosure: an explicit ranking costs political capital once, while an implicit one costs delivery credibility every time a date slips and the reason cannot be given.

Action: Publish an internal ranking of the five with what each of the lower-ranked will not receive, and take the ranking to the department rather than absorbing the conflict inside delivery plans.

Decide Draws on Themes 1 and 4

SI 2: Settle whether the reform is policy delivery or operating-model redesign

The two readings need different people, a different budget and a different seat. The optimistic case is that the window is rare and will not wait (OECD, Nov 2025). The evidence closest to the work is more cautious: the administration's own plan dates automatic taxation after 2035 (the Belastingdienst, Dec 2025), the legacy estate stood at 19.3% (ICT Magazine, May 2025), and the coalition lacks the majority to enact its own measures (KPMG, Feb 2026). A decision taken explicitly can be a modest one. A decision taken by default will be delivery, because delivery is what arrives with a date attached.

Action: Name which of the two the organisation is pursuing, and if it is redesign, fund a workstream with a date earlier than 2035 against it.

Decide Draws on Theme 4

SI 3: Build the detection capability that is defensible inside the current constraints

The constraints are not going to ease first. The audit office has the privacy management of algorithmic selection open (the Algemene Rekenkamer, May 2025), the European framework tightens around high-risk public-sector systems (the European Commission, undated), and the phasing decides what has already bitten (Kennedys Law, undated). Meanwhile fraud's composition is shifting toward multi-step and agentic schemes (Sumsub, Nov 2025). Waiting for room is a strategy that assumes someone else will create it.

Action: Specify the detection capability that would survive the strictest plausible reading of the rules, and build that, treating any later easing as upside rather than as the plan.

Prepare Draws on Themes 1 and 2

SI 4: Separate the value of the incoming data from the cost of receiving it

DAC8 is live and transposed (KPMG, Mar 2026), DAC9 landed with the 2026 package (KPMG, Dec 2025), and the recast may thin the stack later (CFE Tax Advisers Europe, Feb 2026). Nothing in this cycle's evidence shows what the administration intends to do analytically with what it now receives. An information position that is only ever received is a cost centre wearing the language of an advantage.

Action: Name one analytical use for DAC8 or DAC9 data that would not have been possible a year ago, fund it separately from the reporting build, and report on it.

Small mandate, used well

The window is used

The reform agenda arrives with decisions attached and the organisation has already said what it will not do, so it can take the design seat rather than the delivery seat. This is the only quadrant in which an embedded service model becomes more than an aspiration dated after 2035 (the Belastingdienst, Dec 2025).

A mandate larger than the machine

The reform agenda lands in full against an estate still carrying 19.3% legacy (ICT Magazine, May 2025) and a trust position that has not recovered (the Sociaal en Cultureel Planbureau, Mar 2025). Delivery is attempted at a rate the organisation has not demonstrated, which is the precondition that produced the last crisis.

What We Are Not Planning For

Threat horizon

We are not planning for a cyber or quantum discontinuity in this window

Both are real and both are covered in the organisation's own threat material. Neither is differentiated by external scanning at this horizon, and including them would have blurred four mechanism-distinct themes into five overlapping ones. The threat picture this brief does carry is the anti-institutional one, which is already operational rather than prospective (the AIVD, Apr 2026).

↺ Reinstated if a cryptographically relevant quantum milestone is announced, or a peer administration suffers a confidentiality breach at scale.

Workforce

We are not planning for workforce demographics as a strategic variable this cycle

Ageing and succession are operationally important and are not, at a two to five year horizon, what decides the outcomes in this brief. Capacity here means the safe rate of system change, not headcount, and the audit evidence points at the estate rather than at the establishment (the Algemene Rekenkamer, May 2025).

↺ Reinstated if attrition in the IV function rises materially, or the anonymity posture begins to show in recruitment (the Gerechtshof Arnhem-Leeuwarden, Jan 2026).

Regulatory reversal

We are not planning for the European AI rules being loosened for public authorities

The regime is built on making trustworthy AI the condition of market access rather than on restricting it, and its obligations arrive in phases already under way (the European Commission, undated) (Kennedys Law, undated). Planning on relief would mean building a detection capability that only works if someone else changes their mind.

↺ Reinstated if a formal transitional instrument reaches public-authority enforcement systems, or national guidance settles materially below the strictest reading.

Questions For The Next Strategy Meeting

Five questions this cycle's evidence should put on the agenda.

  1. Of the five programmes competing for change capacity, which have we ranked against each other, and would anyone outside this building be able to name the order?
  2. Our own plan dates fully automatic taxation to after 2035 (the Belastingdienst, Dec 2025) while the reform agenda is due this year. Which of those two horizons are our commitments actually written against?
  3. If the European AI rules never loosened, what in our detection roadmap would change? If the answer is nothing, what is that assumption doing in the roadmap (the European Commission, undated)?
  4. We will receive materially more cross-border data this year (KPMG, Mar 2026). Name one analytical question we can now answer that we could not answer last year.
  5. Trust fell seven points across one winter, and furthest among the group that was most positive (the Sociaal en Cultureel Planbureau, Mar 2025). What in the next twelve months is most likely to move it again, and is anyone accountable for that number?
