Climate Policy Has Started Taking Things Away
Spain banned hosepipes with the reservoirs three quarters full.
Most organisations treat water as a utility bill and a drought story on the news. Here are the two things almost nobody is writing about. Rationing has come loose from how much water is actually there, so a full reservoir is no longer a defence. And where water is formally allocated, the entitlement itself is now issued two years at a time, against assets built to last twenty-five.

Three numbers that make the case
72.74%. How full Galicia's supply reservoirs were when the regional government declared scarcity alert across 91 municipalities and banned street washing, filling pools and watering gardens. Not empty, and not close to it, just 14.44 points down on the same date a year before. England did the same thing in the same month with storage at 59.8%: ten areas in drought, ten companies running temporary use bans, around 30 million people restricted. Rationing has become an administrative judgement about the trend rather than a mechanical consequence of an empty reservoir. A site can pass every water-stress screen you own and still be told what it may use.

Two years. The length of a Colorado River operating rule from October 2026. The federal decision did not set a ten-year allocation, it set a ten-year process for issuing rules two years at a time through 2036, against combined Powell and Mead contents at their lowest since before Powell began filling in 1963. The volume cut, 1.25 million acre-feet, is survivable and everyone says so. The tenor is the part that repriced: 5.5 million acres of farmland, including much of the American winter vegetable supply, now sit on an entitlement shorter than an orchard rotation.

Zero. Water-withdrawal applications received from data centres by the regulator of the Delaware basin, where roughly 60 are already operating and about 24 more are planned. They buy from public supply rather than abstracting for themselves, so the fastest-growing load in the basin is invisible to the body that allocates. A regulator that cannot see a load cannot ration it, which means the restriction lands on the municipal system, and on everyone else drawing from it.
Three decisions with dates on them
Prepare
Put water tenor on the capital paper before the next site goes through
Capital committees test power, land and permits, and then write water down as available or unavailable. Three fields fix that: who holds the entitlement, how long it runs, and when it is next re-decided. Apply it back to sites already approved for 2027 construction, because those were signed off against a permission nobody dated. The change is procedural and costs almost nothing. The alternative is learning the answer from a regulator, in a letter, after the decision.
For each site in your 2027 plan, would you hear about a re-determination before it happened, or after?
Decide now
Work out whether the 30 December origin rule reaches anything you sell
Different resource, same movement. From 30 December, placing certain goods on the EU market requires the coordinates of the plot the raw material grew on. The duty follows the commodity into what is made from it, so furniture, chocolate, paper and tyres are inside it while the companies selling them may never have thought of themselves as agricultural. A revision in July changed the covered list in both directions and dropped the filing duty for downstream firms, which much of the trade has read as relief at the deadline. The deadline did not move. Scoping this takes an afternoon. Getting a map reference out of a supplier who has never been asked for one takes months.
Could you produce a list, by product, of what needs plot-level evidence in December?
Decide now
Reprice your 2027 input costs against a harvest that has already been lost
EU forecasts for every summer crop were cut to as much as 14% below the five-year average. France's maize is down around 35% on the year, possibly its smallest crop since 1980, and German cooperatives price the heat damage above 600 million euros. Yet euro area food inflation was 1.2% in August, unchanged on July. That gap is the whole point: the loss is banked and nobody has yet decided who carries it. That argument is being had in autumn contracts, not on shelves, and it closes when they are signed.
Which of your 2027 input assumptions would you still defend on this evidence, and which have simply been carried forward?
Built for people who do not run a sustainability function: anyone siting, financing or insuring a plant, a data centre or a farm, procurement teams buying anything that touches wood, cocoa, coffee, soy, palm, rubber or cattle, food buyers setting 2027 contracts, and investors holding companies in any of the above.
Where we might be wrong
The direction is not uniformly toward restriction. An Arizona judge struck down the regulations behind that state's hundred-year assured water supply rule, which one water policy expert said may be the end of the requirement, and that is loosening rather than tightening. The aggregate numbers are undramatic too: American data centres consumed 17.4 billion gallons directly in 2023, under 1% of national water consumption, and the acute problems sit in the arid Southwest and the Central High Plains. We may be reading three hot months in Europe as a permanent turn. The reason the recommendation survives the doubt is that the aggregate is the wrong denominator. Measured against the capacity of the utility that actually serves them, the burden of ten American data centre sites spans three orders of magnitude, from 0.2% to 134%. A national average cannot reassure you about a particular site, and adding three fields to a capital paper costs a morning.
