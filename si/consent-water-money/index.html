Why This Matters Now

The places that need new buildings, factories and data centres are asking harder questions before they say yes: how much water, how much power, and what the neighbours get in return.

Cities and states are pausing projects until those answers are clear. Governments are starting to publish how efficiently large facilities use energy and water, and new rules on packaging and buildings now judge materials over their whole life. At the same time, investors are putting most of their money into a few large, proven projects, which leaves less for early ideas.

The one thing to take into the next meeting: nature-inspired design wins where it can show measured savings in water, energy and long-term cost on a real project. Bring that evidence to Biomimetics26, and watch which projects in your area are paused or approved this winter.

Executive Synthesis

Which projects on your desk this year assume the community will say yes?

In Minnesota, 63% oppose a data centre where they live, and at least six cities have paused new ones (Minnesota Reformer, Jul 2026).

Nature-inspired design is ready to prove itself on the projects the world is now building, but the pace will be set by three things outside the lab: whether communities consent, whether there is water, and whether patient money backs the first deployments. Data centres show all three at once. The International Energy Agency expects their electricity use to roughly double by 2030 and warns: “Social acceptability is also a growing issue, as communities push back against data centre projects, and concerns about affordability and environmental impacts rise.” (International Energy Agency, Apr 2026). In Minnesota, where Biomimetics26 meets, Minneapolis has paused new data centres (CBS Minnesota, Jun 2026) and 63% of residents oppose one in their community (Minnesota Reformer, Jul 2026). Meanwhile, of 809 planned US data centres, 517 are in places that have been in drought all year (Mother Jones, Jun 2026).

Some things are settled. The European Commission has adopted public energy and water ratings for large data centres (European Commission, Sep 2026), and EU packaging rules have applied since 12 August (European Commission, Aug 2026). Less settled is whether communities will turn pauses into lasting rules, whether money will reach early-stage ventures when the largest rounds took nearly 65% of climate investment (Net Zero Insights, Jul 2026), and whether AI-generated designs can be checked as fast as they are produced.

Looking ahead to 2030, labels for European data centres arrive from August 2027 (Beveridge & Diamond, Sep 2026), whole-life carbon must be disclosed for all new EU buildings from 2030 (European Commission, Dec 2025), and 74% of investors surveyed expect nature-related financing to rise two to five times within five years (Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures, Sep 2026). None of this is certain, and each is a place where readers can still choose their position: section 7 sets out the options.

Where this analysis could be wrong

The weakest assumption is that local pushback lasts. Moratoria are temporary by design (Sabin Center, Columbia Law School, May 2026), states such as Kansas still offer long tax breaks (Bipartisan Policy Center, Jul 2026), and operators have pledged to pay their full power costs (US Environmental Protection Agency, Jul 2026). If those pledges defuse opposition by mid-2027, consent becomes a cost to pay rather than a test to pass, and the opening for nature-inspired design narrows to compliance with the new ratings.

The decisions that cannot be deferred this cycle

  1. Bring measured water, energy and local-benefit evidence to every data-centre project. Councils are pausing projects until they have it, and the EU will publish the figures from 2027.
  2. Keep a checkable evidence trail for every nature-inspired claim, including AI-assisted ones. Designs are now cheap to generate and expensive to verify.
  3. Put whole-life cost and carbon numbers on lead products before the 2028 rule steps. Buyers, public procurers and patient investors are starting to ask for them.

1. Data centres now need local consent to be built

Immediate

Weight: high · Who decides: City councils, state legislatures and, in Europe, the Commission and national governments · Room to act: High for members who design, build or advise on projects; moderate on the rules themselves

Data-centre electricity use is set to double by 2030, and communities have learned to slow the building down. Councils in Minnesota and across the US are pausing projects until they can see the cost to their power bills, water and land. Operators are answering with promises to pay their own way, and Europe is answering with public ratings. For members, this is the opening: a project that can show measured savings in water, energy and heat has a better case for approval, and nature-inspired design is one of the few ways to produce those savings rather than promise them.

  • The International Energy Agency expects data-centre electricity use to roughly double, from 485 TWh in 2025 to 950 TWh in 2030, and names community pushback as a growing constraint (International Energy Agency, Apr 2026).
  • A tracker cited by Columbia Law School's Sabin Center counts at least one hundred local data-centre pauses in the US (Sabin Center, Columbia Law School, May 2026); the Bipartisan Policy Center counts 54 (Bipartisan Policy Center, Jul 2026). In Minnesota, Minneapolis, Eagan, Elk River, Inver Grove Heights, Apple Valley and Rosemount have all paused new projects, and 63% of residents oppose one in their community (Minnesota Reformer, Jul 2026).
  • Seven large AI and cloud companies, including Microsoft and Google, have pledged to pay the full cost of the power their sites use. The pledge now covers 80% of power delivered to US homes and businesses (US Environmental Protection Agency, Jul 2026).
  • The European Commission has adopted a common rating for data centres above 500 kW that makes their energy and water use public (European Commission, Sep 2026). Grades run from A to G, with labels generated each year from 15 August 2027 (Beveridge & Diamond, Sep 2026).

Early indicators

  • Weak signal Opposition now crosses party lines. In Minnesota's race for governor, candidates from both parties back local control over data centres, and one wants their sales tax breaks repealed (Minnesota Reformer, Jul 2026). In the Netherlands, independent counts of jobs per megawatt are being used to challenge the industry's own figures (Computable, in Dutch, Sep 2026). The argument is moving from promises of local benefit to audited numbers.

Outlook to 2030

The EU will generate the first energy and water labels in August 2027 and consult on minimum performance standards, with a legislative proposal planned for the second quarter of 2027 (Beveridge & Diamond, Sep 2026). Germany, by contrast, plans to loosen its own efficiency targets, giving existing data centres until 2030 to reach a ratio of 1.4 rather than 1.3 (CMS, in German, Jul 2026). On that path, by 2030 large data centres in Europe will be judged in public on measured performance, and US councils will have written the rules they paused to draft.

What it means: Treat measured water, energy and heat performance as part of the planning case, not an add-on, on every data-centre project members touch.

Counter-argument

Pauses may not last. The Sabin Center notes that “a moratorium is always temporary, and ultimately will not take the place of a stable, long-term approach to mitigating data center impacts.” (Sabin Center, Columbia Law School, May 2026), Kansas has just given data centres a 20-year sales tax exemption (Bipartisan Policy Center, Jul 2026), and a data-centre owner is suing Eagan over its pause (Minnesota Reformer, Jul 2026). If the power-cost pledges work, the strongest local objection fades.

2. Water is deciding where things can be built

Immediate

Weight: high · Who decides: Water regulators, river-basin bodies, utilities and permitting authorities · Room to act: High: saving water is where nature-inspired solutions are strongest

Water is becoming a test that a project must pass where it is sited, not a utility bill to pay later. New data centres are being planned in drought-hit places, most of their water footprint comes from the power stations that supply them, and regulators are starting to ask for the numbers. For members, this puts a premium on designs that cool, capture and reuse water without extra energy, which is where much of nature's engineering lies.

  • Of 809 planned US data centres, 517 are in places that have been in drought throughout the past year, and the sector could need as much as 73 billion gallons of water a year by 2028, up from about 17 billion in 2023 (Mother Jones, Jun 2026).
  • In seven US states, 78% of the electricity for data centres came from power stations that use water, and 66% of that was generated in water-stressed areas (NC State University, Sep 2026). In California, more than 90% of data-centre water use comes from power generation, and “Transparency regarding water use and environmental impacts is severely limited” (Next 10, May 2026).
  • The EU's new rating grades water efficiency, and improving it from 1.0 to 0.5 halves a data centre's water use (European Commission, Sep 2026). Spain's draft decree would require data centres above 1 MW to reach the top EU grade for both water and energy once the scheme applies (elDiario.es, in Spanish, Aug 2026).
  • Researchers have built a passive system modelled on how termite mounds manage air and moisture. It recovers water vapour from industrial cooling towers and keeps 83% of it; scaled to a 300 MW power station, it would meet the household needs of 2.2 million homes (Nature Water, Apr 2026).

Early indicators

  • Weak signal Water review is moving east and moving earlier. The Delaware River Basin Commission knows of about 24 planned data centres in its basin, none of which had applied for water review by June 2026, and it is already briefing local councils (Delaware River Basin Commission, Sep 2026). Water is becoming an early gate in wetter regions, not only in the arid West.

Outlook to 2030

The EU will issue its first water-efficiency labels from August 2027 and review the scheme by the end of 2028 (European Commission, Sep 2026). The Pacific Institute expects the largest data centres to rise from 48% of capacity in 2025 to 67% by 2031, which concentrates water demand in fewer, larger sites (Pacific Institute, Sep 2026). On that reading, by 2030 water performance will be a published, comparable number that permits can require.

What it means: Lead with water: a design that measurably cuts water use, directly or through the power it draws, will carry more weight with permitting bodies than one that saves energy alone.

Counter-argument

The Pacific Institute stresses: “On a cumulative basis, data centers account for a relatively small percentage of global and even national water withdrawals.” (Pacific Institute, Sep 2026). National water policy may therefore not change for data centres. Its point is that the harm is local, since one site can draw up to 3.9 million gallons a day, which supports judging projects case by case rather than by sector.

3. Patient money is scarce, so long-term savings must be proven

Near-Term

Weight: high · Who decides: Venture investors, public funders and public buyers · Room to act: Moderate: members can shape the evidence investors see, not the size of the market

There is more climate money than a year ago, but it is going to fewer, larger and later-stage deals, many of them the data centres and power plants in Theme 1. Nature-inspired ventures sit mostly at the early and middle stages, where money is harder to find. The case that unlocks patient money, from investors, public buyers and blended funds, is proof of savings over the whole life of a product or building.

  • Climate tech venture funding reached $26.1bn in the first half of 2026, up 55%, but low-carbon data centres took 34%, deal numbers fell 25% to a five-year low, and the share going to Series C, a late funding stage, rose to 40% from 16% (CTVC, Jul 2026).
  • On a broader count including debt, the largest rounds took nearly 65% of total investment (Net Zero Insights, Jul 2026), and the average round grew from $18 million to $27 million (Latitude Media, Jul 2026). In France, growth capital for clean technology fell from 14 deals worth €767 million to 5 deals worth €261 million (J’aime les Startups, in French, Sep 2026).
  • The European Commission has proposed that public contracts should normally be awarded on quality as well as price, with life-cycle costs allowed in the comparison and quality weighted at least 30% (European Commission, Sep 2026).
  • Over 1,000 organisations now publish reports in line with the Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures, double the 2025 count, and 56% of investors surveyed are already exploring or deploying capital into nature-related opportunities (Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures, Sep 2026).

Early indicators

  • Weak signal Nature treated as infrastructure. A new UK fund has a 12-year life and uses a £30 million government stake to draw in insurers and foundations. “For every £1 of public money, the structure is designed to unlock at least £2 of private investment.” (AgFunderNews, Jun 2026). Long-dated, blended funds of this kind are the patient money nature-inspired water and building projects need.

Outlook to 2030

In the taskforce's survey, 74% of investors expect nature-related financing to grow two to five times within five years (Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures, Sep 2026), and governments have committed to raise €200 billion a year for nature by 2030 from all sources (European Commission, May 2026). The EU procurement proposal would apply only after agreement by Parliament and Council. On that path, by 2030 the money exists, but it flows to ventures that can prove long-term savings in terms buyers and investors already use.

What it means: Price every offer over its whole life and say who captures the savings; that is the number patient investors and public buyers can act on.

Counter-argument

The squeeze may be easing. Seed funding has grown 73% in value since 2021 (Latitude Media, Jul 2026), and the UK fund raised £64.6 million at first close (AgFunderNews, Jun 2026). On the evidence here, the gap is at the middle stage, between first pilot and full scale, rather than across the board.

4. AI is speeding up the search for nature's designs

Near-Term

Weight: medium · Who decides: Research teams and funders and, on biological data, the parties to the UN Convention on Biological Diversity · Room to act: High: members choose their tools and their standards of evidence

AI is cutting the time it takes to turn a biological idea into a candidate design or material, and governments are funding laboratories that run themselves. The bottleneck has moved from generating ideas to proving they work. For a field whose credibility rests on a traceable link to biology, that is both an advantage and a risk: AI can produce designs that sound biological without any checkable biological evidence behind them.

  • “Today, anyone with a large enough artificial intelligence model can generate millions of new material designs in minutes.” Checking them is about 90% of the computing cost of producing usable materials and can take weeks or months (MIT News, Aug 2026).
  • A review in the journal Biomimetics finds AI cuts the computing time of structural design by up to 30 times, but warns that biomimetic data are fragmented and costly, and that many models cannot say whether a material can actually be made (Biomimetics journal, May 2026).
  • The White House has committed more than $5 billion to its Genesis Mission for AI in science, with challenges on predicting living systems and on AI-driven laboratories (The White House, Jul 2026). In France, no-code tools are putting biological AI models in the hands of ordinary laboratories (Futura, in French, May 2026).
  • A Nature study shows that language models are rewarded for guessing and make unavoidable errors on one-off facts (Nature, Apr 2026), which is exactly the detail a nature-inspired claim depends on. The share of research papers with a fabricated reference rose from one in 2,828 in 2023 to one in 277 in early 2026 (Forbes, May 2026).

Early indicators

  • Weak signal AI companies may be asked to pay for biological data. A London School of Economics working paper names AI among the sectors expected to contribute to the Cali Fund, the UN biodiversity fund for companies that benefit from genetic data, which could raise $0.9 billion a year, or $3.6 billion with full participation. Its rules are open for revision at the UN biodiversity conference in October (Grantham Research Institute, London School of Economics, Jun 2026).

Outlook to 2030

One market forecast, used here only as a direction, expects AI for materials discovery to reach $2.77 billion by 2030 (Research and Markets, Jul 2026), and the Genesis Mission selected its first 278 projects in July (The White House, Jul 2026). On the analysis's reading, by 2030 the advantage lies with teams that can show each AI-assisted design's biological source and test record, not with those that generate the most designs.

What it means: Adopt AI tools, but attach a biological source and a test result to every design they help produce.

Counter-argument

The risk may be overstated for well-run teams. MIT's method builds chemical rules in before generation and achieved 68% mechanical stability in the materials it produced (MIT News, Aug 2026), and fabricated references are still rare in absolute terms (Forbes, May 2026). The verification gap is real but can be closed with method.

5. New packaging and building rules reward nature-inspired materials

Near-Term

Weight: medium · Who decides: The EU, national governments and US states setting packaging and building rules · Room to act: Moderate: the rules are set, but members choose which products to prove first

Binding rules now judge packaging and buildings by what their materials do over their whole life, which favours bio-based and nature-inspired materials that are lighter, recyclable or store carbon. The advantage goes only to products that can prove their performance with recognised data, and some rule dates are already slipping.

  • The EU packaging regulation has applied since 12 August. Limits on empty space and reuse targets follow from 2030, when all packaging must be recyclable; without action, plastic packaging waste was projected to rise by as much as 46% by 2030 (European Commission, Aug 2026). California's packaging law took effect in May (CalRecycle, May 2026).
  • New EU buildings over 1,000 m² must disclose whole-life carbon from 2028. The Commission says: “This creates a strong incentive for the use of low-carbon construction materials, like clean steel and cement, and to promote carbon storage techniques, such as wood construction, as well as reuse and recycling.” (European Commission, Dec 2025).
  • Bristol start-up Mykor raised £4m for mycelium wall panels that it says use 90% less water and 40% less electricity to make than polystyrene panels, and reports £338m of contractor agreements (Construction Management, May 2026).
  • A University of Pennsylvania team including Shu Yang made a cement tile inspired by elephant skin that stayed 89.6°F under heat that took cracked stucco to 107.6°F (Penn Today, Jul 2026). In France, bio-based insulation has gained market share every year since 2023 (Architecture Bois, in French, May 2026).

Outlook to 2030

Packaging must be recyclable and meet reuse targets from 2030 (European Commission, Aug 2026), and whole-life carbon disclosure extends to all new EU buildings the same year (European Commission, Dec 2025). On that path, by 2030 materials will compete on measured life-cycle data in two of Europe's main material markets, packaging and construction.

What it means: Get recognised life-cycle data for lead products before 2028, so they can be specified when buyers start comparing.

Counter-argument

The rules are slipping and contested. The Commission missed its deadline for the packaging label rules, which now apply from 2028 at the earliest (Envirotec, Aug 2026), and single-use packaging firms asked in April to delay and reopen the regulation (Zero Waste Europe, Apr 2026). Disclosure of building carbon is not yet a cap, so it rewards low-carbon materials only where buyers choose to act on it.

Strategic Implications

Implication 1: Bring measured water, energy and local-benefit evidence to every data-centre project

Councils are pausing projects until they can see the costs (CBS Minnesota, Jun 2026), and the EU will publish energy and water grades from 2027 (Beveridge & Diamond, Sep 2026). Members who design, build or advise on data centres should put measured water use, energy use and heat reuse, and what the neighbours gain, into every planning case now. The BiosVerse Compatible case studies at Biomimetics26 (Biomimetics International, undated) are the place to test that format with operators and permitting bodies.

Action: Members working on data-centre projects to agree a one-page water, energy and local-benefit sheet at Biomimetics26 and use it on every live project by 31 March 2027.

Decide Draws on Themes 1 and 2

Implication 2: Keep a checkable evidence trail for every nature-inspired claim, including AI-assisted ones

AI produces designs far faster than they can be checked (MIT News, Aug 2026), and language models err on exactly the one-off facts biology supplies (Nature, Apr 2026). Research teams and companies should record, for every design, the organism, the published study and the test result behind it. Teams that use genetic data or train models on it should also follow the UN biodiversity conference's decisions on the Cali Fund (Grantham Research Institute, London School of Economics, Jun 2026).

Action: Research and product leads to attach a biological source and a test record to every AI-assisted design from 1 January 2027.

Prepare Draws on Theme 4

Implication 3: Put whole-life cost and carbon numbers on lead products and projects

Public buyers in the EU may soon weigh life-cycle costs (European Commission, Sep 2026), new buildings disclose whole-life carbon from 2028 (European Commission, Dec 2025), and patient investors back proven savings (AgFunderNews, Jun 2026). Businesses and ventures should publish whole-life cost and carbon figures for their two or three lead products, stating who captures the savings, and investors should ask for them before committing.

Action: Members selling materials, products or design services to have independently checked life-cycle figures for their lead products by 31 December 2027.

The global sustainability standard-setter publishes draft nature-disclosure rules in October (Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures, Sep 2026), the UN biodiversity conference revisits the Cali Fund the same month (Grantham Research Institute, London School of Economics, Jun 2026), the EU's consultation on minimum performance standards for data centres closes on 14 December (Beveridge & Diamond, Sep 2026), and Germany's easing of its data-centre rules awaits parliament (CMS, in German, Jul 2026). Rated and routed

In 2030 national and EU governments decide where large facilities go and override most local objections, but they demand published performance. Energy and water grades are a condition of approval, as Spain has proposed (elDiario.es, in Spanish, Aug 2026). Nature-inspired designs win on compliance: they help projects reach the top grades.

Early indicators: EU minimum performance standards adopted; states pre-empt local pauses; grid access tied to grades.

Earned licence

In 2030 communities decide, and they approve projects that show measured local benefits in water, heat and bills. Councils use the rules they wrote during their pauses (Sabin Center, Columbia Law School, May 2026). Members thrive: their designs are how projects earn consent.

Early indicators: Pauses end in new zoning codes; community benefit agreements spread; water-positive pledges audited.

Build first, fix later

In 2030 governments fast-track facilities to win investment, with long tax breaks such as Kansas's (Bipartisan Policy Center, Jul 2026) and looser efficiency rules such as Germany's (CMS, in German, Jul 2026). Speed beats performance, and nature-inspired design stays a niche for a few willing clients.

Early indicators: More statewide incentives; efficiency targets relaxed; local pauses overturned in court.

Stalled build-out

In 2030 local pauses have spread and hardened, but buyers still choose on upfront price. Projects stall or move elsewhere, little money reaches better design, and conflict over water and power continues.

Early indicators: Pauses renewed year after year; projects relocate across borders; venture funding for early stages keeps falling.

Option C · Biggest shift

Design for net local gain

Make reused heat, saved water and nature-inspired cooling a condition of the project, as BiosVerse Compatible case studies propose.

Trade-off: Higher upfront cost and fewer suitable sites, but the clearest route to approval.

⊞ Fits scenarios: Earned licence; Rated and routed

Options: Water (utilities, cities and permitting bodies)

Option A · Least change

Review case by case

Assess each project's water use when it applies, as now.

Trade-off: Familiar, but projects are planned before regulators hear of them.

⊞ Fits scenarios: Build first, fix later

Option B · Middle course

Ask for water disclosure early

Require water figures, including the water behind the power supply, at the first planning contact.

Trade-off: More work for applicants, and earlier warning for the basin.

⊞ Fits scenarios: Earned licence; Rated and routed

Option C · Biggest shift

Make water performance a permit condition

Set a minimum water grade for large facilities, as Spain's draft decree does.

Trade-off: Strongest protection, but may push projects to other areas.

⊞ Fits scenarios: Rated and routed; Stalled build-out

Options: Research and AI tools (researchers, universities and R&D teams)

Option A · Least change

Use AI tools as they come

Adopt generative tools for speed and check results in the usual way.

Trade-off: Fastest, but exposes the field to designs with no checkable biology.

⊞ Fits scenarios: Build first, fix later

Option B · Middle course

Add an evidence trail

Record the organism, study and test result for every AI-assisted design.

Trade-off: Slower, but every claim can be defended to a buyer or regulator.

⊞ Fits scenarios: All four

Option C · Biggest shift

Build shared, tested design data

Pool verified biological design data across members, with fair terms for the source communities.

Trade-off: Needs agreement on ownership and benefit-sharing, but sets the field's standard.

⊞ Fits scenarios: Earned licence; Rated and routed

Options: Funding (investors and ventures)

Option A · Least change

Wait for late-stage proof

Invest only once ventures have scaled and de-risked.

Trade-off: Lower risk per deal, but most nature-inspired ventures never reach that point.

⊞ Fits scenarios: Build first, fix later; Stalled build-out

Option B · Middle course

Co-invest with public money

Join blended funds where a public stake takes the first risk.

Trade-off: Slower to assemble, but brings in long-dated money.

⊞ Fits scenarios: All four

Option C · Biggest shift

Pay for proven savings

Back contracts that pay ventures from the water, energy or carbon savings they deliver over the asset's life.

Trade-off: Needs trusted measurement and long contracts, but rewards performance directly.

⊞ Fits scenarios: Earned licence; Rated and routed

What We Are Not Planning For

Not planned for

A national ban on new data centres

Pauses are local and temporary, and states are still competing with incentives (Bipartisan Policy Center, Jul 2026). Demand is still expected to double by 2030 (International Energy Agency, Apr 2026).

↺ Reinstated if a state or national government enacts a statewide pause lasting more than two years.

Not planned for

AI replacing laboratory testing of nature-inspired designs

Checking designs remains the slow and costly step (MIT News, Aug 2026), and the data behind biomimetic AI are thin (Biomimetics journal, May 2026).

↺ Reinstated if an autonomous laboratory publishes validated results across several material classes without human testing.

Not planned for

A retreat from nature-related reporting

Reporting doubled in a year (Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures, Sep 2026), and the revised EU standards keep biodiversity (European Commission, Jul 2026).

↺ Reinstated if the global standard-setter drops its nature project or the EU removes biodiversity from its standards.
