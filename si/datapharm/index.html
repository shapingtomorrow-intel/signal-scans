Cycle motif: data, decisions and dispensing converging on Datapharm's 2026-2029 repositioning window.

Executive Synthesis

Datapharm enters this cycle with a structural advantage and a structural deadline. The advantage: emc has already migrated to HL7 FHIR, the same standard the EU has adopted as its ePI Common Standard, and Datapharm has positioned itself publicly as the UK's transition vehicle into structured medicines information. The deadline: the EMA's draft ePI implementation roadmap puts vaccines into voluntary ePI submission in Q3 2026 and oncology in Q4 2026, with mandatory ePI for all newly authorised medicines once the revised pharmaceutical legislation enters into application. The legislation itself was endorsed by Coreper on 6 March 2026. The repositioning window is now measured in quarters, not years.

The cycle's spine is whether Datapharm captures the value of being the FHIR data-service layer underneath the new ePI economy, or gets disintermediated. Three forces are arriving simultaneously: the September 2026 day-one independent-prescriber cohort plus the NHS England Medium Term Planning Framework instruction to ICBs to commission prescribing services in 2026/27; the FDB Multilex repositioning to cloud delivery alongside AI-native entrants; and the CGE Partners growth-investment mandate that brings new capital, an explicit international thesis and a CTO and CCO hired in the months since.

What has materially changed since this report's last reference points: the EMA's roadmap is no longer a position paper but a dated rollout; ICBs are no longer being asked to consider Pharmacy First but instructed to maximise it; OpenEvidence has withdrawn from European markets citing EU AI Act uncertainty, leaving an unclaimed UK-and-EU clinical-AI-search position; and the EU pharmaceutical legislation reached political agreement, removing one of the largest sources of timing uncertainty for ePI adoption. The MHRA's International Reliance Framework, due Autumn 2026, signals divergence-by-design rather than alignment.

The non-obvious read: Datapharm's traditional competitive moat (the UK's authoritative aggregated medicines-information source) is collapsing into infrastructure precisely as the addressable market expands. Pharmacist independent prescribers, AI clinical-search vendors, ePMA suppliers and pharma manufacturers all need the same FHIR-structured medicines data, but increasingly through APIs rather than through emc's web interface. The strategic question is whether Datapharm productises the data layer (and prices it like infrastructure) or builds first-party decision support (and competes for the AI-search seat) or both.

Datapharm's traditional competitive moat is collapsing into infrastructure precisely as the addressable market expands.

This is also the cycle in which CGE Partners' platform mandate becomes visible in the cap table. The VIDAL partnership in France and the new CTO and CCO appointments are early markers; Pecém-style infrastructure investment, an EU-jurisdiction acquisition, or a clinical-decision-support bolt-on would each be consistent with the thesis CGE has stated publicly.

Where this analysis could be wrong

The synthesis rests on the assumption that the EU ePI roadmap's Q3 to Q4 2026 voluntary windows actually convert into meaningful manufacturer adoption rather than slipping a year. If the IRIS-eSubmission integration timeline drifts, or if the revised pharmaceutical legislation enters force later than expected, the data-layer demand curve flattens and Datapharm's first-mover advantage erodes without a forced inflection. A material revision would be triggered by EMA delaying the Q3 vaccines window, by fewer than ten manufacturers volunteering ePI submissions in 2026, or by the Common Standard being substantively reopened for industry consultation. None of these are the central case, but each is plausible enough to warrant tracking.

The decisions that cannot be deferred this cycle

  1. Decide whether emc's FHIR layer is productised as a separate, priced data service. The architecture is in place; the commercial decision is whether to expose it.
  2. Prepare a build-versus-partner answer to the AI clinical-search question. FDB has moved; OpenEvidence is gone from Europe; the seat is open for the next eighteen months.
  3. Decide the first material acquisition under the CGE thesis. A clinical-decision-support, ePI-services or EU-jurisdiction target would each carry a different signal.

Each is developed below, with a decision posture, in the four Strategic Implications.

1. The ePI mandate transition and the eMC reposition

Immediate

The EU ePI rollout has moved from position paper to dated implementation. Vaccines and oncology go voluntary in Q3 and Q4 2026 respectively, and the revised pharmaceutical legislation that triggers mandatory submission was endorsed by Coreper in March 2026. For Datapharm this is the cycle's most consequential cluster: emc is already FHIR-based, but the value capture question is whether the FHIR layer is productised as a priced data service, or whether it remains the engine room of a free-to-access destination. The window for that decision closes as ePMA vendors, AI-search tools and direct-to-EMA pipes establish their preferred data sources.

EU ePI rollout: from voluntary to mandatory

Source basis: EMA draft ePI implementation roadmap, March 2026; EU pharmaceutical legislation political agreement March 2026.

  • The EMA's draft roadmap puts vaccine (ATC J07) ePI on voluntary submission in Q3 2026, oncology (L01, L04) in Q4 2026, with all remaining Centrally Authorised Products following voluntarily before transition to mandatory submission once the revised legislation enters into application per the EMA ePI roadmap (March 2026).
  • The EU pharmaceutical legislation reform was endorsed by Coreper on 6 March 2026 with the new acts expected to enter force in 2026 and a two-year transition period to 2028 per the European Commission (March 2026).
  • The EU ePI Common Standard is FHIR-based; the v1.0.0 implementation guide is published and live at epi.ema.europa.eu/fhirig (May 2025), giving manufacturers a definitive technical target.
  • Datapharm has already publicly framed emc as transitioning to FHIR and positions itself as the UK conversion vehicle for SmPCs into ePI in FHIR per Datapharm (February 2026).

Counter-argument

The roadmap is a draft, not a regulation. Voluntary submission windows historically attract minority uptake among manufacturers, and the mandatory trigger depends on legislation that has only reached political agreement, not adoption. The transition period through 2028 may also see industry push back on ePI scope, particularly multilingual delivery. A scenario in which Q3 to Q4 2026 voluntary go-live attracts only single-digit manufacturer participation is plausible, in which case Datapharm's first-mover advantage in FHIR delivery converts to value more slowly than this cycle assumes.

2. CGE Partners ownership and the growth-by-acquisition mandate

Immediate

CGE Partners' growth investment, announced September 2025, comes with an explicit mandate: accelerate new product development, international expansion and strategic acquisitions. CGE's stated thesis is information, data and software businesses across Northern and Western Europe at €30m to €200m EV. The first eight months of post-investment activity (a CTO and CCO hire, the VIDAL partnership in France, the Datapharm Hub SSO consolidation) read as platform-building moves. The cluster is Immediate because the next twelve months should produce the first material capital allocation that signals which corner of the market CGE intends Datapharm to occupy.

  • CGE Partners announced its growth investment in Datapharm to accelerate new product development, international expansion and strategic acquisitions, naming emc as the trusted source of UK medicines information used by 300+ global life sciences companies per CGE Partners (September 2025).
  • CGE's investment thesis is information, data and software businesses across Northern and Western Europe at EV €30m to €200m, with healthcare data named as a focus sector per CGE Partners (January 2026).
  • Travers Smith's confirmation of the transaction provides independent corroboration of the deal structure per Travers Smith (September 2025).
  • Post-investment leadership build-out is visible: a new CTO and CCO appointed in late 2025 per Datapharm (December 2025); the VIDAL France partnership executed in February 2026 with 11 global pharma companies showing interest per Datapharm and corroborated by PharmaTimes.
  • The Datapharm Hub launched 12 March 2026 as a single sign-on layer over emc publisher, emc PI portal and emc icomply per Datapharm, indicating execution of platform-consolidation prerequisites for bolt-on acquisitions.

CGE may take 18 to 24 months to identify, diligence and close the first material acquisition; the VIDAL partnership is a distribution deal rather than M&A; and platform-consolidation work could absorb senior bandwidth through 2026. A counter-reading is that the first three years of the CGE hold are about preparing Datapharm to scale rather than scaling it, in which case Immediate-tier framing overstates the near-term capital activity.

3. Pharmacist independent prescribing as a structural demand shock

Near-Term

From September 2026 every newly qualified pharmacist registers as an independent prescriber on day one. NHS England's Medium Term Planning Framework instructs ICBs to maximise Pharmacy First and introduce prescribing-based services in 2026/27. A third of GB pharmacists are already independent prescribers, with the cohort set to expand by several thousand each year. Each new IP needs a structured medicines reference of record, decision-support tooling and documentation aligned with NHS standards. The strategic question for Datapharm is whether emc remains the canonical source-of-truth for that cohort or whether AI-search tools and ePMA-embedded decision support intermediate.

  • From September 2026 all newly qualified pharmacists are independent prescribers on day of registration, with several thousand more pharmacists joining the register each year per NHS England (March 2026).
  • Pathfinder sites delivered 33,000+ patient consultations across 164 pharmacy sites with 59% resulting in prescribing interventions that started, stopped or changed medicines per NHS England (February 2026).
  • One third of GB pharmacists are now independent prescribers per The Pharmacist (April 2026).
  • NHS England's planning guidance instructs ICBs to maximise Pharmacy First and introduce prescribing-based services in 2026/27 per The Pharmaceutical Journal (October 2025), with the Medium Term Planning Framework formalising the requirement per NHS England (October 2025).
  • The new prescriber cohort needs structured supervision, e-portfolio infrastructure and decision-support tools to deliver safely at scale per Nuffield Trust (March 2026).

Counter-argument

The IP cohort expansion is real but does not automatically translate into new Datapharm revenue. Independent prescribers in community pharmacy will rely heavily on ePMA-embedded decision support (FDB Multilex remains dominant) and on free-to-access NICE BNF for prescribing reference; emc is more useful at the SmPC-detail level, which is consulted episodically rather than continuously. The structural-demand framing risks overstating Datapharm's natural share of the new wallet unless first-party decision-support tooling is built or partnered.

4. AI evidence search and clinical decision support disruption

Near-Term

The clinical AI vendor field has reshuffled in the past six months. FDB Multilex (used in 10,000+ NHS sites) has repositioned to cloud delivery and is winning new GP-system embeds. OpenEvidence, the global category leader at $12bn valuation, has withdrawn from European markets citing EU AI Act uncertainty, leaving an open seat. UK-native iatroX is positioning as MHRA-registered Class I and powered partly by eMC content. The MHRA's new AI-medical-device framework is due in 2026. Datapharm's choice is whether to remain the source-of-truth data layer that AI vendors consume, build a first-party AI search layer over emc, or partner with one of the contenders.

Weak signals worth tracking

  • Weak signal The Medicus EPR for general practice integrating FDB Multilex per FDB (July 2025) suggests new-build clinical systems are now embedding incumbent decision support by default. Gains weight if a second new-build system makes the same call in 2026.
  • Weak signal OX.DH partnering with FDB on cloud-native medicines management per HTN (October 2025) signals primary-care platform consolidation around the incumbent data layer. Gains weight if NHS England names cloud-native medicines management as a procurement preference.
  • Weak signal iatroX referencing emc-powered medicines content per iatroX (December 2025) suggests Datapharm is already an upstream supplier to AI tools, raising the question of pricing rather than relationship.
  • FDB launched Multilex in the Cloud in October 2025 with NHS Microsoft Azure delivery and continuous updates, repositioning the embedded category leader for the next decade per FDB.
  • OpenEvidence reached $12bn valuation in January 2026 with 15 million monthly clinician consultations but withdrew from European markets citing EU AI Act regulatory uncertainty per iatroX and iatroX market analysis (March 2026).
  • The MHRA is preparing a new AI-in-medical-devices regulatory framework for publication in 2026, with transparency, explainability, AI assurance, cybersecurity and lifecycle governance named as priorities per Mills & Reeve and Periculo (April 2026).
  • AI-enabled consultation systems show 15 to 20 minute time savings per Pharmacy First consultation in pilot data per iatroX (April 2026), evidence the AI-enabled value proposition is operationally credible at the pharmacy point of care.

Counter-argument

The AI-search disruption story is loud but the unit economics are unproven. OpenEvidence's withdrawal from Europe demonstrates that valuation does not translate into a defensible foothold; FDB's incumbent position is reinforced by ePMA integration, not threatened by it; iatroX's free-to-pharmacist business model is yet to be tested at scale. A counter-reading is that the AI-search category settles into a complementary layer alongside the FHIR data layer, and Datapharm's optimal play is upstream supplier rather than downstream competitor.

5. UK-EU regulatory divergence and dual-track compliance

Near-Term

The UK and EU regulatory tracks are now diverging by design rather than by accident. The EU's revised pharmaceutical legislation reached political agreement in March 2026; the MHRA's International Reliance Framework is due Autumn 2026 and explicitly leans on Australia, Canada, US and other comparator regulators rather than the EMA. The UK Life Sciences Sector Plan targets top-three European MedTech access by 2030. For Datapharm's 300+ pharma manufacturer clients, the result is two parallel ePI obligations to maintain. The strategic question is whether Datapharm sells one bundled multi-jurisdiction service or remains a UK-specialist letting EU-native vendors capture the cross-border data layer.

  • The MHRA confirmed delivery of its targets to increase access to medicines and reinforce the UK's life sciences position, with the International Reliance Framework due Autumn 2026 per GOV.UK MHRA (April 2026).
  • Independent legal analysis confirms MHRA international reliance enables faster GB market access by relying on overseas regulator approvals, structurally separating UK from the EU pipeline per Browne Jacobson (March 2026).
  • The UK Life Sciences Sector Plan published July 2025 sets ambition for top-three European MedTech access by 2030, anchoring divergent UK regulatory architecture per Osborne Clarke and NHS Confederation.
  • The EMA welcomed political agreement on the revised pharmaceutical legislation in March 2026, anchoring the EU divergence track that pharma manufacturers must navigate alongside UK MHRA reform per EMA.
  • The EU regulatory network's adoption of the FHIR-based ePI Common Standard in late 2024 set the cross-border data architecture that UK manufacturers must mirror to retain EU market access per EMA.

Counter-argument

UK-EU divergence may be more rhetorical than operational. International reliance, by accepting third-country approvals, can in practice run alongside continued EMA alignment for medicines (as opposed to medical devices), and the largest pharma manufacturers maintain dual UK and EU regulatory teams as a matter of course. Datapharm's 300+ clients may experience the divergence as a manageable extension of existing dual-track work, in which case a bundled multi-jurisdiction service answers a willingness-to-pay question that was already partly satisfied by internal teams.

SI 1: Productise the emc FHIR layer as a priced data service

The CPO and CTO should propose, by Q3 2026, a pricing structure and SLA for API-led access to emc's FHIR-structured medicines data. The technical architecture is in place; the open question is whether the FHIR layer is treated as the engine room of a free destination or as a separately monetised infrastructure tier. Voluntary EU ePI submission opens for vaccines in Q3 2026; a Datapharm-priced FHIR data service that launches alongside it captures both UK pharma compliance demand and downstream AI-vendor and ePMA-vendor consumption.

Action: CPO and CTO joint paper to the board by 30 September 2026 with three pricing models, two anchor-client commitments and a 12-month commercial runway.

Scenario Matrix

The two critical uncertainties for Datapharm's 2026 to 2029 window are (i) whether the EU ePI mandate converts into rapid manufacturer adoption or slips into a long voluntary tail, and (ii) whether AI clinical search consolidates into one or two dominant platforms or fragments across multiple specialist tools. The four scenarios below are planning aids, not forecasts.

Infrastructure provider

Rapid ePI adoption meets fragmented AI-search field. Pharma manufacturers move quickly to FHIR submission; multiple AI tools compete for the prescriber-facing seat; all of them need authoritative FHIR-structured medicines data. Datapharm sits as the priced upstream data layer, charging API-rate-limited access to manufacturers and AI vendors alike. Margins are infrastructure-grade and recurring; competitive moat is data quality and regulatory alignment. CGE thesis vindicated through scaling rather than M&A.

Early indicators: 20+ manufacturers volunteer ePI submission in Q3 to Q4 2026; three or more distinct AI tools embed emc FHIR feeds.

Slow voluntary tail

EU ePI rollout slips into 2028 to 2029 voluntary uptake; manufacturers prioritise other compliance work; the FHIR demand curve flattens. Datapharm's first-mover advantage in FHIR converts to customer-friction reduction rather than priced data revenue. Growth comes from incremental product layers (publisher, PI portal, icomply) rather than infrastructure pricing. CGE thesis pivots to platform consolidation and bolt-ons.

Early indicators: Fewer than ten manufacturers volunteer ePI submission in 2026; revised legislation enters force later than 2027.

Default decision-support layer

Rapid ePI adoption combines with AI-search consolidation around one or two dominant platforms. The winning AI tool becomes the default UK pharmacist and prescriber interface, and either embeds emc upstream or builds an alternative. If Datapharm has built or acquired a first-party AI layer in time, it occupies the default seat; if not, it remains the data layer underneath someone else's interface. The cycle is decisive: who lands the AI-search seat sets the next decade's revenue mix.

Early indicators: One AI clinical-search tool reaches majority adoption among UK pharmacist independent prescribers by end-2027; ePMA vendors standardise on one CDS layer.

UK specialist, EU bystander

UK MHRA divergence and EU ePI both proceed but Datapharm chooses to remain UK-only. EU-native vendors (VIDAL, EMA-direct services) capture the cross-border data layer; pharma manufacturers maintain Datapharm for UK obligations and a separate EU stack for EU obligations. Revenue is stable but the addressable market shrinks relative to multi-jurisdiction peers. CGE thesis recalibrates to a UK platform play with adjacent product expansion rather than geographic.

Early indicators: VIDAL or another EU-native vendor wins material UK pharma manufacturer contract; CGE M&A activity stays UK-domestic through 2027.

What We Are Not Planning For

Direct competition from NICE BNF

NICE's British National Formulary serves a different segment (free-to-NHS prescribing reference) and operates on a different content cadence. The BNF is a complementary reference rather than a substitute for Datapharm's pharma-manufacturer-funded SmPC and PI service. The two have coexisted for decades and there is no signal in this cycle of NICE moving into pharma-manufacturer-paid services.

Reinstate if: NICE announces a paid pharma-services tier or absorbs a pharma-data vendor.

Patient-direct ePI displacing pharma-funded compendia

Patient-facing ePI delivery (QR-code triggered leaflets, multilingual smartphone access) is part of the EMA roadmap but is positioned as additive to, not replacement for, manufacturer-funded HCP-targeted aggregators. Patient adoption of AI tools for medicines information remains low (4% of emc users in Datapharm's 2025 survey).

Reinstate if: a major regulator mandates patient-direct ePI as the sole channel for any medicine class.

Datapharm building its own ePMA system

The ePMA market is mature, deeply integrated and dominated by specialist vendors. A Datapharm move into ePMA would require a step-change in capital and capability outside the CGE thesis. The natural play is upstream data supply and decision-support partnerships, not vertical integration into prescribing-system delivery.

Reinstate if: CGE Partners signals an ePMA-vertical acquisition or a major ePMA vendor enters administration.

Discussion Points for the Leadership Team

  1. If voluntary EU ePI uptake in Q3 to Q4 2026 attracts only single-digit manufacturer participation, do we still launch a priced FHIR data service in 2026, or hold to a 2027 launch tied to the mandatory trigger?
  2. The OpenEvidence European withdrawal opens a UK-and-EU AI-search seat. Are we structurally a contender for that seat, or are we content to be the data layer underneath whoever wins it?
  3. CGE Partners' first major acquisition under our ownership will signal market positioning. Which of clinical-decision-support, ePI-services or EU-jurisdiction targets best advances the FY29 destination, and which carries the lowest integration risk?
  4. The September 2026 IP cohort is structurally favourable but requires distribution through ePMA systems and AI tools we do not own. What share of the prescriber-facing wallet is realistic without first-party tooling?
  5. The Nuffield Trust flag on prescriber-supervision gaps suggests an adjacent product opportunity in IP supervision and e-portfolio infrastructure. Is this a Datapharm play, or one we explicitly leave to others?
