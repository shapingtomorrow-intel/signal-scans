How the US Gap, AI, Cloud Suppliers and Research Act on Each Other

4 November 2026 · Companion to the Decision Intelligence briefing of 4 November 2026, Europe Will Have to Defend Itself With Tools It Doesn't Control · Horizon: to 2030 Built from the briefing’s 66 verified sources. Links drafted by analysis.

The Decision Intelligence briefing says what is changing as the US draws down in Europe while the tools European forces depend on remain largely American. This System Map shows why the pieces move together: which changes feed each other, which push back, and where an organisation’s effort changes how the whole system behaves rather than one part of it.

Read it by following the arrows. A + means the two move in the same direction; a − means they move in opposite directions. Where the arrows close into a circle, they form a loop: an R loop reinforces itself, so change feeds on change, and a B loop balances, pushing back against change. Hover over a loop label or an arrow for its explanation and evidence.

2. The four loops this quarter

Reinforcing loops run in both directions: the same circle that compounds decline would compound recovery. The status on each card is this cycle’s reading, and is what the monthly Change Tracker updates. Priming means the parts are in place but the circle has not yet closed at scale. Turning means every step in the loop has current evidence, so it is running now. Hover over a status for its meaning.

Priming

R1 Each gap filled alone

The US supplied 58% of the major weapons European NATO members imported from 2021 to 2025 (Military Times, on RAND, Oct 2026), and only a third of EU counter-drone programmes are expected to deliver by 2030 (Euronews, Nov 2026). The loop has not yet closed, because joint European projects are also being funded.

Watch: whether the European instrument for strategic enablers is proposed and funded, and how many joint purchases follow.

Turning

R2 Scale draws more users

AWS became the first provider cleared for sensitive information in every NATO member (Defense One, Sep 2026), three non-EU providers hold over 70% of the European market (Council of the EU, Jun 2026), and officials warn European rules could leave forces with less capable services (Defence Matters, Sep 2026).

Watch: the EU Cloud and AI Development Act, and whether defence gets an exemption.

Priming

B1 Speed crowds out checking

Maven’s users doubled to over 100,000 (DefenseScoop, Sep 2026) while operators can face hundreds of recommendations with too little time (Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists, Oct 2026). No error has yet forced a pause, so the correcting step has not arrived.

Watch: published inquiries into military errors, and allied rules on practising without AI.

Priming

B2 Secrecy deters researchers

Norway’s defence research call drew 112 bids (Research Council of Norway, in Norwegian, Jun 2026), while Germany’s rectors say many universities are not prepared for the security demands (German Rectors’ Conference, in German, May 2026). The deterrent effect is feared rather than measured.

Watch: the University of Kassel’s civil clause vote and Germany’s research security platform.

What the map shows that the briefing alone cannot
  1. Filling gaps alone pulls states toward American tools. Separate national purchases are the link between the US drawdown and dependence on American cloud and AI: a state buying quickly on its own usually buys from the market leader. Joint purchases are the one lever that slows both.
  2. The check on AI is a habit, not a rule. Loop B1 only corrects after an error is found. Training staff to challenge AI advice, and practising without it, puts the correction in place before the error rather than after.
  3. Research money can build alternatives, if researchers stay. Defence research money is the only arrow in the map that pushes against loop R2, through European alternatives, but loop B2 can drain it if security duties deter the people the money is meant to attract.

3. Four leverage points

Each point is numbered on the map, and each ties to a Strategic Implication already in the briefing, so the map changes the reasoning behind the plan rather than adding a second plan.

  1. 1

    Fill gaps together

    Separate national purchases are where loop R1 (each gap filled alone) turns. Agreeing with neighbours which gaps to fill first, and buying them jointly, is the lever that stops the loop.

    DecideBriefing Implication 1: list the US enablers each plan depends on and cost a shared replacement for the top three, by 31 March 2027

  2. 2

    Train to challenge

    The time and habit of checking is where loop B1 (speed crowds out checking) acts. Training staff to question AI advice, and keeping planning without AI in exercises, restores the check before an error does.

    DecideBriefing Implication 2: an AI-challenge step and a no-AI planning drill in staff training, by 31 March 2027

  3. 3

    Plan the exit

    Use of US cloud and AI is where loop R2 (scale draws more users) draws in each new user. An exit plan does not stop the use, but it keeps the choice open.

    PrepareBriefing Implication 3: map critical functions on non-European services and write an exit plan for each, by 30 June 2027

  4. 4

    Agree the terms

    Security duties are where loop B2 (secrecy deters researchers) can tighten. Terms agreed with universities and research organisations keep researchers in while protecting the work.

    PrepareBriefing Implication 4: shared terms for defence and dual-use work, by 31 December 2027

4. How the map explains the scenarios

The two axes of the briefing’s Scenario Matrix 2030 are the map’s two dominant uncertainties: whether loop R1 runs, which decides whether each state fills the gap alone or Europe fills it together, and whether loop R2 keeps running, which decides whether European defence runs on American or European tools. The Scenario Stress Test tracks which of the four is gaining momentum.

Joint purchases break R1 and European alternatives slow R2: slower to build, more resilient once running.
American tools · each state

Bilateral Clients

Both reinforcing loops run: each state buys American, alone.
American tools · Europe together

Common Buyer, Foreign Tools

Joint purchases break R1, but R2 runs: Europe buys together, mostly from American providers.

Read this way, the scenarios stop being four stories and become four states of the same system, each with early indicators already on the map.

6. How the map stays live

A map drawn once goes stale as fast as the plans it informs. Inside a continuous intelligence cycle, every format updates it:

FormatWhat it does to the map
Weekly Signal ScanAttaches new evidence to existing links, and proposes new links or factors when something does not fit.
Monthly Change TrackerScores each loop as strengthening, stable or weakening, which is the status shown in section 2.
Quarterly Decision IntelligenceRedraws the map, re-weights the loops and tests the leverage points against the scenarios.
Action TriggersFire when a loop changes state: for example, a published inquiry naming unchecked AI advice in a military error would mark loop B1 (speed crowds out checking) turning.

7. How an organisation would use it

The value of a System Map comes as much from arguing over the arrows as from the finished picture. This draft is a starting point:

Challenge sessionOne facilitated session of about 90 minutes in which planners, technologists, researchers and policy staff test every arrow, add what the evidence missed and agree the leverage points. Their corrections become part of the map, and the argument is where the assumptions surface.
Institution lensesThe same structure, shaded by institution: the loops are shared, but their strength differs, so a defence staff, a university and a research and technology organisation can sit on one map and see where they depend on each other.
Other topicsSpace, cognitive security or a single technology area can be mapped the same way, so a growing foresight team learns one method and applies it step by step.
