From Three Pathways to One: SAF Hardens, Hydrogen Retreats, eVTOL Crystallises

Aviation decarbonisation momentum is collapsing toward SAF dominance: Airbus ZEROe slipped 10 years to the late 2040s, the UK SAF Mandate is in force on a 22% by 2040 trajectory, and the CAA's CAP 3169 Delivery Model has dated eVTOL commercial passenger flights to end-2028.

Change Tracker · The Future of Flight · Department for Transport · Cycle: 18 May 2026

The Department for Transport's three-pathway aviation decarbonisation strategy is being structurally repriced. Airbus pushed ZEROe from 2035 to the late 2040s with a 25% budget cut; the European Destination 2050 roadmap cut hydrogen's contribution to 2050 net zero from 20% to 6%; Universal Hydrogen folded in July 2024. Simultaneously, the UK SAF Mandate is in force with a 10% by 2030 and 22% by 2040 trajectory, and the CAA's CAP 3169 Delivery Model dates first commercial passenger eVTOL flights to end-2028. The strategic question: should the Jet Zero Strategy continue to assume three parallel pathways, or plan for SAF-dominant with hydrogen as long-tail?

Pattern narrative

eVTOL regulatory crystallisationEmerging Opportunity

The eVTOL signals (CAA CAP 3169, Vertical Aerospace VX4 testing, the broader AAM framework) compound: the UK has a dated commercial-passenger eVTOL launch on the regulatory calendar for end-2028, with Vertical Aerospace as the credible UK-headquartered OEM. The cluster is the closest to a hard regulatory inflection in this cycle and the most consequential for airport-side capital, airspace integration and vertiport planning.

SAF dominance hardensTransformation Driver

The Mandate ramp, airport capital reallocation toward SAF supply, and the UK SAF plant pipeline together describe a pathway structurally crowding out alternatives. As hydrogen retreats, SAF becomes the only credible near-term route for the existing narrow-body fleet. The transformation moves from "three parallel pathways" to SAF-dominant with eVTOL adjacent and hydrogen long-tail. Supply-side tightness against the 2030 trigger is the operational risk.

Hydrogen-aviation pathway narrowingEvolving Risk

Airbus ZEROe, Universal Hydrogen, Destination 2050 revision and ZeroAvia scope narrowing all signal structural deprioritisation of hydrogen-aviation as a near-term pathway. The Jet Zero Strategy's hydrogen optionality is being pushed out by a decade. The evolving risk is single-pathway dependency on SAF: if SAF supply tightens beyond the mandate trajectory, the multi-pathway hedge that justified the original strategy is no longer available.

Signals gaining momentum

  1. UK SAF Mandate ramp. A 2027 trajectory review would convert demand-side certainty into binding industrial-strategy decisions on UK plant capacity.
  2. CAA CAP 3169 eVTOL Delivery Model. Final regulations ahead of 2028 would let Vertical Aerospace, Joby and Archer commit to UK commercial operations.
  3. Airbus ZEROe deprioritisation. A second narrow-body OEM retreating from hydrogen would lock in SAF-dominance as industry consensus.
  4. Vertical Aerospace VX4 certification. The first milestone would crystallise UK eVTOL credibility and trigger second-wave airport capital.

Wild Cards to Watch

UK SAF supply shortfall in 2027-28

Disruptive Risk Potential impact: Very High

Surprise characteristics: UK SAF pipeline misses the 10% 2030 trigger, forcing import dependency or penalty payments at scale.

Early warning indicators: A flagship UK SAF plant (Velocys, Lanza, OXCCU, Equilon) is cancelled or delayed beyond 2028; compliance shortfall in Q2 2027.

Five SAF plants were intended under construction by 2025 per Jet Zero. If the pipeline thins, SAF becomes a supply-side bottleneck and the mandate calendar collides with available production. The risk binds because hydrogen is no longer a credible near-term hedge.

Renewable hydrogen cost cliff by 2028

Disruptive Opportunity Potential impact: High

Surprise characteristics: Chinese electrolyser scale-up plus EU Hydrogen Bank drives renewable hydrogen below $2/kg LCOH by 2028, restoring unit economics ahead of schedule.

Early warning indicators: EU Hydrogen Bank Round 3 or 4 in 2026-2027 shows cost convergence; major Chinese electrolyser deployment at commercial scale in UK or EU.

Hydrogen-aviation has been deprioritised partly because the supporting hydrogen ecosystem is 5-10 years behind projection. A cost cliff would re-open the pathway from a different OEM base (Boeing, Embraer, Chinese OEMs) in the late 2020s, restoring multi-pathway optionality.

Implications

For Department for Transport, the aviation decarbonisation calendar is collapsing toward SAF dominance with eVTOL adjacent and hydrogen long-tail. The next twelve months reward monitoring rather than commitment. The SAF Mandate trajectory is settled but supply tightness is worth tracking against the 2030 trigger; the CAA's eVTOL delivery model has dated commercial flights but airport-readiness is a separate calendar; the Jet Zero Strategy's hydrogen optionality may need revision in light of Airbus's retreat. The 2026 spending review is the right place to consider whether multi-pathway optionality remains appropriate or pick-winners discipline now applies.
