Grids Will Ration Power to Large Users, Starting With Data Centres

Grid operators have gained and rehearsed emergency powers to cut supply to data centres, so access to power is no longer a place in a queue but a condition that can be withdrawn. Water and grid equipment now limit new sites too.

Initial signal

We identified that US and British operators were re-pricing grid access around demand flexibility, conditioning fast interconnection on a large load's willingness to be curtailed.

Now evident
  • FERC RM26-4: a flexible-load fast lane that ties interconnection speed to curtailability, echoed by Texas's mandatory-curtailment law.
  • PJM, 30 June 2026: two DOE 202(c) emergency orders authorising curtailment of data centres holding backup generation during the heatwave.
  • 16 July verdict: curtailment named as the Refusal Layer, with the authority rehearsed under heat stress though not yet executed.

Assumption changes since the May scan

Cycle-over-cycle assumption changes, in decreasing order of strategic weight.

Shift 1 of 5

"A sustained 15%+ hyperscaler capex cut would break the demand thesis"

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle held that the demand thesis had one clean disconfirming test: a sustained cut of 15% or more in hyperscaler capital guidance through Q3 would break it.

This cycle reads

The threshold broke upward, not down. The four hyperscalers each raised Q1 2026 guidance and are tracking roughly $725bn for the year, up about 77%; the buildout is confirmed, and the open question is returns, where a capex-to-revenue gap near $600bn now exceeds the 2001 telecom divergence.

Forward thesis

Next cycle the fragile variable is returns, not demand: a Q2 or Q3 guidance break, not a demand miss, is the thing to watch.

Shift 2 of 5

"The insurability frontier is hardening"

Prior cycle held

Prior thinking placed climate physical risk on a one-way hardening path, with a sixth consecutive $100bn-plus insured-loss year lifting the underwriting base.

This cycle reads

The frontier reversed on price while migrating venue. Mid-2026 catastrophe-reinsurance rates fell roughly 16 to 20% in an abundant-capacity buyers' market helped by a 2026 Atlantic hurricane outlook that turned below-normal, the steepest drop since the late 1990s, yet the stress surfaced in residual markets, with the California FAIR Plan at $724bn (up 230% since September 2022), and in household mortgage credit.

Forward thesis

Next cycle the signal to watch is the credit channel, the premium-to-delinquency path, not the softening cat-cover price.

Shift 3 of 5

"The binding constraint is the transmission and interconnection queue"

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle placed the binding constraint firmly in the interconnection queue, with roughly 1,400 GW of generation waiting on grid evacuation.

This cycle reads

The constraint would not hold still. It walked downstream across the cycle: from the queue to grid-hardware manufacturing, then to power-plant water, and finally to the grid's own authority to refuse load.

Forward thesis

Next cycle the plan should be built against a moving constraint, not a fixed one, with the current binding edge re-identified each cycle.

Shift 4 of 5

"Reliability equals adequacy, measured by the reserve margin"

Prior cycle held

Prior thinking equated reliability with adequacy: enough reserve margin held above the normal peak.

This cycle reads

Reliability is now carried by system strength, the inertia, fault current and reactive power procured explicitly, and by the right to refuse load. The 2 July peak held on curtailment authority and stability services, not on spare capacity.

Forward thesis

Next cycle, reserve-margin adequacy alone will understate reliability risk; system strength and curtailment authority carry it.

Shift 5 of 5

"Solar is the cheapest power and its build-out is unstoppable"

Prior cycle held

The prior consensus held solar as the cheapest power on an unstoppable build-out path, with cost the only variable that mattered.

This cycle reads

Solar's own success is collapsing its capture price. EU-27 day-ahead negative-price hours more than doubled year on year to 1,223 in Q1 2026, and close to half of April solar output in France and Germany cleared below zero, moving the binding constraint from generation cost to captured revenue.

Forward thesis

Next cycle carries a new exposure the May thesis did not: the merchant-renewables revenue model, not the price of the panel.

Decide Action 1 Signal Scan Decision Intelligence

Take a capital-committed position in Refusal-Layer flexibility tools before the next heat event.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because PJM secured two DOE 202(c) emergency orders on 30 June 2026 authorising data-centre curtailment, because the curtailable-connection re-pricing has made flexibility the price of grid access, and because transformer and grid-hardware lead times of up to four years mean the supply the plan will need cannot be bought on demand.

Primary decision ownerCSO / CFO Decision windowImmediateBefore the 2026 winter peak
Why this now matters

Latent curtailment has moved from a tail scenario to a priced condition on every large load. The scarce assets that blunt it, flex capacity, backup, transformers and closed-loop cooling, are exactly the ones with the longest lead times.

Who gains

The premium accrues to the suppliers of the scarce flexibility: grid-forming storage and inverter vendors, demand-response aggregators, backup-generation and closed-loop-cooling providers, and the transformer and grid-hardware makers holding near-term delivery slots.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: secure demand-response and flexible-capacity positions, and place transformer and grid-hardware orders against the four-year queue.
  • Immediate: specify closed-loop cooling for new sites to cut both water and curtailment exposure.
  • Follow-on: negotiate curtailable-connection terms that trade flexibility for interconnection speed.
Review conditions

This recommendation should be reviewed if a second heat event forces actual curtailment execution, if FERC's co-location rulemaking resolves the terms of grid access, or if transformer lead times ease materially below three years.

Prepare Action 2 Signal Scan Decision Intelligence

Build a water-availability and interconnection-timing gate into siting and project-finance underwriting.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because power-plant indirect water has surfaced as the binding siting limit, because 90.9% of the US Southwest sits in drought with two-thirds of post-2022 build in water-stressed basins, and because water rights are beginning to act as a permit veto ahead of the grid connection, a gate the current underwriting does not model.

Primary decision ownerCOO / General Counsel Decision windowNext planning cycle2027 capital plan
Why this now matters

A project cleared on power and grid access can still fail on a water permit, an exposure that lands after capital is committed. Pricing it at underwriting, not at permitting, is the difference between a repriced deal and a stranded one.

Who gains

Value migrates to those who can clear the water gate: water-rights and hydrology advisers, closed-loop and air-cooling technology vendors, and siting and permitting consultancies, while owners of already-permitted, water-secure sites hold option value the market has not yet priced.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: add water-availability and indirect-water disclosure to site screening.
  • Next quarter: set an interconnection-timing threshold below which projects do not pass finance.
  • Follow-on: embed the gate in the 2027 capital plan as a standing underwriting condition.
Review conditions

This recommendation should be reviewed if a permit-veto case blocks a peer's flagship site, if federal or state water-disclosure rules standardise the metric, or if drought conditions in the key basins ease durably.

Prepare Action 3 Signal Scan Decision Intelligence

Reprice climate physical exposure through the credit channel, not the softening cat-cover market.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because catastrophe-reinsurance rates fell 16 to 20% mid-2026 in a buyers' market that masks the underlying risk, because the frontier migrated into residual markets such as the California FAIR Plan at $724bn, and because the Dallas Fed and Bank of England have traced a premium-to-delinquency path into household mortgage credit that the cat-cover price no longer signals.

Primary decision ownerCFO / CRO Decision windowNext quarterQ3 2026 risk review
Why this now matters

Reading exposure off falling reinsurance prices would understate it precisely as the risk relocates into credit. The venue changed; the exposure did not close.

Who gains

The repricing favours parametric and specialty underwriters, residual-market capacity, and the climate-risk analytics and rating providers that can price the credit channel the softening cat-cover market no longer signals.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: map physical-risk exposure onto residual-market and mortgage-credit indicators, not cat-cover pricing.
  • Next quarter: fold the credit channel into the Q3 risk review and counterparty screens.
  • Follow-on: stress-test asset and lending books against a residual-market and delinquency scenario.
Review conditions

This recommendation should be reviewed if a residual market such as the FAIR Plan needs a public backstop, if reinsurance pricing reverses back upward, or if the mortgage-delinquency signal fails to broaden beyond the exposed basins.
