Governments Will Decide Who Gets Power First, Before Agreeing Who Pays for It

With more requests for power than grids can serve, governments are deciding who connects and who is cut off first, while the bill for new supply waits. Pennsylvania's final order in January and the federal ruling on the largest US grid's emergency purchase will test it.

Gas contracts signed in this winter's scramble will carry methane proof duties from 2029, even though the rule's start has moved.

Initial signal

In early November we read both texts delaying the EU methane rule for importers as keeping the 4 August 2024 contract cut-off, when the delay was being reported as relief.

Now evident
  • The delay: the Commission proposed on 14 October, and Parliament voted on 22 October, to delay importers' obligations, which moves only the proof date.
  • The duty: contracts signed after 4 August 2024 stay in scope, so contracts signed now carry proof duties due in 2029 to 2031.
  • The scramble: QatarEnergy extended force majeure on LNG deliveries through November, so European buyers are seeking replacement supply now.

Backup generators and old coal plants may not be there this winter if the emergency orders that free them to run are challenged again.

Initial signal

In late September we flagged that a federal appeals court had ruled the first emergency order keeping a Michigan coal plant open unlawful, while such orders were still being renewed.

Now evident
  • PJM: its list of 2026 emergency orders shows five that let it direct backup generators at large users, and it publishes no megawatt total.
  • Permit limits: the 1 September order lets those generators run beyond their permit limits as a last resort before customers are cut off.
  • New orders: two weeks after the ruling, the Department of Energy ordered a Colorado coal unit kept available to 25 December 2026.

Battery projects counted on for winter cover could slip on town approvals rather than on money.

Initial signal

In early October we identified that New York pays for contracted grid batteries only after every local approval, while some towns keep extending moratoria.

Now evident
  • New York: delivery of the 950 MW that planners count on turns on town and county decisions, because the contract requires fire-code compliance, not local consent.
  • United States: storage installations set a record quarter in the second quarter of 2026.
  • Europe: batteries more than doubled to 38 GW since last winter, and the grid operators find the winter position favourable.
Monitor

Planners should date battery capacity by when local consent is secured, not by when the contract is signed.

Lead time 6 weeks aheadSignal Scan Signal Scan Decision Intelligence

Europe's grid operators will compete for the same transformers, switchgear and cable between 2028 and 2032.

Initial signal

From late September we flagged gaps in Europe's grid supply: power cables have no repair fleet of their own, and a 2028 switchgear gas ban would leave one EU-based supplier with capacity below demand.

Now evident
  • The peak: Europe's grid operators plan additions in almost every type of equipment to peak at the same time, between 2028 and 2032, with about 34,000 km of new cable by 2035.
  • The wait: transformer lead times have stretched from about one year to up to five.
  • The rules: the US is licensing grid equipment through an Energy Department vendor list rather than banning it, which decides who may supply.
Equipment peak2028 to 2032

Grid operators and developers should secure delivery slots for 2028 to 2032 now, and check each supplier against the new rules.

Governments will choose which data centres connect, and who is cut off first, before they publish how they choose.

Initial signal

In late September we identified regulators testing data-centre demand before paying for it, with Texas halting permits pending an audit of a queue in which about 90% of new requests are data centres.

Now evident
  • Pennsylvania: a tentative order of 1 October would cut certain large new loads before other customers; the final order is planned for 28 January 2027.
  • Britain: the 28 October response lets ministers give capacity to sponsored projects before the selection criteria are published.
  • Spain: the revised decree would let Red Eléctrica cut data centres first, without compensation.
  • Texas: new data centres cannot connect until the state's power and water audit is complete.

Assumptions that have shifted

Cycle-over-cycle assumption changes, in decreasing order of strategic weight.

Shift 1 of 4 Replaced

The bill for the power shortage would be settled first

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle held that "Who pays for the grid became the live political question", and expected the payer to be named in regulatory filings within months.

This cycle reads

The payer was deferred and access moved first. The federal regulator suspended PJM's emergency purchase to 28 February 2027 and sent cost allocation to a hearing, and PJM's amended filing leaves retail costs to the states.

  • Who connects: Britain will let ministers choose; Texas will not connect new data centres until its audit is done.
  • Who is cut first: Pennsylvania's tentative order and Spain's revised decree put large new loads and data centres first in line.
  • Who pays: still open: the market monitor says data-centre load has added $29.4 billion to the cost of capacity.
Forward thesis

Plan against each market's access order first and its cost rule second. The tripwire is Pennsylvania's final order on 28 January 2027.

Shift 2 of 4 Overturned

One federal rule would set the terms for every large power user

Prior cycle held

Prior thinking held that "The regulator rewrote the rules for every large load", placing the federal regulator at the centre of connection terms.

This cycle reads

The federal regulator rejected the provisions that would have decided which retail customers are cut, leaving that choice to the states. Terms are now being written state by state and country by country: Texas, Pennsylvania and California in the US, and Britain, Germany and Spain in Europe.

Forward thesis

Expect a patchwork through 2027 rather than one standard. The tripwire is a second US state adopting a rule that cuts large new loads first.

Shift 3 of 4 Holding

Money committed to data centres and power actually connected are two different forecasts

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle split the demand forecast in two: "Treat capital commitment and deliverable load as two separate forecasts with two separate tripwires."

This cycle reads

That reading survived a direct test. PJM's market monitor counts 42% of forecast data-centre growth as delayed or cancelled, which would shrink the 6,831.3 MW shortfall to 3,055.0 MW, while GE Vernova's turbine queue still grew to 122 GW.

Forward thesis

Keep the two forecasts apart. The tripwire is the market monitor reporting more than half of forecast data-centre growth delayed or cancelled.

Shift 4 of 4 Split

The weather threat to power supply comes from hot, dry summers

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle held that "Heat and low water stopped being a weather story", placing the weather risk in summers when rivers run warm and low.

This cycle reads

That still holds for summer: France lost more than 10 TWh of nuclear output to river limits in 2026, a record. But the test that matters next is winter: gas stores entered it low, a strong El Niño is likely, and last winter the US grid held only with every emergency tool in use.

Forward thesis

Plan for two weather seasons, not one. The tripwire is EU gas storage on 1 February 2027 against its five-year average.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because Pennsylvania has approved a tentative order to cut certain large new loads first, with the final order planned for 28 January 2027, because Spain's revised decree would let its grid operator cut data centres first without compensation, and because Britain will let ministers choose which projects connect before the criteria are published.

Primary decision ownerStrategy and Regulatory AffairsDecision windowImmediatePennsylvania final order, 28 January 2027
Why this now matters

Access terms written now will decide which sites run in a shortage for years, and they are being set market by market with no federal standard to fall back on. Taking part costs little; inheriting the terms can cost a site.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: list every site by market with the access rule in force or in draft: Britain's framework, Germany's grid package, Spain's decree, Pennsylvania's order and the Texas audit.
  • Immediate: file or join a response wherever the rule is still open, starting with Pennsylvania before 28 January 2027.
  • Next quarter: price what being cut first would cost each site, and decide where to accept interruptible terms in return for an earlier connection.
Review conditions
  • EscalateIf Spain approves its decree with unpaid cut-offs and a second EU country follows by 31 December 2027, set one group position on interruptible terms across Europe.
  • Scale backIf Britain publishes criteria that put your sector outside the priority list, move British sites to the standard queue and keep the other markets active.
  • Stand downIf Pennsylvania's final order drops the cut-first rule and no other US state adopts one by 30 June 2027, return US sites to normal connection planning.

Fix part of next year's energy cost before the January price cap and the 2027 refill season.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because EU gas stores were just over 71% full on 28 September against a five-year average of 86%, because bills are rising again in Britain, France and Italy, and because Europe's stores may refill to only 65% by September 2027 if LNG stays tight.

Primary decision ownerFinance director, with procurementDecision windowImmediateBy 31 January 2027
Why this now matters

A budget that assumes energy costs fall back after this winter carries the refill risk without pricing it. Fixing part of the cost gives up some savings for a number the business can plan against.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: fix or hedge part of 2027 gas and power costs, and set the 2027 budget against prices at this winter's level.
  • Immediate: check any new gas supply contract for the EU methane proof duties due in 2029 to 2031.
  • Next quarter: list where heat or processes could move from gas to electricity, and what each switch would cost.
Review conditions
  • EscalateIf an EU member state orders cuts in industrial gas use this winter, move from hedging to securing volumes for the sites most exposed.
  • Scale backIf Qatari LNG deliveries resume and EU storage is back on its five-year average path by 31 March 2027, fix only the share already contracted.
  • Stand downIf the gas network operators project stores above 90% by September 2027, return to normal purchasing.

Firm the equipment slots you need for 2028 to 2032, check each supplier against the new rules, and release the rest.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because Europe's grid operators plan almost every type of equipment to peak between 2028 and 2032, because transformer lead times have stretched to up to five years, because US rules now license who may supply the grid, and because 58 GW of GE Vernova's 122 GW queue are still reservations rather than orders.

Primary decision ownerCapital planning and CFODecision windowNext quarterEvery reservation firmed or released by 30 June 2027
Why this now matters

When every operator needs the same equipment in the same five years, a firm slot decides which projects energise on time. A reservation held without a decision costs the holder and delays everyone else.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: list the transformers, switchgear and cable each project needs before 2030, with the supplier and slot for each.
  • Next quarter: check each supplier against the Energy Department's vendor list and the EU's 2028 switchgear rule, then firm the critical slots and release the rest.
  • Follow-on: for offshore links, contract cable repair capacity of your own, because Europe's funded repair fleet covers telecommunications cables only.
Review conditions
  • Raise to DecideIf GE Vernova reports at least 130 GW of gas backlog and reservations at year-end, commit capital to the critical slots this quarter.
  • Scale backIf the EU pauses its 2028 switchgear gas ban, drop the single-supplier constraint from switchgear orders.
  • Stand downIf makers report reservations released or slots resold, return to ordering equipment as projects are approved.
