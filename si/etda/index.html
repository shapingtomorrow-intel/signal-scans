Executive Synthesis

At a glance

ETDA's 2-5 year strategic perimeter is being reshaped on five legs at once. AI governance is moving from voluntary guideline to enforceable architecture under EU AI Act and regional pressure. Cross-border digital ID is contested between domestic-only and ASEAN trust-anchor postures, with eIDAS 2.0 and ASEAN DEFA defining the interoperability template. Inter-agency cyber and data architecture must absorb AI-augmented threats that cross NCSA, PDPC, BoT and ETDA perimeters in a single incident. Cross-border digital trade is on the eIDAS QTSP-equivalence path or it is not. Tokenisation is the next layer where ETDA's identity and signature mandate touches the Thai SEC and BoT mandates.

These five legs are not five independent files. They compound inside FY27: the EU AI Act reaches full application 2 August 2026; the EU MiCA transitional period closes 1 July 2026; ASEAN DEFA targets November 2026 signing with digital-trust components in scope; the EUDI Wallet rollout begins from 2026; ETDA's own AI Governance Week 2026 lands in late June 2026. The marginal mover between this cycle and the next is whether ETDA lands an internally consistent posture across the five legs or treats them as separate workstreams.

The one thing to brief the board: the cycle's central question is not what ETDA does on any single leg, but which combination of postures (regulator, auditor, assurance-infrastructure provider) is internally consistent across all five, and whether the coordination with MDES, Thai SEC, BoT and NCSA required to deliver that combination can be built inside the FY27 window. Each Strategic Implication that follows operationalises one piece of that posture decision.

This cycle's evidence has produced four data points that, read together, reframe ETDA's posture decision. The EU AI Act reaches full application on 2 August 2026, setting the de facto international floor that ASEAN trading partners increasingly track. Vietnam's Law on Artificial Intelligence was approved 11 December 2025, the first binding AI law in ASEAN. The eIDAS 2.0 Regulation entered into force 21 May 2024, with the EUDI Wallet rolling out from 2026 and member-state notification deadlines in train; Thailand's ETDA Phase 2 Digital ID architecture (May 2025) advances the NDID consortium domestically against this cross-border template. The EU-Singapore Digital Trade Agreement entered into force 1 February 2026 and the EU-Korea Digital Trade Agreement was signed 10 June 2026, both setting the regional benchmark for the e-Signature and trust-services interoperability question Thailand has not yet answered.

Read together, these four points describe one connected change. The international floor for AI governance is being legislated, not negotiated: the EU AI Act, Vietnam's December 2025 statute and the Council of Europe Convention each take a different design path (risk-tier regulator; sandbox-plus-fund model; binding treaty), and ASEAN trading partners are now obliged to read each as a regulatory benchmark. Cross-border digital identity is on a parallel hardening curve: eIDAS 2.0 sets the European Digital Identity Wallet architecture; the ASEAN Hanoi Digital Declaration commits ASEAN members to interoperable digital identity as a regional baseline; ASEAN DEFA negotiations carry the digital-trust-services chapter Thailand has the most to gain or lose from. Cross-border digital trade is now operationally bilateral: the EU has signed with Singapore and Korea on QTSP-grade trust-services recognition, and Japan is next; Thailand has not yet been positioned to enter that recognition tier. Tokenisation infrastructure is moving from pilot to operative regime: BIS Project Agorá (May 2026) demonstrated atomic wholesale settlement across tokenised commercial bank deposits and central bank reserves; MAS Project Guardian (November 2025) published the tokenised-funds operational playbook; EU MiCA closes its transitional period on 1 July 2026. Each leg has its own regulatory clock, and the clocks overlap inside FY27.

The cycle's central tension for ETDA is that these data points compound. The EU AI Act, Vietnam's AI Law and the Council of Europe AI Convention (opened for signature 5 September 2024) collectively close the voluntary phase; ETDA's announcement of AI Governance Week 2026 (June 2026) signals the directional response. The ASEAN Hanoi Digital Declaration (January 2026) and the targeted November 2026 DEFA signing put cross-border digital ID and trust-services interoperability inside the same 18-month window. ETDA's ETR e-Transactions overhaul (June 2026) and the BoT supervisory pages on digital-asset businesses (December 2025) put the tokenisation-adjacency question in the same FY27 calendar. The five legs are visible in different evidence bases but they describe one connected reframing of ETDA's mandate.

ETDA's posture decision (regulator, auditor or assurance-infrastructure provider) cuts across all five legs of the cycle, and the FY27 choice sets the trajectory for the rest of the decade.

Where this analysis could be wrong

The cycle's central assumption is that the five legs compound and that the FY27 posture decision sets the long-term trajectory. The analysis is wrong if the Thai AI Act draft stalls and the voluntary AIGC guideline becomes the de facto Thai position through 2028, or if ASEAN DEFA misses its November 2026 signing target and the cross-border trust-services question slips to 2028-29, or if the EU AI Act's 2 August 2026 full application produces enforcement-fragmentation that downgrades the international floor signal. Watch the EU AI Office's first FY27 reports, the November 2026 ASEAN summit DEFA outcomes, and ETDA's AI Governance Week 2026 (June) for the posture-direction signal.

Themes

Five themes, ordered by impact tier. Each names one leg of ETDA's 2-5 year strategic perimeter and the structural condition that holds it in place.

1. AI governance moves from voluntary guideline to enforceable architecture

Immediate

AI governance is moving from voluntary guideline to enforceable architecture, and ETDA's posture decision lands inside the same FY27 window. The EU AI Act reaches full application on 2 August 2026 and sets the de facto international floor that ASEAN trading partners track. Vietnam approved its Law on Artificial Intelligence on 11 December 2025, the first binding AI law in ASEAN. The Council of Europe AI Convention opened for signature on 5 September 2024 with a binding-treaty trajectory. Singapore IMDA updated its Model AI Governance guideline with a GenAI Companion in January 2026; the ASEAN Expanded Guide on AI Governance and Ethics (January 2025) covers GenAI; BIS papers (January 2026) frame AI provider concentration as a systemic operational-risk issue for the financial system. ETDA's AIGC voluntary instrument and CARIA self-assessment tool sit one step short of the trajectory; AI Governance Week 2026 signals directional response. The strategic question is which of three postures (regulator, auditor, assurance-infrastructure provider) ETDA pursues, on what timeline, and with what coordination across MDES, Thai SEC, BoT and NCSA.

  • The international anchor: EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) reaches full application on 2 August 2026, setting risk-tier obligations across general-purpose AI and high-risk systems; the de facto international floor for AI governance.
  • The Council of Europe binding treaty: Convention on AI opened for signature 5 September 2024 with human-rights and rule-of-law obligations; ratification path under way across Council of Europe states.
  • The first binding ASEAN AI law: Vietnam Law on Artificial Intelligence was approved on 11 December 2025, the first AI-specific binding statute in the region; sets a precedent for ASEAN peers including Thailand.
  • The ASEAN harmonisation anchor: the Expanded ASEAN Guide on AI Governance and Ethics (January 2025) covers generative AI and provides the regional principles ASEAN peers (including ETDA) increasingly cite as the shared floor.
  • The Singapore comparator: IMDA launched the updated Model AI Governance guideline for GenAI in January 2026, advancing the assurance-infrastructure posture Singapore has pioneered.
  • The financial-stability frame: the Bank for International Settlements January 2026 paper names AI provider concentration as a systemic operational-risk issue for the financial system; relevant to ETDA's coordination with BoT.
  • ETDA's own positioning: ETDA AI Governance Week 2026 (announced June 2026) is the headline platform on which ETDA signals its FY27 posture and engages international and ASEAN counterparts.

The case against this framing

The "moving from voluntary to enforceable" framing may overstate the speed at which ASEAN as a whole, and Thailand specifically, will codify AI obligations. The Thai AI Act draft has been in circulation since 2023 with uncertain timetable; ASEAN governance traditionally favours soft-law guides; the Vietnam AI Law's enforcement architecture is untested. This counter would gain weight if the Thai AI Act draft fails to advance through Parliament inside 2027, or if the EU AI Act's 2 August 2026 full application produces enforcement-fragmentation that reduces its international-floor signal, or if Vietnam's implementing regulations slip materially.

2. Cross-border digital ID: NDID becomes the regional anchor or stays domestic

Immediate

NDID is mature domestically at scale (about 60 million identities, eleven Identity Providers across major banks and telcos) but has not been positioned as an ASEAN cross-border trust anchor. The interoperability template is now legislated: EU eIDAS 2.0 (Regulation 2024/1183), in force 21 May 2024, sets the European Digital Identity Wallet architecture; the ASEAN Hanoi Digital Declaration (16 January 2026) commits members to interoperable digital identity as a regional baseline; ASEAN DEFA targets November 2026 signing with digital-trust components in scope; Singapore Singpass and India Aadhaar each export their identity stacks bilaterally. ETDA opened Phase 2 Digital ID architecture for 2025-27 in May 2025. The strategic question is whether NDID becomes the ASEAN cross-border trust anchor (Bangkok as regional digital-trust hub) or stays a domestic credential, and what eIDAS-style bridge would be required to make the anchor posture operationally credible inside FY27.

Sources: EUR-Lex (eIDAS 2.0); ETDA Phase 2 Digital ID architecture (May 2025); ASEAN Secretariat (January 2026); ASEAN DEFA negotiation track; European Commission DG CNECT (EUDI Wallet rollout).

  • The ASEAN regional commitment: ASEAN Secretariat Hanoi Digital Declaration (16 January 2026) sets interoperable digital identity as a regional baseline under the "Adaptive ASEAN: From Connectivity to Connected Intelligence" frame; the political anchor for any NDID cross-border positioning.

  • ETDA's own Phase 2 architecture: ETDA (Thai-language) sets out the Thai Digital ID architecture for B.E. 2568-2570 (2025-2027), the domestic platform on which any ASEAN anchor posture would have to rest.
  • The scale anchor: NDID (National Digital ID Co.) documents about 60 million identities and 11 Identity Providers across major banks and telcos; the domestic-maturity evidence for an anchor positioning argument.

The case against this framing

The "NDID as ASEAN trust anchor" framing may overstate the realism of a regional-anchor positioning given that Singapore Singpass already has a head-start on cross-border bilateral arrangements, that ASEAN members differ sharply in domestic digital-ID maturity (Indonesia, Vietnam and the Philippines are each at very different stages), and that ASEAN trust-services negotiations under DEFA are more likely to converge on a multi-vendor recognition regime than a single-anchor architecture. This counter would gain weight if ASEAN DEFA signs in November 2026 without a binding digital-trust chapter, or if Singpass extends bilateral recognition with two or more major ASEAN partners before NDID does.

3. Inter-agency cyber and data architecture under AI-augmented threat

Near-Term

AI-enabled cyber attacks now cross NCSA, PDPC, BoT and ETDA perimeters in a single incident, and the inter-agency MOU architecture is partial. UNODC (March 2026) and UN News / OHCHR (February 2026) document the scale of Southeast-Asian scam-centre operations driving cross-border AI-augmented fraud; INTERPOL Asia and South Pacific Cyberthreat Assessment Report 2025-2026 formalises the regional picture. The regulatory clock for incident response, breach notification and AI-system audit obligations is increasingly set by the EU NIS2 Directive timeline that ASEAN trading partners track. Thailand has the instruments in place individually (Cybersecurity Act 2019 at NCSA; PDPA at PDPC; BoT AI Risk Management Guidelines for FIs (September 2025); the Electronic Transactions Act at ETDA). The strategic question is who owns what in the cross-perimeter AI-augmented incident, and what the FY27 inter-agency MOU map needs to look like to make incident leadership unambiguous.

  • The EU benchmark for incident-response and AI-system audit obligations: European Commission DG CNECT (January 2026) sets out the NIS2 Directive baseline that ASEAN trading partners increasingly track for breach-notification and supply-chain cyber-resilience obligations.
  • The regional scam-centre evidence base: UNODC (March 2026) frames Southeast Asian organised fraud as a global wake-up call, with AI-augmented social engineering, deepfake-driven KYC fraud and synthetic-identity attacks at scale.
  • The OHCHR documentation: UN News / OHCHR (February 2026) documents the human-rights dimension of Southeast Asian scam-centre operations driving the cross-border fraud picture.
  • The INTERPOL regional read: INTERPOL Asia and South Pacific Cyberthreat Assessment Report 2025-2026 sets out the regional threat picture for cross-border AI-augmented cyber and fraud, with explicit ASEAN coverage.
  • The Thai sector-specific instrument: Tilleke & Gibbins legal commentary on the Bank of Thailand AI Risk Management Guidelines for financial service providers (September 2025); the operational anchor for cross-perimeter AI-incident handling in the FI sector.
  • ETDA's own roadmap: Bangkok Post (June 2026) reports ETDA unveiling its roadmap to strengthen AI governance with explicit inter-agency coordination language.
  • The corporate threat-landscape view: Microsoft Digital Defense Report 2025 sets out the AI-augmented threat-actor playbook (synthetic media, automated reconnaissance, deepfake-driven KYC) that the Thai inter-agency architecture has to absorb (directional).
  • The Thai 2026 outlook: Chiang Rai Times (April 2026) surveys the Thai 2026 cybersecurity-risk picture and the regulatory load on NCSA, PDPC, BoT and ETDA.

The case against this framing

The "AI-augmented inter-agency incident is the binding architectural question" framing may overstate the operational gap given that the Thai inter-agency coordination across ETDA, NCSA, PDPC and BoT is sectorally bounded by clear statutory mandates, that the documented AI-augmented incident pattern has not yet produced a single regulator-overlapping incident at scale in Thailand, and that bilateral working-level coordination may be sufficient absent a formal MOU architecture. This counter would gain weight if the 2026 Thai cybersecurity incident record produces no cross-perimeter AI-augmented incident, or if the BoT, NCSA, PDPC and ETDA each publish standalone AI-risk supervisory regimes that operate without visible inter-agency friction through FY27.

4. Cross-border digital trade and the e-Signature interoperability gap

Near-Term

Thai e-Signatures are technically interoperable but not legally recognised at the EU's eIDAS Qualified Trust Service Provider (QTSP) equivalence level that the EU now uses as a digital-trade baseline. The EU-Singapore Digital Trade Agreement entered into force on 1 February 2026 (signed 7 May 2025) with trust-services interoperability provisions; the EU-Korea Digital Trade Agreement was signed on 10 June 2026; Singapore, Korea and Japan each pursue bilateral arrangements with the EU on this layer. ASEAN DEFA negotiations include digital-trust components targeting November 2026 signing; the Hinrich Foundation DEFA analysis sets out the bargaining frame. ETDA is advancing the ETR (Electronic Transactions Reform) overhaul (Thai Press, June 2026); Tilleke & Gibbins (June 2026) signals a major e-Transactions regime refresh. The strategic question is whether Thailand pursues eIDAS QTSP equivalence individually, leads an ASEAN-level trust-services negotiation under DEFA, or accepts the current asymmetry through FY28.

  • The structural anchor: the UNCITRAL Model Law on Electronic Signatures (2001) is the international alignment instrument that Thailand's ETA was built against; it remains the technical interoperability baseline.
  • The EU-Singapore precedent: European Commission DG Trade confirms the EU-Singapore Digital Trade Agreement entered into force 1 February 2026, including trust-services interoperability provisions; Singapore's template for ASEAN peers including Thailand.
  • The EU-Korea signal: EEAS confirms the EU and Korea signed a landmark Digital Trade Agreement on 10 June 2026, extending the EU bilateral trust-services arrangement into northeast Asia.
  • The EU-Korea cooperation track: European Commission DG Trade reports the April 2026 EU-Korea ministerial trade and technology cooperation track, with trust services among the digital-economy work-stream items.
  • The DEFA negotiation analytics: Hinrich Foundation White Paper (Kati Suominen, June 2025) sets out the DEFA stakes for ASEAN including the digital-trust-services chapter; the analytical anchor for ETDA's engagement strategy.
  • The WEF tracking note: World Economic Forum tracks the DEFA negotiation milestones with explicit reference to the digital-trust components.
  • ETDA's own response: The Thai Press (June 2026, Thai-language) reports ETDA accelerating the ETR (Electronic Transactions Reform) overhaul as the Thai response to the digital-trade interoperability question.
  • The legal-commentary corroboration: Tilleke & Gibbins (June 2026) signals a substantial Thai e-Transactions regime overhaul is in train; the operational anchor for any FY27 cross-border trust-services positioning.

The case against this framing

The "eIDAS QTSP equivalence is the binding lever" framing may overstate the practical importance of QTSP recognition for Thai cross-border trade. Most Thailand-EU trade in goods does not depend on QTSP-grade electronic signatures; the bilateral approach may take three to five years to negotiate; and ASEAN DEFA may produce a multilateral trust-services regime that is more cost-effective than bilateral QTSP equivalence. This counter would gain weight if ASEAN DEFA signs in November 2026 with a robust trust-services chapter that Thailand can use as a multilateral anchor, or if EU-Thailand bilateral trade growth fails to translate into demonstrable demand for QTSP-grade electronic signatures.

5. Tokenisation and digital assets: ETDA's adjacent role to the Thai SEC and BoT

Near-Term

ETDA's identity, signature and transaction-attribution mandate touches tokenised-asset infrastructure even though the Thai SEC owns digital-asset securities (2018 Digital Asset Decree) and the Bank of Thailand owns CBDC and stablecoin policy. The regional comparator set is moving rapidly: MAS Project Guardian published its tokenised-funds operational playbook in November 2025; HK SFC ASPIRe covers VA perpetuals and the Digital Asset Accelerator; the EU MiCA transitional period closes 1 July 2026 with the ESMA grandfathering table setting the deadline architecture; VARA established formal exchange-traded VA derivatives from 31 March 2026; SIX-SDX consolidated under FINMA approval. BIS Project Agora (May 2026) demonstrated atomic wholesale settlement across tokenised commercial bank deposits and central bank reserves in seven currencies. In the Thai context, BoT supervisory pages on digital-asset businesses (December 2025) and the Thai SEC consultation on crypto-futures licensing (Siam Blockchain, April 2026) signal the moving landscape. The strategic question is what the trust-layer architecture for tokenised assets in Thailand looks like, and where ETDA's mandate begins and ends against the SEC and BoT primary mandates.

  • The structural Thai anchor: the Office of the SEC, Thailand Emergency Decree on Digital Asset Businesses B.E. 2561 (2018) sets the Thai jurisdiction over digital-asset securities; the legal foundation for any tokenisation conversation.
  • The cross-border wholesale demonstrator: BIS Project Agora (May 2026) demonstrates atomic multi-currency settlement using tokenised commercial bank deposits and tokenised central bank reserves across seven currencies; the technical viability proof for any centre-level tokenisation strategy.
  • The Singapore tokenised-funds anchor: MAS Project Guardian playbook (November 2025) formalises operational rules for tokenised funds (governance, NAV, onboarding, compliance); the regional comparator for any Thai tokenisation regime.
  • The Hong Kong roadmap: SFC ASPIRe roadmap advances on Virtual Asset perpetual contracts and the Digital Asset Accelerator; HK's positioning as Asia's tokenisation accommodation venue.
  • The EU operative deadline: ESMA MiCA grandfathering table sets the Member-State-by-Member-State deadlines for CASP transition by 1 July 2026; the operational deadline structure.
  • The UAE upgrade: Pinsent Masons (Out-Law) reports VARA established a formal derivatives regime from 31 March 2026 with clarified guidance on VA exchanges.
  • The Swiss consolidation: SIX / SDX consolidation into SIX SIS approved by FINMA in May 2026; over CHF 2 billion of digital securities issued through SDX.
  • The Thai BoT supervisory framing: Bank of Thailand (December 2025, Thai-language) sets out the BoT supervisory regime for digital-asset businesses including stablecoin policy direction.
  • The Thai SEC consultation signal: Siam Blockchain (April 2026, Thai-language) reports the Thai SEC consultation on permitting crypto businesses to apply for futures licences; the moving Thai regulatory landscape.

The case against this framing

The "ETDA's adjacent role" framing may overstate ETDA's practical contribution to Thai tokenisation given that the Thai SEC has primary jurisdiction over digital-asset securities and BoT owns CBDC and stablecoin policy. ETDA's identity and signature layer may operate at a level of abstraction that does not require new architecture for tokenisation, and the SEC and BoT may have sufficient operational coordination already. This counter would gain weight if the Thai SEC consultation on crypto-futures licensing produces an operational regime through 2026-27 that does not visibly draw on ETDA's trust-services architecture, or if BoT's stablecoin policy clarification proceeds without explicit ETDA technical coordination.

Strategic Implications

Four strategic implications for ETDA across the four internal-function lenses, each pointing to a decision the next 18-36 months will not let ETDA defer. Each carries a decision posture (Decide / Prepare / Monitor) and a theme reference.

SI 1: Land an explicit FY27 posture across regulator / auditor / assurance-infrastructure provider for each of the five mandate legs

Decide

The EU AI Act's 2 August 2026 full application, Vietnam's December 2025 AI Law, EUDI Wallet rollout from 2026, ASEAN DEFA November 2026 target signing and EU MiCA 1 July 2026 transitional close all land inside FY27. ETDA's posture decision (regulator / auditor / assurance-infrastructure provider) sets the trajectory for each of the five legs, and the postures need to be internally consistent across AI governance, digital ID, inter-agency cyber, cross-border digital trade and tokenisation. By Q3 2026 the board should review and approve the posture matrix; by Q4 2026 the resource and coordination plan with MDES, SEC, BoT and NCSA should be signed off. Draws on Theme 1 and Theme 2.

SI 2: Land the NDID cross-border positioning decision (ASEAN trust anchor vs domestic-only) inside the FY27 window

Prepare

The ASEAN Hanoi Digital Declaration (16 January 2026) commits ASEAN members to interoperable digital identity as a regional baseline; ASEAN DEFA targets a November 2026 signing with digital-trust components in scope; EU eIDAS 2.0 (Regulation 2024/1183) sets the European cross-border interoperability template; and Singapore Singpass plus India Aadhaar are each pursuing bilateral cross-border arrangements. NDID is mature domestically at about 60 million identities across eleven Identity Providers. The Digital ID and Trust Services function should prepare a board-grade NDID positioning paper by Q3 2026 articulating the two postures (regional trust anchor with eIDAS-style bridge architecture vs domestic-only credential), the resource and partnership implications of each, and the trigger conditions for choosing between them; the board should land the positioning decision by Q4 2026 so the FY27 international-engagement plan can be built against it. Draws on Theme 2.

SI 3: Negotiate and sign a four-agency inter-agency MOU on cross-perimeter AI-augmented incidents inside H2 2026

Prepare

The UN, UNODC and INTERPOL evidence base on Southeast Asian organised fraud is unambiguous and AI-augmented; the EU NIS2 Directive sets the international incident-response benchmark; the BoT has issued AI risk-management guidelines for FIs. The current Thai inter-agency coordination across ETDA, NCSA, PDPC and BoT is sectorally bounded; the cross-perimeter incident (AI-driven KYC fraud against an e-commerce platform with PDPA data and bank-linked NDID identities) does not have a single owner. A formal four-agency MOU defining incident leadership, breach-notification pathways, AI-system audit triggers and supervisory coordination should be drafted by Q3 2026 and signed by Q4 2026. Draws on Theme 3.

SI 4: Decide the cross-border trust-services strategy (eIDAS QTSP equivalence bilateral, ASEAN DEFA multilateral, or default) and engage internationally accordingly

Monitor

The EU-Singapore Digital Trade Agreement's 1 February 2026 entry into force is the operational precedent; the EU-Korea DTA signed 10 June 2026 extends the precedent into northeast Asia; ASEAN DEFA November 2026 target signing is the multilateral alternative; the Thai SEC, BoT and ETDA tokenisation-adjacency question is a parallel cross-border lever. ETDA's International Cooperation and Standards Engagement function should land the strategic-direction recommendation by Q4 2026 (after DEFA outcome is visible), and the FY27 budget allocation across bilateral, multilateral and OECD / ITU / ISO standards-engagement should follow that direction. Monitor the November 2026 DEFA outcome, the EU-Thailand bilateral trade dialogue, and the Thai SEC consultation outcomes through the same window. Draws on Theme 4 and Theme 5.

What We Are Not Planning For

Three scenarios deliberately excluded from this cycle's planning envelope. Each is named with the indicator that would force a reinstatement.

A material reorganisation of ETDA's statutory mandate

This briefing assumes the Electronic Transactions Act jurisdiction held by ETDA, the Cybersecurity Act jurisdiction held by NCSA, the PDPA jurisdiction held by the PDPC, and the BoT and Thai SEC perimeters remain as currently constituted. We are not planning for a parliamentary or cabinet reorganisation that merges ETDA with another agency, transfers AI-governance jurisdiction wholesale to a new authority, or restructures the MDES portfolio in a way that materially alters ETDA's strategic perimeter.

Reinstatement trigger: a cabinet or parliamentary proposal to reorganise the MDES agency cluster; or a draft AI Act that establishes a standalone AI authority outside ETDA's perimeter.

A frontier-AI safety event triggering a US compute-export or model-restriction regime affecting Thailand

This briefing assumes US AI capital and compute remain broadly available to Thai operators and that the Stanford AI Index 2027 reads as a continuation of the 2026 picture. We are not planning for a major AI safety incident producing a US Bureau of Industry and Security restriction that affects ASEAN access to frontier-model compute, training data or APIs.

Reinstatement trigger: a US BIS Entity List action against an ASEAN AI hub; or a documented frontier-AI safety incident producing a G7 or G20 emergency response with cross-border compute-access implications.

An ASEAN DEFA process collapse

This briefing assumes ASEAN DEFA either signs in November 2026 with a substantive trust-services chapter (the two Fast scenarios), or slips by 12-18 months without collapsing (the two Slow scenarios). We are not planning for an outright collapse of the DEFA process or a member-state withdrawal that fundamentally alters the regional digital-trust architecture.

Reinstatement trigger: a documented ASEAN member-state withdrawal from DEFA negotiations; or a Chair statement that formally pauses the negotiation track without a re-launch timetable.

  5. Where does ETDA's mandate begin and end against the Thai SEC and BoT on tokenisation, and what is the explicit operating-model coordination that defines the trust-layer architecture for tokenised assets in Thailand by FY27?
