Action Triggers · Financial Services & Future of Money · 25 September 2026

Money left the bank. Its protections stayed behind.

Over this cycle, the question changed from whether money would leave the bank to what protects it once it has. Nine weeks ago the central question was whether a US yield rule would set the pace of deposit flight. That rule never arrived: the Senate rejected cloture on the Clarity Act 49 to 50, and the stablecoin float fell to a six-month low. What arrived instead were terms set by supervisors: federal charters for digital-dollar firms that take no insured deposits, a January gate on foreign-minted coins, and capital charges for risk that comes back. Meanwhile retail private credit funds capped withdrawals at 5%. What follows is the warnings this portfolio carried early, the assumptions that changed, and the decisions now ready to take.

Assumptions that have shifted

Cycle-over-cycle assumption changes, in decreasing order of strategic weight.

Shift 1 of 6 Replaced

One US vote would decide how fast money left the bank

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle held that "the still-unwritten stablecoin yield rule is the single switch that sets the pace", and keyed its lead action to the Clarity Act text due in the fourth quarter.

This cycle reads

The switch never moved, and the pace was set anyway. The successor is a split line drawn by supervisors, no interest for holding digital dollars but rewards for using them, plus the licences that decide who may issue and custody them.

  • Statute: cloture on the Clarity Act failed 49 to 50 on 15 September.
  • Rewards line: the Bank of England's final policy bans interest but allows rewards linked to use; Korea's budget office points the same way.
  • Licence: uninsured trust charters and a Treasury gate on foreign-issued coins from 18 January 2027.
Forward thesis

Plan against the rewards line and the licence in each market, not against a US vote. The tripwire is the OCC final rule targeted for November, and whether it defines rewards narrowly.

Shift 2 of 6 Replaced

The danger was markets that fail together at machine speed

Prior cycle held

Prior thinking asked firms to add "a machine-speed, correlated-liquidity stress scenario" to their risk models, reading the systemic threat as tokenised, round-the-clock markets failing together and faster.

This cycle reads

The stress that arrived was slow and old-fashioned: a promise of easy withdrawal from assets that cannot be sold quickly, made by balance sheets outside the bank. It showed in retail credit funds, in risk transfers that circle back to banks, and in offshore reinsurance that supervisors now charge for recapturing.

Forward thesis

Stress-test the liquidity promise, not the settlement speed. The tripwire is a fund that cannot honour its 5% cap for a third quarter, or suspends repurchases outright.

Shift 3 of 6 Weakened

Deposits could drain out of banks fast once stablecoins took hold

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle held that "The pace became a policy variable", citing modelled deposit losses of up to $3.7tn if stablecoin yield were allowed.

This cycle reads

The fast-drain case lost ground on the evidence. The stablecoin float fell to a six-month low from a May peak of $300bn to $316bn, the deposit-drain signal read Fading in August and left the register in September, and estimates of lost bank lending now run from almost nothing to $60bn to $126bn for every $100bn that leaves and does not return.

Forward thesis

Keep a drain scenario, but size it against both estimates rather than the worst case. The tripwire is the float regaining its May peak.

Shift 4 of 6 Split

Governments would fight over whether central bank money pays interest

Prior cycle held

Prior thinking held that "the real sovereign question became remuneration and access, not a retail digital dollar".

This cycle reads

That still divides China from the West, but in Europe the live question is dependence and resilience. The European Parliament backed a digital euro by 416 votes to 169, partly because Visa and Mastercard handle most euro-area card payments, and the argument is over a holding cap of €3,000. Sweden meanwhile legislated a duty on grocers and pharmacies to accept cash, and Australia a regime for distributing it, both as a fallback layer.

Forward thesis

Read the European question as who keeps payments running in a crisis. The tripwire is the holding cap agreed in trilogue, which the Irish presidency aims to settle by the end of 2026.

Shift 5 of 6 Weakened

Whoever verified the AI agent would capture the value in payments

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle held that the rail was commoditising and "the position to stake is in agent identity and mandate verification".

This cycle reads

Identity still matters, but it no longer looks sufficient. The open question this cycle was who pays when an agent gets a payment wrong: the Bank of England says its rules were not built for agents acting on their own, and a US banking group warns the usual cap on customer losses may not apply. The customer record is being priced too, as US banks start charging aggregators for data that was free by rule.

Forward thesis

Stake the position in liability terms and data terms, not identity alone. The tripwire is the FSB's final AI report in October, and whether it names agentic payments.

Shift 6 of 6 Holding

Banks would answer stablecoins by building their own digital money

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle held that "the banks' answer is a build, not a veto", with a shared US tokenised-deposit network targeted for the first half of 2027.

This cycle reads

That reading survived a direct test. Federal Reserve staff classified tokenised deposits as money already inside M1, the ECB argued that tokenised deposits settling in central bank money do what stablecoins were meant to do, and more than half of the largest US banks are building or evaluating them. The weak point is connection: most schemes are being built separately.

Forward thesis

Hold the thesis and watch interoperability rather than adoption. The tripwire is the first Pontes transaction settling a tokenised-deposit leg.

Actions triggered

Each trigger names why it activates now, why it matters, the recommended actions, the primary decision owner, the decision window, and the conditions under which the recommendation should be reviewed.

Decide Action 1 Change Tracker Decision Intelligence

Rebuild the deposit defence around payment rewards, now that no statute will settle interest.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because the Clarity Act failed cloture 49 to 50 and is unlikely to pass this Congress, because the UK and Korea have drawn the same line (no interest for holding, rewards for using), and because the deposit plan written in July was keyed to a yield rule that will not now arrive.

Primary decision ownerCFO and Head of Retail DepositsDecision windowImmediateOCC final rule, targeted November 2026
Why this now matters

A deposit defence waiting on a vote that failed is not a plan. The contest is now fought on rewards, and the customers most likely to move for one can be named today.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: retire deposit-flight scenarios keyed to the Clarity yield text and rebuild them on rewards, using both published estimates of lost lending.
  • Immediate: identify which customer segments would move balances for a payments reward, and what matching it would cost.
  • Next quarter: choose between matching rewards, partnering an issuer and offering a tokenised deposit; if you build a token, join a shared network first.
Review conditions

This recommendation should be reviewed if a post-midterm cloture motion revives the Clarity Act with yield language, if the OCC's final rule defines rewards narrowly enough to close the gap, or if the stablecoin float stays below its May peak for another two quarters and the drain case weakens further.

Prepare Action 2 Signal Scan Change Tracker

Rate uninsured trust banks and foreign-minted stablecoins as exposure classes of their own.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because the OCC is converting digital-asset trust charters into operating banks on $6m to $25m of capital, because Treasury proposes to let exchanges list foreign coins from 18 January on the issuer's own word, and because no equivalence process yet exists in the US, the EU or Singapore.

Primary decision ownerGroup Treasurer and Chief Risk OfficerDecision windowNext quarterUS foreign-issuer gate, 18 January 2027
Why this now matters

Counterparty policy written before 2026 assumed one entrance to the federal banking system, insured and deposit-taking. A policy that cannot tell the two apart will price an uninsured custodian as a bank.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: list every token you hold or accept and every custodian of your token float, with the issuing entity, charter type and redemption terms.
  • Immediate: respond to the open consultations where you list or hold coins: the EU by 30 September, MAS by 16 October, Treasury by 19 October.
  • Next quarter: set limits for uninsured trust banks separately from insured banks, and treat each jurisdictional leg of a coin as its own holding.
Review conditions

This recommendation should be reviewed if a court upholds the state supervisors' challenge to the charter's legal basis, if a first equivalence finding makes a coin fungible across two regimes, or if the OCC adopts the reserve-based capital requirement of about 0.5% of assets that the states are pressing for.

Prepare Action 3 Signal Scan Change Tracker Decision Intelligence

Find every place where risk you paid to shed can come back onto your balance sheet.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because retail private credit funds met their first live test with withdrawals capped at 5%, because the PRA proposes charges of around 10% on funded reinsurance against 2 to 4% previously discussed, and because the NAIC's new CLO charges apply to the 31 December filing whatever the rating says.

Primary decision ownerChief Risk Officer and Chief Investment OfficerDecision windowNext quarterYear-end capital filing, 31 December 2026
Why this now matters

Each of these was sold as risk that had left: through a fund, a hedge counterparty or an offshore reinsurer. The cycle's evidence is that supervisors now price its return, and a plan that books the relief without the return overstates its capital.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: list every product or fund you hold or sell that promises regular withdrawals from private credit, and test a one-year cap.
  • Immediate: name the investors behind your risk transfers and net them against your own lending, repo and prime-brokerage exposure to them.
  • Next quarter: model required capital under adverse regulatory designation as well as adverse credit, and price reinsurance on both sides of the PRA's 30 September savings date.
Review conditions

This recommendation should be reviewed if a gated fund is forced to remark its book, which would escalate it to Decide, if a supervisory data collection finds bank financing of risk-transfer investors immaterial, or if the PRA materially dilutes its counterparty charge in the final policy.

Monitor Action 5 Signal Scan

Watch the Treasury's financing calendar, not the reserve count, for the price of overnight dollars.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because Federal Reserve staff find that $100bn of net coupon issuance adds 3.9 basis points to the tri-party repo spread while daily moves in reserves add nothing, because Treasury held coupon sizes flat on 5 August and at least doubled long-end buybacks two weeks later, and because the Fed still reads reserves as ample.

Primary decision ownerGroup Treasurer and Head of FundingDecision windowMonitor onlyReinstate as Prepare when coupon sizes rise
Why this now matters

Funding costs priced off SOFR follow the input that moves the spread. Watching the wrong input means the move arrives unannounced.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: add Treasury refunding statements and buyback schedules to the funding-cost watch.
  • Immediate: track the tri-party repo spread over the rate paid on reserves each week.
  • Follow-on: hold a drafted hedging stance for SOFR-linked books ready for the first coupon increase.
Review conditions

Reinstate this as Prepare if Treasury announces larger coupon auctions at any refunding before the end of March 2027, which primary dealers expect early in the year. Stand it down if coupon sizes rise and the spread does not move.

The body of work, in one sentence

Over this cycle, money kept leaving the bank, but the protections that came with the deposit did not follow it. The July plan hung on one switch, the US yield rule, and the switch never moved; supervisors set the terms instead, through charters without deposit insurance, equivalence findings nobody has made and capital charges on risk that comes back. The load-bearing variable for the next two quarters is who stands behind money outside the bank when a customer asks for it back. The dates that answer it are already set: 31 December, 18 January, and whichever gated fund is first forced to revalue.
