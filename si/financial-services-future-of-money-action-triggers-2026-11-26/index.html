Savers and Platforms Will Pay More When Finance Goes Wrong

Losses that banks used to absorb are moving to savers in funds that can cap withdrawals, to the platforms where scams begin and to governments paying more for long-term money. Year-end fund withdrawal results and the UK's scam refund consultation in December will test it.

Early warnings

Signals the portfolio surfaced before consensus formed, in the order they hardened.

Lead time 1 week aheadSignal Scan Decision Intelligence

Dollar stablecoins sent to emerging markets may stop at the platform, where governments now check who receives them.

Initial signal

In mid-November we identified three countries moving their controls from the border to the licensed platform, while the consensus held that regulators were losing control of dollar stablecoins.

Now evident
  • Brazil: since 1 October, stablecoins may not settle payments between a foreign-exchange provider and its counterparty abroad.
  • Nigeria: from 5 November, a provider may release a token only to a verified name and bank account, and a transfer to an unverified wallet counts as cross-border.
  • Thailand: draft rules of 12 November cap transfers at a licensed platform at 5 million baht per customer per day and ban transfers to another person's wallet.
Thai consultation ends26 November 2026
Monitor

Payment firms should map which emerging-market flows go to another person's wallet, because those are the flows stopped first.

Lead time 3 weeks aheadSignal Scan Change Tracker Decision Intelligence

Funds that cap savers' withdrawals may also have to pay borrowers on demand, from the same small pool of cash.

Initial signal

In early November we flagged that direct-lending funds which cap investor exits at 5% a quarter have also promised borrowers money on demand, with no cap on that promise.

Now evident
  • Commitments: the IMF finds 4 of 10 large business development companies, holding about $90 billion, have commitments above their available liquidity.
  • Queues: semi-liquid funds paid out $7.1 billion against $3.8 billion of new money in the second quarter, and $10.7 billion of requests went unmet.
  • Cash: the funds hold cash of only 5.5% of net assets, and Blackstone's flagship fund again paid out about half of what was asked in the third quarter.
Investor cap5% a quarterNext resultsFourth quarter, around year-end
Prepare

Fund boards and the banks that lend to them should test investor exits and borrower draws together, not apart.

Lead time 7 weeks aheadSignal Scan Decision Intelligence

Platforms and phone networks will be asked to share the scam refunds that banks now pay alone.

Initial signal

In early October we identified that UK banks refund most push-payment scam losses although most scams start on platforms and phone networks the banks do not control.

Now evident
  • UK: 88% of money lost to push-payment scams has been refunded, while 30% of fraud by value and 69% by volume starts on online platforms.
  • Australia: scam duties on banks, platforms and telecoms commenced on 1 September 2026, with fines of up to $50 million per contravention.
  • EU: draft duties on phone networks and very large platforms would apply 21 months after entry into force, subject to formal adoption.
UK consultationDecember 2026Revised UK rulesMay 2027
Prepare

Banks and payment firms should record where each scam began, so a claim on the platform is ready when a route exists.

Lead time Still aheadSignal Scan Decision Intelligence

Banks holding tokenised bonds at the capital cost of paper ones could pay more once the legal test is written.

Initial signal

In late October we identified that capital parity for tokenised securities rests on supervisory letters, on condition that the token carries identical legal rights, a test no rule defines.

Now evident
  • Interim: the Prudential Regulation Authority treats parity as interim and expects to consult in 2028 at the earliest.
  • EU gap: the European Banking Authority says the missing EU definition of a financial instrument can only be remedied by legislation.
  • Growth: DTCC's tokenisation service launched on 28 October with 20 participants signed up.
Basel updateEnd of 2026PRA consultation2028 at the earliest
Prepare

Collateral desks should price tokenised positions at the stricter of their booking jurisdictions until a rule fixes the test.

Lead time Still aheadSignal Scan

Insured deposits at the largest US banks could reach customers more slowly if the FDIC's untested systems replace the banks' own.

Initial signal

In early November we flagged that the FDIC's proposal to end in-house deposit insurance calculation at 31 large banks rests on its belief that its own capacity has improved, not on a published test.

Now evident
  • Scale: five of the 31 banks hold about $6.8 trillion of domestic deposits.
  • Savings: the FDIC puts industry savings at about $33 million a year, and expects most insured deposits to be available by the next business day.
  • Run risk: Atlanta Fed research finds that reducing bailouts raises run-driven failures.
Comments close4 January 2027
Prepare

Treasurers with large balances at these banks should test how fast insured money would come back, and say so in a comment.

Assumptions that have shifted

Cycle-over-cycle assumption changes, in decreasing order of strategic weight.

Shift 1 of 4 Split

More savings would flow into funds that lend, with individual savers playing a growing part

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle held that "by 2030 more savings will sit in funds that lend than today, with retail and insurance money playing a growing part".

This cycle reads

That holds for the market as a whole but not for funds sold to individuals. The Federal Reserve now counts $1.9 trillion in US private credit vehicles, yet semi-liquid funds had their first net outflow in the second quarter, and queues have formed behind the 5% cap.

Forward thesis

Plan for growth through institutions and insurers, and for queues where individuals invest. The tripwire is fourth-quarter requests above the 5% cap at most non-traded funds reporting.

Shift 2 of 4 Replaced

The digital euro's holding cap would be fixed in this year's law

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle read the cap under discussion as €3,000 per person, with "the holding cap agreed in trilogue, which the Irish presidency aims to settle by the end of 2026" as the tripwire.

This cycle reads

The figure is leaving the law. The ECB will recommend a ceiling after the regulation is adopted, Parliament and Council disagreed at the end of September on who sets it, and the limit may not be set until 2028.

  • Range: ECB simulations found no stability risk between €500 and €3,000 per person.
  • Deal: the presidency sought a deal on 18 November; we found no announcement of one by 23 November.
  • Timetable: the ECB says Christine Lagarde's departure on 7 February 2027 will not delay the project.
Forward thesis

Plan funding against three ceilings and commit only once the number is known. The tripwire is the ECB's recommended ceiling, not the political deal.

Shift 3 of 4 Split

Stablecoins would keep growing, and banks would defend deposits on rewards

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle held that "the contest for deposits will be fought on rewards for using digital money", and kept a drain scenario with "the float regaining its May peak" as the tripwire.

This cycle reads

The rewards line held, but the growth did not.

  • Rule: the OCC's final rule, in force from 18 January 2027, bans issuers paying interest and leaves rewards paid by third parties to case-by-case review.
  • Supply: stablecoin supply fell by $14.2 billion in the 30 days to 9 November, the fastest fall since 2022.
  • Congress: the market-structure bill is treated as tabled until January after the midterms.
Forward thesis

Keep the deposit defence on rewards, but size the drain case small. The tripwire is stablecoin supply back above its May peak.

Shift 4 of 4 Holding

Firms would have to set the rules for AI agents that pay before regulators did

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle advised "Stake the position in liability terms and data terms, not identity alone", with the Financial Stability Board's final AI report in October as the tripwire.

This cycle reads

That held. No authority has yet said who pays when an agent spends beyond its owner's mandate, and retailers are setting limits first.

  • FSB: the final report of 15 October set 12 sound practices for firms' own AI use, and nothing specific for agents that pay.
  • UK: HM Treasury's consultation closed on 6 October with no draft rule.
  • Market: issuers tend to side with the cardholder where no signed mandate exists; Walmart lets customers cap an agent's spending.
Forward thesis

Write mandate records and loss terms now. The tripwire is a card network publishing a dispute rule for agent-initiated payments.

Decide Action 1 Signal Scan Change Tracker Decision Intelligence

Set a limit on how much client and company money sits in funds that can stop withdrawals.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because semi-liquid private credit funds had their first net outflow in the second quarter and left $10.7 billion of requests unmet, because Blackstone's flagship fund again paid out about half of what was asked, and because the same funds owe borrowers money on demand from cash of only 5.5% of net assets.

Primary decision ownerInvestment committees, wealth managers and treasurersDecision windowNext quarterLimit set and told to clients by 31 March 2027
Why this now matters

Money in these funds can wait several quarters to come back, and the wait lengthens if borrowers draw at the same time. A limit set before the year-end results is a choice; one set after them is a reaction.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: list every fund you hold or sell that can limit withdrawals, with its cap, its cash and its undrawn commitments to borrowers.
  • Immediate: ask each fund for its commitments to borrowers against its cash and borrowing capacity before adding money.
  • Next quarter: set a maximum share of client or company money in such funds, and tell clients what it is.
Review conditions
  • EscalateIf fourth-quarter repurchase requests exceed the 5% cap at most non-traded funds reporting, bring the limit forward and pause new subscriptions until it is set.
  • Scale backIf the Bank of England's private markets exercise finds borrower draws on committed facilities immaterial, drop the borrower test and keep the limit.
  • Stand downIf requests at Blackstone's flagship fund and its peers fall below the 5% cap for two quarters in a row, return to normal allocation limits.

Rate each place you hold cash by who pays you back, and how fast, if it fails.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because the FDIC proposes to stop 31 large banks calculating their own deposit insurance and to rely on its own systems, with no published test, because the US stablecoin rule takes effect on 18 January 2027 with anti-money-laundering rules still to come, and because stablecoin supply fell by $14.2 billion in 30 days.

Primary decision ownerGroup Treasurer and Chief Risk OfficerDecision windowImmediateFDIC comments close 4 January 2027
Why this now matters

Counterparty limits assume insured money comes back the next business day and a stablecoin redeems on request. Both now rest on systems and rules that have not been tested at scale.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: list balances by where they sit: insured and uninsured at each large bank, stablecoins by issuer, and money at uninsured trust banks.
  • Immediate: file a comment before 4 January 2027 asking the FDIC to publish a test of its own payout systems.
  • Next quarter: set limits by how fast each holding pays back in a failure, not by the holder's credit rating alone.
Review conditions
  • Raise to DecideIf the final FDIC rule keeps the proposal without a published time-to-pay measure, cut uninsured balances at the affected banks to operating needs.
  • Raise to DecideIf a stablecoin issuer delays or limits redemptions before 18 January 2027, hold tokens only for settlement.
  • Scale backIf the FDIC publishes a test result for its own systems at the banks affected, drop the payout review and keep the stablecoin review.

Prepare Action 3 Signal Scan Decision Intelligence

Test portfolios and pension funds against a fall in AI shares that spreads to credit and long-dated government bonds.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because debt raised for advanced computing passed $680 billion this year, because euro area households hold around €440 billion of US technology shares mostly through funds, and because the ECB says euro area government debt is passing from the Eurosystem to foreign investors, short-end non-banks and leveraged hedge funds.

Primary decision ownerChief investment officers and pension trusteesDecision windowNext planning cycleResult to the board by 30 June 2027
Why this now matters

A test that hits shares alone misses the quarter when the same savers lose on credit and long bonds together. The Dutch pension switch fixes the dates on which the long end loses its natural buyers.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: list where savers' and the scheme's money is exposed to AI shares, AI-related credit and bonds of 30 years and longer.
  • Next quarter: run one combined test: AI shares down, AI-related credit repriced and long yields up, with the 10-year to 30-year spread widening within a week.
  • Follow-on: report the result to the board and set limits where the three losses land together.
Review conditions
  • Raise to DecideIf a euro area debt office cancels, cuts or moves to syndication a scheduled 30-year auction in 2027 citing demand, set liquidity and maturity limits that quarter.
  • Scale backIf De Nederlandsche Bank's first 2027 update shows fewer than half of Dutch funds switched on 1 January 2027, drop the long-bond leg until the switch resumes.
  • Stand downIf the euro 10-year to 30-year spread stays within ING's 10 to 20 basis point estimate through 2027, return to the standard annual stress test.

Monitor Action 5 Signal Scan Decision Intelligence

Track which countries put checks on the platforms your cross-border payments use.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because Brazil, Nigeria and Thailand have put identity, destination or settlement checks on stablecoin platforms in seven weeks, because Thailand's draft would ban transfers to another person's wallet, and because research finds border restrictions have largely failed to stop stablecoin flows, which is why the checks are moving to the platform.

Primary decision ownerTreasury and payments teamsDecision windowMonitor onlyQuarterly, from the Thai consultation closing 26 November 2026
Why this now matters

Each new check is a cost on a payment route that is cheap to know about and expensive to discover. A register turns the next rule into a planned change rather than a failed payment.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: list every cross-border route that uses stablecoins or sends to a wallet you do not control.
  • Next quarter: keep a register of the checks on each route, starting with Thailand's final rules.
  • Follow-on: keep a route through authorised foreign-exchange dealers ready for corridors where the checks bite.
Review conditions
  • Raise to PrepareIf Thailand's final rules, issued before 30 June 2027, keep both the daily cap and the ban on transfers to another person's wallet, re-route Thai flows to the customer's own wallets.
  • Raise to PrepareIf a further G20 emerging market limits platform transfers to the customer's own verified wallets before 31 December 2027, redesign the affected corridors.
  • Stand downIf Thailand drops the wallet ban and no other market copies the checks by 31 December 2027, end the register and review the routes once a year.
