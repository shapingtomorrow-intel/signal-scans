Every arrow is a claim with a source, a sign and a figure. Where no source states a link directly, the map says so.

Read it by following the arrows. A + means the two move in the same direction; a − means they move in opposite directions. 2. The four loops this quarter

Reinforcing loops run in both directions: the same circle that compounds decline would compound recovery. The status on each card is this cycle’s reading, and is what the monthly Change Tracker updates. Priming means the parts are in place but the circle has not yet closed at scale. Priming

R1 Forced sales feed falling prices

Funds hold more AI shares and debt (Lovguiden, Nov 2026), insurers and pension funds more illiquid assets (International Monetary Fund, Oct 2026), and debt raised for advanced computing passed $680 billion (Federal Reserve, Nov 2026); but no sustained fall has started.

Watch: Fund outflows after any fall in AI shares, and the ECB’s and Federal Reserve’s next reviews in May 2027.

Turning

R2 Queues prompt more requests

Blackstone’s flagship fund met about half of requests (CRE Daily, Nov 2026), and $10.7 billion of requests is unmet (Knowledge at Wharton, Oct 2026); the Federal Reserve says requests at certain funds trended lower (Federal Reserve, Nov 2026).

Watch: Fourth-quarter requests at the largest funds, reported in January and February 2027.

Priming

B1 Higher yields bring buyers back

Japan’s 30-year yield hit a record 4.25% and Meiji Yasuda is buying (Reuters, in Japanese, Oct 2026), while Dutch pension funds cut long hedges (ING, Jun 2026) and Britain cancels long gilt auctions (UK Debt Management Office, Oct 2026).

Watch: Britain’s 2027-28 gilt remit in March 2027, and Dutch pension transitions on 1 January 2027.

Turning

B2 Refunds push prevention upstream

UK fraud losses fell by about £73 million a year after reimbursement (Payment Systems Regulator, Jul 2026); Australia now fines platforms and phone networks (Dynamic Business, Jul 2026), and the EU’s rules are due at the end of 2026 (Addleshaw Goddard, Sep 2026).

Watch: The UK regulator’s consultation in December 2026, and the EU’s Payment Services Regulation in the Official Journal.

What the map shows that the briefing alone cannot
  1. The loss travels through funds, not banks. Loop R1 (forced sales feed falling prices) runs through funds, insurers and pension schemes. Banks sit at the edge of the map, reached through the credit lines funds draw. That is why the horizontal axis of the Scenario Matrix asks whether savers or the state end up carrying the loss.
  2. Exit limits protect the fund and queue the saver. Loop R2 (queues prompt more requests) is reinforcing: a limit stops a fire sale, but the queue it creates gives savers a reason to ask early. It slows only when requests fall or the money in the queue was never needed quickly.
  3. Two balancing loops are doing the steadying. Loop B1 (higher yields bring buyers back) and loop B2 (refunds push prevention upstream) both work, slowly: a 4% yield draws some buyers back, and refund rules have cut UK fraud. Neither is fast enough to stop a shock, only to settle one.

3. Four leverage points

Each point is numbered on the map, and each ties to a Strategic Implication already in the briefing, so the map changes the reasoning behind the plan rather than adding a second plan.

  1. 1

    Cap locked-up money

    Withdrawal limits and their queues sit inside loop R2 (queues prompt more requests). Money held only where it can wait several quarters is money that never joins the queue.

    DecideBriefing Implication 1: a published maximum in funds that can limit withdrawals, by 31 March 2027

  2. 2

    Run the combined test

    Forced sales are where loop R1 (forced sales feed falling prices) turns a fall into a loss. A combined test shows which holdings would be sold first, before the loop turns.

    PrepareBriefing Implication 2: one combined test reported to the board by 30 June 2027

  3. 3

    Agree who pays

    Loss-sharing terms decide how loop B2 (refunds push prevention upstream) runs: whoever carries the loss has the reason to stop the scam.

    PrepareBriefing Implication 3: loss-sharing terms with the three largest partners by 30 September 2027

  4. 4

    Track the checks

    Checks on platforms are an outside switch on every route money takes. A register of them turns each new rule into a known cost.

    MonitorBriefing Implication 4: a quarterly register of checks on cross-border routes

4. How the map explains the scenarios

The two axes of the briefing’s Scenario Matrix 2030 are the map’s two dominant uncertainties: where loops R1 and R2 end, with savers or with the state, and whether money moves freely or national controls keep it in national lanes. The Scenario Stress Test tracks which of the four is gaining momentum.

Open Season for Risk

R1 and R2 end with savers, and money moves wherever returns are highest.
National lanes · the state carries

Fortress Finance

Controls keep money at home, and the state protects it there.
National lanes · savers carry

Locked In

Controls keep money at home, and savers carry the losses they cannot move away from.
Who carries losses: the state → savers

Read this way, the scenarios stop being four stories and become four states of the same system, each with early indicators already on the map.

6. How the map stays live

A map drawn once goes stale as fast as the plans it informs. Inside a continuous intelligence cycle, every format updates it:

FormatWhat it does to the map
Weekly Signal ScanAttaches new evidence to existing links, and proposes new links or factors when something does not fit.
Monthly Change TrackerScores each loop as strengthening, stable or weakening, which is the status shown in section 2.
Quarterly Decision IntelligenceRedraws the map, re-weights the loops and tests the leverage points against the scenarios.
Action TriggersFire when a loop changes state: for example, fund outflows after a fall in AI shares would mark loop R1 (forced sales feed falling prices) turning.

7. How an organisation would use it

The value of a System Map comes as much from arguing over the arrows as from the finished picture. This draft is a starting point:

Challenge sessionOne facilitated session of about 90 minutes in which investment, treasury, payments, risk and strategy leads test every arrow, add what the evidence missed and agree the leverage points. Their corrections become part of the map.
Team lensesThe same structure, shaded for each team: the loops are shared, but investment, treasury and payments see different arrows as their own.
Other questionsA new fund, a funding plan or a payments partnership can be mapped the same way, so leaders see one method across their decisions.
