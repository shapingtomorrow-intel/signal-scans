In brief

Two linked pictures, built from public sources. The first shows six outside shifts and which parts of FXCM’s business, now being sold to Trade Nation and AvaTrade, each one reaches: through clients’ positions, or through the liquidity providers, banks and payment routes the business depends on. The second opens those counterparties and routes and tests seven assumptions against the latest evidence.

An illustrative example for discussion, built only from public sources dated up to 6 November 2026, as a companion to the Decision Intelligence briefing Spotting Geopolitical Shocks Before They Reach the Price. It is not a view of the finances of FXCM, AvaTrade or Trade Nation, or of any counterparty’s credit. Which liquidity providers, prime brokers and banks the firms use is not public, so the web shows kinds of counterparty, not named ones. A colour shows what public evidence suggests about a shift or an assumption, not a rating of a business.

The transfer is the shift that touches most of this web at once. Hedging, prime credit, client money and payment routes all have to be re-established or moved under new owners within weeks, while the outside shifts in Part 1 keep running. That is why the last assumption is mixed rather than green: nothing in the public record says the move will go wrong, but the public record does not say how it will be done.

3. What a running programme would add

  • Every colour re-tested each month against new evidence, with a small triangle showing which way it moved since the last edition.
  • Named counterparties, privately. With the firm’s own list of liquidity providers, prime brokers, banks and payment providers, each kind of player on the left becomes the real names, and each assumption is tested for them.
  • Thresholds agreed with risk and treasury. When an assumption turns red, or crosses a threshold, it goes on the agenda as an Action Trigger, with an owner and a date, tied to the event playbook in the briefing.

How this example was built

  • Built on 25 public sources, each with a dated page: 11 from regulators, central banks, standard setters and the companies’ own disclosures, 1 from research, 12 from quality journalism and trade press, and 1 from a market participant’s own account, used only as corroboration.
  • Traced all 24 figures in the text to the exact wording of a source.
  • Every quote is checked against the live page before this page is published; two paywalled pages were read from their open text only.
  • Businesses and counterparties are never coloured. Colour sits on the lines and on the assumptions, so the page shows what the evidence says, not a judgement of any business.
