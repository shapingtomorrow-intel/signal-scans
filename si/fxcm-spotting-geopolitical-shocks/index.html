Why This Matters Now

A geopolitical shock rarely hits prices first. In 2026 the war in the Gulf showed up first in insurers’ decisions and in ship counts, new rules on chips appeared as draft lists before they took effect, and sanctions reached banks outside Russia after public warnings.

Supervisors in Europe, the UK and Japan now measure geopolitical risk directly and test banks against it. Markets, meanwhile, bounced back quickly from the first shock, so a broker that reacts to every headline pays for it in lost clients and wider prices.

The one thing to take into the next meeting: sort every development by the route it would take to the business, through shipping and energy, trade rules or payments, and agree in advance what each early sign sets off for margins, liquidity and the banks the firm depends on.

Geopolitical shocks leave signs before they reach the price. In 2026 Gulf tanker traffic fell from 138 transits to fewer than eight within days of insurers withdrawing cover (Australian Naval Institute, Aug 2026), before Brent rose 75% by early April (Oesterreichische Nationalbank, in German, Aug 2026). China published a draft list of 23 AI technologies to control before any took effect (China’s Ministry of Commerce, in Chinese, Oct 2026), and the US warned banks worldwide about Banque Misr UAE as it moved against it (US Treasury, Aug 2026).

Some of this is settled: 91% of firms in the Bank of England’s survey cite geopolitical risk (Bank of England, Sep 2026). Three things are not. Will the next shocks come from sea lanes or from rules and payments? Will chip makers’ weight in indices, 41.5% of Taiwan’s for TSMC alone (Reuters, via The Star, Jun 2026), keep turning export rules into index events? Will payments split into blocs as China’s payment system reaches banks in 192 countries and regions (International Finance News, in Chinese, Sep 2026)?

Looking ahead to 2030, Gulf pipelines that bypass the Strait of Hormuz could eventually carry as much as 11.5mn barrels a day (Gulf Times, Aug 2026), Asian chipmakers’ profits are projected to grow at +70% a year to 2029 (Allianz Research, Jun 2026), and more central banks plan to cut their dollar holdings than raise them over the next 10 years (OMFIF, the central bank forum, Jul 2026). None of this is certain, and each is a place where readers can still choose their position: section 9 sets out the options.

Where this analysis could be wrong

The weakest assumption is that early signs come soon enough to act on. In 2026 they did, but the market effect also faded fast: share prices rebounded to or above pre-conflict levels (European Securities and Markets Authority, Sep 2026). If shocks get shorter and sharper, early warning protects margin and liquidity over a weekend and little more.

The decisions that cannot be deferred this cycle

  1. Sort developments by channel. Name the products, banks and currencies each would hit.
  2. Act on early signs, not prices. Set triggers per channel and the action each sets off.
  3. Test the banks and payment routes. Find the event that would remove one.
  4. Write the event playbook. Agree margins, spreads and client messages before the shock.

Each is developed below, with a decision posture, in the four Strategic Implications, and with alternatives in the Options.

Immediate

Weight: high · Who decides: Governments on sanctions and export rules; insurers and shipowners on sea lanes; banks and liquidity providers on who will still deal · Room to act: Wide on what the firm watches and what each sign sets off; narrow on the shocks themselves

For a retail FX and CFD broker a geopolitical shock is several events at once: a weekend gap in oil or a safe-haven currency, client accounts hitting margin together, a liquidity provider stepping back, a payment route removed by sanctions. In 2026 each shock travelled through a repeatable channel and left signs before it reached the price, shown here through FXCM’s three example signals: chip export controls, maritime disruption and financial sanctions.

Early indicators

  • Weak signal War probabilities are becoming a product. Verisk’s new war index would have shown a 66% probability of war in Iran six weeks before it began, in a backtest (Insurance Journal, on Bloomberg, Jun 2026). If such scores reach risk models, margins will move on the chance of conflict.
  • Weak signal China’s controls are moving from minerals to know-how. Magnet exports ran 20% above last year (Shanghai Metals Market, Oct 2026) while the new draft targets AI models and computing clusters (China’s Ministry of Commerce, in Chinese, Oct 2026), so the next shock may be a rule on knowledge, with no cargo stopped.

Outlook to 2030

Markets in July priced oil above $75 until mid-2027 (Oesterreichische Nationalbank, in German, Aug 2026), and Gulf pipelines that bypass Hormuz could carry as much as 11.5mn barrels a day, with a Saudi expansion tipped for 2030 or 2031 (Gulf Times, Aug 2026). Allianz Research projects a +70% annual rise in Asian chipmakers’ profits through 2026-2029 (Allianz Research, Jun 2026), deepening index concentration, and more central banks plan to cut than raise dollar holdings over the next 10 years (OMFIF, the central bank forum, Jul 2026). On this briefing’s reading, by 2030 more of the shocks that matter to a broker arrive as rules and payment bans than as closed sea lanes.

What it means: build early warning for rules and payment routes as carefully as for oil and shipping, because those channels are growing.

Counter-argument

Markets absorb most geopolitical shocks quickly. Share prices rebounded to or above pre-conflict levels, although the regulator that reports it reads the rebound as optimism masking vulnerabilities, not resilience (European Securities and Markets Authority, Sep 2026); an index built from 19,543 news messages finds the link from geopolitical news to prices weak at daily frequency (arXiv preprint, Jun 2026), and China’s magnet exports hit a 2026 record despite its controls (Shanghai Metals Market, Oct 2026). The test of this theme is therefore whether early signs protect margin, liquidity and payment routes, not whether they call prices.

System Map

The theme’s three channels run through four loops. Insurers bring ships back (B1): cover withdrawn closes a strait, and high premiums later draw ships back. Controls and workarounds race (R3): export rules push buyers to reroute, and rerouting brings tighter rules. Sanctions follow the payments (R2): bans push payments into longer chains and non-dollar routes, whose banks become the next targets. All three reach the firm through thin liquidity widens gaps (R1), where price gaps make liquidity providers step back and wider gaps follow.

Strategic Implications

Implication 1: Classify every geopolitical development by the channel it travels through and the books it would hit

In 2026 one conflict reached markets through insurers (Australian Naval Institute, Aug 2026), oil (Oesterreichische Nationalbank, in German, Aug 2026) and the Swiss franc (swissinfo.ch, Sep 2026), while chip rules (China’s Ministry of Commerce, in Chinese, Oct 2026) and bans on banks in third countries (Mayer Brown, Jul 2026) opened two more routes. The European Central Bank already sorts geopolitical risk into defence, trade and capital (European Central Bank, May 2026). A register naming the products, counterparties and currencies each channel touches would show at once which books a development would hit.

Action: The risk function to build a channel register, mapped to products, counterparties and settlement currencies, by 31 March 2027.

Decide Draws on Theme 1. On the System Map: leverage point 1, where developments enter the map from outside.

Implication 2: Watch each channel’s earliest signs and tie trigger levels to actions agreed in advance

The first signs in 2026 were not prices: insurers withdrew cover before transits collapsed (Australian Naval Institute, Aug 2026), the price effect of lost Hormuz traffic built over months (Bank for International Settlements, Sep 2026), and cargo was held at ports before record exports (Shanghai Metals Market, Oct 2026). Headline news indices add little at daily frequency (arXiv preprint, Jun 2026), so each channel needs its own sign and an agreed action: a margin change, a position limit, a product pause or a client message.

Action: Dealing and risk to agree two or three early indicators per channel, each with a trigger level and the action it sets off, tested against the 2026 events, by 30 June 2027.

Decide Draws on Theme 1. On the System Map: leverage point 2, insurers’ war-risk cover, the first step in loop B1 (insurers bring ships back).

Implication 3: Stress-test liquidity providers, banks and payment routes against a sanctions or chokepoint shock

The European Central Bank asked banks to design a geopolitical scenario that would cut their core capital ratio by 300 basis points, and found liquidity responses muted (European Central Bank Banking Supervision, Jul 2026). Sanctioned banks keep access only through more fragile chains (National Bureau of Economic Research, Jul 2026). A reverse stress test names the event that would remove one of the firm’s few liquidity providers or banks before it happens.

Action: Treasury to run a reverse stress test in the first half of 2027, with a tested fallback for each liquidity provider, bank and settlement currency.

Prepare Draws on Theme 1. On the System Map: leverage point 3, liquidity providers’ and banks’ capacity, where loops R1 and R2 reach the firm.

Implication 4: Agree in advance how margins, spreads and client messages change when a trigger fires

Brent closed 5% or more higher on 18 days in the 2026 shock (Oesterreichische Nationalbank, in German, Aug 2026), and the European Securities and Markets Authority warns that optimism masks vulnerabilities and raises the risk of an abrupt correction (European Securities and Markets Authority, Sep 2026). Decisions agreed beforehand are faster, and easier to explain to clients and supervisors, than decisions made in the hour a market gaps.

Action: Dealing, risk and compliance to agree an event playbook of margin, spread and product steps and client messages for each trigger, and rehearse it by the end of March 2027.

Prepare Draws on Theme 1. On the System Map: leverage point 4, client margin calls and losses, where every loop reaches clients.

Early indicators: New export-control lists each quarter; chip makers’ index weights rise; Gulf traffic normal.

Blocs and Blockades

↗ Shift from todayThe yuan’s share of China’s cross-border financing, 62% in June 2026 (International Finance News, in Chinese, Sep 2026), keeps rising while a major strait stays shut.

In 2030 the world trades through two money systems and still fights over sea lanes. A price gap and a lost settlement route can arrive in the same week, when liquidity is thinnest. It is the costliest world for a leveraged broker.

Early indicators: Strait closures recur; more Gulf oil sold for yuan; banks banned by one bloc or the other.

Two Payment Systems

↗ Shift from todayThe EU power to ban crypto dealings with a whole third country, so far unused (Mayer Brown, Jul 2026), is used, and sanctions follow money into new routes.

In 2030 the sea lanes are calm but money is split. Each sanctions package removes a route through banks, payment firms or crypto platforms in third countries, so the risk sits with the broker’s banks, liquidity providers and settlement currencies.

Early indicators: China’s payment system grows; bans name more third-country banks; non-dollar funding squeezes recur.

Which future is gaining momentum: the Scenario Stress Test scores twelve leading indicators on these two axes. In November no future leads: the May reading sat in the corner of Blocs and Blockades, and the reading has since moved to the centre on where shocks travel and towards the dollar system on how money moves.

Potential Surprises to 2040

Emerging

War-probability scores become a standard input to margin and capital decisions

Window: 2030 to 2036 · Probability band: Low-Medium (20 to 40%)

Why it would surprise: Margin models rest on past price moves, not the chance of war.

A catastrophe modeller now sells war probabilities by country, which in a backtest would have shown 66% for Iran six weeks before the war (Insurance Journal, on Bloomberg, Jun 2026).

Signposts to watch:

  • By December 2027: a European or UK supervisor builds a stress scenario on a measured probability of war.
  • By June 2028: a major clearing house cites a geopolitical risk score when raising margins.

What it touches today: How the firm sets margins for event weekends.

Weak signal

Sanctions start to cut off a whole country’s crypto platforms, not just named firms

Window: 2029 to 2035 · Probability band: Low-Medium (20 to 40%)

Why it would surprise: Sanctions name firms and banks, not every platform in a country.

The EU’s 21st package bans 11 crypto-asset service providers and creates a still unused power to ban crypto dealings with a whole third country (Mayer Brown, Jul 2026).

Signposts to watch:

  • By December 2027: the EU first uses its power to ban crypto dealings with a third country.
  • By December 2028: the UK or the US adopts a country-wide measure against crypto platforms.

What it touches today: Which crypto products and payment partners the firm offers clients.

Weak signal

A shortage of yuan, not dollars, sets off a market funding squeeze outside China

Window: 2031 to 2038 · Probability band: Low (under 20%)

Why it would surprise: Every major funding squeeze so far has been a dollar shortage.

When Russian banks ran short of yuan in March 2026, intraday funding cost reached 59% (Bank of Russia, in Russian, Jun 2026).

Signposts to watch:

  • By June 2028: a central bank outside Russia reports a yuan funding squeeze.
  • By December 2029: China extends yuan swap lines or clearing banks to most Gulf oil exporters.

What it touches today: Which currencies the firm’s banks and liquidity providers settle in.

Each option is graded against all four futures and a wild card in the Scenario Stress Test: three hold in every future (build a channel register with early signs and triggers; run a reverse stress test and line up fallbacks; agree an event playbook tied to the triggers), five are bets, strong in some futures and weak in others, and one backfires in at least one future (change margins case by case).

Options: Spotting and sorting (risk and treasury)

Option A · Least change

Follow a headline risk index

Review a published risk index monthly.

Trade-off: Cheap, but such indices predict little and do not say which books a shock would hit.

⊞ Fits scenarios: Chokepoints Set the Price

Option B · Middle course

Build a channel register with early signs and triggers

Sort developments by channel and set triggers for each channel’s early signs.

Trade-off: Needs an owner and a few data feeds, but works whichever channel carries the next shock.

⊞ Fits scenarios: All four

Option C · Biggest shift

Build an in-house early-warning model

A model of ship counts, rule notices, bans and funding spreads that feeds limits directly.

Trade-off: Costly and easy to over-trust, but fastest where shocks are frequent.

⊞ Fits scenarios: Blocs and Blockades; Two Payment Systems

Options: Liquidity, banks and payments (treasury and dealing)

Option A · Least change

Keep the current counterparties

Review counterparties once a year, as now.

Trade-off: No new cost, but a sanctions package could remove one between reviews.

⊞ Fits scenarios: Chokepoints Set the Price

Option B · Middle course

Run a reverse stress test and line up fallbacks

Name the event that would remove each counterparty, and agree a fallback.

Trade-off: Costs time and some standby fees, but shows weak links before a shock does.

⊞ Fits scenarios: All four

Option C · Biggest shift

Spread across currency zones and settlement routes

Add counterparties in other jurisdictions and keep committed standby liquidity.

Trade-off: Expensive and adds screening work, but strongest if payments split.

⊞ Fits scenarios: Blocs and Blockades; Two Payment Systems

Options: Client exposure on event days (dealing and compliance)

Option A · Least change

Change margins case by case

Leave margin decisions to the dealing desk on the day.

Trade-off: Flexible, but slow in a gap and hard to explain to clients and supervisors.

⊞ Fits scenarios: None for long

Option B · Middle course

Agree an event playbook tied to the triggers

Set margin, spread and product steps and client messages for each trigger.

Trade-off: Commits the firm to rules it may want to bend, but keeps responses fast and fair.

⊞ Fits scenarios: All four

Option C · Biggest shift

Reshape the product set around the exposures

Cap or reprice the most concentrated index and commodity products.

Trade-off: May cost revenue in calm years, but shrinks what a single shock can reach.

⊞ Fits scenarios: The Rulebook Shock; Blocs and Blockades

What We Are Not Planning For

Payments

A collapse of the dollar-based payment system before 2030

The dollar still makes up 58% of foreign reserve holdings worldwide (Atlantic Council, Oct 2026), and outside heavily sanctioned economies the shift to the yuan remains minimal on average (National Bureau of Economic Research, Jul 2026).

↺ Reinstated if the dollar’s share of global reserves falls below half, or the largest Gulf oil exporters sell most of their oil for other currencies.

Markets

A lasting fall in world share prices caused by the 2026 conflict

Share prices fell sharply at the outbreak but rebounded to or above pre-conflict levels (European Securities and Markets Authority, Sep 2026), and Japanese share prices rose despite continuing uncertainty in the Middle East (Bank of Japan, in Japanese, Oct 2026).

↺ Reinstated if major share indices stay below their pre-conflict levels for a full quarter while Hormuz stays closed.

Supply

A full stop to rare-earth supplies from China

China’s rare-earth magnet exports reached a 2026 monthly record of 6,165.12 tonnes in September (Shanghai Metals Market, Oct 2026), and global exports in July were back above July 2024 levels (Silverado Policy Accelerator, Aug 2026).

↺ Reinstated if China’s monthly magnet exports fall below half their 2026 level for two months running.
