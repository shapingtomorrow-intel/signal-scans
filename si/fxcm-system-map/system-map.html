How Sea Lanes, Trade Rules and Payment Routes Reach a Broker's Books

6 November 2026 · Companion to the Decision Intelligence briefing of 6 November 2026, Spotting Geopolitical Shocks Before They Reach the Price · Horizon: to 2030 Built from the briefing’s 30 verified sources. Links drafted by analysis.

The Decision Intelligence briefing says what is changing in how geopolitical shocks reach an FX and CFD broker. This System Map shows why the pieces move together: which changes feed each other, which push back, and where the firm’s choices change how much of a shock reaches its clients and capital.

Read it by following the arrows. A + means the two move in the same direction; a − means they move in opposite directions. Where the arrows close into a circle, they form a loop: an R loop reinforces itself, so change feeds on change, and a B loop balances, pushing back against change. Hover over a loop label or an arrow for its explanation and evidence.

2. The four loops this quarter

Reinforcing loops run in both directions: the same circle that compounds decline would compound recovery. The status on each card is this cycle’s reading, and is what the monthly Change Tracker updates. Priming means the parts are in place but the circle has not yet closed at scale. Turning means every step in the loop has current evidence, so it is running now. Hover over a status for its meaning.

Priming

B1 Insurers bring ships back

Insurers withdraw war-risk cover, ship transits fall, oil and freight prices rise, and high premiums then draw cover and ships back, even while attacks continue. It is priming: premiums for tankers run at 6% to 10% of hull value and only 13 vessels crossed Hormuz on 4 October (Insurance Business, Oct 2026), though premiums have halved before while attacks went on (Australian Naval Institute, Aug 2026).

Watch: war-risk quotes and daily ship counts through Hormuz and the Red Sea, and whether pipelines that bypass the strait add capacity.

Priming

R1 Thin liquidity widens gaps

Price gaps make liquidity providers and banks step back, and thinner liquidity makes the next gap wider. It is priming: Brent closed 5% or more higher on 18 days in the 2026 shock (Oesterreichische Nationalbank, in German, Aug 2026), and the European Central Bank found banks’ liquidity responses to geopolitical scenarios muted (European Central Bank Banking Supervision, Jul 2026).

Watch: spreads quoted by liquidity providers on event weekends, and the next round of supervisory stress tests.

Turning

R2 Sanctions follow the payments

Bans push payments into longer chains through third countries and non-dollar routes, and banks on those routes then become the next targets. It is turning: EU bans now reach banks in Kyrgyzstan, Mongolia, India and Nigeria (Mayer Brown, Jul 2026), and the US moved against Banque Misr UAE for processing payments for Iran-linked firms (US Treasury, Aug 2026).

Watch: the next EU sanctions package, US actions against banks outside the target countries, and growth in China’s payment system.

Turning

R3 Controls and workarounds race

Export controls push buyers to reroute through third countries, and the rerouting brings tighter controls. It is turning: after Malaysia’s AI chip shipments rose 366%, the US closed the route through subsidiaries abroad (Tom’s Hardware, Jun 2026), and China drafted controls on 23 AI technologies (China’s Ministry of Commerce, in Chinese, Oct 2026).

Watch: China’s final list of controlled technologies, US licence rules, and chip import surges in third countries.

What the map shows that the briefing alone cannot
  1. Two loops are already turning, and both run through rules rather than ships. Loops R2 and R3 have current evidence at every step: bans now reach banks in third countries, and new chip rules followed the rerouting of chip purchases. The sea-lane loop is still waiting to close.
  2. Every channel reaches the firm in the same two places. Whatever the shock, it arrives through liquidity providers and banks or through client margin. That is why the counterparty test and the event playbook protect against all three channels at once.
  3. The earliest signs sit outside the firm, but are public. Insurers’ decisions, draft rules and warnings to banks are all published before the effect lands, so watching them costs little compared with the gap they can avoid.

6. How the map stays live

A map drawn once goes stale as fast as the strategy it informs. Inside a continuous intelligence cycle, every format updates it:

FormatWhat it does to the map
Weekly Signal ScanAttaches new evidence to existing links, and proposes new links or factors when something does not fit.
Monthly Change TrackerScores each loop as strengthening, stable or weakening, which is the status shown in section 2.
Quarterly Decision IntelligenceRedraws the map, re-weights the loops and tests the leverage points against the scenarios.
Action TriggersFire when a loop changes state: for example, a first EU ban on crypto dealings with a whole third country would mark loop R2 (sanctions follow the payments) as accelerating.

7. How a broker would use it

Product lensesThe same structure, shaded by product line: the loops are shared, but their strength differs between oil, index and currency CFDs.
