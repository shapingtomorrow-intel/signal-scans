Why This Matters Now

Companies are buying more technology advice, but Gartner’s consulting business is getting smaller, and whoever leads its high-tech practice in London must close that gap.

Three forces are at work. Tech firms are spending heavily on AI while cutting staff, so they want proof it pays back. Rivals, and now the AI companies themselves, are moving towards fees tied to results. And new cloud and software rules in the UK and Europe give tech firms reasons to reopen their biggest contracts.

The one thing to take into the next meeting: lead with work only Gartner’s data makes possible, contract, cost and sourcing advice backed by benchmarks, and test a first deal paid on results. Watch whether consulting revenue grows again in the next two quarterly results.

Executive Synthesis

Which plans on your desk this week assume high-tech clients will keep buying advice by the day?

The evidence in this briefing says they are starting to pay for results instead.

Demand for technology advice is rising, but Gartner’s share of it is not. In the UK, computer programming and consultancy made the largest contribution of any industry to growth in the three months to August 2026 (Office for National Statistics, October 2026), and consultancies expect revenue to grow 6% in 2026 (Management Consultancies Association, via Consultancy.uk, July 2026). Gartner’s consulting revenue was $142 million in the second quarter, against $156 million a year earlier (Gartner’s second-quarter 2026 earnings call, August 2026), and contract value for research sold to technology leaders grew just 1.1% (Gartner’s second-quarter 2026 results, August 2026).

Three things are unsettled: whether the fall is a dip or a verdict on the model, with the backlog up about 9% (Gartner’s second-quarter 2026 earnings call, August 2026); whether tech clients keep paying for advice when AI answers general questions, a fear that, with slow growth and a guidance cut, helped take nearly 49% off Gartner’s share price in the first half of 2026 (The Motley Fool, July 2026); and whether AI companies take the hands-on work, as OpenAI’s new services venture intends (Reuters on Yahoo Finance, May 2026).

Looking ahead to 2030, Technology Business Research forecasts consulting and systems integration at $739 billion, with AI lowering contract values (Technology Business Research, May 2026); Boston Consulting Group projects that technology services could grow 6% to 8% a year through 2030 if providers win new AI-driven work (Boston Consulting Group, February 2026); and Gartner’s own forecast is that 35% of single-purpose software-as-a-service tools will be replaced by AI agents by then (Deloitte’s 2026 technology, media and telecoms predictions, November 2025). None of this is certain, and each is a place where readers can still choose their position: section 8 sets out the options.

Where this analysis could be wrong

This briefing assumes the consulting decline comes from what Gartner sells and how it prices, not from weak demand. If consulting revenue returns to year-on-year growth in the next two quarterly results on today’s pricing, the case for changing the offer weakens sharply.

The decisions that cannot be deferred this cycle

  1. Lead the high-tech portfolio with contract, cost and sourcing work. Licensing and cloud deadlines fall in early 2027, and Gartner’s benchmarks are hard to copy.
  2. Agree a first engagement priced on measured results. Rivals already charge this way, and buyers prefer it.
  3. Build offers for tech firms that AI is forcing to restructure. Software, telecoms and service firms need a neutral view of what works.

Each is developed below, with a decision posture, in the four Strategic Implications, and with alternatives in the Options.

1. Demand for technology advice is growing while Gartner's consulting revenue shrinks

Immediate

Weight: high · Who decides: Gartner Consulting leadership on offers and pricing; high-tech clients’ CIOs, CEOs and buyers on what they pay for · Room to act: Wide on what the practice sells and how it charges; narrow on Gartner’s research model and share price

The incoming Managing Partner inherits a consulting line that is shrinking inside a market that is growing. Gartner holds what most rivals lack, a store of prices, contract terms and peer benchmarks, but has been selling consultants’ time. On the available evidence, the practice grows again only if it sells that data as verdicts and prices some of it on results.

Who is growing in technology advice: latest reported change in revenue, 2026

Latest reported year-on-year change in each consulting line; periods differ and are named on each bar. The UK figure is member firms’ forecast for 2026. Sources: Management Consultancies Association via Consultancy.uk, CPA Practice Advisor, Gartner, Forrester.

  • Gartner’s consulting revenue is falling while its order book grows. Consulting revenue fell 13% to $133.6 million in the last quarter of 2025 (Investing.com, May 2026), yet by June the backlog stood at $214 million and contract optimisation work had brought in $148 million over twelve months (Gartner’s second-quarter 2026 earnings call, August 2026).
  • The research base that gives consulting its way into clients is thinning. Contract value for research sold to technology leaders grew 1.1%, against 3.3% for other business functions (Gartner’s second-quarter 2026 results, August 2026); client firms in that channel fell from 14,149 to 11,487 (Richard Stiennon, citing Lusher Advisory, July 2026); and the shares lost 47.9% in 2025 amid US federal cost cuts, slowing growth and IT budgets moving to AI (The Motley Fool, citing S&P Global Market Intelligence, January 2026).
  • High-tech clients are spending on AI, cutting people and asking for proof. Only 37% of companies attribute any operating profit to AI (McKinsey, The state of AI in 2026, August 2026), buyers expect IT spending in 2026 to stay broadly flat (Bain & Company survey, April 2026), and AI was cited in 116,175 US job cut announcements from January to August (Challenger, Gray & Christmas, September 2026).
  • Research-led rivals are shrinking, and fees are moving to results. Forrester’s consulting revenue fell 15% (Forrester’s second-quarter 2026 earnings call, July 2026) and Deloitte’s technology and transformation revenue grew only 2.5% (CPA Practice Advisor on Deloitte’s results, September 2026), while about a quarter of McKinsey’s fees now come from outcome-based pricing (TheStreet, May 2026).

Weak signals to watch

  • Weak signal Gartner’s AI forecasts now move share prices in the industry it advises. Its October forecast that more than 80% of AI cloud providers will stop operating independently by 2030, alongside a warning that more than $3.5 trillion of AI infrastructure spending could face write-downs (Bloomberg, October 2026), was followed the same day by CoreWeave shares falling by up to 17% (Stocktwits on Yahoo Finance, October 2026). That authority can strain relationships with AI firms the practice courts. It gains weight if a technology client publicly disputes a Gartner forecast.

Outlook to 2030

Forecasts point to a larger market that buys fewer consultant hours. Technology Business Research expects consulting and systems integration to grow from $556 billion in 2025 to $739 billion in 2030 while AI lowers contract values (Technology Business Research, May 2026), and Gartner forecasts that at least 40% of enterprise software-as-a-service spending will move to usage-, agent- or outcome-based pricing by 2030 (Deloitte’s 2026 technology, media and telecoms predictions, November 2025).

What it means: Plan for growth in value, not days sold: fees tied to results and data clients cannot get elsewhere.

Counter-argument

The fall may be a dip, not a verdict on the model: the backlog is rising and management kept its full-year consulting outlook (Gartner’s second-quarter 2026 earnings call, August 2026). Buyers say they want more help: 81% intend to rely more on consultants (Source Global Research, via Consultancy.uk, December 2025). If that holds, the practice recovers with the market, and changing the offer adds risk without need.

Strategic Implications

Implication 1: Lead the high-tech portfolio with contract, cost and sourcing work backed by Gartner’s benchmarks

The Managing Partner should make contract, cost and sourcing advice the front door to high-tech accounts, because Gartner’s price data is hardest to copy there and clients face fixed dates. The UK competition regulator is examining whether customers can choose which cloud runs their software licences (Competition and Markets Authority, October 2026), and EU rules remove cloud switching charges from 12 January 2027 (European Commission, Data Act explained, December 2025).

Action: within the first 90 days, the Managing Partner names ten high-tech accounts with a major renewal before the end of 2027 and puts a benchmark-led sourcing proposal to each.

Decide Draws on Theme 1

Implication 2: Put a share of engagements on fees tied to measured results

UK leadership and the Managing Partner should agree a few engagements paid partly on verified results, starting with contract and cost work measured against a benchmark. In customer service and outsourcing, more than 70% of buyers prefer outcome-linked models, yet around 60% of providers of AI-enabled services still contract on time and materials or fixed prices (Boston Consulting Group, February 2026).

Action: by March 2027, the Managing Partner and UK leadership sign off two pilot engagements with part of the fee tied to benchmarked savings.

Prepare Draws on Theme 1

Implication 3: Build offers for software, telecoms and service firms that AI is forcing to restructure

The practice should prepare offers for software firms changing how they charge, telecoms operators cutting cost, and service providers whose delivery teams AI is shrinking. BT expects to employ no more than 80,000 people by 2030 (Light Reading, July 2026), and Bain warns service providers that wait could see 30% or more of their revenue erode (Bain & Company survey, April 2026).

Action: by June 2027, the Managing Partner launches offers for software pricing, telecoms cost and service-provider delivery, each with a reference client.

Prepare Draws on Theme 1

Implication 4: Track the AI companies’ move into advice and the health of the research base each quarter

Two things would change the plan: AI companies building advisory teams, and a weakening research base. Anthropic’s venture with Blackstone and Goldman Sachs has about $1.5 billion of committed capital, Fortune reports citing the Wall Street Journal, and competes directly with the largest consultancies (Fortune, May 2026). If such ventures hire senior advisers, the practice needs partners or a sharper niche.

Action: from Gartner’s third-quarter 2026 results, the Managing Partner logs technology-channel contract value and AI companies’ services moves each quarter, with a review if contract value growth turns negative.

Paid on Proof

↗ Shift from todayOutcome-based fees, about a quarter of McKinsey’s today (TheStreet, May 2026), become the normal way tech firms pay independent technology advisers.

By 2030 high-tech clients still choose independent advisers, but pay for verified results. Firms that own benchmark data win; advisers selling opinion by the day lose share. Gartner grows if it priced early on results and kept its research base.

Early indicators: outcome clauses in advisory tenders; rivals disclose results-based fee shares.

The AI Companies’ Mandate

↗ Shift from todayAI companies’ services arms, starting from OpenAI’s team of around 150 engineers (Reuters on Yahoo Finance, May 2026), become the main advisers on tech firms’ AI and sourcing choices.

By 2030 tech firms buy AI strategy and delivery from the model makers, bundled with the software. Independent advisers mainly check that work. Gartner keeps value as a neutral judge, but the practice shrinks to assurance and procurement.

Early indicators: AI companies win multi-year outcome contracts; they buy consultancies.

Less of the Same

↗ Shift from todayThe number of firms buying Gartner research for their technology teams, 11,487 in early 2026 (Richard Stiennon, citing Lusher Advisory, July 2026), keeps falling while advice is still bought by the day.

By 2030 the market looks much as today, with fewer people. Advisers keep charging for time, but AI does most of the analysis, so engagements are shorter. Gartner’s consulting line is stable at best, growing only by taking share from weaker rivals.

Early indicators: junior hiring cut but day rates kept; Gartner’s consulting revenue flat.

Engineers on Tap

↗ Shift from todayThe roughly 60% of AI-enabled service providers still charging for time and materials or fixed prices (Boston Consulting Group, February 2026) keep that model, but sell AI engineers rather than advisers.

By 2030 tech firms buy capacity rather than advice: engineers from AI companies and large integrators, billed by the month. Strategy is decided in-house with AI tools. Gartner’s best position is benchmarking those contracts, a narrow but profitable niche.

Early indicators: services priced by the engineer-month; advisory tenders shrink to price checks.

Potential Surprises to 2040

Emerging

AI model companies, not consultancies, become tech firms’ main technology advisers

Window: 2031 to 2038 · Probability band: Low-Medium (20 to 40%)

Why it would surprise: Advisers assume AI companies sell tools and leave judgement to consultants.

Reporting Anthropic’s new services venture, Fortune notes that for every dollar companies spend on software, they spend six on services (Fortune, May 2026).

Signposts to watch:

  • By December 2027: an AI company’s services arm wins a sourcing or strategy mandate from a large listed tech firm.
  • By June 2028: an AI company’s services venture buys a firm with an IT advisory or benchmarking practice.

What it touches today: whether to partner with AI companies’ services arms or compete with them.

Weak signal

Research subscriptions give way to pay-per-question access and the client base shrinks to a core

Window: 2031 to 2037 · Probability band: Low-Medium (20 to 40%)

Why it would surprise: Gartner’s model assumes clients pay a yearly subscription per seat.

Forrester’s AI product brought in about $10 million of bookings in nine months (Forrester’s second-quarter 2026 earnings call, July 2026), and one industry analyst argues Gartner needs a cheaper offer (Richard Stiennon, July 2026).

Signposts to watch:

  • By December 2027: Gartner or Forrester reports revenue from a product priced by use rather than by seat.
  • By December 2028: Gartner reports a usage-priced product line separately.

What it touches today: consulting proposals that assume every client holds research seats.

Weak signal

Technology advisers are licensed, like auditors, to sign off high-risk AI systems

Window: 2032 to 2039 · Probability band: Low (under 20%)

Why it would surprise: AI checks are planned as firms’ own compliance work, not a licensed adviser’s sign-off.

EU rules for stand-alone high-risk AI systems now start on 2 December 2027 (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, July 2026), creating demand for someone independent to check compliance.

Signposts to watch:

  • By December 2027: an EU or UK regulator consults on approved third parties to assess AI systems already in use.
  • By June 2029: a large tech firm names an independent AI assessor in its annual report.

What it touches today: whether to build an AI assurance offer.

What We Are Not Planning For

Portfolio change

Gartner selling or closing its consulting business

Management kept its full-year consulting outlook in August and the backlog is rising (Gartner’s second-quarter 2026 earnings call, August 2026). A sale or closure within two years is unlikely enough to leave out.

↺ Reinstated if Gartner announces a review of its consulting business or reports it as a business it intends to sell.

Market downturn

A collapse in UK demand for technology advice

Official figures show computer programming and consultancy growing (Office for National Statistics, October 2026), and UK consultancies expect growth in 2026 and 2027 (Management Consultancies Association, via Consultancy.uk, July 2026). The risk is a shift in what clients buy, not how much.

↺ Reinstated if the Office for National Statistics reports falling output in computer programming and consultancy for two quarters in a row.

Leadership succession

A new chief executive overturning the consulting strategy

Gene Hall has led Gartner since August 2004, and the board calls succession planning one of its most critical functions (Gartner’s 2026 proxy statement, April 2026). A successor would likely act first on research and sales, not UK consulting.

↺ Reinstated if a successor is named with a stated plan to restructure Consulting.
