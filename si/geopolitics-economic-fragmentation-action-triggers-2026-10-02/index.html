Supply Is Now Rationed, Even Between Friends

Over this cycle, the strategic constraint changed from what supply costs to whether governments release it at all. Nine weeks ago the central question was whether the machinery of fragmentation would last: the tariff wall had outlived its court defeat and the dollar had blinked. It lasted, and it turned inward. Washington used a trade law for the first time against Canada, Europe and Britain rationed steel by volume, China answered each Western listing with its own, US research funding began to follow trade blacklists, and diesel exporters, possibly soon including the United States, started metering supply. Allies answered by building ties with each other. What follows is the warnings this portfolio carried early, the assumptions that changed, and the decisions now ready to take.

This winter's diesel will go to buyers with contracts, reserves or a government deal, not to the highest bidder.

Initial signal

We identified that the states that refine and export diesel had turned their exports into a metered flow, set by bans, stock-linked permits and levies reset every few weeks, and that the United States could be the next to join them.

Now evident
  • Washington: the President said on 22 September he may support a ban or cap on diesel exports, and the Energy Secretary has asked refiners to cut exports voluntarily.
  • Weekly exports: US distillate exports fell 17.5% to 1.331 million barrels a day in the week to 18 September, the lowest since March.
  • Other exporters: Russia's general fuel export ban runs to 31 January 2027, and India has set its diesel export levy twelve times since 27 March.

US tariffs will keep sorting allies by what they legislate, and partners that refuse to align will route trade around Washington.

Initial signal

We flagged that the rebuilt US tariff wall sets rates by partner conduct, with lower tiers for partners that adopt US rules, and that it would reach Canada through a trade law no president had used before.

Assumptions that have shifted

Cycle-over-cycle assumption changes, in decreasing order of strategic weight.

Shift 1 of 5 Replaced

Coercion is aimed at rivals, and allies stay in one bloc

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle held that "coercion has become plumbing", aimed at Russia through sanctions and running both ways between Washington and Beijing through mineral controls, with the US and its partners on the same side.

This cycle reads

Coercion fell inside the bloc. Washington used a trade law for the first time to put 50% duties on Canadian goods, and its closest partners answered by building trade, defence and energy ties with each other.

Forward thesis

Partners will keep their US ties but build alternatives beside them. The tripwire is whether Canada and the US resume talks, and whether the EU-Australia agreement is signed in late 2026 or early 2027.

Shift 2 of 5 Replaced

A supply shock can be met by qualifying another supplier

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle asked planners to "model a shock in which both a Chinese export control and a US content rule bite the same bill of materials", and to answer it with qualified alternative suppliers and stock.

This cycle reads

Governments now allocate supply directly, so a second supplier can be switched off as well. Diesel exporters meter what they release, and Western governments are contracting a separate, higher price for minerals from outside China.

  • Diesel: Russia, China, India, Thailand and Kazakhstan restrict exports, and Washington is weighing a ban or cap.
  • Minerals: floor-price offtakes and G7 work on price floors put a policy price on supply from outside China.
Forward thesis

Plan supply around which governments will release it, not only who can make it. The tripwire is a formal US export ban or cap, or China cutting its fourth-quarter export permissions.

Shift 3 of 5 Split

Conditional market access can be bought with pledges or lower tariffs

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle found that "access became managed, negotiated and conditional", bought with purchase and investment pledges and with policy changes that earn a lower tariff tier.

This cycle reads

The reading splits. Tariffs still work by price, with lower rates for partners that adopt US rules. Elsewhere access is now rationed by volume or granted by list, and no payment buys it.

  • Steel: the EU cut tariff-free steel quotas by 47% and the UK by 51% on 1 July.
  • Research: from FY2027, US National Science Foundation funds cannot support collaboration with anyone on twelve restricted-party lists.
  • Carbon: Britain's list of 16 recognised carbon-pricing schemes binds from 1 January 2027, while the EU tests each consignment.
Forward thesis

Allocation rules spread when they are copied. Watch for a fourth jurisdiction adopting the steel quota design, or an allied research funder naming the US lists.

Shift 4 of 5 Weakened

The dollar's dip shows fragmentation reaching the monetary system

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle read the dip in the dollar's reserve share, and gold passing Treasuries at market value, as "the summer's clearest sign that fragmentation has reached the monetary core".

This cycle reads

The dip has not become a trend. The dollar's share recovered to 57.13% in the first quarter, central-bank gold buying slowed to a steady pace, and BRICS payment links stayed at the discussion stage.

Forward thesis

Hold the reading unless the data turns. The tripwire is the IMF's second-quarter reserve figures, due around 1 October, showing the share back below 57%.

Shift 5 of 5 Holding

The 2026 controls will outlast court rulings and trade truces

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle named its pattern as "the machinery holds": tariffs, sanctions and export controls hardened into standing arrangements that a ruling or a truce would not unwind.

This cycle reads

It survived a direct test this cycle. The US Court of International Trade left the metals tariffs standing, the EU's next sanctions package is the largest yet, and China extended its pause on rare-earth controls rather than repealing them.

Forward thesis

The next tests are dated. Watch the 12 October sanctions vote and the 10 January 2027 end of the US-China truce.

Decide Action 1 Signal Scan Decision Intelligence

Put a 90-day non-US diesel supply plan in place before the 3 November elections.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because the President has said he may support a ban or cap on diesel exports, because weekly US exports have already fallen to their lowest since March, and because the other large exporters are metering their own supply, so there is no open market to fall back on.

Primary decision ownerHeads of fuel procurement and logistics, Europe and Latin AmericaDecision windowImmediateby 31 October 2026
Why this now matters

A ban would land in the middle of winter demand. Buyers who secured term supply beforehand will be served first, and the rest will bid for what exporting governments release.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: sign term supply with refiners outside the US and confirm how many days of stock each operation holds.
  • Immediate: decide which operations get fuel first if US cargoes stop.
  • Follow-on: write the exporting government's policy into supply contracts as a named risk, rather than meeting it later as force majeure.
Review conditions
  • EscalateIf Washington imposes a formal ban or cap on diesel exports, by executive order or rule, at any level, move to emergency allocation across operations.
  • Stand downIf weekly US diesel exports recover above their March level and the administration rules out a ban after 3 November, release the term supply as it expires.
  • Stand downIf the Strait of Hormuz reopens and two or more of the metering exporters lift their curbs in the same quarter, return to spot purchasing.

Extend the July control map to cover quotas, lists and recognition decisions, including those made by allies.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because Europe and Britain now ration steel by volume, because China answers Western listings with its own within a day, because US research funding follows trade blacklists from FY2027, and because the map built in July covered controls between rivals, not allocation decisions by friends.

Primary decision ownerChief Risk Officer and General CounselDecision windowNext quarterby 31 March 2027
Why this now matters

An allocation cannot be bought down like a tariff. Exposure is managed through eligibility, paperwork and timing, and few plans yet give that work an owner.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: screen suppliers, customers and research partners against the US restricted-party lists and China's export control list before the EU vote on 12 October.
  • Next quarter: confirm steel quota allocations and melt-and-pour evidence for EU and UK shipments.
  • Next quarter: check whether the carbon schemes behind your exports are on Britain's recognised list before it binds on 1 January 2027.
  • Follow-on: name one owner for each allocation decision and re-run the map after every sanctions package.
Review conditions
  • Raise to DecideIf a fourth jurisdiction adopts a volume quota with a fixed reference period, or an allied research funder adopts the US restricted-party lists by reference, give the map a budget line and a named owner this quarter.
  • Scale backIf the 22nd EU package is adopted and MOFCOM has not listed a European entity within 30 days, drop the China-response screen from the map.
  • Scale backIf a WTO or bilateral panel rules the EU or UK steel quotas unlawful, remove quota allocation from the map and watch the compensation route instead.

Track the consents a host government must give, not only the risk of seizure, in resource and political-risk exposures.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because a US state lender approved $414.2 million for a uranium mine in Niger that cannot be drawn until the government extends the permit, signs a direct agreement and approves repayments, and an export route opens.

Primary decision ownerHead of project finance and political-risk underwritingDecision windowMonitor onlypermit and export-route decisions over the next 12 months
Why this now matters

Where the host government holds most of the conditions, the risk is delay at each step. That is priced differently from a single seizure, and most country-risk models price only the seizure.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: list every government act a Sahel or similar exposure depends on, by date and counterparty.
  • Follow-on: model delay as well as loss when pricing political risk on these exposures.
Review conditions
  • Raise to PrepareIf a second state lender publishes consent-linked disbursement conditions for a Sahel mine before 30 June 2027, map every sovereign act in each exposure and price delay.
  • Stand downIf the Benin-Niger border reopens with uranium named in the transit terms and the Niger loan records a first drawdown, return to the standard country-risk model.

The body of work, in one sentence

Over this cycle, the binding constraint moved from what supply costs to whether governments release it. The July question was whether the machinery would last; it did, and it turned on allies and on essentials, with tariffs tiered by partner conduct, steel rationed by volume, research funding sorted by trade blacklist and diesel metered by exporting governments. The load-bearing variable for the next cycle is permission: which list, which quota, which club and whose consent a cargo, a partner or a loan now needs. The first dates that test it are already set: 12 October, 3 November and 10 January.
