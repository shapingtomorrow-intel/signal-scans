Exposure now depends on politics, not tariff lines.

Pattern narrative

Political risk widens at the coreEvolving Risk

Soldiers attacked positions in Niamey on August 29, and Critical Threats expects the deterioration behind it to worsen (Critical Threats, September 2026). Maplecroft finds protest activity up over two years (Verisk Maplecroft, December 2025). Tankers were attacked in Hormuz (Maritime News, September 2026), yet Al Jazeera frames Iran's leverage as weakening as flows recover (Al Jazeera, September 2026). Brazil votes on 4 October, with a runoff on 25 October (The Rio Times, October 2026). Sanctions are being codified: the Act authorises tariffs on top purchasers of Russian energy (Baker McKenzie, September 2026), after the Council's 21st package (Council of the European Union, July 2026); Washington had preferred threats to allied sanctions (UK House of Commons Library, August 2026).

Statecraft reaches allies and spaceTransformation Driver

Five proclamations impose outright import bans on specified Canadian alcoholic beverages, dairy products and motorcycles from September 29 (Troutman Pepper Locke, September 2026). The White House calls them a reply to Canada's discrimination and retaliation (The White House, September 2026). Canada's counter-tariffs of 15, 25 and 50 per cent match the US rate on the same goods (Department of Finance Canada, August 2026). In space, CSIS argues a supporting role has become a centerpiece in how nations compete and fight (Center for Strategic and International Studies, May 2026). On this evidence statecraft now reaches allies and orbit.

Leverage is bargained as well as exercisedEmerging Opportunity

Brookings argues the truce is likely to hold because the US depends on China for rare earths and China on foreign semiconductors (Brookings Institution, September 2026). CSIS finds the Trump-Xi summit light on substance (Center for Strategic and International Studies, September 2026), and BISI judges the rollover buys time without altering the structural dependency (Bloomsbury Intelligence and Security Institute, September 2026). Producers bargain too: African governments demand larger equity stakes and sometimes seize assets (Miningmx, September 2026), while Indonesia postponed royalties and export levies (ANTARA, May 2026).

Signals gaining momentum

  1. Sanctions and economic statecraft escalation. A first tariff under the Act would show authority becoming use.
  2. North American trade bloc fractures. Whether the bans draw a further Canadian round.
  3. Coups, civil unrest and regime instability. A second attempt would suggest contagion.

Collision 1slows down −2

Mutual chokepoints restrain how far secondary tariffs can reach China

Mineral chokepoints → Sanctions

The Act lets the President tariff buyers of Russian energy and enablers of evasion. Brookings argues the truce holds because each side depends on the other's chokepoints, so using the new power against China would test that balance.

Watch: Whether the first tariff under the Act spares China, and whether the next truce rollover carries new conditions.

Collision 2speeds up +2

Executive discretion ties sanctions enforcement to the next election

Election reversal → Sanctions

The Act codifies sanctions while keeping waiver authority with the President. With Congress constrained, J.P. Morgan expects reliance on executive powers, so enforcement follows the administration's priorities as much as the statute.

Watch: The first waiver or tariff decision under the Act, and any change in control of Congress after the midterms.

Collision 3speeds up +2

Bargained mineral terms slow the escape from Chinese processing

Resource nationalism → Mineral chokepoints

Producers extract equity and revenue from miners, and miners need predictable terms to build capacity outside China. BISI finds new capacity cannot yet remove Chinese leverage, so higher host-country demands lengthen that period.

Watch: New equity or royalty demands on rare earth projects, and announced processing capacity outside China.

Implications

Three dates organise the next cycle: Brazil's first round on 4 October, the import bans from September 29, and the first tariff under the Act. Canada exposure warrants scenario work now, since matched counter-tariffs make escalation mechanical. Price political risk by country and election date, not tariff line. For miners and defence suppliers, rare earth licensing and host-government equity demands are the decision points.
