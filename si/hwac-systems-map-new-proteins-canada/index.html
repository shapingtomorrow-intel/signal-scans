The Decision Intelligence briefing says what is changing. This map shows why the pieces move together: which changes feed each other, which push back, and where Humane World’s effort changes how the whole system behaves rather than one part of it.

Read it by following the arrows. A + means the two move in the same direction; a − means they move in opposite directions. Where the arrows close into a circle, they form a loop: an R loop amplifies itself, and a B loop pushes back against growth.

2. The four loops this quarter

Reinforcing loops run in both directions: the same circle that compounds success compounds decline. The status on each card is this cycle’s reading, and is what the monthly Change Tracker would update.

Running backwards

R1 Approval flywheel

Clear rules, investor confidence, capital, scale, products on shelves and public familiarity should feed one another. Today the circle is turning the wrong way: capital raised fell from $139 million to $73.9 million in a year, the number of companies fell from 155 to 140, Believer Meats closed despite holding US approval, and no company has filed in Canada.

Watch: the first cultivated meat application to Health Canada; a rise in capital raised in the next industry report.

Primed in Canada

R2 Label vacuum

An unsettled label invites opponents to define the product first, which erodes trust and slows the flywheel. Canada has already watched this loop run on a neighbouring technology: gene-edited pork was approved without a label, and “Verified No Cloning or Gene Editing” packs followed across the country.

Watch: whether Ottawa names a new home for food labelling standards after the closure of the body that ran the voluntary standard.

Strong abroad, dormant here

B1 Backlash

This is the system’s reply to growth: visible products alarm incumbent sectors, whose lobbying produces bans and name limits that cut access and deter investors. Seven US states, Italy and Hungary have bans, the EU has limited meat names, and a US appeals court has upheld a state ban. No Canadian province has moved.

Watch: a farm organisation in a livestock province calling for a ban; Texas renewing its ban before September 2027.

Turning

R3 Fermentation flywheel

Approvals attract investment, investment builds capacity, capacity puts animal-free proteins into everyday foods, and each approval makes the next routine. Fermentation raised $357 million in 2025, regulators in Canada, Australia, New Zealand and China are clearing products through ordinary review, and Nova Scotia and ADM are adding capacity.

Watch: new Health Canada decisions on fermented proteins; a large Canadian food maker using them.

What the map shows that the briefing alone cannot
  1. One backlash answers both technologies. The farm lobby that could ban cultivated meat is the same force that will fight dairy and egg names. Leading with fermentation does not avoid the backlash loop; it meets it on dairy and egg ground. That is the mechanism behind the briefing’s warning that a fermentation-first strategy “draws Humane World into dairy and egg politics”.
  2. Order matters more than any single move. Both B1 and R2 are triggered by visibility. Labels and tastings settled before the first product arrives shape the system; the same work done afterwards has to fight the system’s own loops. This is why timing, not effort, is the scarce resource to 2028.
  3. The cheapest push on the stalled flywheel may come from the turning one. The precedent link from everyday fermentation use to clear Canadian rules means each fermentation approval makes cultivated meat review more routine, at a fraction of the cost of trying to restart capital directly.

3. Five leverage points

  1. 1

    Settle a plain label before the first application

    Cuts the label vacuum (R2) at its root, so the first product arrives with a name Humane World helped shape rather than one opponents wrote.

6. How the map stays live

A map drawn once goes stale as fast as a five-year plan. Inside a continuous intelligence cycle, every format updates it:

FormatWhat it does to the map
Weekly Signal ScanAttaches new evidence to existing links, and proposes new links or nodes when something does not fit.
Monthly Change TrackerScores each loop as strengthening, stable or weakening, which is the status shown in section 2.
Quarterly Decision IntelligenceRedraws the map, re-weights the loops and tests the leverage points against the scenarios.
Action TriggersFire when a loop changes state: for example, the first provincial bill would mark B1 becoming active in Canada.
