Where a Small Funder’s Grants Can Still Change Dementia Research

Produced using Shaping Tomorrow’s six-stage process for converting external change into decision-ready intelligence.
Scanned and filtered by AI. Stress-tested and judged by humans.

In brief

Two linked pictures, built from public sources and the public research registries. The first shows seven outside shifts, where they reach the research landscape, and whether they help or hurt four options for where an early-career funder’s grants go. The second tests six working assumptions behind a small funder against the evidence, each with one signpost whose status would move week by week.

An illustrative example for discussion, built only from public sources dated up to 9 November 2026 and from the dementia research landscape we pulled from NIH RePORTER, ClinicalTrials.gov and OpenAlex on 27 October 2026. A registry record is a commitment (a grant awarded, a trial registered), not a result. The six assumptions in Part 2 are our reading of public material, not the Trust’s own; in a programme they would be agreed with you. A colour shows what the evidence suggests, not a judgement of any organisation.

A solid line works through money and people. A dashed line works through rules, eligibility and access: who can apply, who can stay, and who decides.

3. What a running programme would add

  • Signposts moved every week by the scans, each move dated and linked to its evidence, so the Trust acts only when a signpost triggers.
  • The registries refreshed every cycle: grants, trials and papers re-pulled, so the landscape shows where the money and people are moving before anyone writes about it.
  • Your assumptions and your data, not ours. The six here are read from public material. In a programme they are agreed with the Trust, and its own application and scholar data can be tested alongside the public record, under agreement.

How this example was built

  • Built on 16 sources: 8 Tier 1 (a parliamentary research service and the public research registries, 7 of them registry pulls or records), 3 from universities, 3 from quality journalism, and 2 from charities and foundations writing about their own work, used only as corroboration.
  • Traced all 24 figures in the text to a source’s own wording or to the dated registry pull.
  • Every quote from a published page is checked against the live page before this page is published. Registry figures come from our dated pull of 27 October 2026 and are counted as commitments, not results.
  • Organisations are never coloured. Colour sits on the lines, the assumptions and the parts of the landscape, so the page shows what the evidence says, not a judgement of anyone.
