Which Parts of Denmark’s AI Stack It Can Own, Share or Rent

Produced using Shaping Tomorrow’s six-stage process for converting external change into decision-ready intelligence.
Scanned and filtered by AI. Stress-tested and judged by humans.

In brief

Two linked pictures, built from public sources. The first shows seven outside shifts, which layers of Denmark’s AI stack each one reaches, and whether it helps or hurts Denmark’s three options for each layer: own it, share it with partners, or rent it from US firms on their terms. The second tests seven working assumptions behind Danish defence AI against the latest evidence.

An illustrative example for discussion, built only from public sources dated up to 9 November 2026, as a companion to the Decision Intelligence briefing Keeping Denmark’s AI Options Open When the Hyperscalers Hold the Keys. The seven assumptions in Part 2 are our reading of public statements, not assumptions Danish Defence has set out; in a programme they would be agreed with you. A colour shows what public evidence suggests, not a judgement of any institution or supplier.

A dashed line works through law, rules and the terms of access: who can see the data, who can switch a service off, and who decides.

Denmark starts from a high dependence: about 69% of Danish companies bought cloud services in 2025, in a market US companies dominate, and the government has set aside DKK 80 million for 2026 to 2029 to strengthen digital sovereignty (Danish Ministry of Digital Affairs, in Danish, February 2026).

(Bruegel, June 2026; Centre for European Reform, July 2026; Danish Agency for Digital Government, in Danish, January 2026)Price premiums in the first sovereign-cloud tenders under the new EU rules.

Norway’s parliament has asked for a continuous map of the country’s digital dependencies, with the first report due by the end of 2026. (Bundeswehr, in German, June 2025; SVT, in Swedish, April 2026; Norwegian parliament, in Norwegian, April 2026; Dutch government, August 2026; AFCEA SIGNAL, June 2026)Norway’s first dependency report and white paper, and whether the Nordic countries agree any joint arrangement.
Buying European first keeps the best technology in reachEvidence is mixedThe government’s line is to prioritise Danish and European purchases, but the Defence Minister says there may still be non-European suppliers, including Palantir, because Denmark cannot cut itself off from better technology. Invoices show Danish Defence has been a Palantir customer. (Altinget, in Danish, June 2026; Altinget, in Danish, May 2026; Council of the EU, September 2026; Ingeniøren, in Danish, October 2026)Who builds the Danish alternative to Palantir, and whether the next large defence software purchase is European.

The two red assumptions are the ones to argue about first. If sovereignty costs more than a modest premium, Denmark has to choose which layers are worth owning. If neighbours are not converging, that choice cannot be copied from anyone else. Both lead to the decision the briefing sets out: which layers to own, share or rent, and on what conditions.

3. What a running programme would add

  • Every colour re-tested each month against new evidence, with a small triangle showing which way it moved since the last edition.
  • Your assumptions, not ours. The seven here are read from public statements. In a programme they are agreed with Danish Defence, the Centre for Military Studies and the Danish Technological Institute in a working session, where disagreeing about a colour is the point.
  • Thresholds agreed with the ecosystem. When an assumption turns red, or crosses a threshold, it goes on the agenda as an Action Trigger, with an owner and a date.

How this example was built

  • Built on 34 public sources, each with a dated page, 16 of them in languages other than English: 19 from governments, parliaments, regulators and intergovernmental bodies, 3 from research, 7 from quality journalism and trade press, and 5 from companies, law firms and industry bodies, used only as corroboration.
  • Traced all 13 figures in the text to the exact wording of a source.
  • Every quote is checked against the live page before this page is published; one paywalled page was read from its open headline and standfirst only.
  • Institutions and suppliers are never coloured. Colour sits on the lines and on the assumptions, so the page shows what the evidence says, not a judgement of anyone.
