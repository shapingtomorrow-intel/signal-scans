Which Moves Hold for the Royal Southern in Every Future to 2040

27 October 2026 · Built on the Decision Intelligence briefing Who Will Sail From the Hamble in 2040? and its System Map · Horizons: 2040 Uses the briefing’s scenarios and options, with evidence from the briefing; no other sources. Grades and indicator readings are drafted by analysis.
Scanned and filtered by AI. Stress-tested and judged by humans.

Scenarios are not forecasts. Their job is to show how differently the next fifteen years could turn out, so that the club’s plans can be built to survive any of them, and then to be watched, so that the plans change when the evidence does.

This page does both. It takes the nine options in the briefing and runs each one through four plausible futures to 2040, plus a wild card. A move that works in every future is robust and can be committed to now. A move that works well in some futures and poorly in others is a bet, held ready with a clear trigger. A move that makes things worse in any future is fragile and should not be relied on. It then scores leading indicators on the matrix’s two axes, shows how that reading has moved since May, and turns the reading into investment priorities.

1. Four futures to 2040, and a wild card to 2040

The two axes are the system’s two biggest uncertainties, the same two the System Map identifies: whether members sail their own boats or boats the club supplies, which is whether supplied boats take over from dearer boats and fewer owners, and whether the Solent stays open or becomes restricted, which is how far protection and rising seas reach.

Open water · members’ own boats

The Owners' Club

Members still own most of the boats and the Solent is used much as today. The club thrives on berths and moorings, but its members are older and fewer.

By 2040: A smaller, older club, with young sailors racing elsewhere.

Open water · supplied boats

The Sailing Hub

Most new members sail boats the club owns or leases, on open water, and pay for use rather than berths. The membership is younger and larger.

By 2040: A younger club, where owners are one group among several.

Restricted water · members’ own boats

The Guarded Harbour

Members own their boats, but anchorages are closed to protect the seabed and some moorings are lost to higher water. Owners pay more for less water.

By 2040: Only the most committed owners stay.

Restricted water · supplied boats

The Managed Fleet

The club supplies most boats, many electric, and they sail in water managed like a park, with marked zones, permits and cleaner rivers.

By 2040: A club whose licence to operate rests on careful stewardship of the river.

Wild card

Moorings by permit

Moorings and anchorages across the Solent are allocated by environmental permit rather than by waiting list and fee, with conditions on where and how boats may lie.

Built from the briefing’s Potential Surprise to 2040: moorings and anchorages are allocated by environmental permit (2031 to 2038, Low-Medium).

What the stress test shows
  1. There is a robust core, and it matches the briefing’s four Implications. A membership with access to club boats, supplied-boat league racing, and mapping exposure while planning grid capacity and battery safety hold up in all four futures.
  2. Each area has one fragile move, and it is the move that changes least. Staying a club for owners, racing only around members’ boats and waiting for rules each backfire in at least one future: where younger sailors join clubs that supply boats, or where restrictions arrive with no plan.
  3. Each bet turns on one axis. Becoming a sailing hub and owning a one-design fleet pay off where the boats are supplied; investing ahead in charging and buildings pays off where the water is restricted and managed. The signposts in section 6 settle them.

3. What it means for the plan

Commit now

Robust: works in every future.

  • A membership with access to club boats (Implication 1).
  • Supplied-boat league racing for young sailors (Implication 2).
  • An exposure map, a grid assessment and battery rules for the marina (Implications 3 and 4).

Prepare, and act on a trigger

Bets: strong in some futures, weak in others.

  • Becoming a sailing hub: release it if the reading moves firmly towards supplied boats.
  • A club one-design fleet and year-round league: release it on the same reading.
  • Charging on every pontoon and adapted buildings: release it when Ofgem allows marinas a return on charging, or the Hamble's coastal plans name the club’s frontage.

Do not rely on

Fragile: backfires in at least one future.

  • Staying a club for boat owners alone.
  • Running racing only around members’ boats.
  • Waiting for rules and demand before planning.

4. Which future is gaining momentum, and how it has moved since May

Each future is a corner of the matrix, so the question “which future is gaining momentum” becomes two questions the evidence can answer: are members sailing their own boats or supplied ones, and is the Solent staying open or becoming restricted? Twelve leading indicators answer them, six on each axis. Scored at each reading, they give a reading on each axis and a point on the matrix; the trail of points shows where the evidence is heading.

The pale band (the no-lead zone) marks readings within 15 points of an axis, where no future can lead. Hover over a point for its reading.

Use of the Solent: restricted, at every reading so far−68 in October. The first prohibited anchorage, seabed restoration on the Hamble, tightening water rules and coastal plans that put moorings in question all point the same way; only a better spill record pulled against them, and it has since turned.
Boats sailed: back to the centre0 in October. Supplied-boat leagues and running costs push towards supplied boats; full coastal berths, growing owned one-design classes and rising home ownership among young adults push back.
Leading future: none yetNo future has held a corner for two readings. The July reading sat in The Managed Fleet; October’s sits between The Guarded Harbour and The Managed Fleet.
What would settle itA Solent club launching a supplied-boat membership, or league entries rising, moves the reading towards supplied boats; strong sales of entry-level boats move it back towards owners. A prohibited anchorage on the Hamble would settle the vertical axis.
How the reading is scored
  1. Each indicator is marked Moving (+1), Pending (+½), Not yet (0) or Against (−1), on the evidence published by the date of the reading.
  2. Each carries a weight: 3 if it would settle its axis on its own, 2 if it is strong evidence, 1 if it supports.
  3. An axis reading is the weighted total for that axis, scaled from −100 (members’ own boats, or restricted water) to +100 (supplied boats, or open water).
  4. A future leads when the reading sits in its corner, at least 15 points from both axes, for two readings running.
  5. A shift is a move of 10 points or more on either axis between readings. It is flagged and reviewed in the next quarterly briefing.
  6. A signpost reached (section 6) overrides the score and triggers its decision directly.

7. How the monitoring runs over time

Every weekSignal Scans attach new evidence to each indicator and signpost, including weak signals that do not yet fit any future.
Every monthThe Change Tracker rescores each indicator, the two axis readings are recalculated, and the trail on the matrix gains a point.
Every quarterThe Decision Intelligence briefing reviews any flagged shift, and redraws the scenarios, the System Map and this stress test.
When a signpost is reachedAction Triggers fire, releasing the bet prepared for it and naming the decision it changes.

8. Running it with your own plan

The options above are the briefing’s. The real value comes from replacing them with the club’s own plans and asking the same question of each: does it hold in every future?

BeforeThe club shares its membership, sailing, marina and capital plans for the next few years; we grade each against the scenarios and the evidence base.
Session, 2 hours20 minutes in the four futures; 40 minutes in which each group lives in one future and answers “it is 2040: what do we wish we had done in 2027?”; 40 minutes testing the plans against the matrix; 20 minutes agreeing the robust core and the trigger for each bet.
AfterThe agreed matrix, indicators and signposts join the programme, and the monitoring cycle above keeps them current.
