Compliance Spending Will Need to Follow Where Regulators Can Actually Enforce

The rules are largely settled and their deadlines keep slipping, but neither now decides exposure. What matters is whether a country has a funded regulator ready to act, so a compliance budget spread evenly across Europe is spent in the wrong places.

Early warnings

Signals the portfolio surfaced before consensus formed, in the order they hardened.

Lead time 3 weeks ahead Signal Scan Decision Intelligence

We said the binding constraint had moved from the rules to the machinery that enforces them, three weeks before a briefing was built on exactly that.

Initial signal

The 20 July scan argued that the EU's real constraint was no longer rule-writing but the certification machinery underneath it: notified bodies and harmonised standards that barely exist, making market access rather than legislation the operative lever across 2026 to 2028.

Now evident
  • Germany created a national AI supervisor on 29 July, giving the Bundesnetzagentur a coordination role, a service desk and funded sandboxes, nine days after the scan ran.
  • Article 50 transparency and general-purpose AI enforcement went live on 2 August, with the Commission's own guidance confirming that enforcement falls mainly to national market-surveillance authorities rather than to the AI Office.
  • The European Parliament's research service had recorded only eight of twenty-seven member states notifying a single point of contact as of March 2026, which is the same gap the scan named from the certification side.
  • The 13 August Decision Intelligence made this its whole thesis rather than one theme among several.

Compliance effort weighted by obligation rather than by the enforcement capacity of each jurisdiction is now mispriced, and the mispricing is largest in the markets that look quietest.

Lead time 11 weeks ahead Signal Scan Decision Intelligence

We named managed federalism as the mechanism of US AI regulation before the courtroom fight made it visible.

Initial signal

The 25 May scan read the federal push against state AI law not as preemption in the ordinary sense but as managed federalism: pressure applied through funding conditions and litigation rather than through a statute that displaces state rules.

Now evident
  • The Department of Justice did not merely file a brief in the challenge to the Colorado AI Act; it intervened and filed its own complaint, arguing extraterritorial burden on interstate commerce rather than federal displacement.
  • Congress declined preemption twice, in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and again in the defence authorisation, leaving the executive order without the authority it would need.
  • States legislated anyway: Colorado rewrote rather than repealed its statute in May, and 109 state AI laws were enacted by 1 July 2026.
  • The surviving federal vehicle narrowed from a decade-long moratorium to a three-year preemption inside a discussion draft.

The exposure is judicial rather than legislative: a court accepting the interstate-commerce theory would reach far more state law at once than any bill currently drafted.

Lead time 13 weeks ahead Signal Scan Change Tracker Decision Intelligence

We said the disclosure retreat was a reshaping rather than a repeal, three months before California withdrew its own finished rule.

Initial signal

The 15 May scan argued that sustainability disclosure was not disappearing under political pressure but being re-cut, and that the durable obligations would outlast the headline retreat.

Now evident
  • CARB approved its implementing regulation on 26 February, withdrew it from final review on 24 June, and re-proposed modified text on 27 July with comments closing 11 August.
  • The first Scope 1 and 2 reporting deadline moved from 10 August to 10 November 2026, and remains contingent on approval by the Office of Administrative Law.
  • The assurance escalation did not move at all, because limited assurance from 2026 and reasonable assurance from 2030 sit in statute rather than in the rulemaking.
  • The EU ran the same pattern from the other direction: the revised ESRS were published on 3 July but sit in Parliament and Council scrutiny, applying from FY2027.

Prepare

The planning anchor is assurance-grade data readiness, not the filing date, because the date moves by administrative act and the assurance standard does not.

Lead time 5 weeks ahead Signal Scan Decision Intelligence

We flagged the liability handover while the rulebook was still being read as softening.

Initial signal

The 6 July scan argued that strict liability for software and AI was arriving in Europe precisely as the regulatory rulebook was being simplified, opening a second enforcement channel that owes nothing to supervisory capacity.

Now evident
  • The revised Product Liability Directive must be transposed by 9 December 2026 and brings software expressly within the definition of a product.
  • Article 9 lets a claimant compel disclosure on a showing of mere plausibility, and Article 10 lets a court presume defectiveness where that disclosure is not met.
  • The AI Liability Directive was withdrawn in October 2025, leaving claims outside the strict-liability perimeter to twenty-seven national fault regimes.
  • Only Hungary had fully transposed by late June 2026, so commencement will be staggered and forum-shopping is the near-term consequence.

Assumptions that have shifted

Cycle-over-cycle assumption changes, in decreasing order of strategic weight.

Shift 1 of 4 Replaced

“Regulatory volatility is the binding constraint”

Prior cycle held

The June briefing organised the cycle around postponement and calendar splits, treating the instability of deadlines as the thing that had to be planned against.

This cycle reads

The deadlines did keep moving, but the decisive variable turned out to be whether an enforcer exists and is funded. Germany built one; most member states have not; the obligations are identical either way.

Forward thesis

The fragile variable next cycle is whether the designation gap closes through 2027, or hardens into a permanent asymmetry in which the same rule carries different odds in different markets.

Shift 2 of 4 Broken

“The first California reporting deadline is 10 August 2026”

Prior cycle held

The June briefing tracked 10 August 2026 as a fixed first reporting date for Scope 1 and 2 emissions under SB 253, and planned the disclosure theme around it.

This cycle reads

CARB withdrew a regulation its own board had already approved, re-proposed it in modified form, and moved the date to 10 November 2026. The replacement is still not final.

Forward thesis

The fragile variable next cycle is whether the November date survives approval by the Office of Administrative Law, or moves a second time and confirms that the calendar is no longer a planning instrument at all.

Shift 3 of 4 Weakened

“The AI Act rollback reverses the Brussels effect”

Prior cycle held

The 1 June scan read the Digital Omnibus and the high-risk postponements as a genuine retreat, with Europe's regulatory gravity weakening as a consequence.

This cycle reads

Two later scans read the same events differently: rules stayed on the books through simplification, and detection tightened rather than loosened. The 2 August obligations landed on schedule and a large member state built a supervisor rather than standing down.

Forward thesis

The fragile variable next cycle is whether simplification continues to mean re-cutting rather than repeal, in which case the retreat thesis was a misreading of tempo rather than of direction.

Shift 4 of 4 Holding

“The Annex III postponement is a calendar split, not a free deferral”

Prior cycle held

The June briefing insisted that deferring high-risk obligations did not defer the whole regime, and that the 2 August 2026 dates would hold.

This cycle reads

They held exactly. The Omnibus was approved on 16 August terms that preserved them, and a second and quieter clock appeared underneath: content-marking duties deferred to 2 December 2026 for systems already on the market.

Forward thesis

The fragile variable next cycle is whether the December marking date is treated as a real deadline, or is missed by organisations that read August as the end of the sequence rather than the middle of it.

Decide Action 1 Signal Scan Decision Intelligence

Weight the FY27 compliance plan by enforcement capacity per jurisdiction, not by obligation alone.

Trigger condition

This recommendation is activated because the 20 July scan named enforcement machinery as the binding constraint, and the 13 August briefing then evidenced it: a funded German supervisor from 29 July, Article 50 enforcement devolved to national authorities from 2 August, and only eight of twenty-seven single points of contact notified.

Primary decision ownerChief Compliance Officer Decision windowImmediateBy 30 September 2026
Why this now matters

An undifferentiated European programme over-invests in states with no supervisor and under-invests in the two or three that will actually act, and the difference is now large enough to show up in a budget.

Who gains

The advantage accrues to organisations that treat a funded supervisor as an opportunity for early engagement rather than only as a risk to be managed.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: produce a jurisdiction map naming, for each material market, whether an AI Act competent authority is designated, funded and operational.
  • Next quarter: re-weight compliance headcount and external spend against that map before the FY27 plan is signed off.
  • Follow-on: refresh the map each cycle, since designation is administrative and can change quickly once political attention arrives.
Review conditions

Revisit if enforcement actions cluster at EU level rather than in the states with functioning authorities, which would mean the AI Office carries the practical weight and the geography argument weakens.

Prepare Action 2 Signal Scan Change Tracker Decision Intelligence

Re-anchor the sustainability reporting programme on assurance readiness rather than on the filing date.

Trigger condition

This recommendation is activated because the 15 May scan called the disclosure reshaping, and California then demonstrated it: an approved rule withdrawn on 24 June, the first deadline moved to 10 November 2026, and the statutory assurance ramp untouched throughout.

Primary decision ownerChief Financial Officer and audit committee chair Decision windowNext quarterBefore the FY27 budget is fixed
Why this now matters

A programme built to a filing date has to be re-planned every time a regulator reopens a rulemaking; a programme built to limited assurance in 2026 and reasonable assurance by 2030 is indifferent to that churn and is the harder capability to acquire late.

Who gains

The advantage accrues to organisations already producing auditable Scope 1 and 2 data, who can absorb a moving deadline without re-planning.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: restate programme milestones as assurance milestones rather than filing milestones.
  • Next quarter: scope the five Scope 3 categories proposed for 2027 separately from the remaining ten, which are voluntary.
  • Follow-on: hold the assurance ramp to 2030 as the fixed point in the plan, since moving it requires legislation rather than a notice.
Review conditions

Revisit if the Office of Administrative Law rejects the modified Californian text, which would move the anchor again, or if the Ninth Circuit rules on SB 261 and resolves the second source of uncertainty.

Prepare Action 3 Signal Scan Decision Intelligence

Review AI technical documentation and retention on the assumption that its next reader is a claimant, before 9 December 2026.

Trigger condition

This recommendation is activated because the 6 July scan flagged the liability handover, and the 13 August briefing then set out the mechanism: transposition due 9 December 2026, disclosure compellable on a plausibility showing, and defectiveness presumable where disclosure is not met.

Primary decision ownerGeneral Counsel, jointly with the technology function Decision windowImmediateBy 30 November 2026
Why this now matters

Records created to satisfy a supervisor become the evidence file in a civil claim, and a gap that reads as untidy to a regulator can read as a presumption of defect to a court.

Who gains

The advantage accrues to organisations whose documentation discipline predates the deadline, since retrospective reconstruction is visible and slow.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: inventory AI systems by whether they were placed on the market before 2 August 2026, which also determines the December marking obligation.
  • Immediate: review retention, versioning and completeness of technical documentation and logs across EU-facing systems.
  • Next quarter: take a written position on the development-risk defence in each material jurisdiction, since it already diverges between member states.
Review conditions

Revisit if national transposition slips materially past December in your principal markets, which would delay exposure without removing it, or if early case law narrows the disclosure mechanism.

Trigger condition

This recommendation is activated because the 25 May scan named managed federalism as the mechanism, and the cycle then produced the evidence: a DOJ complaint on constitutional grounds, two congressional refusals, and 109 state AI laws enacted by 1 July.

Primary decision ownerGeneral Counsel Decision windowMonitor onlyStanding, reviewed quarterly
Why this now matters

The executive order asserts no preemptive authority of its own, so deferring multi-state compliance work in expectation of federal displacement is currently the expensive choice.

Who gains

The advantage accrues to organisations that build to the strictest state standard and treat any preemption as upside rather than as a plan assumption.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: add the Colorado litigation to the quarterly legal risk report with a standing trigger on any interstate-commerce ruling.
  • Next quarter: confirm readiness for Colorado's developer documentation obligations from 1 January 2027.
  • Follow-on: watch whether the three-year discussion draft receives committee action or acquires a Senate companion.
Review conditions

Revisit immediately if a court accepts the dormant Commerce Clause theory against a state AI statute, since that reasoning would reach most state AI law at once and invert the planning assumption.
