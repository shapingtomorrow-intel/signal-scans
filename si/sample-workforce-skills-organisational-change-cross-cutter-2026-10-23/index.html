Careers Stayed Inside. The Rules for AI Moved to the Bargaining Table.

Over this cycle, the question moved from whether organisations can absorb change to who sets its terms. Ten weeks ago the central question was whether employers could build careers inside once the job market stopped paying people to move. The market stayed slow, and part of the reason is fewer workers rather than fewer jobs: the monthly job growth needed to hold US unemployment steady is now estimated at fewer than 10,000. Meanwhile the terms were being written elsewhere. 108 collective agreements in European services already contain provisions on AI, ahead of an EU proposal expected in December, and accountability for AI agents is settling with IT while only 24% of leadership teams agree on who owns them. What follows is what we saw early, what we now read differently, and what is ready to decide.

Lead time 9 weeks aheadSignal Scan Change Tracker Decision Intelligence

Employers rehiring experienced staff could find too few people left in the pipeline by the mid-2030s.

Initial signal

We identified firms bidding up experienced staff after AI-driven cuts while young-worker employment fell in codified-knowledge occupations, and read it as buying a stock of judgement rather than restarting its production.

Now evident
  • United States: employment of 22 to 25 year olds in the most AI-exposed occupations is about 19% below less-exposed peers, up from 15% a year earlier.
  • France: permanent hiring of 21 to 29 year olds fell 28.3% in the occupations most exposed to AI between late 2022 and early 2026.
  • Internships: UK internship and placement hiring fell 8% while applications for internships rose 27%.
Last intake that yields a senior by the mid-2030sabout 2029
Decide

HR directors should set the 2027 intake on the experienced staff needed in 2030, not on this year's savings from AI.

Lead time 4 weeks aheadSignal Scan Decision Intelligence

Accountability for AI agents could settle with IT before HR has a say in how they are managed.

Initial signal

We identified enterprises giving AI agents managers and headcount ratios while making the chief information officer, not HR, contractually accountable for them.

Now evident
  • Ownership: only 24% of leadership teams are aligned on who should be accountable for AI agents, in a Gartner survey of 297 chief HR officers.
  • Visibility: 82% of firms cannot inventory the agents they already have.
  • Default: Gartner expects 80% of Global 500 firms to name their CIO or chief AI officer the 'Evidence Custodian' for AI by 2030.
Prepare

Chief executives should decide who owns agents before the IT tooling decides it for them.

Lead time 7 weeks aheadSignal Scan Change Tracker

Layoff announcements may stop showing AI's effect just as boards and regulators start asking for it.

Initial signal

We flagged that calling a layoff AI had become a claim others can test, through union clauses, EU rulemaking and investor scrutiny, and that employers would retreat into vaguer language.

Now evident
  • Attribution: AI fell to the fourth-most cited reason for announced US cuts, with 3,462 in August, the lowest since December 2025.
  • Volume: total announced cuts rose 58% in the same month.
Monitor

Strategy teams should stop reading AI attribution in layoff data as a measure of AI's effect on jobs.

Assumptions that have shifted

Cycle-over-cycle assumption changes, in decreasing order of strategic weight.

Shift 1 of 5 Split

The job market has closed, so progression must now happen inside organisations

Prior cycle held

August's synthesis concluded that "the exit has closed" and that progression now has to be delivered inside the firm.

This cycle reads

The market is slow rather than shut, and partly for a different reason. Switchers' pay growth was 4.7% against 3.4% for stayers in September, a wider gap than in June, and the slowdown reflects a shrinking labour force as well as weak demand. The inside route is also narrowing: 57% of employers made fewer lateral internal hires than a year earlier.

  • Lock-in migrates: where non-compete bans arrive, notice, garden leave and repayment clauses stay, and training-cost repayment already binds 9% to 16% of employees.
Forward thesis

Plan for low mobility in both directions, outside and inside. The tripwire is a second quarter of a widening switcher pay premium, in the fourth quarter of 2026.

Shift 2 of 5 Replaced

The limit on internal careers is how many people training can move in a year

Prior cycle held

August held that "the limit on movement has moved to how many people can be put through training in a year", and said to fund the throughput first.

This cycle reads

Volume matters less than what training buys. More than 40% of certificate-granting training sits in low-return topics, while the largest premium, above 8.5%, attaches to teamwork, leadership and project management. UK training has also shortened, from 7.8 days per trainee in 2011 to 5.7 in 2024.

Forward thesis

Grade training by the roles and pay it leads to, not by hours delivered. The tripwire is the first large employer to report training by topic and outcome.

Shift 3 of 5 Holding

The EU will not pass a dedicated law on algorithmic management before 2028

Prior cycle held

The August briefing recorded the call: "There will be no dedicated EU law on algorithmic management before 2028."

This cycle reads

It held, and the gap is being filled by bargaining. The Commission's leading option is guidance on existing data-protection and AI rules, employers back bargaining over law, and European unions decline the negotiation route while 18 member states fall below 80% bargaining coverage.

Forward thesis

Expect agreements, not statute, to set most AI terms to 2028. The tripwire is a binding article on algorithmic management in the Quality Jobs Act proposal, December 2026.

Shift 4 of 5 Holding

New duties bind before the national laws that enforce them exist

Prior cycle held

August graded workforce disclosure as weakened, because "the duty binds and the machinery does not".

This cycle reads

Still true, and the pattern has spread from pay to contracts and hours. Only four member states met the pay transparency deadline in full, the platform-work presumption falls due on 2 December with Germany still without a published draft, and Britain's guaranteed-hours duty runs off the rota rather than the contract.

Forward thesis

Prepare on the EU and UK dates and track national laws country by country. The tripwire is Commission infringement proceedings after the 2 December 2026 platform-work deadline.

Shift 5 of 5 Weakened

AI agents are joining HR's records as a second workforce

Prior cycle held

August warned that "agents reached the employment system of record ahead of any workforce plan" and that governance was running ahead of accounting.

This cycle reads

Agents are arriving, but accountability for them is going to technology teams rather than HR, and they are still not counted with staff. Accenture's latest annual report counts about 814,000 people and no agents.

Forward thesis

Treat agent ownership as undecided and contestable for the next year. The tripwire is a chief HR officer publicly named as co-owner of enterprise agent governance.

Actions triggered

Decide Action 1 Signal Scan Change Tracker Decision Intelligence

Redesign junior roles before setting the 2027 graduate and apprentice intake.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because the employment gap for young workers in AI-exposed US jobs widened from 15% to 19%, because permanent hiring of young people in France's most exposed occupations fell 28.3%, and because 87% of UK employers expect AI to change graduate roles rather than remove them, while 43% say AI has already altered entry-level roles that have not been formally redesigned.

Primary decision ownerChief HR officer, with business unit headsDecision windowNext quarter2027 intake set by 31 March 2027
Why this now matters

Hiring restarts in a quarter but a decade of experience does not, so this year's intake decides who can supervise AI-assisted work in the 2030s.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: list the junior tasks AI now does in each main entry route.
  • Next quarter: rebuild each route around supervised work that still builds experience, then set the 2027 intake.
  • Follow-on: protect internship and placement numbers, which are falling faster than graduate hiring.
Review conditions
  • Scale backIf the Stanford young-worker gap narrows across two quarterly updates, keep the redesign but hold intake at current levels.
  • Stand downIf junior hiring recovers at AI-adopting firms in 2027, return intake planning to demand alone.

Open talks on an AI framework with worker representatives before terms are set elsewhere.

Trigger condition

Primary decision ownerChief HR officer, with the general counselDecision windowImmediatescope agreed before the December 2026 proposal
Why this now matters

Terms designed in advance cost less than terms conceded in a dispute or written into law, and the agreements now being signed are the template the next ones copy.

Recommended actions

  • Next quarter: open talks with unions or works councils, or an employee forum where none exists, by 31 March 2027.

  • Scale backIf the German round settles without any job-security or technology terms, keep the framework but drop gain-sharing from the opening offer.

Prepare Action 3 Signal Scan Decision Intelligence

Move discretionary training spend to the topics that pay, and tie it to internal moves.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because more than 40% of certificate-granting training sits in low-return topics, because UK training averages 5.7 days per trainee, and because 57% of employers made fewer lateral internal hires than a year earlier.

Primary decision ownerChief HR officer, with the chief financial officerDecision windowNext planning cycle2027 training budgets
Why this now matters

With the job market slow, training that does not lead to a role leaves people stuck, and stuck people are the cost of a frozen market.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: split the training budget into mandated and discretionary spend, and record topics for each.
  • Next quarter: shift discretionary spend towards teamwork, leadership and project management, and attach each programme to a named internal role.
  • Follow-on: report internal fill rates alongside training spend to the board from the first quarter of 2027.
Review conditions
  • Raise to DecideIf internal fill rates fall again in the 2027 first-quarter report, make role-linked training a condition of the training budget.
  • Stand downIf a causal study shows compliance-heavy training raising wages, rebalance towards the current mix.

Prepare Action 4 Signal Scan Decision Intelligence

Put HR into agent governance now, with a register that names an owner for every agent.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because only 24% of leadership teams agree on who is accountable for AI agents, because 82% of firms cannot inventory the agents they have, and because the tooling that defines ownership is being built by IT and security teams.

Primary decision ownerChief executive, with the chief HR and chief information officersDecision windowNext planning cycleregister in place by 30 June 2027
Why this now matters

Whichever function builds the identity and accounting tools for agents is likely to keep the role, so the choice of owner is made by default unless it is made deliberately.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: ask each business unit to list the agents it runs and who checks their work.
  • Next quarter: set up a joint HR and IT register with a named human owner for every agent.
  • Follow-on: report the agent count alongside headcount in the 2027 workforce plan.
Review conditions
  • Raise to DecideIf a peer or regulator publishes agent counts alongside headcount, adopt the same disclosure in the next annual report.
  • Scale backIf agent use stays at pilot level, as with the 11% of German companies using or considering AI that already run agents, keep the register to production agents only.

The body of work, in one sentence

Over this cycle, the terms of work began to be set by agreement and by default rather than by law. The job market stayed slow, partly because the labour force is shrinking, so careers still have to be built inside organisations whose training is short and aimed at compliance. The load-bearing variables for the next cycle are Germany's metal and electrical round, where warning strikes can begin on 1 November, and the Commission's Quality Jobs Act proposal in December: together they decide whether AI terms are agreed with workers or set by employers alone.
