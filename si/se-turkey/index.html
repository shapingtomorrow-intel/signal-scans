The cycle's five signal clusters mapped onto the report's title frame: wires (the energy build), roads (the corridor build), and resilience — the three clusters where climate stress is repricing the construction decade.

Executive Synthesis

South-east Turkey is being asked to deliver three simultaneous build streams (wires, roads and resilience) in the same eleven provinces over the same five years, while the climate underlying all three is repricing the assumptions on which the bets were sized. The renewables build is consolidating around the GAP perimeter: EPDK's 2026 transmission-level unlicensed allocations total around 3.5 GW with 75 MW directly on the Şanlıurfa-Mardin corridor (Anadolu Ajansı, March 2026), and Turkey's installed solar passed 25,827 MW at end-January 2026 (Anadolu Ajansı, February 2026). The road build is closing financing: the USD 17 billion Development Road framework was finalised under the 2024 four-country Memorandum (Türkiye Today, January 2026), and Şanlıurfa, Mardin and Cizre are being connected to the national rail network for the first time under the 2026 investment programme (T.C. Ulaştırma ve Altyapı Bakanlığı, December 2025). The resilience build remains the binding constraint: 360,455 people were still living in containers (Hatay 156,973, Malatya 67,664, Kahramanmaraş 55,264) across 242 container cities as of 26 January 2026, against TL 3.6 trillion (USD 91.5 billion) spent from central budgets 2023 to 2025 (T.C. SBB, February 2026).

The central tension is that all three build streams converge on the same skilled-labour pool, the same grid, the same water and the same eleven provinces, exactly when the climate signal is no longer trailing. The Tigris-Euphrates basin is in a five-year drought that World Weather Attribution rates as the driest year in Iraq since 1933 and a 1-in-10-year event under the current 1.3C-warmer climate (World Weather Attribution, November 2025). Silopi (Şırnak), inside the GAP perimeter, recorded a national 50.5C reading on 25 July 2025 (Phys.org / AFP, July 2025), and 3,224 fires burned around 81,000 hectares in 2025 with Hatay among the worst-affected provinces (Turkish Minute, March 2026). On the available evidence, the climate signal is no longer a downside risk; it is an operating parameter that prices the cost of every other bet upward.

All three build streams converge on the same provinces in the same five years, exactly when the climate signal is no longer trailing.

This report's reading is that the spine of the cycle is Cluster 1, the earthquake reconstruction stress-test. It is the only cluster running at Immediate impact, the only one where the lived-experience gap (360,455 in containers against 455,357 independent units reported complete) is visible in primary sources (Cumhuriyet, February 2026; Türkiye Today, February 2026), and whose absorptive capacity (200,000-strong workforce across 3,481 sites; Antakya master-plan implementation at around 20%, per Middle East Eye, February 2025) sets the labour and engineering ceiling on every other programme in the region. The rebuild-code-enforcement gap, with at least 75,000 buildings in the affected zone having received construction amnesties bypassing certification (ENR, February 2023), is the structural reason the other four bets can fail even when financing is in place: a region cannot underwrite a corridor, a YEKA pipeline and a 1.11 million hectare irrigation expansion against a building stock that has not yet been certified as compliant with TBDY 2018.

Taken together, the sources suggest the three build streams and the two climate streams are not five separate planning problems but one infrastructure-and-adaptation programme carved into five ministries' workplans. The reconstruction workforce is the labour pool the corridor motorway and the YEKA solar parks need. The grid that has to evacuate Şanlıurfa solar has to absorb a 50C heatwave demand spike on the same days. The Cizre Dam that anchors the water-cooperation framework with Iraq sits in the corridor's footprint at Şırnak. The OECD records Turkey's effective carbon price at EUR 8 per tonne CO2 equivalent against an OECD average of EUR 62, with coal still supplying about a third of electricity (OECD, April 2025); the pricing signal that would force these problems together is not yet there. The leadership question is whether the FY27 capital programme treats the Triple Build as one programme, or as five.

Where this analysis could be wrong

The central assumption is that the three build streams are competing for the same physical inputs (skilled labour, grid headroom, water) on the same five-year clock, and that climate stress (drought, heat, wildfire) has already moved from a downside scenario to an operating parameter. If 2026 to 2027 winter precipitation reverts toward the long-run basin mean and 2026 summer heat extremes fall outside the 2025 tail, the binding-constraint story compresses materially; the Triple Build then operates as three serial programmes rather than three concurrent ones, and the labour-sequencing call this report flags becomes a coordination question rather than a strategic one. The single observable that would force a material revision is the 2026-27 winter precipitation reading for the Tigris-Euphrates basin against the 30-year mean, due by April 2027 from the Turkey State Meteorological Service; a reading at or above the long-run mean is the disconfirming signal.

The decisions that cannot be deferred this cycle

  1. Sequence the FY27 capital programme around the southeastern skilled-labour pool, not around individual programme budgets. The reconstruction workforce of around 200,000 across 3,481 sites is the same engineering and construction pool that the corridor motorway, the GAP irrigation expansion and the YEKA solar build are bidding for.
    Lead-check. Proposed lead: T.C. Cumhurbaşkanlığı Strateji ve Bütçe Başkanlığı (SBB) with the Ministry of Environment, Urbanisation and Climate Change as joint owner. Resourcing: significant, full FY27 budget-cycle workstream. Dependencies: TOKI workforce census across 174 site locations; cross-ministry visibility into corridor-segment and YEKA hiring pipelines.
  2. Make rebuild-code enforcement a precondition for the next reconstruction tranche, not a parallel workstream. TBDY 2018 set modern seismic standards, but enforcement remained uneven and at least 75,000 buildings in the affected zone had received construction amnesties bypassing code certification by paying a fee.
    Lead-check. Proposed lead: T.C. Çevre, Şehircilik ve İklim Değişikliği Bakanlığı with TOKI, the chambers of architects and engineers, and provincial municipalities. Resourcing: incremental in policy, significant in inspection capacity. Dependencies: a published audit of code-compliance on completed units and a named enforcement mechanism on private rebuilds.
  3. Take an explicit basin position before Cizre Dam commissions inside the drought regime. Cizre's 49-year operating licence, Iraq's worst-drought-in-a-century framing and the November 2025 Framework Agreement on Financial Mechanism for Water Cooperation Projects are one diplomatic question, not three.
    Lead-check. Proposed lead: T.C. Dışişleri Bakanlığı with DSI and the GAP-BKI Presidency. Resourcing: stretch, requires reallocation from technical-track diplomacy. Dependencies: Iraqi Council of Representatives confirmation of the Financial Mechanism account; KRI clearance on transboundary monitoring data.
  4. Confirm a heat-and-grid contingency posture before summer 2026. 2025 wrote a 50.5C national reading at Silopi (within the GAP perimeter), a 2025 fire season of 3,224 fires across around 81,000 hectares with Hatay among the worst-affected provinces, and cotton-flowering losses in Anatolia; the FY26 grid plan and the agricultural-insurance envelope were sized against a different summer.
    Lead-check. Proposed lead: GAP-BKI Presidency with TEIAS, the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry, AFAD and provincial governorships. Resourcing: incremental on monitoring, significant on peak-demand and wildfire-suppression capacity. Dependencies: 2026 summer heat-day forecast; TARSIM premium-subsidy uptake at the raised 70% rate; provincial climate-adaptation plan publication.

Risk Snapshots

Four risk lenses on the same evidence base. Each card names the cycle's binding question on a single risk axis (drought, heat, seismic, geopolitical) with the named shift behind it and the brief to leadership.

Drought

Does the 1.11 million hectare irrigation target hold inside the basin's worst drought since 1933?

The shift: World Weather Attribution attributes the five-year drought across Iran and the Tigris-Euphrates basin to anthropogenic climate change compounded by socio-economic water stressors, classifying 2025 as the driest year in Iraq since 1933 and rating the event as a 1-in-10-year occurrence under the current 1.3C-warmer climate (World Weather Attribution, November 2025). The GAP-BKI Action Plan still targets +436,000 hectares of new irrigation and more than 500,000 additional jobs by 2028 through smart agriculture and smart irrigation (Anadolu Ajansı, September 2025).

The question to brief: Syria's 2025 wheat harvest collapsed to 0.9 to 1.1 million tonnes near record lows, with about 95% of rainfed wheat damaged and 16 million people at food-insecurity risk (FAO, July 2025); the basin-stress signal is no longer downstream news. Does GAP cropping shift before the basin forces it?

Heat

Does the grid and the cotton crop hold at 50C?

The shift: Turkey recorded a national 50.5C reading at Silopi in Şırnak (inside the GAP perimeter) on 25 July 2025, with the mayor's office issuing an appeal-for-help during the peak heatwave (Phys.org / AFP, July 2025). Cotton's optimum range is 15 to 35C and severe stress sets in above 35C with critical pollen-viability failure at or above 40C; the July 2025 heatwave hit the Mediterranean cotton flowering window, with major yield losses documented across affected basins including Anatolia (Wikifarmer, July 2025, treated directionally on quantum).

The question to brief: 2025 was among Turkey's most destructive wildfire years recorded, with 3,224 fires burning around 81,000 hectares (Turkish Minute, March 2026) and Diyarbakır, Şanlıurfa and Gaziantep facing urban heat-island load alongside the agricultural and grid stress. Is the FY26 summer-readiness envelope sized for the 2025 envelope or the 2024 one?

Seismic

Does the rebuild close out the container population before code enforcement is tested again?

The shift: As of 26 January 2026, 360,455 people remained in containers (Hatay 156,973, Malatya 67,664, Kahramanmaraş 55,264) across 242 container cities, against TL 3.6 trillion (USD 91.5 billion) spent 2023 to 2025 and 391,245 housing-unit lotteries completed with 201,431 units delivered (T.C. SBB, February 2026). TBDY 2018 set modern seismic standards but enforcement remained uneven across the southeast, with at least 75,000 buildings in the affected zone having received amnesties bypassing code certification (ENR, February 2023).

The question to brief: The 2026 budget allocates a further TL 653 billion to the earthquake zone with a ministry target of 500,000 housing-unit deliveries by year-end (T.C. Çevre, Şehircilik Bakanlığı, November 2025). Will the next tranche carry an enforceable code-compliance precondition, or repeat the pre-2023 pattern?

Geopolitical

Does the Iraq financing close survive the post-election cabinet?

The shift: The USD 17 billion Development Road financing framework was finalised across the 2024 Turkey-Iraq-Qatar-UAE Memorandum, with groundbreaking scheduled after Iraq's post-election green light (Türkiye Today, January 2026). On 2 November 2025, Foreign Ministers Fidan and Hussein signed a Framework Agreement on Financial Mechanism for Water Cooperation Projects, routing Iraqi oil-export revenue into an account funding Turkish-built water infrastructure including dams, wastewater and irrigation modernisation (Al-Monitor, November 2025).

The question to brief: Carnegie frames the Development Road and KRI cross-border trade as central instruments of Turkey's 2026 Iraq-policy recalibration (Carnegie Endowment, February 2026). Are the corridor and the oil-for-water account being read as one negotiating set, or two?

Signal Clusters

1. Year-3 earthquake reconstruction as infrastructure stress-test

Immediate

The 6 February 2023 Kahramanmaraş-Hatay earthquakes are now three years past, and the gap between official delivery numbers and the population still in temporary housing is the binding constraint on every other regional bet. The TOKI delivery line of 455,357 independent units reported under the "Reconstruction of the Century" programme sits against 360,455 people still in containers as of 26 January 2026 (T.C. SBB, February 2026; Türkiye Today, February 2026). The cluster forces leadership to confront a single question: a region cannot simultaneously absorb a corridor build, a GAP irrigation expansion and a YEKA solar pipeline while its own labour-and-records system is still working through the recovery backlog and its rebuilt stock has not yet been certified against TBDY 2018.

The Triple Build at a glance: capacity, capital and budget

Comparative scale of the three concurrent build streams in the GAP region's eleven provinces. Sources: T.C. Enerji ve Tabii Kaynaklar Bakanligi via Anadolu Ajansı, February 2026; Türkiye Today, January 2026; T.C. SBB, February 2026; T.C. Çevre, Şehircilik Bakanlığı, November 2025.

  • As of 26 January 2026, 360,455 people remained in containers (Hatay 156,973, Malatya 67,664, Kahramanmaraş 55,264) across 242 container cities; TL 3.6 trillion (USD 91.5 billion) was spent from central budgets 2023 to 2025 and 391,245 housing-unit lotteries had completed with 201,431 units delivered (T.C. SBB, February 2026); independently corroborated by Turkish-language press giving the urban-rural split (urban 223,072, rural 137,383) and naming Hatay as the largest container population (Cumhuriyet, February 2026).
  • The 2026 budget allocates a further TL 653 billion to the earthquake zone with the ministry targeting 500,000 housing-unit deliveries by year-end (T.C. Çevre, Şehircilik Bakanlığı, November 2025); the third-anniversary status records a 200,000-strong workforce across 3,481 construction sites in 174 locations, with around 85% of displaced people returned to home cities but Hatay and Kilis unemployment still above the national average (Türkiye Today, February 2026); Gulf business press records total recovery spend topping USD 91 billion with the TL 653 billion 2026 earmark (AGBI, February 2026).
  • The World Bank extended an additional EUR 200 million matched by AFD EUR 200 million (total EUR 400 million) to scale the rural-housing programme from 7,000 to 16,000 beneficiaries across seven additional quake-affected provinces, to a build-back-better standard (World Bank, March 2025); the AFD half is a sovereign loan to the same architecture (Agence Francaise de Developpement, March 2025); EBRD records 2025 commitments of EUR 195 million for two infrastructure projects in Adıyaman and Hatay, total earthquake-related investment of EUR 1.6 billion over three years, and EUR 110 million for Gaziantep municipal water and wastewater reconstruction (EBRD, January 2025).
  • Recovery on the lived-experience side remains uneven: around 20% of the Antakya master plan is being implemented, the trans-Orontes historic area sees little progress, and ownership and records issues persist over 100,000-plus buildings destroyed or severely damaged in Hatay (Middle East Eye, February 2025); the Atlantic Council's blueprint argues reconstruction must be resilient not only to future earthquakes but also to climate-intensified hazards including flooding, heat and wildfire, applied to more than 160,000 collapsed or damaged buildings across Hatay, Kahramanmaraş, Adıyaman, Gaziantep and Malatya (Atlantic Council, April 2024); Hatay Airport's post-quake reconstruction and expansion is targeted for completion in 2026 (Türkiye Today, January 2026).
Spotlight

Building-code enforcement gap: the structural risk in the rebuild

The 2018 Turkish Earthquake Building Code (TBDY 2018) provides modern seismic standards on paper. ENR's reporting in the immediate aftermath of the February 2023 earthquakes flagged that code enforcement remained uneven across the southeast and that at least 75,000 buildings in the affected zone had received construction amnesties bypassing code certification by paying a fee (Engineering News-Record, February 2023). The Atlantic Council frames the same problem at strategic level: reconstruction must be resilient not only to future earthquakes but to climate-intensified hazards including flooding, heat and wildfire, applied across more than 160,000 collapsed or damaged buildings (Atlantic Council, April 2024). On the available evidence, the rebuild is recreating two stocks at once: a TBDY-compliant tranche led by TOKI and the WB-AFD-EBRD-financed rural and municipal programmes, and a private-rebuild tranche whose certification record has not yet been audited at scale. The strategic question for FY27 is whether the next reconstruction tranche pays for delivery counts or for verified compliance.

Counter-argument

The container-city total has fallen materially from the 2024 peak and the housing-delivery rate is genuinely scaling: the 200,000-strong workforce across 3,481 sites is a coordinated reconstruction effort at significant scale. The realistic counter is that the third-anniversary gap is a transitional snapshot of a programme that is on track to clear the container-city baseline inside 2026 to 2027, and that overlaying a cross-programme labour-sequencing constraint on a recovery already operating at full domestic capacity double-counts. If the 500,000-by-end-2026 ministry target lands within twenty percent, the cross-programme bottleneck story compresses. The code-enforcement gap is a legacy stock issue more than a flow issue if the new tranche is TBDY 2018 compliant.

Decision link: Strategic Implications 1 (sequence FY27 around labour), 2 (code-enforcement precondition).

2. Renewables build-out across the GAP perimeter

Near-Term

The southeastern provinces are becoming the spatial centre of Turkey's solar build. Turkey's installed solar PV capacity reached 25,827 MW by end-January 2026, expanding 641-fold over twelve years and with the Energy and Natural Resources Minister naming 2026 as a record year for renewables (Anadolu Ajansı, February 2026). The cluster forces leadership to confront whether the GAP perimeter is being planned as a renewables corridor in its own right (with the YEKA pipeline, EPDK 2026 allocations, mini-YEKA awards and the Şanlıurfa-Hatay-Niğde Solar-4 1 GW tranche all landing inside it) or as a spillover from the national programme whose grid-integration question is left unowned.

  • EPDK's 2026 transmission-level unlicensed-generation allocations total around 3.5 GW (transmission 1,500 MW plus distribution near 2,000 MW), with 75 MW directly allocated on the Şanlıurfa-Mardin corridor and Gaziantep rooftop PV at 530 MW installed (Anadolu Ajansı, March 2026); the 2025 mini-YEKA tender programme received 850 MW of solar applications on 4 November 2025 and 1.15 GW of wind applications on 18 November, with awards spread across Mardin, Kahramanmaraş, Van and other provinces (40 MW solar tranches each in Mardin and Kahramanmaraş) and the grid-connection bottleneck flagged as the principal risk (Ember, December 2025).
  • Eksim Energy commissioned the first 45 MW of the Şanlıurfa-Viranşehir solar park (final 191.4 MWp, YEKA GES-4 lots 2, 6 and 10, 50 MW grid each, 30-year PPA from 2022), positioning it as Turkey's second-largest PV plant in Şanlıurfa province (Balkan Green Energy News, June 2025); the country-level pipeline puts Karapınar (Konya), outside the GAP perimeter, as the 1,350 MW Europe-largest-at-completion benchmark (May 2023) (Balkan Green Energy News, May 2023), with the Şanlıurfa-Hatay-Niğde Solar-4 1 GW tranche the named follow-on landing two of three names inside the south-east (Daily Sabah, January 2026).
  • Wind and solar reached 22% of Turkey's electricity in 2025 (solar 10.5%, 37.3 TWh in 2025 from 18.4 TWh in 2023; wind 11.1%), with combined wind-and-solar additions of 6.5 GW in 2025 against the 8 GW per year required to hit the 2035 target (Ember, April 2026); SHURA records total electricity capacity at 122 GW by end-2025 with renewables at 62%, 99% of the 6.3 GW net new capacity from renewables (solar +4.9 GW, wind +1.7 GW), and renewable-generation share falling to 44.1% from 46% mainly on lower hydropower output linked to the basin drought (SHURA via SolarQuarter, January 2026); pv magazine records 4.7 GW of solar added in 2025 to a 24.9 GW total at end-December (pv magazine International, January 2026).
  • Grid backbone capacity is being financed in parallel: the World Bank approved the Turkey Transforming Power Transmission System Project (TPTS) with an EUR 625 million IBRD loan plus EUR 32.8 million CTF loan and USD 2 million CTF grant, which will directly enable 1.7 GW of renewable energy and finance new high-voltage substations, modernised existing ones and HVDC corridors (World Bank, August 2025); EBRD is considering backing a USD 30 billion power-transmission upgrade on top of its record EUR 2.6 billion 2024 commitment book at 60% green (EBRD, December 2024).
Spotlight

Karapınar adjacency: the GAP perimeter hosting the national mega-solar pipeline

The benchmark project for Turkey's renewables build is Kalyon's 1,350 MW Karapınar solar park in Konya (outside the GAP perimeter), completed May 2023 with around 3.5 million panels over 20 km² and 80% local-content panels from the Kalyon PV Ankara plant (Balkan Green Energy News, May 2023). The follow-on Solar-4 1 GW tranche names Şanlıurfa, Hatay and Niğde (Daily Sabah, January 2026), placing two of three names inside the southeast. The Şanlıurfa-Viranşehir 191.4 MWp YEKA park is positioned as Turkey's second-largest PV plant in Şanlıurfa province on completion (Balkan Green Energy News, June 2025). Taken together, the sources suggest the centre of gravity of Turkey's utility-scale solar pipeline is shifting into the GAP perimeter while the headline benchmark remains a Konya project. The strategic implication is sequencing: the same labour pool that the corridor motorway and the reconstruction need is the labour pool that builds the southeastern solar parks, and the grid-integration constraint that Ember flags is unsolved at the corridor level.

Counter-argument

The renewables build is national, not regional, and treating the GAP perimeter as a self-contained corridor risks overstating the southeast's leverage on the national pipeline. The 122 GW capacity number includes hydropower at scale outside the southeast, and the 25.8 GW of installed solar is overwhelmingly distributed across Anatolia and the western coast. Ember's reading is that the binding constraint on the build is grid integration nationally, not project pipeline locally; if the World Bank TPTS and the EBRD candidate USD 30 billion transmission programme land at pace, the GAP solar parks evacuate cleanly and the southeastern concentration is a planning consequence rather than a strategic position. If the grid build slips, every node, southeastern or otherwise, sits on a partial constraint.

Decision link: Strategic Implications 1 (sequence FY27) and 3 (basin position; the renewables-water-grid intersection).

3. The Development Road as physical infrastructure

Near-Term

The Development Road is moving from diplomatic framework to physical infrastructure inside this cycle. The Turkish Ministry of Transport and Infrastructure has confirmed Şırnak (Ovaköy) as the corridor's entry node into Turkey, with a 501 km Gaziantep-Şanlıurfa-Mardin-Nusaybin-Ovaköy line and a 331 km motorway segment (Ovaköy-Cizre 23 km plus Cizre-Şanlıurfa 308 km) costing USD 2 billion (T.C. Ulaştırma ve Altyapı Bakanlığı, November 2025). The cluster forces leadership to confront whether to plan the southeast as one integrated logistics-and-energy corridor (with co-located transmission, rail-first connectivity for Şanlıurfa, Mardin and Cizre, and named industrial-rebuild standards) or as two parallel programmes whose intersection is left to resolve itself on site.

Counter-argument

The corridor's transition from financing framework to physical works is already in motion on the Turkey segment. The 2026 investment programme has connected Mardin, Şanlıurfa city centre and Cizre to the rail network, the motorway tender is structured at USD 2 billion across 331 km, and the February 2026 Baghdad summit moved the MoU into the works phase (T.C. Ulaştırma ve Altyapı Bakanlığı, February 2026). The realistic counter is that even if Iraq's post-election politics complicate the corridor's southern segment, the Turkey segment is a domestic logistics-and-rail upgrade with standalone return profile, and the 2 to 5 year horizon for activation extends but does not collapse. The corridor's commercial yield is on a slower clock; the construction risk is on the labour-and-sequencing clock this report flags as the binding one.

Decision link: Strategic Implications 1 (FY27 sequencing) and 3 (basin position; the corridor's water-cooperation dimension).

4. Water infrastructure under climate stress

Near-Term

The GAP-BKI Action Plan 2024 to 2028 is pushing irrigated area from 675,250 hectares to 1,111,355 hectares inside the same hydrological window in which the Tigris-Euphrates basin is running its worst attributed drought since 1933. The cluster forces leadership to confront a trilemma with three corners: GAP expansion targets are politically and economically committed (GAP-BKI, December 2024); basin flow is structurally compressed by climate and upstream withdrawal (Chatham House, August 2025); and the November 2025 Iraq Framework Agreement on Financial Mechanism for Water Cooperation Projects converts an upstream-downstream stress into a commercial water-infrastructure relationship (Al-Monitor, November 2025). The three are moving; this report's reading is that they are not moving toward the same equilibrium.

Spotlight

Cizre Dam: 49-year licence against a basin in drought

Cizre Barajı in Şırnak is the cluster's load-bearing project. Industry specification puts installed capacity at 330.51 MWe generating 1.208 billion kWh per year, with a 49-year operating licence granted in February 2023, 76,591 hectares of irrigation planned and a 1.1 m³ per second drinking-water supply (Enerji Atlası, accessed May 2026; treated directionally as project-spec corroboration). Cross-confirmed at the diplomatic level: Chatham House records that GAP now spans 22 dams and 19 hydro plants and explicitly flags Cizre as expected to compress downstream flow further than Ilısu has already done (Chatham House, August 2025). On the available evidence, bringing Cizre online inside a five-year drought regime, in the same months as the November 2025 Financial Mechanism account is being operationalised and the Development Road corridor is moving into the works phase, requires an explicit basin position. Without one, the licence becomes a flashpoint rather than a hydroelectric and irrigation asset.

Counter-argument

The basin-stress framing risks attributing too much causal weight to GAP. The structural Tigris-Euphrates flow compression is climate-and-Iran-driven as much as GAP-driven: World Weather Attribution names anthropogenic climate change and socio-economic water stressors in the plural, not GAP alone. Iraq's own intra-country water management and Iranian dam-building are at least as significant in the basin balance. If 2026 to 2027 winter precipitation reverts toward the long-run basin mean, the cluster's near-term shape changes: the oil-for-water framework operates as a normal commercial water-infrastructure relationship rather than a crisis-management vehicle, and the GAP expansion target lands without diplomatic friction.

Decision link: Strategic Implication 3 (basin position) and 4 (heat-and-grid contingency, the drought-heat overlap).

5. Heat, wildfire and agricultural adaptation

Longer-Range

2025 wrote the climate signal into the operating record. Turkey recorded a national 50.5C reading at Silopi in Şırnak (inside the GAP perimeter) on 25 July 2025 (Phys.org / AFP, July 2025); 3,224 fires burned around 81,000 hectares with Hatay among the most-affected provinces (Turkish Minute, March 2026); World Weather Attribution rates the conditions driving the deadly Turkey, Cyprus and Greece wildfires as 10 times more likely under 1.3C of human-induced warming (World Weather Attribution, August 2025); and the OECD records Turkey's effective carbon price at EUR 8 per tonne CO2 equivalent against an OECD average of EUR 62, with coal still supplying about a third of electricity (OECD, April 2025). The cluster forces leadership to confront whether 2025 is a tail event or the new operating envelope, and whether the FY27 plan prices it as the latter.

  • Turkey recorded a national 50.5C reading at Silopi (Şırnak, inside the GAP perimeter) on 25 July 2025, with the local mayor issuing an appeal for help during the peak heatwave (Phys.org / AFP, July 2025); World Weather Attribution rates the hot, dry and windy conditions driving deadly wildfires in Turkey, Cyprus and Greece as 10 times more likely and 22% more intense under 1.3C of human-induced warming, with winter rainfall down around 14% since pre-industrial era and 2025 fires across the eastern Mediterranean killing 20, evacuating 80,000 and burning more than 1 million hectares (World Weather Attribution, August 2025).
  • 3,224 fires burned around 81,000 hectares in Turkey in 2025, around three times the prior year; July alone saw 177 forest fires destroying around 60,000 hectares, with Hatay among the worst-affected provinces alongside the western coastal belt (Turkish Minute, March 2026); Hatay communities still in earthquake recovery were hit by July 2025 wildfires, with the Dörtyol district fire forcing around 2,000 evacuations on 4 to 5 July, the residents' framing recorded as "We've Been Ruined" (Balkan Insight, July 2025).
  • The Mediterranean cotton flowering window in early July 2025 was hit by the heatwave with significant yield-loss documented across affected basins including Anatolia; cotton's optimum is 15 to 35C, severe stress sets in above 35C, and pollen viability drops to near zero at or above 40C (Wikifarmer, July 2025, treated directionally on quantum); OECD records that Turkey's agricultural sector navigated heightened climate risk and structural transition in 2025, with widespread frost and deepening drought, critical soil moisture in central Anatolia and southeastern Turkey, and the TARSIM Village-Based Yield Insurance (drought) premium subsidy raised from 60% to 70% (OECD, October 2025); only around 36 million decares of 234 million cultivable decares are insured, with 22% of registered farmers using TARSIM (Daily Sabah, December 2025).
  • The institutional anchor for adaptation is Turkey's Climate Change Adaptation Strategy and Action Plan (CCASAP) 2024-2030, covering 11 sectors with 40 strategic goals and 129 actions to 2030 and explicitly addressing wildfire, drought and heat stress (T.C. Çevre, Şehircilik ve İklim Değişikliği Bakanlığı / Directorate of Climate Change, March 2024); the OECD recommends higher GHG pricing, coal-to-renewables shift and expanded adaptation policies against rising wildfire risk, noting Turkey's effective carbon price at EUR 8 per tonne CO2e against an OECD average of EUR 62 (OECD, April 2025).
Spotlight

Heat-load on the grid: 50C demand against renewables intermittency

The 50.5C Silopi record sits at the intersection of the renewables build and the heat cluster. Heatwave peak-demand spikes typically land in the late afternoon as residential and commercial cooling load tops out, which is the same window in which solar generation begins to fall from its midday peak. Taken together, the sources suggest the FY26 summer-readiness envelope was sized against a different operating envelope: SHURA records renewable-generation share falling to 44.1% from 46% in 2025 mainly on lower hydropower output linked to the basin drought (SHURA via SolarQuarter, January 2026), Ember flags the 8 GW per year build rate as below the 2035 target requirement (Ember, April 2026), and the World Bank's TPTS programme is sized to enable 1.7 GW of renewables and modernise high-voltage substations (World Bank, August 2025). The strategic question is whether the southeastern grid plan is sized for 50C peak demand or for the pre-2025 operating envelope.

Weak signals to watch

  • Weak signal TARSIM's premium subsidy for the drought cover under Village-Based Yield Insurance has moved from 60% to 70% (OECD, October 2025). Would gain weight if 2026 uptake by registered farmers rises above 30% (from 22% in 2025) or if private-reinsurer capacity tightens specifically on cotton and southeastern basins.
  • Weak signal The CCASAP 2024-2030 framework is in place but provincial adaptation plans for the 11 GAP-perimeter provinces are not yet published at a comparable level of detail (T.C. Çevre, Şehircilik Bakanlığı, March 2024). Would gain weight if Hatay, Şanlıurfa or Gaziantep publishes a province-level adaptation plan with named heat-day thresholds and grid-and-water contingencies in 2026.

Counter-argument

2025 may be a tail event rather than a step change. The 50.5C reading was a single-station record in a particularly hot summer; the 81,000-hectare wildfire total, while larger than the prior year, sits within the range of bad fire seasons recorded across the Mediterranean basin in the last decade. World Weather Attribution's framing is probabilistic (10 times more likely, 22% more intense) rather than deterministic; the FY26 envelope can reasonably price these as elevated risk parameters rather than as a new operating regime, until 2026 to 2028 data show a sustained shift. The TARSIM premium adjustment and the CCASAP framework are the relevant adaptive responses; reading the FY27 plan against the 2025 tail rather than the underlying trend risks over-fitting.

Decision link: Strategic Implication 4 (heat-and-grid contingency).

Strategic Implications

Four decisions turn this cycle's signals into the FY27 capital programme. Each follows the four-question discipline (who, by when, what, why), names a concrete action, carries a decision posture and a Lead-check inset.

SI 1: Sequence the FY27 capital programme around skilled-labour capacity, not programme budgets

The T.C. Cumhurbaşkanlığı Strateji ve Bütçe Başkanlığı (SBB), with the Ministry of Environment, Urbanisation and Climate Change, the Ministry of Transport and Infrastructure and GAP-BKI as joint owners, should, by the end of the FY27 budget cycle, publish an integrated sequencing plan for the three build streams (reconstruction, Development Road motorway segments and rail-first connectivity, and the YEKA and EPDK southeastern renewables build) against a single regional skilled-labour pool. The reconstruction effort is already running at a 200,000-strong workforce across 3,481 sites in 174 locations (Türkiye Today, February 2026); the same engineering and construction pool is the binding input for the other two streams, and the GAP irrigation expansion to 1.11 million hectares (GAP-BKI, December 2024) sits on the same pool. Without a sequencing decision, each programme defaults to its own pace and the region under-delivers against all three.

Action: by end-FY27 budget cycle (end-2026), an integrated capital-programme sequencing plan owned by SBB and published as part of the 2027-2029 medium-term programme.

Scenario Matrix

Two uncertainties will shape the next three to five years in south-east Turkey more than any other. First, basin and climate severity by 2028 (the 2025 envelope persists versus reverts toward the long-run mean on drought, heat and wildfire). Second, the financing pipeline (the Development Road financing close and the multilateral grid-and-water co-finance hold versus retrench). The four scenarios below are planning aids, not forecasts.

Triple Build, on Schedule

Climate severity reverts, financing holds. Winter 2026-27 and 2027-28 precipitation moves back toward the long-run basin mean, the 2025 heat and wildfire tail is treated as event-not-regime, and the GAP 1.11 million hectare 2028 target lands. The Development Road financing close survives the post-election Iraqi cabinet, the World Bank TPTS and the EBRD candidate transmission programme proceed, and the southeastern YEKA solar pipeline evacuates cleanly. Reconstruction clears the container baseline through 2026-27. The wires, roads and resilience streams compound; the southeast emerges as Turkey's logistics-and-energy pivot inside the cycle horizon.

Early indicators: 2026-27 winter precipitation in or above the 30-year basin mean by April 2027; Iraqi post-election cabinet ratifies the 2024 four-country MoU without material change; ministry 500,000-housing-delivery target lands within 20% by end-2026.

Climate Operates, Financing Holds

2025 envelope persists, financing holds. The five-year drought, the 50C heat tail and the elevated wildfire season become the operating baseline. GAP-BKI revises the 1.11 million hectare target downward or compresses cropping intensity. The Iraq Framework Agreement on Financial Mechanism moves from contingency to permanent commercial water-infrastructure relationship. The corridor and the renewables build proceed, but on a different climate envelope: grid heat-load constrains summer evacuation, southeastern agriculture rationalises away from cotton, and provincial adaptation plans become operational. The Triple Build delivers, on a re-priced cost base.

Early indicators: 2026-27 winter precipitation 20% below 30-year basin mean by April 2027; GAP-BKI publishes a revised hectare target or crop-transition envelope; TARSIM drought-cover uptake rises above 30% in 2026.

Wet Years, Financing Retrenches

Climate reverts, financing retrenches. Basin hydrology improves but the post-election Iraqi cabinet revisits the 2024 four-country MoU, the World Bank TPTS slips its timeline and the EBRD candidate transmission programme is held over. The corridor's commercial yield extends past the cycle horizon; the southeastern renewables build is paced by the local grid backbone rather than the planned national upgrade. Reconstruction proceeds on the central-budget path. The region's strategic position holds but the corridor narrative is deferred a cycle; capital that priced the 2 to 5 year horizon re-rates.

Early indicators: Iraqi post-election cabinet asks for material revision of the financing model in 2026; World Bank TPTS disbursement slips by more than six months; EBRD candidate USD 30 billion transmission programme deferred to next strategy cycle.

The Squeeze

Climate operates, financing retrenches. The hardest scenario. The drought, heat and wildfire envelope persists; the corridor financing close is revised; the World Bank and EBRD pipelines slip. The three build streams compete for skilled labour against compressed agricultural output, a constrained grid build and an Iraqi political settlement that has not landed. The container-city population shrinks more slowly than planned; Hatay industrial occupancy underperforms; renewables build slips against the 2028 milestones and southeastern cropping is rationed under continued drought. The region falls back on direct central-government transfers; strategic optionality compresses.

Early indicators: 2026-27 winter precipitation in the drought-regime band; named retrenchment of the Development Road financing close in 2026; ministry slips the 500,000-housing-delivery target by more than 20%; province-level adaptation plans not published by end-2026.

What We Are Not Planning For

EU-Turkey accession and CBAM economics beyond infrastructure

This cycle treats the corridor and the renewables build as Turkey-Iraq-Gulf-funded infrastructure rather than as instruments of EU-Turkey accession-track economics. Customs Union modernisation, CBAM exposure on Turkish coal-heavy exports and IPA-III allocations are policy-relevant but operate on a different clock from the FY27 capital programme.

Reinstate if: Customs Union modernisation reopens at heads-of-government level; or IPA-III allocations explicitly target corridor segments or southeastern provinces above EUR 1 billion; or CBAM enforcement tightens enough to re-price Gaziantep manufacturing.

A Kurdish political-settlement and peace-process deep-dive

This cycle brackets the political-settlement question. The peace-process trajectory through 2025-2026 is materially relevant to investment-grade re-rating, but anchoring on it would require a consolidation of public-record outputs (TBMM commission, integration framework, trusteeship-reversal architecture) the evidence base has not yet reached.

Reinstate if: a constitutional amendment timetable is announced; or trusteeship architecture is formally withdrawn over named southeastern municipalities; or a named successor armed group reframes the regional security baseline.

A southeastern labour-market deep-dive

Skilled-labour capacity is a central thread of this cycle's strategic implications, but a peer-grade province-level labour-market quantification (wage trajectories, sector switching, in-migration of construction workers, returnee employment in Hatay and Kilis) would require evidence the public record does not yet consolidate. The report treats labour as a binding constraint without claiming a province-level quantification.

Reinstate if: TUIK publishes a southeastern construction-labour wage and supply index; or a major contractor on the corridor or YEKA tranche reports a binding labour shortage publicly.

Agriculture beyond climate-adaptation framing

The cluster treatment touches agriculture through climate adaptation (cotton flowering, TARSIM expansion, soil moisture in SE Turkey). A wider GAP agricultural-economy deep-dive (commodity crop economics, agroprocessing, water-intensive crop substitution at province level) would require an evidence base distinct from the climate-anchored sources used here.

Reinstate if: a published GAP-BKI cropping-mix revision lands; or a peer-grade province-level agroprocessing study quantifies the value-add downstream of the irrigation expansion.

Discussion Points for the Leadership Team

  1. If the FY27 capital programme is sequenced around the southeastern skilled-labour pool rather than around individual programme budgets, which of the three build streams (reconstruction, corridor motorway and rail, YEKA solar) is the one we slow first, and who in cabinet owns that decision?
  2. If TBDY 2018 code-compliance enforcement is made a precondition for the 2026 TL 653 billion reconstruction tranche, are we prepared to accept a delivery-pace slow-down inside 2026 in exchange for verified compliance, and how do we communicate that publicly inside an environment where delivery counts are the lead political metric?
  3. If Cizre Dam commissions inside a continued five-year basin-drought regime and Iraq invokes the November 2025 Financial Mechanism account as basin-stress relief rather than infrastructure finance, what is the Turkey position, and who has the authority to state it before the next basin diplomatic round?

If the 2025 climate envelope (50C summer, 81,000 hectares burned, cotton-flowering yield loss) is the new operating baseline rather than a tail, which crop-transition envelopes and which peak-demand reserves is the region prepared to underwrite in FY27, and what is the social and labour-market consequence of moving away from cotton across Şanlıurfa, Mardin and the wider GAP perimeter?
