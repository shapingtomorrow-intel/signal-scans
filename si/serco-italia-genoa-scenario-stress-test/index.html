Scenarios are not forecasts. Their job is to show how differently the years to 2040 could turn out, so that a plan can be built to survive any of them, and then to be watched, so that the plan changes when the evidence does.

This page does both. It takes the nine options in the briefing and runs each one through four plausible futures to 2040, plus a wild card. A move that works in every future is robust and can be committed to now. A move that works well in some futures and poorly in others is a bet, held ready with a clear trigger. A move that makes things worse in any future is fragile and should not be relied on. It then scores leading indicators on the matrix’s two axes and turns the reading into investment priorities.

1. Four futures to 2040, and a wild card to 2040

The two axes are the system’s two biggest uncertainties, the same two the System Map identifies: whether Genoa stays a transhipment hub or becomes an inland gateway, which sets how much load the congestion brake carries, and whether risk is run as one system or terminal by terminal, which is whether the seeing-the-risk loop is built for the whole port.

One system · Hub

The Shared Harbour

Ships again call at Genoa and Vado to swap containers; the terminals run storms and heat to one shared protocol, and most cargo never leaves the quay.

By 2040: A joint operations centre closes berths in sequence on a live picture of risk.

One system · Gateway

The Corridor Port

Genoa is mainly a gateway for northern Italy and the Alpine countries, and the port and its rail, road and grid partners plan works and outages together.

By 2040: The live picture of risk reaches up the rail line and motorways, and cargo is routed round failures.

Each terminal · Hub

The Sea Wall

Transhipment returns behind the new breakwater, but each terminal runs its own limits, sensors and backup power.

By 2040: The system holds until a storm during a power or cyber outage hits several terminals at once.

Each terminal · Gateway

The Gridlocked Gateway

Inland cargo grows while each partner plans alone; gate closures, rail outages and storm seasons coincide.

By 2040: Cargo diverts to rival ports in bad months and uninsured losses fall on hauliers and small firms.

Wild card to 2040

Heat sets the hours

Summer heat, not storms, becomes the main reason Genoa’s terminals change how and when they work.

What the stress test shows
  1. There is a robust core, and it matches three of the briefing’s Implications. A shared dependency register, common weather limits with ground sensors, and a live risk layer shared with insurers hold up in all four futures.
  2. Doing nothing is not neutral. Coordinating case by case backfires where inland cargo grows and partners plan alone, and keeping each terminal’s own weather limits backfires in the heat wild card.
  3. The big structural moves are bets on the vertical axis. A port-corridor body and a single operations centre are strong only where risk is run as one system; the protocol and the register are the signposts that settle them.

3. What it means for the plan

Commit now

Robust: works in every future.

  • A shared dependency register with named owners (Implication 1).
  • Common weather and heat limits read from shared ground sensors (Implication 2).
  • A live risk layer on the breakwater, quays and access routes, shared with insurers (Implication 3).

Prepare, and act on a trigger

Bets: strong in some futures, weak in others.

  • A port-corridor resilience body: release it if the reading settles on the gateway side.
  • One port operations centre: hold ready until a port-wide protocol is signed.
  • Cover that pays on measured triggers: prepare with insurers once live data exists.

Do not rely on

Fragile: backfires in at least one future.

  • Coordinating outages case by case as inland cargo grows.

4. Which future is gaining momentum, and how it has moved at this briefing

Each future is a corner of the matrix, so the question “which future is gaining momentum” becomes two questions the evidence can answer: is Genoa staying a hub or becoming a gateway, and is risk being run as one system or terminal by terminal? Twelve leading indicators answer them, six on each axis. Scored at each reading, they give a reading on each axis and a point on the matrix; the trail of points shows where the evidence is heading.

The pale band (the no-lead zone) marks readings within 15 points of an axis, where no future can lead. Hover over a point for its reading.

Main role: leaning gateway+43 at this briefing: transhipment fell and inland cargo grew, but the carbon-price rule and a Suez return could reverse it.
Risk run: leaning terminal by terminal−46 at this briefing: no protocol or register yet, and investment is terminal-led.
Leading future: none yetA future leads only after two readings in a corner; this is the first reading.
What would settle itA signed port-wide protocol or a shared dependency register moves the reading up towards the one-system futures; the carbon-price listing and Suez transits move it along the hub-gateway axis.
How the reading is scored
  1. Each indicator is marked Moving (+1), Pending (+½), Not yet (0) or Against (−1), on the evidence published by the date of the reading.
  2. Each carries a weight: 3 if it would settle its axis on its own, 2 if it is strong evidence, 1 if it supports.
  3. An axis reading is the weighted total for that axis, scaled from −100 (transhipment hub, or by each terminal) to +100 (inland gateway, or as one system).
  4. A future leads when the reading sits in its corner, at least 15 points from both axes, for two readings running.
  5. A shift is a move of 10 points or more on either axis between readings. It is flagged and reviewed in the next quarterly briefing.
  6. A signpost reached (section 6) overrides the score and triggers its decision directly.

7. How the monitoring runs over time

Every weekSignal Scans attach new evidence to each indicator and signpost, including weak signals that do not yet fit any future.
Every monthThe Change Tracker rescores each indicator, the two axis readings are recalculated, and the trail on the matrix gains a point.
Every quarterThe Decision Intelligence briefing reviews any flagged shift, and redraws the scenarios, the System Map and this stress test.
When a signpost is reachedAction Triggers fire, releasing the bet prepared for it and naming the decision it changes.

8. Running it with your own plan

The options above are the briefing’s. The real value comes from replacing them with the port’s and its partners’ own priorities and asking the same question of each: does it hold in every future?
