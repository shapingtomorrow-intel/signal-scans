Early warnings

Four developments the portfolio caught before consensus formed, in the order they hardened.

Lead time 5 weeks aheadSignal Scan, 18 May DI cycle 2

Agent identity, not model capability, decides which AI is allowed to act on whose behalf

The 18 May Signal Scan named the agent-identity-and-authorization stack (NIST AI Agent Standards Initiative, the NCCoE concept paper, OpenID Foundation's federal RFI response, Okta and Microsoft Entra agent-ID releases) as the rail that would decide which agents got to act on whose behalf. Five weeks later the 25 June briefing recorded the consensus catch-up: Singapore IMDA's January 2026 Model Governance Framework, the OECD's February 2026 paper, the IMF's April 2026 note on agentic payments and Mastercard's all-European-issuers Agent Pay enablement on 2 June 2026 all treat agent identity as a procurement, regulatory and settlement primitive. Cycle 2 elevates this to a Decide posture; the question is not what an agent can do, it is whether it will be allowed to do it.

Lead time 4 weeks aheadSignal Scan, 23 May DI cycle 2

The binding constraint on agentic AI is insurability, not capability

The 23 May Signal Scan flagged the underwriting brake before it was widely read as the deployment gate: US carriers and the standards body for commercial general liability were already inserting AI exclusions into casualty, D&O, E&O and fiduciary lines, while only a thin affirmative-cover market was filling the gap. A month later the 25 June briefing confirmed the brake had hardened: roughly 82% of US P&C policies now carry Verisk/ISO generative-AI exclusion endorsements, the Lloyd's Market Association launched its AI Adoption Toolkit on 23 April 2026, captive practitioners (Marsh, Hylant, Milliman, Iowa Insurance Division) are absorbing the layers commercial insurers will not write, and D&O carriers are probing board-level AI governance at renewal. Cycle 2 elevates this to a Decide posture for any operator deploying AI in the credit, claims, contracting or trading perimeter.

Lead time 3 weeks aheadSignal Scan, 30 May DI cycle 2

Hyperscaler balance-sheet strength is the wrong place to look for AI financing risk

The 30 May Signal Scan caught what BIS Bulletin No 120 (7 January 2026) had quietly named but the wider market had not yet absorbed: a growing share of the AI build-out has migrated off balance sheet into special-purpose vehicles, private credit and residual-value-guaranteed leases that published debt figures do not reflect. The IMF, FSB, ECB and Bank of England flagged the concentration between February and May 2026; the 25 June briefing records BIS Bulletin 120's warning that AI-related investment now exceeds 1% of US GDP and that outstanding private credit to AI firms is projected at USD 300 to 600 billion by 2030. A disappointing AI-revenue print would no longer be only a tech-equity correction; it would land on insurers, pension funds and a private-credit book several times the visible bank exposure. Cycle 2 holds this as a Prepare posture for CFOs and asset allocators.

Lead time Pre-consensusSignal Scan, 13 June

Cheaper tokens are producing higher AI bills, not lower ones

The 13 June Signal Scan surfaced the inference-cost inversion before consensus framing has caught up: per-token prices are collapsing (the 'LLMflation' narrative) but agentic and reasoning workloads consume tokens faster than prices fall, so total inference spend on a useful task is rising rather than falling. This is the unit-economics counterpart to the underwriting brake: the consensus narrative is deflation, the invoice is inflation. Cycle 2 does not yet carry this directly, which is the point: the cycle's CFO-facing capex stress-test (SI 4 in the May briefing) was sized around a falling marginal cost. The disconfirming signal is the enterprise invoice. Posture: Prepare, with the trigger of the first frontier-lab earnings call naming agentic-workload cost inflation explicitly.

Assumption changes since the May briefing

Five explicit cycle-over-cycle assumption changes, in decreasing order of strategic weight.

Shift 1 of 5

"AI is moving from tool to colleague to manager-of-tasks"

This was the May briefing's spine, held inside the org-chart frame. The June briefing's evidence sharpens it: the agent has moved past joining the org chart and started becoming the counterparty itself. IMDA, the IMF, the OECD, UNCITRAL, the BIS, Visa and Mastercard have all moved within five months to build identity, authentication, settlement and contract law around agent-initiated transactions. The forward-thesis implication: an organisation can have a perfectly clean agent operating model and still be unprepared for the question of who answers when the agent signs.

Shift 2 of 5

"Verification is now a procurement capability, not a policy artefact" (cycle 1, SI 2)

Held by cycle 1 as a hard EU AI Act 2 August 2026 deadline, with the trust stack arriving on cue. The cycle's body of work confirms the procurement framing but adds a layer underneath: verification is now also an insurance procurement question. Roughly 82% of US P&C policies carry generative-AI exclusion endorsements (Munich Re, March 2026), and the underwriter due-diligence questionnaire is operating as a parallel compliance regime, in many jurisdictions setting governance standards before any AI-specific statute lands. The procurement clause was the right artefact; it now sits inside a policy wording that may decline the cover.

Shift 3 of 5

"Human-in-the-loop is the operating control"

Implicit in cycle 1's verification theme and in the cycle's reading of the EU AI Act. Refuted by the 25 June briefing's Theme 2: the Bank of England recorded firms saying human-in-the-loop is 'challenged by the rise of agentic AI'; the Fed, OCC and FDIC carved generative and agentic AI out of US model-risk guidance on 17 April 2026; IOSCO's 25 May 2026 Supervisory Toolkit notes member experiments using 'AI as a judge'; the FSB's 10 June 2026 consultation places board and senior-management accountability at the centre. The control model has reset from supervising what an agent does to governing what an agent is allowed to do. Any AI governance policy still anchored on human-in-the-loop describes the past, not the present.

Shift 4 of 5

"The $7.6 trillion AI capex envelope binds at hyperscaler free cash flow" (cycle 1, Theme 5)

Cycle 1's capex reckoning located the binding constraint at the hyperscaler income statement (FCF deficits, depreciation schedules, silicon useful life). Two cycle-2 evidence streams move the constraint elsewhere. The 30 May Signal Scan shows the financing layer has migrated off balance sheet into SPVs, private credit and residual-value-guaranteed leases held by insurers, pension funds and private-credit funds. The 25 June briefing's Theme 5 shows the physical layer is binding: NERC lifted its ten-year US summer peak forecast by 69% in a single revision, Germany's four TSOs are rationing 270 GW of grid-connection requests against ~94 GW of forecast 2037 need, and more than 50 US cities have enacted moratoria on new data-centre construction. The substation, not the spreadsheet, is the gate.

Shift 5 of 5

"The AI haves and have-nots gap is a measurement lag" (cycle 1 counter-argument framing)

The May briefing presented the J-curve reading as a credible counter to the haves-and-have-nots distribution story. The 20 June Signal Scan sharpens the call: at near-saturation adoption, returns are pooling in roughly the fifth of firms that have rebuilt workflows around the technology, while the rest run pilots that never reach the income statement. Adoption has gone wide; the return on it has stayed narrow. The divide is scaling and value capture, not access. For boards, the forward thesis is that AI portfolios under-deliver not because the tools fail, but because the bolt-on integration pattern caps the return at the integration boundary.

Actions triggered

Each trigger names why it activates now, why it matters, the recommended actions, the primary decision owner, the decision window, and the conditions under which the recommendation should be reviewed.

Decide Action 1 DI cycle 2

Name a senior accountable owner for agentic AI and rewrite the governance policy from human-in-the-loop to outcome-based before 2 August 2026

Boards and General Counsel teams should treat the rewrite as a board-cycle item, not a compliance refresh. The Fed, OCC and FDIC have moved accountability onto firms; the FSB has placed it on boards and senior management; the EU AI Act takes full force on 2 August 2026 with its human-oversight rules intact. The evidence base in DI cycle 2's Theme 2 is now sufficient for a Decide call: name the senior accountable owner (distinct from the CIO, reporting to the board), and replace the human-in-the-loop language with objectives, guardrails, action logging and outcome thresholds. The cost of standing on a stale policy at the next supervisory engagement is materially higher than the cost of writing the new one now.

Prepare Action 2 Signal Scan, 23 May DI cycle 2

Re-examine every live AI deployment against the actual insurance map by end Q3 2026; stand up the captive route for layers commercial insurers will not write

The Chief Risk Officer and Group Risk function should treat the cycle's underwriting evidence as the binding gate on AI deployment, not a downstream compliance task. By end Q3 2026, every live or proposed AI deployment carrying material residual exposure should be tested against the current cyber, professional indemnity and D&O wording and flagged where the policy no longer covers the loss. For operators at captive scale, the next FY27 insurance buying cycle is the window to absorb the excluded layers via difference-in-conditions wrappers; for sub-captive operators, the choice is between accepting the exclusion, switching carrier or restructuring the deployment to avoid the excluded fact pattern. The Prepare posture reflects that the insurable wording is still being written.

Prepare Action 3 Signal Scan, 30 May Signal Scan, 13 June DI cycle 2

CFOs rebuild the AI capex business case around physical-infrastructure dependency and inference-cost trajectory, not falling marginal cost

The CFO and CTO should treat the May briefing's AI-return stress test as superseded. Two pieces of cycle-2 evidence move the inputs. The 30 May Signal Scan and the 25 June briefing's Theme 5 together show physical infrastructure is the binding constraint on the three- to seven-year horizon: grid-connection queues run four to seven years, water-rights pushback is municipal-ban territory, and behind-the-meter generation triggers utility-classification risk. The 13 June Signal Scan on inference-cost inversion shows the per-task unit cost of agentic workloads is rising as token prices fall. By the FY27 planning round, every AI capex commitment above the materiality threshold should carry a named substation, a named water source and a named inference-cost trajectory by workload class, signed off by the CFO.

Monitor Action 4 Signal Scan, 23 May DI cycle 2

Watch the Lloyd's model agentic-AI wording and the open-versus-closed capability gap as the cycle-3 escalation trigger

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because the Lloyd's model wording is expected in H2 2026 and will set the market baseline, the open-versus-closed capability gap is closing faster than mid-cycle assumptions, and both markers will decide whether cycle 3's counterparty thesis holds or has to be reframed.

Boards and Strategy functions should track two named milestones over the next six months: a Lloyd's Market Association model clause for insurable agentic AI, and Epoch AI's monthly capability index for the gap between best open-weight and best closed-proprietary models. Escalate this Monitor to Prepare if either: (a) the LMA publishes a sub-limited agentic-AI cyber wording by end-2026 (which would soften the underwriting brake and reopen capacity, reshaping SI 2); or (b) the open-versus-closed gap stays inside six months for three consecutive Epoch readings (which would confirm the post-moat reading of cycle 2's Theme 4 and force vendor renegotiation). Absent either trigger, hold posture and revisit at the next Action Triggers cycle.

Review conditions

This recommendation should be reviewed if the Lloyd's wording is delayed beyond H2 2026 (buys time), if an open-weight release closes the capability gap decisively (shifts the risk map), or if a major closed-model provider publishes a warranty on its outputs (which would change the counterparty structure the cycle assumed).
