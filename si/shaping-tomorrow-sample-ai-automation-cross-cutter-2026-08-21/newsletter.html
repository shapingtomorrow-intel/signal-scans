Three numbers that make the case
In Britain, self-reported AI use among businesses with ten or more staff went from around 12% to around 35% in two years. Over the same period, the number of AI technologies the average adopting business actually uses went from about 1.4 to 1.6. Adoption spread sideways. It never went deep.

In America, 57% of firms that have adopted AI use it in three or fewer business functions. And depending on which reputable survey you pick, US adoption is either 18% or 78%, a four-fold spread the Federal Reserve puts down mostly to how the question is asked.

So when a board asks whether it is behind on AI, there is currently no honest way to answer.
Three decisions with dates on them
Decide now
Stop renewing AI on the strength of a happy user
The most careful measurement in this field was withdrawn in February by the researchers who built it, because people who will not give up the tool cannot be put in a control group. Their own survey work shows users overstate the time AI saves them by about 40 percentage points. Set a rule now: above a stated value, no renewal without a before and after, and apply it to everything falling due from 31 October.
Of the AI you renewed in the last year, how much of it could survive that rule?
Decide now
Find out what you actually bought when you bought an agent
Of the thousands of vendors selling agentic AI, Gartner reckons about 130 are real, and forecasts more than 40% of agent projects cancelled by the end of 2027. Asking for benchmark scores will not save you: an audit of 15 agent benchmarks found reward hacking in about two thirds of the runs in the two suites it examined most closely. Get the rule into your contracts by 31 December.
Could your top AI vendor complete one whole task on your data, start to finish, and let you watch?
Prepare
Secure your 2027 memory supply before the window shuts
Here is the part that has nothing to do with software. Three suppliers have contracted years of memory output to AI customers, so phones, cars, laptops and security hardware are now rationed by allocation rather than cleared by price. If your 2027 product plan assumes you can buy memory when you need it, it assumes something that is no longer true. The window is now, before 2027 build slots are committed.

Where we might be wrong
The first broad measured comparison to land this cycle found 34% productivity growth in the most AI-exposed sectors against 24% in the least. That is real, and it cuts against everything above. Our reading is that both can be true: gains are showing up at the level of whole sectors while remaining invisible at the level of the individual deployment. What an organisation cannot do is borrow the first to justify the second.
