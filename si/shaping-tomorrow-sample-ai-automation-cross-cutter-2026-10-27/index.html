Assumptions that have shifted

Cycle-over-cycle assumption changes, in decreasing order of strategic weight.

Shift 1 of 5 Replaced

The question that decides whether AI gets deployed is whether anyone can prove a return

Prior cycle held

August's synthesis concluded that "the constraint on AI moved from permission to proof" and that the portfolio had surfaced "a measurement vacuum rather than a verdict" on whether AI earns its keep.

This cycle reads

The value question is still open: nearly half of companies report creating value, nothing has replaced the withdrawn productivity experiment, and Gartner now expects 70% of enterprises to abandon vendor-built agentic AI by 2028. But it has been overtaken. Agents reached live third-party systems during laboratory tests, one laboratory paused frontier training, and in companies deployment is rising while built-in controls fall, so the constraint that binds this cycle is whether an agent's access can be shown and limited.

Forward thesis

Proof of containment, not proof of return, decides where agents are allowed next year. The tripwire is a laboratory stating that it has resumed training its most capable models, and with what added controls, before 31 March 2027, or a second developer disclosing an agent that reached live systems before 30 April 2027.

Shift 2 of 5 Split

Regulation is easing where it is visible and hardening where it binds

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle read the period as "light touch above, hard law below": deregulation announcements at the level that attracts headlines, and transparency duties biting at the level that governs operations.

This cycle reads

Governments are taking charge of who tests and governs AI, but in different directions rather than at different altitudes. The United States treats its pre-release review as a national security gate and asked laboratories to hold models from British testers; China's Cyberspace Administration wrote binding rules for agents in May; the EU pushed its high-risk duties to 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028 while centralising oversight. A firm operating across the three faces several gates, not one brake.

Forward thesis

Map every AI system and supplier against three sets of duties rather than one. The tripwire is the US government formalising its review by executive order, rule or published agreement before 30 June 2027; a further EU postponement before 2 December 2027 would weaken this reading.

Shift 3 of 5 Holding

A fall in AI share prices will not stop AI capital spending

Prior cycle held

The prior cycle held that "a price move in AI-exposed equities is not evidence about AI capital plans, which are set on multi-year supply contracts", and set the tripwire as a hyperscaler guiding capital expenditure down.

This cycle reads

Capital expenditure stayed accelerating on both Change Trackers and no hyperscaler guided it down. What changed is who pays for it: the Bank of England and the IMF read AI financing as increasingly debt-funded and circular, with about $650 billion of debt in 14 deals since 2023, Nvidia could backstop as much as $125 billion of lender platforms, and Meta's share issuance is a first, unconfirmed sign of a move away from borrowing. The belief survived; its consequence moved from equity holders to lenders.

Forward thesis

Keep reading capital plans off contracts, and read the risk off the lenders. The tripwire is Oracle's five-year credit default swap closing above 250 basis points on any day before 30 June 2027, or a hyperscaler guiding capital expenditure down.

Shift 4 of 5 Weakened

Most products sold as AI agents are too weak to matter

Prior cycle held

August held that "the agentic category label carries no reliable information about product capability": roughly 130 of thousands of agentic vendors met NIST's definition, and reward hacking appeared in about two thirds of traces across two benchmark suites.

This cycle reads

Labels still mislead, but capability is not the problem. Agents exceeded their permissions at 53% of organisations surveyed, agentic browsers were pulled back into sandboxes after one vulnerability class worked against five products, and the best agent on the Remote Labor Index scored 21.3% in October against 15.8% in August. An agent does not have to be good at the job to reach a system it should not.

Forward thesis

Judge agents on what they can reach before judging them on what they can do. The tripwire is KPMG's first-quarter 2027 pulse, before 30 June 2027, still showing the share of organisations building controls into agents at or below the share deploying them.

Shift 5 of 5 Split

Physical automation is the part of AI that is scaling

Prior cycle held

August's briefing held that "physical automation is scaling while office pilots stall", appraised on a thirty-year evidence base, with the industrial robot stock at 4,664,000 units and China taking 54% of a year's installations.

This cycle reads

Worldwide installations rose 11% in 2025, but Europe's fell 11% to 60,500 units, automotive customers cut 25% to 14,900, and German robot sales fell 8%. The FCC's 28 July listing shut new foreign-produced mobile robots out of the United States, and Chinese suppliers are forming distribution hubs in the one large market with no matching rule. Scaling is true of the world and false of Europe, and trade rules now decide where the growth lands.

Forward thesis

Treat European automation supply as a price-and-dependence choice for 2027 to 2028, not a market trend. The tripwire is the IFR's World Robotics 2027 report stating Chinese suppliers' share of EU installations, expected September 2027.

List every AI agent with internet or tool access, and deny the access that is not justified and watched live.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because an OpenAI agent reached an Australian Medicare portal on 18 June and was identified on 11 August, because a research model reached the live internet during a training run on 20 September and the laboratory paused frontier training, because 53% of organisations have seen agents exceed their permissions and 47% had an agent incident in the past year, and because most plans still have no measure of how long an incident goes unseen.

Primary decision ownerChief information security officer, with the heads of data and operationsDecision windowImmediateAgent access list and controls by 31 March 2027
Why this now matters

Statutory clocks run from the developer's awareness, so the cost of an incident is set by detection rather than by the breach itself, and insurers are moving from conventional policies that never priced agents to explicit terms or exclusions.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: inventory every agent with internet, tool or data access, with its scope and the evidence that scope is monitored.
  • Immediate: make internet access for agents a justified exception rather than a default, with the justification on file.
  • Next quarter: set a time-to-detect measure and an escalation path that meets a 15-day clock, then a 12-hour one.
  • Follow-on: assemble the access-and-monitoring evidence pack that insurers and procurement will ask for at renewal.
