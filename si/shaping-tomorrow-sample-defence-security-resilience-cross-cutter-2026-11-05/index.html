Rearmament Will Leave Companies Paying for Sabotage, Call-Ups and Shelters in 2027

Governments are claiming workers, plants, ships and basements for defence while leaving the cost with their owners, and a court has let insurers treat unattributed sabotage as war. The January renewals and Germany's first compulsory call-ups in 2027 will show who pays.

Early warnings

Signals the portfolio surfaced before consensus formed, in the order they hardened.

Lead time 9 weeks aheadSignal Scan Signal Scan Change Tracker

Sabotage losses with a suspected but unproven state link may stay with the operator when policies renew in 2027.

Initial signal

We identified in early September that deniable sabotage of European infrastructure falls between the war clause and the terrorism pool, so it is uninsured by default while the market redrafts its war wordings.

Now evident
  • The ruling: a court held that the Russia-Ukraine war only needed to be a significant cause of the Nord Stream loss, and found that met despite uncertainty about the perpetrator and outside an active war zone.
  • The attacks: German authorities recorded 165 suspected sabotage cases since the start of 2026, and 747 drone incidents from January to the end of August.
  • The capacity: global political violence and terrorism premium of around US$1.5 billion against insured loss estimates approaching double that, with many reinsurers declining critical infrastructure.
  • The response: Berlin's two new ordinances of 28 October tighten controls and staff vetting at critical sites, and say nothing about who bears a loss.
Insurance renewalsJanuary 2027US terrorism backstop (TRIPRA) expiresend of 2027
Decide

Finance chiefs of grid, pipeline, telecoms and defence-supply operators should settle in writing how their cover treats an unattributed attack.

Lead time 7 weeks aheadSignal Scan Change Tracker

Arms plants will be held to security standards raised after each attack, with little public money to meet them.

Initial signal

We read three governments telling the owners of drone and munitions plants to raise their own security after arson and explosions, with no designation, contract standard or funding line behind the request.

Now evident
  • Poland: an incendiary attack at WB Electronics caused damage of at least 15 million złoty, and the deputy interior minister promised recommendations and more police.
  • Bulgaria: after explosions at an Emco depot the interior minister said the state will step in, while the firm said the site had passed seven ministry inspections this year.
  • The money: the EU pays up to €500,000 per project for site protection, and duties for critical entities start in May 2027.
EU critical-entity duties startMay 2027
Prepare

Primes and tier-2 suppliers should cost the standard a ministry would impose after a fire at their own site and put it into current bids.

Lead time 6 weeks aheadSignal Scan

Employers will pay for most of the time their staff spend on German reserve call-ups from 2027.

Initial signal

We identified that Germany's Reserve Strengthening Act ends the employer's consent to call-ups while its impact statement finds that business compliance costs fall slightly.

Now evident
  • The veto: firms can no longer simply veto a call-up, and the Bundeswehr decides on deferral requests; call-up notice doubles to eight weeks.
  • The gap: replacement costs are repaid at about €76 to €190 a day for at most 30 days and one exercise a year, against annual caps of three to twelve weeks.
  • The scale: the 7,500 reserve posts funded for 2026 equal about 2.73 million service days, before a reserve that must reach at least 200,000 by 2033.
Employer consent endsearly 2027Reserve target200,000 by 2033
Prepare

HR and finance chiefs, above all in the Mittelstand, should map reserve liability and price a replacement day now.

Lead time 5 weeks aheadSignal Scan

NATO's reinforcement plans may find too few commercial ships and crews available when they are called on.

Initial signal

We read NATO's plans resting on the assumption that commercial shipping will be available when required, an assumption the Council on Geostrategy calls obsolete, while Washington pays to guarantee its own.

Now evident
  • The US: guaranteed access to 60 commercially operated ships and more than 2,400 trained mariners, at a $6.5 million retainer per vessel and $390 million in 2026.
  • NATO: its Assured Access Contract gave access to up to 15 Ro/Ro ships, and the Ankara declaration's only sea sentence concerns Hormuz.
  • Crews: a 15% shortfall in mariners for US emergency sealift, and only 38% of EU shipping-sector seafarers are EU citizens.
US retainer$6.5 million per shipNATO pooled contractup to 15 ships
Prepare

Shipowners, war-risk underwriters and movement planners should price a retainer and agree military-cargo war-risk terms before a crisis.

Defence orders will bunch up before the end of 2028, and suppliers' order books will lengthen faster than their output.

Initial signal

We identified that the EU's fiscal waiver for defence covers deliveries after 2028 only when they were contracted before the end of 2028, so ministries have reason to sign early whatever factories can build.

Now evident
  • The rule: the Commission's recommendation for Greece covers deliveries after 2028 only if contracted before end-2028, inside a cap of 1.5% of GDP.
  • The orders: Germany plans more than €60 billion of military procurement in 2027, against €45 billion this year.
  • The backlogs: Rheinmetall's backlog reached €80.5 billion, Saab's a record SEK 364.2 billion, and sector backlogs stand at more than five times revenue.

Assumptions that have shifted

Cycle-over-cycle assumption changes, in decreasing order of strategic weight.

Shift 1 of 5 Split

Permits and approvals, more than factory capacity, limit how fast Europe rearms

Prior cycle held

The August synthesis concluded that "the load-bearing variable for the next two years is permission rather than capacity", after the gates moved into recruitment, engagement authority, permitting and repair data.

This cycle reads

Both now bind. The permission gates have acquired dates and paperwork, and order books show that capacity still limits output.

  • Certificates: the faster EU transfer licence needs both parties certified, the law sets no deadline for deciding an application, and fifty-five firms were certified at the last count, in 2016.
  • US sourcing: from 1 January 2027 contractors lose non-availability waivers unless they file a funded plan to qualify a second source.
  • Capacity: the August call that order books would keep growing faster than output is on track: Rheinmetall's backlog of €80.5 billion sits against first-half sales of €5,227 million.
Forward thesis

Plan each delivery against both a dated permission and a capacity slot. The tripwire is a member state's certifying authority publishing criteria and a decision period before 30 June 2027, which would turn the certificate into a timetable.

Shift 2 of 5 Weakened

The money is settled, so delivery is the only question left

Prior cycle held

The August synthesis held that "money is no longer the question": budgets had cleared, and the open question was whether industry could absorb them.

This cycle reads

Money is settled in Germany and in the EU's loan instrument, which has begun paying out. It is not settled in the United Kingdom or in most smaller allies.

  • United Kingdom: funded spending at 2.6% of GDP in 2026–27 and 2.7% in 2027–28 and 2028–29, about £4.7 billion still to be identified before the 28 October Budget, and the date for 3% waiting on a 2027 spending review.
  • Allies: of 16 baseline NATO allies, few have published a roadmap for financing their budgets.
  • Germany: more than €60 billion of military procurement planned for 2027, against €45 billion this year.
Forward thesis

Treat the UK and the smaller allies as funded to a plateau, not a ramp. The tripwire is the Treasury confirming a defence spending review later than 30 April 2027.

Shift 3 of 5 Holding

Attacks on Europe's infrastructure will keep coming

Prior cycle held

The August synthesis graded "critical infrastructure under live attack" as the condition to plan under, with drones over NATO's eastern members and jamming at sea.

This cycle reads

The attacks continued and reached defence suppliers' own sites. The August call that shooting down drones would stay routine through the autumn is on track.

  • Airspace: Romania has logged 54 drone intrusions since Russia's full-scale invasion, 35 of them this year, and shot down six in 2026; NATO jets shot down a drone over Lithuania.
  • Sabotage: German authorities recorded 165 suspected sabotage cases in 2026 and two grid substations were targeted within 24 hours in early September.
  • Plants: arson at a Polish drone maker, explosions at a Bulgarian depot, a fire at an Estonian supplier under investigation and a foiled arson attempt in Slovakia, all within weeks.
Forward thesis

Plan for continued attack, and for the cost of protection and loss to fall on owners. The tripwire is a member state notifying a defence manufacturer as a critical entity, which would bring public standards, and possibly public money, to plant security.

Shift 4 of 5 Weakened

Investors in defence technology are locked in with no way out

Prior cycle held

The August synthesis held that "capital was trapped without exits", and the August briefing found the defence-tech boom funding very few winners.

This cycle reads

Private capital restarted after two cycles without movement. Helsing raised $1.8 billion and Quantum Systems €1 billion, and the October tracker moved defence-tech capital back to Stable. The money is still going to a few leaders.

Forward thesis

Treat late-stage money as open for a handful of leaders and still closed for most. The tripwire is a second round of comparable size going to a firm outside the current leaders.

Shift 5 of 5 Weakened

China's suspended export controls come back on 10 November

Prior cycle held

The August briefing called it: "China's suspended export controls will return on 10 November 2026, catching civil products in which Chinese-origin controlled inputs reach 0.1% of value."

This cycle reads

China's commerce ministry said on 28 September that the arrangement behind the suspension runs to 10 January 2027, but no notice had formally moved the 10 November date by 22 October. The rule is unchanged; its date is likely to move.

Forward thesis

Keep the screen and plan against the later date. The tripwire is a formal notice moving the expiry to 10 January 2027, or the controls taking effect on 10 November.

Decide Action 1 Signal Scan Signal Scan Change Tracker

Ask brokers in writing how the war exclusion would treat a sabotage loss with a suspected state link, and size the loss you would keep.

Trigger condition

This recommendation has been activated because a court applied a war exclusion to the Nord Stream loss despite uncertainty about the perpetrator, because German authorities recorded 165 suspected sabotage cases this year, because political violence premium runs at around half its loss estimates, and because most insurance programmes still treat sabotage as an insured peril.

Primary decision ownerChief financial officer, with the risk manager and brokerDecision windowImmediateBefore the January 2027 renewals
Why this now matters

A minister's verdict does not settle attribution, so the policy wording decides who pays, and the renewal is the last point to change it before a loss.

Recommended actions
  • Immediate: obtain a written broker position covering property, business interruption, cyber and political violence.
  • Immediate: size a retained-loss case for a multi-day outage at the most exposed site.
  • Next quarter: price standalone sabotage or political violence cover where the property policy may not respond.
  • Follow-on: carry the gap into lender covenants and continuity plans.
Review conditions
  • EscalateIf an insurer cites the Nord Stream judgment to decline or cut a European infrastructure sabotage claim, fund the retained loss in the 2027 budget and buy standalone cover.
  • Scale backIf a market body or major insurer publishes a clause on state-linked sabotage before 30 June 2027, renegotiate to that clause and drop the standalone search.
  • Stand downIf courts read war exclusions narrowly enough that grey-zone losses are paid under all-risk property policies, return to ordinary renewal.

Why this now matters

  • Immediate: map every cross-border supply link by certification status and decide which suppliers to sponsor.
  • Next quarter: start certification files, including the compliance programme the senior executive must countersign.
  • Next quarter: map each large programme by signature date, delivery slot and payer, and book supplier capacity before the cut-off crowds it.
  • Follow-on: fund second-source qualification for covered materials where US contracts depend on it.
