Why This Matters Now

Britain's reliance on American suppliers is usually discussed as a question about the future. Will the United States stay reliable? The evidence of the last eighteen months suggests a different question is overdue. The direction has already been set, by a series of separate decisions none of which announced itself as a choice about dependency.

The UK left the European defence financing instrument, deepened the American export-control arrangement, and consolidated its cloud buying into a single larger agreement. Each was defensible on its own terms. Together they amount to a settled position that no document states, no metric tracks and no minister has been asked to defend as a whole.

The one thing to take into the next meeting: the problem is not that Britain chose dependence, it is that nobody had to argue for it, so there is no statement of how much would be too much.

Executive Synthesis

Which document states how much dependence on a single foreign supplier this country is willing to carry, and who signed it?

The defence industrial strategy narrows sovereignty to a short list of protected capabilities and accepts allied supply elsewhere, the procurement policy statement contains no supplier origin requirement at all, and the national security debarment list has been open since February 2025 with nothing on it.

Take the eighteen months in order. In May 2025 the UK and the EU signed a Security and Defence Partnership that commits both sides to regular exchanges on defence industry and defers every industrial and procurement question to a later negotiation (the Security and Defence Partnership, May 2025). In June the Strategic Defence Review observed that the United States is clear the security of Europe is no longer its primary focus, and answered with NATO First (the Strategic Defence Review, Jun 2025). In September the Defence Industrial Strategy reserved full or majority UK industrial capability for a short list, the nuclear deterrent, submarines and cryptography, and accepted allied supply elsewhere, with an offset policy as the compensating mechanism (CP 1388, Sep 2025). In December the negotiation on joining the European financing instrument ended without agreement (the Paymaster General, Dec 2025), and the Paymaster General told the Commons he had chosen not to participate because it did not represent value for money (Hansard, Dec 2025). Thirteen days later the American export-control exemption took effect (the US Department of State, Dec 2025).

Each of those is defensible. What none of them does is state a position on dependence. The gap shows most clearly in what the December decision actually cost. Under third country terms UK firms may supply up to 35% of the content of a SAFE contract (the Paymaster General, Dec 2025), which sounds like access until you read why that number exists: the regulation requires at least 65% of component value to come from eligible European sources (Regulation (EU) 2025/1106, May 2025). The French Senate, examining the same instrument, records that the United States supplied 64% of the arms imported by European NATO states between 2020 and 2024 and argues that European preference has to be hardened into a condition of European money or Europeans remain subcontractors (the French Senate, Dec 2025). Britain is not inside that argument. It is the third country the rule is drawn against.

Meanwhile the dependency that would be hardest to unwind is not in defence at all. UK cloud spending reached 10.5 billion pounds in 2024, growing by nearly 30% a year, with two American firms holding between 60% and 80% of the market (POSTnote 777, Aug 2026). Government spends at least 14 billion pounds a year on digital, of which 9 billion ran through central Crown Commercial Service agreements (the National Audit Office, Jan 2025), and the Public Accounts Committee found the Government Commercial Function had fifteen people managing the nineteen largest technology suppliers (the Public Accounts Committee, Jun 2025). None of that was a strategic decision about the United States. It was routine central buying, extended and enlarged: G-Cloud 14 was extended by 1.65 billion pounds and six months, after a mid-contract uplift of 89% to one hyperscaler deal (DatacenterDynamics, Jul 2025), and its successor was put to market at 14 billion pounds (the tender notice, Oct 2025).

The problem is not that Britain chose dependence. It is that nobody had to argue for it, so there is no statement of how much would be too much.

What makes this the moment to say so is that other governments have stopped treating supply reliability as a diplomatic matter and started writing it into instruments. The European regulation requires contractors to be able to decide, without restrictions imposed by third countries, on the design of what is procured, including the legal authority to remove components subject to such restrictions (Regulation (EU) 2025/1106, May 2025). That is reliability as an engineering specification. France went further on the civil side: a decree made in April 2026 requires state bodies processing particularly sensitive data on private cloud to use a service holding the national security agency's qualification, and to be protected against access by third-country authorities (the French finance ministry, Apr 2026). The interministerial digital directorate has told every ministry to produce a dependency reduction plan by autumn in French, “L'État ne peut plus se contenter de constater sa dépendance, il doit en sortir”, which reads in English as “The State can no longer be content to observe its dependency, it must exit it” (the French interministerial digital directorate, Apr 2026). The European Commission has since proposed writing a European Preference chapter into the base procurement regulation, and is explicit that this sits inside the Government Procurement Agreement rather than against it (COM(2026) 590, Sep 2026).

Set the British instruments beside those. The National Procurement Policy Statement, which contracting authorities must have regard to, contains no supplier origin requirement and handles security as a supply chain control standard (the National Procurement Policy Statement, Feb 2025). The June 2026 policy note on protecting national security tells buyers they may “Deploy the national security exemption in the Procurement Act 2023 where appropriate and justified in a relevant procurement” (PPN 025, Jun 2026), names four critical sectors, shipbuilding, steel, artificial intelligence and energy infrastructure, and does not name cloud or digital infrastructure at all. The statutory machinery exists: a National Security Unit for Procurement, powers of exclusion, termination and debarment, live since February 2025. The published debarment list is blank (the National Security Unit for Procurement, Mar 2025). This is not an argument that Britain should copy France. It is an observation that Britain has chosen discretion, and discretion leaves no record of a decision.

The final piece is that the American arrangement Britain did deepen is narrower than it reads. The export-control exemption removes licensing for authorised users, and over 700 entities have been authorised, but the State Department's own assessment is that “it monitored licensing requests for Australia or the United Kingdom against the ETL over a three-month period and assesses approximately 18% of such licensing requests would not be eligible for transfer under the exemption because of the ETL” (the US Department of State, Dec 2025). Independent analysis of the reforms reached the same structural conclusion before the rule was final, that this is a free trade zone defined by country and not by technology, so the carve-outs fall on exactly the advanced work the arrangement exists to share (the United States Studies Centre, Aug 2024). On the flagship programme, the Public Accounts Committee records a formal UK policy requirement of freedom of action alongside a weapons integration path the UK cannot influence (the Public Accounts Committee, Oct 2025), while the American audit office reports that upgrade descoped, more than 6 billion dollars over and five years late (the Government Accountability Office, Sep 2025).

Where this analysis could be wrong

The value for money case may simply be right. Nothing in this evidence shows what the European instrument would have cost the UK to join, because that number is not public. A government that declined an expensive loan facility offering uncertain industrial return made a defensible call, and the Opposition's criticism of it was political rather than analytical. The narrower claim here is that the decision was justified on a transactional test, in a sentence about value for money, and that no assessment was published weighing it against the dependency position it locks in. That is a gap in the record, not proof of a wrong answer.

The American relationship is not only a liability, and the same committee that warned about it said so. The Lords committee recommending a twin-track hedge also welcomed the export-control exemptions as removing a significant regulatory barrier, and separately judged that Britain's own failure to meet initial order commitments has dented its reputation as a partner (the House of Lords International Relations and Defence Committee, Apr 2026). Dependence runs in both directions and the United States is not the only party whose reliability is in question. Any policy built on the assumption that Britain is the aggrieved side will be badly calibrated.

The legal reading here is one reading. The non-discrimination duty owed to treaty state suppliers under the Procurement Act would constrain a European preference rule (Procurement Act guidance, Sep 2024), but the European Commission's own proposal shows that a party to the same trade agreement believes preference can be designed within it (COM(2026) 590, Sep 2026). Whether the UK could do likewise is a question for law officers rather than for this briefing, and it is raised as a question rather than settled. One further caveat on evidence: two figures in the framework theme, the single-bidder award value and the mid-contract uplift, rest on trade press because the official tender records could not be retrieved, and the appendix says so.

The decisions that cannot be deferred this cycle

  1. Ask for the number that does not exist. No published measure states what share of defence or digital procurement flows to US-controlled suppliers, so no one can say whether the position is getting better or worse.
  2. Treat the blank debarment list as a finding, not an absence. Powers of exclusion and debarment have been live since February 2025 and have produced nothing on the published list, which is either evidence that no supplier warrants it or evidence that the lever is unusable.
  3. Decide whether reliability is a specification or a relationship. Europe has begun writing design authority and third-country access protection into binding rules, and Britain has not, which is a choice worth making deliberately rather than by default.
  4. Put cloud inside the national security conversation. The June policy note names shipbuilding, steel, artificial intelligence and energy infrastructure as critical sectors, and omits the cloud estate that carries most of government's data.

Each is developed below, with a decision posture, in the four Strategic Implications.

Themes

The cycle's signals are organised into four themes, ranked by impact on near-term decisions. Immediate: changes the near-term plan, the structure or the proposition. Near-Term: changes position over the next twelve months. Longer-Range: a multi-year factor to track and revisit each cycle.

1. Britain has already chosen, and did not call it a choice

Immediate

Between May 2025 and September 2026 the United Kingdom took a series of decisions that together settle its supply relationship with the United States for the rest of the decade. It signed a European security partnership that defers industrial questions (the Security and Defence Partnership, May 2025), published a defence review organised around NATO First (the Strategic Defence Review, Jun 2025), published an industrial strategy narrowing sovereign capability to a protected list (CP 1388, Sep 2025), walked away from the European financing instrument (the Paymaster General, Dec 2025), and let the American export-control exemption come into force (the US Department of State, Dec 2025). No single one of those was framed as a decision about dependence. Together they are one.

  • The Paymaster General told the Commons he chose not to participate in the European instrument because it did not represent value for money (Hansard, Dec 2025). That is the whole of the published reasoning, and no assessment weighing it against the resulting supply position has been laid.
  • Third country access lets UK firms supply up to 35% of the content of a contract under that instrument (the Paymaster General, Dec 2025). The figure is the residue of the regulation's requirement that at least 65% of component value come from eligible European sources (Regulation (EU) 2025/1106, May 2025).
  • The Security and Defence Partnership contains no reference to procurement, industrial capability, programmes or financial contribution, and one reference to industry (the Security and Defence Partnership, May 2025). That absence was checked against the text rather than inferred.
  • The Defence Industrial Strategy reserves full or majority UK capability for the nuclear deterrent, submarines and cryptography, accepts allied supply for everything else, and offers an offset policy as the compensating mechanism (CP 1388, Sep 2025).
  • Official statistics record 623 million pounds of Foreign Military Sales payments to the US Government in 2024-25, plus 1,202 million pounds to the US Government and 372 million to the US Treasury, described as predominantly F-35 and Trident (MOD official statistics, Sep 2025). There is no published headline figure for total UK spend with US-based suppliers.

Early indicators

  • Weak signal The instrument Britain declined is now the venue for the argument it is not in. The French Senate, examining the same regulation, records that the United States supplied 64% of arms imported by European NATO states between 2020 and 2024, and argues European preference must be hardened into a condition of European money (the French Senate, Dec 2025). That debate will set the terms on which British firms sell into Europe, and Britain is outside it as the third country the rule is drawn against.
  • Weak signal The trade arrangement underneath all this is explicitly not binding. Parliament's own research service describes the Economic Prosperity Deal as legally non-binding and only partially implemented, with the metals exemption contingent on the progress of negotiations (the House of Commons Library, Apr 2026). A supply relationship resting on a conditional arrangement is a different thing from one resting on a treaty.

Counter-argument

The strongest objection is that this reads a pattern into decisions that were each taken on their merits by different departments over sixteen months, which is how government works and is not evidence of drift. It is also possible the European instrument genuinely was poor value, and nothing here shows otherwise, because the price was never published. The claim made is narrower than a charge of negligence: it is that no instrument states an acceptable degree of dependence, no measure tracks it, and therefore no one is in a position to say whether the accumulated position is the one they would have chosen. The thesis weakens the moment any department publishes a dependency assessment covering more than one programme.

2. The dependency that is load bearing was bought through frameworks

Immediate

The dependency most discussed is in defence. The dependency hardest to reverse is in the cloud estate, and it was never decided at all. It was bought through central buying agreements, extended administratively and enlarged mid-contract, in a market where two American firms hold most of the supply. This is where an adviser looking for the real exposure should look, because it is the part with the least deliberation behind it and the most switching cost in front of it.

  • UK cloud spending reached 10.5 billion pounds in 2024 after growing nearly 30% a year since 2020, with Amazon Web Services and Microsoft holding between 60% and 80% of the market (POSTnote 777, Aug 2026).
  • Government spends at least 14 billion pounds a year on digital programmes and technology, of which 9 billion ran through central Crown Commercial Service agreements in 2022-23, and three providers hold over 60% of global cloud provision (the National Audit Office, Jan 2025).
  • The Public Accounts Committee found the approach to buying cloud fragmented and not right, and recorded fifteen officials dedicated to managing the nineteen largest technology suppliers (the Public Accounts Committee, Jun 2025).
  • The channel itself compounds. G-Cloud 14 was extended by six months and 1.65 billion pounds, following a mid-contract uplift of 89% to one hyperscaler hosting deal (DatacenterDynamics, Jul 2025), and its successor went to market consolidating three vehicles at a value of 14 billion pounds (the tender notice, Oct 2025).
  • The competition regulator decided in March 2026 to open a market status investigation into one firm's business software and not into the largest cloud provider, accepting voluntary commitments on egress fees and interoperability instead (the Competition and Markets Authority, Mar 2026). No enforceable cloud remedy exists.

Early indicators

  • Weak signal The government's own answer is a demand signal, not a rule. The 2026 cloud challenge book tells the market that single in-country regions are not sufficient, that public cloud as implemented today struggles against the most capable adversaries, and that approaches must avoid structural lock-in (the Cloud Challenge Book, Jul 2026). It is an invitation to suppliers and it obliges no department to do anything.
  • Weak signal The legal exposure is real and the traffic runs the other way. American law can compel US providers to hand over data held in their UK data centres (the House of Commons Library, Mar 2026). But Parliament's science office records that the UK used the bilateral data access agreement 20,142 times between 2022 and 2024 against 63 US uses (POSTnote 777, Aug 2026). The dependency is genuine; the picture of a one-directional American reach into British data is not.

Counter-argument

It is fair to answer that hyperscale cloud is simply better, that no British or European provider offers the same capability at the same price, and that concentration is the normal shape of a market with these economics. The regulator, having looked, accepted commitments rather than imposing remedies, which is a considered judgement by the body best placed to make it. The argument here is not that the buying was wrong. It is that a position of this size was reached without anyone being required to state what degree of concentration was acceptable, and that administrative extension is a mechanism for compounding commitments without a decision point. The thesis weakens if the successor award publishes concentration limits or exit terms.

3. Reliability is becoming a specification rather than a diplomatic question

Near-Term

The most consequential shift in this evidence base is not about American behaviour. It is that other governments have stopped treating supplier reliability as something to be managed through relationships and started writing it into rules, contracts and designs. Once reliability is a specification, a country that treats it as a relationship is not making a different choice. It is failing to make one.

  • The European regulation requires contractors to be able to decide, without restrictions imposed by third countries or third-country entities, on the definition and evolution of the design of what is procured, including the legal authority to substitute or remove restricted components (Regulation (EU) 2025/1106, May 2025). Reliability is written as design authority.
  • The American exemption is bounded by a list. The State Department assesses that “it monitored licensing requests for Australia or the United Kingdom against the ETL over a three-month period and assesses approximately 18% of such licensing requests would not be eligible for transfer under the exemption because of the ETL” (the US Department of State, Dec 2025), and over 700 Australian and British entities have become authorised users.
  • Independent analysis before the rule was final concluded the reforms would exempt 70% to 80% of trilateral trade while producing a zone defined by country rather than technology, so the carve-outs fall on the advanced work the arrangement exists to share (the United States Studies Centre, Aug 2024).
  • On the flagship programme the Public Accounts Committee records a formal UK requirement of freedom of action alongside weapons integration dependent on software the UK cannot influence (the Public Accounts Committee, Oct 2025), while the American audit office reports that upgrade descoped, over 6 billion dollars above estimate and at least five years late (the Government Accountability Office, Sep 2025).
  • A French government body reports that a single controlled component can contaminate an entire system and that restrictions may follow for commercial as well as strategic reasons, and records that only 52% of European NATO contracts between February 2022 and September 2024 concerned European systems (the Haut-commissariat à la Stratégie et au Plan, Jun 2026).

Early indicators

  • Weak signal One allied buyer has already shown what treating this as a contract looks like. Told to expect a Patriot delivery delay of five to seven years, Switzerland suspended payments and went back to the market: in German, “Aufgrund dieser Unsicherheiten hält die Schweiz an der Sistierung der Zahlungen an die US-Regierung für Patriot fest”, which reads in English as “Because of these uncertainties Switzerland is maintaining its suspension of payments to the US Government for Patriot” (the Swiss defence department, May 2026), with responses sought from five further suppliers offering German, French, Israeli and South Korean systems. Switzerland is neither an EU nor a NATO member, so the case does not transfer cleanly, but the behaviour is the point.
  • Weak signal Britain's own committee has already recommended the hedge. The Lords committee recommends engaging with the United States on shared interests while hedging against US volatility through increased sovereign capability and cooperation with European allies (the House of Lords International Relations and Defence Committee, Apr 2026). The recommendation exists. What does not exist is an instrument that would carry it.

Counter-argument

The honest counter is that specification is cheap and capability is not. A design authority clause does not build an alternative engine, radar or satellite constellation, and a country that writes stringent third-country conditions into its rules may simply find that nothing compliant exists at an affordable price. The same Lords committee that recommended hedging welcomed the export-control exemption as removing a significant regulatory barrier (the House of Lords International Relations and Defence Committee, Apr 2026), which is the honest recognition that access to American technology is worth something. The claim is not that Britain should adopt European-style conditions. It is that reliability is now being priced and contracted for elsewhere, and that a country with no position on it will be a price taker.

4. Europe legislates sovereignty, Britain leaves it to the buyer

Near-Term

The clearest contrast in this cycle is institutional rather than political. Continental governments are converting sovereignty preferences into binding requirements: certification thresholds, origin tests, mandated reduction plans. Britain has the machinery to do something similar and has chosen instead to empower individual buyers to exercise judgement. That is a legitimate choice. Its consequence is that nothing is recorded, nothing accumulates and no one can be asked to account for the aggregate.

  • A French decree made in April 2026 requires state bodies processing particularly sensitive data on private cloud to use a service holding the national security agency's qualification or an equivalent European certification, and to be protected against access by third-country public authorities (the French finance ministry, Apr 2026). It is a legal precondition of the contract rather than a buyer's judgement.
  • The interministerial digital directorate requires every ministry and its operators to produce a dependency reduction plan by autumn 2026 covering workstations, collaborative tools, antivirus, artificial intelligence, databases, virtualisation and network equipment (the French interministerial digital directorate, Apr 2026).
  • The European cloud framework grades sovereignty into four assurance levels, reaching providers owned and controlled from the EU at level three and full software supply chain control with no third-country interference at level four (the European Commission, Jun 2026).
  • In September 2026 the Commission proposed replacing the procurement directives with a single regulation carrying a European Preference chapter, and stated repeatedly that the mechanism operates within the Government Procurement Agreement (COM(2026) 590, Sep 2026).
  • The British statutory statement that authorities must have regard to contains no supplier origin requirement, treating security as a supply chain control standard (the National Procurement Policy Statement, Feb 2025), and the June 2026 policy note is permissive, naming shipbuilding, steel, artificial intelligence and energy infrastructure as critical sectors while omitting cloud (PPN 025, Jun 2026).

Early indicators

  • Weak signal The lever has been available for nineteen months and the list is empty. The National Security Unit for Procurement has overseen powers of exclusion, termination and debarment since February 2025, exclusion on national security grounds requires ministerial agreement, and the published debarment list remains blank (the National Security Unit for Procurement, Mar 2025). That is either a finding about suppliers or a finding about the instrument, and which it is has not been established.
  • Weak signal Germany shows there is a third option, and how slow it is. The federal government reports 8,756 openDesk licences in the federal administration and that of 577 software development contracts awarded in 2025, 109 were commissioned as open source and only 42 published openly (the German federal government, Apr 2026). Supply-side substitution avoids the legal difficulty of an origin rule and, on the government's own figures, moves slowly.

Counter-argument

The serious objection is legal rather than political. The Procurement Act owes a non-discrimination duty to suppliers of every state with which the UK has a relevant agreement, so a European preference rule could not simply be announced (Procurement Act guidance, Sep 2024). Discretion may therefore be less a philosophy than a constraint. Against that, the European Commission has just proposed preference inside the base procurement statute while insisting it is compatible with the same international agreement (COM(2026) 590, Sep 2026), which suggests the constraint shapes the design rather than forbidding it. The thesis weakens if law officers publish advice that the UK's commitments are materially narrower than the EU's.

Strategic Implications

Four implications, each tied to a theme and each carrying a posture. Decide means the decision is live now and deferring it is itself a choice. Prepare means the work has to start now for a decision that lands later. Monitor means the evidence does not yet support acting, and says what would change that.

SI 1: Commission the dependency measure that does not exist

The official statistics record Foreign Military Sales payments and collaborative programme payments as accounting lines, with no headline figure for total spend with US-controlled suppliers (MOD official statistics, Sep 2025), and the position across digital is assembled from an audit report and two library briefings rather than from any single published measure (the National Audit Office, Jan 2025). Without a number there is no trend, and without a trend every individual procurement decision looks locally reasonable. This is the cheapest of the four recommendations and the one everything else depends on.

Action: Specify what a published dependency measure would contain: share of spend by ultimate supplier control rather than contracting entity, coverage of defence and digital together, and treatment of framework call-offs. Then identify who could be asked to own it, whether the audit office, the Cabinet Office or a select committee, and ask the question in a form that produces a dated answer.

Decide Draws on Theme 1

SI 2: Attach conditions at the award, because there is no later moment

The successor cloud agreement went to market at 14 billion pounds, consolidating three vehicles (the tender notice, Oct 2025), after its predecessor grew by administrative extension and a mid-contract uplift of 89% to one hosting deal (DatacenterDynamics, Jul 2025). The regulator has declined to impose cloud remedies and accepted voluntary commitments (the Competition and Markets Authority, Mar 2026). Once departments migrate, switching is expensive, complex and disruptive on the Committee's own evidence (the Public Accounts Committee, Jun 2025). The award is the decision point, and there is not another one.

Action: Press for the award to carry things a later negotiation cannot recover: published exit and data egress terms, a concentration measure reported annually, and a standing requirement that call-offs above a threshold record why an alternative was not viable. None of that requires an origin rule or new legislation.

Decide Draws on Theme 2

SI 3: Decide whether the UK will specify reliability or keep negotiating it

The European instrument requires design authority free of third-country restriction as a condition of funding (Regulation (EU) 2025/1106, May 2025), France has made a certification a legal precondition for sensitive data (the French finance ministry, Apr 2026), and one allied buyer has already suspended payments and reopened a competition when an American delivery date slipped (the Swiss defence department, May 2026). Britain has a formal requirement of freedom of action on its flagship programme and an upgrade path it cannot influence (the Public Accounts Committee, Oct 2025). The gap between those two facts is the policy question.

Action: Draft, as a test, the clause the UK would use if it decided reliability were a specification: what design authority would mean in a British contract, which capabilities it would apply to, and what it would cost in supplier choice. The point of drafting it is to find out whether the reason Britain has not done this is principle, law or capability, because the answer determines everything downstream.

Prepare Draws on Theme 3

SI 4: Treat the empty debarment list as the test of whether discretion works

The powers exist. Exclusion, termination and debarment have been available since February 2025 through a dedicated unit, exclusion on national security grounds requires ministerial agreement, and the published list is blank (the National Security Unit for Procurement, Mar 2025). The June 2026 policy note is permissive and omits cloud from its critical sectors (PPN 025, Jun 2026), and the statutory statement carries no origin requirement (the National Procurement Policy Statement, Feb 2025). Either no supplier has warranted action, which would be worth knowing, or the lever is not usable, which would be worth knowing sooner.

Action: Watch three things over the next two cycles: whether any entry appears on the debarment list, whether cloud or digital infrastructure is added to the named critical sectors, and whether the European preference chapter survives into the adopted EU regulation (COM(2026) 590, Sep 2026). The third matters most, because it will settle whether a Government Procurement Agreement party can lawfully prefer on origin, which is currently the strongest argument that Britain cannot.

Scenario Matrix

Two uncertainties shape the next four years. The first is whether Britain acquires any instrument that states a position on supplier dependence, or continues to leave it to individual buyers. The second is whether the United States behaves in ways that force the question, through export control, delivery or terms. The second is not in British hands. The first is entirely.

The comfortable default

Nothing forces the issue. Agreements renew, the exemption works well enough for most trade, and the debarment list stays empty (the National Security Unit for Procurement, Mar 2025). Dependence deepens quietly because every individual decision is defensible and nothing aggregates them. This is the current path.

Early indicators: Successor agreement awarded without published exit or concentration terms; no dependency measure commissioned; critical sector list unchanged.

Improvising under pressure

A licence refusal, a delivery slip or a tariff move forces a response Britain has no prepared instrument for. Decisions get made quickly, department by department, under the same discretion that produced the position. The Swiss example shows what a prepared buyer does (the Swiss defence department, May 2026); improvisation is what an unprepared one does.

Early indicators: An export licence or delivery dispute becomes public; departments diverge in their responses; emergency use of the national security exemption (PPN 025, Jun 2026).

Deliberate dependence

Britain publishes a measure and a threshold and concludes, on the evidence, that substantial American supply is the right answer. Dependence is unchanged and its status is transformed, because it has been argued for, costed and signed. This is the outcome the briefing is arguing for, and it is not the same as reducing dependence.

Early indicators: A published dependency assessment covering defence and digital; an explicit ministerial statement of acceptable concentration; awards carrying reported measures.

Hedged and ready

The hedge the Lords committee recommended exists in instruments rather than in prose (the House of Lords International Relations and Defence Committee, Apr 2026), so disruption meets a prepared response: known alternatives, contract terms that permit substitution, and a position on design authority (Regulation (EU) 2025/1106, May 2025). Expensive to build and only visible in value when tested.

Early indicators: Design authority language appears in a UK contract; alternative suppliers qualified in advance; cloud added to the critical sectors.

What We Are Not Planning For

Out of scope this cycle

A British Buy European or Buy British rule

No instrument in this evidence base proposes one, and the statutory non-discrimination duty owed to treaty state suppliers would have to be addressed first (Procurement Act guidance, Sep 2024). The briefing examines whether Britain has a position on dependence, which is a prior and different question from whether it should prefer a bloc.

↺ Reinstated if the European preference chapter is adopted in the EU regulation (COM(2026) 590, Sep 2026) and the UK publishes any assessment of whether equivalent design is open to it.

Out of scope this cycle

The AUKUS submarine programme

The submarine pillar is a multi-decade capability programme with its own governance and its own parliamentary scrutiny, and treating it as an instance of procurement dependence would obscure both. This cycle uses the export-control exemption and the combat air programme, where the instruments are visible and dated.

↺ Reinstated if a scrutiny body publishes findings on submarine programme supply dependence comparable to the F-35 work (the Public Accounts Committee, Oct 2025).

Deliberately not modelled

A rupture in the relationship

Scenarios in which the United States withdraws cooperation wholesale are not modelled. Nothing in the evidence supports them, the Lords committee found a transactional relationship rather than a failing one (the House of Lords International Relations and Defence Committee, Apr 2026), and modelling a rupture would crowd out the more likely and more actionable case, which is friction at the margins of an arrangement Britain cannot adjust.

↺ Reinstated if a documented refusal of a capability transfer to the UK, or suspension of an existing programme obligation.

Questions For The Next Strategy Meeting

Five questions this cycle's evidence should put on the agenda.

  1. What proportion of UK defence and digital procurement flows to suppliers ultimately controlled from the United States, and if nobody can answer that, who should be asked to (MOD official statistics, Sep 2025)?
  2. The powers of exclusion and debarment have been live since February 2025 and the published list is empty (the National Security Unit for Procurement, Mar 2025). Is that a fact about suppliers or a fact about the lever?
  3. The successor cloud agreement is a 14 billion pound buying channel replacing three others (the tender notice, Oct 2025). What conditions attach at award, and is there any later moment at which they could be attached instead?
  4. Europe has written design authority free of third-country restriction into binding law (Regulation (EU) 2025/1106, May 2025). What would the equivalent British clause say, and what would it cost us in supplier choice?
  5. The June policy note names shipbuilding, steel, artificial intelligence and energy infrastructure as critical sectors (PPN 025, Jun 2026). On what basis was the cloud estate that carries most government data left out?

Claim-fidelity self-disclosure

This appendix records where the evidence base is thinner than a source count suggests, and what was done about it.

Four themes, where the format carries five or six. The analyst commissioning this cycle asked for four. The structure audit flags that as a deviation rather than a pass, and no fifth theme was invented to satisfy the format.

A minister's sentence is summarised rather than quoted, and here it is. The clearest statement of why the UK left the European instrument is the Paymaster General's, and as printed in Hansard it contains two em-dashes, which this house's orthography rule forbids. Altering a quotation to satisfy a formatting rule is not permitted, so the body summarises it and the exact wording is recorded in the source notes (Hansard, Dec 2025). Separately, the widely reported characterisation of the EU's terms as extortionate belongs to the Opposition spokesman in that exchange and not to the minister, and is not used here.

Two figures rest on trade press because the official records could not be retrieved. The single-bidder award value and the mid-contract hosting uplift (Computer Weekly, Mar 2026) (DatacenterDynamics, Jul 2025) are reported by specialist publications. The underlying tender and modification notices exist, but the Find a Tender service returned repeated errors and a mirror refused the request, so those records are not verified here and their URLs are deliberately not cited. Both figures are carried as trade reporting, and anyone relying on them should pull the notices directly.

One central text is summarised rather than quoted, deliberately. The European regulation's design authority provision is the analytical centre of the third theme. It was read and confirmed in the Official Journal, but only in the PDF: two HTML routes to the same instrument returned unrelated documents. A quotation taken from the PDF would be exempted by the systemic override for binary files rather than actually checked by the provenance gate, so the provision is reproduced almost in full in the source record and paraphrased in the body instead (Regulation (EU) 2025/1106, May 2025).

Absences are used as evidence three times, and each was checked. The Security and Defence Partnership contains no procurement or industrial language (the Security and Defence Partnership, May 2025), the Strategic Defence Review does not mention the European instrument (the Strategic Defence Review, Jun 2025), and the procurement policy statement contains no supplier origin requirement (the National Procurement Policy Statement, Feb 2025). Each of those is a verified negative from a read of the document, not an inference from a summary. An absence is weaker evidence than a statement, and the body says so where it relies on one.

Twelve of the thirty-four sources sit outside the twelve-month window. They are carried as structural anchors and each is named as one in the register. On this subject that category is doing real work, because the instruments that govern the question are standing law, standing statements and multi-year programmes rather than recent reporting. Two were reclassified as anchors during the audit, the American audit office's annual programme review and the standing guidance on the procurement security unit, both of which are recurring or permanent rather than reports of an event.
