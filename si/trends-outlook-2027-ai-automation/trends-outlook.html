You Don’t Have to Use AI to Be Caught by It in 2027

Edition 1. One topic a week to mid-December, looking ahead to 2027 and beyond from a year of our deliverables.

In 2027 the tightest limits on AI will come from outside the technology: insurance policies, power supplies, chip supplies, payment rules and government approvals. Most of them will reach businesses that have never built an AI product. Below are eight developments we flagged during 2026, when each is likely to reach your desk, and who should take the decision.

Websites are starting to charge AI companies to read them

What’s happening

Cloudflare, which sits in front of about a fifth of the world’s websites, now blocks AI data-gathering bots by default on many ad-supported pages and is testing payment per AI answer (Remote Work Europe, September 2026).

What to expect

Any business whose prices, listings or product details feed AI tools needs a policy: block, charge or allow.

AI has bought up the memory chips that phones, cars and PCs need

What’s happening

Three companies make almost all the world’s memory chips and have promised years of output to AI. Prices are on course to rise more than 400% between 2024 and the end of 2026.

What to expect

No relief before the end of 2027. Carmakers and medical-device makers are hit hardest because they cannot switch suppliers quickly.

AI is getting cheaper per question but dearer per job

What’s happening

Each AI answer costs less, but AI “agents” that work through a task step by step can use 5 to 30 times as much computing. 93% of firms surveyed are over their AI budgets (McKinsey, September 2026).

What to expect

Gartner expects more than 40% of agent projects to be cancelled by the end of 2027.

AI can now buy things, but nobody has decided who pays when it gets it wrong

What’s happening

Visa, Mastercard and American Express now let AI assistants buy things. The card industry’s new draft standard still does not say who pays for a mistake (Finovate, September 2026).

What to expect

Card rules follow in 2027 and laws from 2028. Until then, sellers who cannot prove what the customer asked for are likely to lose.

The AI building boom is running on borrowing that is hard to see

What’s happening

The biggest tech firms are on course to spend more on AI data centres than their businesses bring in (Epoch AI, June 2026), so more is borrowed in ways that stay off headline debt figures, often from insurers and pension funds.

What to expect

From 5 October AI computing gets a public daily price (CME Group, August 2026). If it falls, pension funds and insurers take the first losses.

The question 2027 will ask: does AI pay?

This year the best-known study of AI’s effect on skilled work was withdrawn by its own authors, and their later research finds people overestimate the time AI saves them by about 40 percentage points. 89% of organisations use AI, but only 37% see any positive effect on profit (McKinsey, September 2026). By the end of 2027, expect boards, insurers and regulators to ask for before-and-after evidence on every AI project.
