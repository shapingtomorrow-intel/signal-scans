In 2027 Europe’s defence bill will reach firms that have never made a weapon

Edition 2. One topic a week to Christmas, looking ahead to 2027 and beyond from a year of our deliverables.

The question for 2027

How much of Europe’s defence will companies pay for?

Rearmament is passing costs the state used to carry, from sabotage losses to staff called up, to firms that have never sold a weapon.

In 2027 Europe’s rearmament will reach companies that have never sold a weapon. It will arrive through insurance renewals, call-ups, shelter requests, sourcing rules and a queue for factory capacity, as America decides how much protection to withdraw. Below are eight developments from our 2026 deliverables, when each is likely to reach your desk, and who should decide.

Key risks and opportunities

What could go wrong, and what could go right, as these land. Each links to the card that holds the evidence.

Key risks

  • Losses that stay with whoever was attacked. If insurers treat suspected state sabotage as war, the grid operator, cable owner or plant that was hit is likely to carry the cost, and its lenders with it; the chain to watch is How sabotage losses could land on companies. See signal 1 →
  • Key people away when the state calls. Firms whose engineers, drivers and technicians are reservists could lose them for weeks, at moments the armed forces choose. See signal 5 →
  • Civilian buyers pushed down the queue. If ministries rush to sign before fiscal cover ends, makers of cars, machinery and buildings that use the same steel, chemicals and electronics may wait longer and pay more. See signal 8 →

Key opportunities

  • Orders for what America stops providing. Each US capability Europe replaces, from air defence to transport and satellites, is likely to turn into orders for European suppliers able to deliver it. See signal 2 →
  • Winning the work others give up. Suppliers that can show where their materials come from stand a better chance of winning US work that firms without a second source walk away from. See signal 4 →
  • Helping write the shelter rules. Owners who already know what their basements and car parks could hold, and on what terms, are likely to shape the rules rather than receive them. See signal 6 →

What 2027 brings

Numbered in the order the decisions fall. Red numbers need a decision now, amber next quarter, blue in the next planning cycle.

Insurers may treat sabotage nobody can prove as an act of war

What’s happening

A London court applied a war exclusion to the €580 million Nord Stream claim without naming who set the blasts (Stewarts Law, August 2026). Germany has logged 165 suspected sabotage cases this year (hromadske, September 2026).

What to expect

The January 2027 renewals are the first test: a site hit by an attack blamed on a hostile state, but never attributed in court, may go unpaid.

Before the January 2027 renewals

2Decision windowImmediateto Dec 2026

America is deciding how many of its troops to pull out of Europe

What’s happening

The Pentagon is weighing pulling 25,000 to 40,000 of the roughly 80,000 US troops out of Europe (The Express Tribune, September 2026), in a review expected to wrap up in early November (Breaking Defense, November 2026).

What to expect

Whatever the US withdraws, Europe must buy: a Kiel Institute paper puts replacing critical US capabilities at €150 billion to €200 billion by 2030 (Defense News, May 2026).

3Decision windowImmediateto Dec 2026

China’s pause on export rules for rare-earth magnets is running out

What’s happening

China’s pause on rules requiring a licence for magnets made abroad with 0.1% or more Chinese rare earths by value ends on 10 November, and no notice had extended it by late October (Nami Tech Solutions, October 2026).

What to expect

The wider US–China arrangement now runs to 10 January 2027 without naming these rules (Xinhua, September 2026), so buyers of magnets and the motors built with them should plan for either date.

4Decision windowImmediateto Dec 2026

From January, US defence buyers stop excusing suppliers that can’t source at home

What’s happening

From 1 January 2027 the US stops waiving home-sourcing rules for critical materials unless a supplier funds a plan to qualify another source, and it wants every material traced to its origin (Federal Register, July 2026).

What to expect

Suppliers several tiers down, including European makers of castings, magnets and machined parts, are likely to be asked to prove where their materials come from.

5Decision windowNext quarterJan–Mar 2027

German employers will lose the right to say no when staff are called up

What’s happening

Germany’s reserve bill, debated in the Bundestag on 15 October (Deutscher Bundestag, October 2026), ends the employer’s right to refuse a call-up and aims for at least 200,000 reservists by 2033 (BMVg, July 2026).

What to expect

Once in force, planned for 2027, firms must release reservists for three to twelve weeks a year, with only part of the cost of cover repaid (Personalwirtschaft, August 2026).

6Decision windowNext quarterJan–Mar 2027

Owners of basements and car parks will be asked to shelter the public

What’s happening

Germany has 579 public shelters, none of them operational, and plans to record basements, car parks and metro stations instead (Deutscher Bundestag, July 2026); Poland counts 85,000 shelter points (Notes from Poland, September 2026).

What to expect

Requests will reach owners before anyone says who pays for upkeep and access: Germany’s budget pays to find spaces and names no split of costs with owners (Deutscher Bundestag, July 2026).

Britain has named 2030 for 3% of GDP on defence, but not found the money

What’s happening

The 28 October Budget closed a £4.7 billion gap in the defence plan by moving money within government and set 2030 for 3% of GDP, leaving the funding to the 2027 Spending Review (Navy Lookout, October 2026).

What to expect

Until then defence holds at about 2.7% of GDP from 2027–28 (Mishcon de Reya, October 2026), so UK suppliers and lenders should plan on a plateau, not a ramp.

Defence orders will crowd into 2027 and 2028, and civilian buyers may queue

What’s happening

EU fiscal room for defence after 2028 covers only deliveries contracted by the end of 2028 (European Commission, September 2026), while Germany plans more than €60 billion of military procurement in 2027 (Deutscher Bundestag, September 2026).

What to expect

With backlogs above five times revenue (Allianz Trade, October 2026), ministries are likely to sign early, and suppliers shared with civilian buyers, from steel to electronics, to fill up first.

Back to the question: how much of Europe’s defence will companies pay for?

More than any budget shows. Germany’s reserve bill says the cost to business falls slightly, yet the chambers of commerce count about 2.73 million service days for this year’s reserve posts alone (DIHK, June 2026). By the end of 2027, expect insurers and governments to settle who carries sabotage losses, and employers and property owners to learn what the state’s requests cost them.
