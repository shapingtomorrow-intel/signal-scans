In 2027 More of the Cost of Care Lands on Employers and Families

Preview of edition 7, shared for feedback. One topic a week to Christmas, from a year of our deliverables.

Some medicines get cheaper in 2027, but care and cover get harder to find, and more of the cost moves to employers, families and clinicians. It will reach firms far from health through benefit bills, staff who become carers, insurance and travel. Below are eight developments we flagged in 2026, when each will reach your desk, and who should decide.

Key risks and opportunities

What could go wrong, and what could go right, as these land. Each links to the card that holds the evidence.

Key risks

  • Working people losing cover over paperwork. Each state’s checks, more than work itself, will decide who loses cover, so one employer’s staff may fare very differently. See signal 4 →
  • Staff who quietly become carers. As care gets harder to qualify for, more staff will cut hours or quit to care for a parent. See signal 5 →
  • Outbreaks that run longer. Thinner aid and slower sharing of virus samples delay tests and vaccines, with knock-on effects on travel and supply. See signal 7 →

Key opportunities

  • Suppliers who stand behind their AI. While clinicians carry the risk, AI suppliers that accept liability for errors are likely to win cautious health buyers. See signal 3 →
  • Work for factories and suppliers. An EU law favouring European-made medicines, and US pressure to make drugs locally, bring work for manufacturers and suppliers. See signal 6 →
  • Treatment for far more people at lower cost. Generic weight-loss drugs let insurers, employers and health systems reach far more people for less, if they plan for cash sales. See signal 8 →

US employers face their biggest health-cost rise in over 20 years

What’s happening

US employers expect health costs per employee to rise 8.2% in 2027, the most since 2003 (Marsh, September 2026). Over 20,000 firms now give staff a sum to buy their own cover (Becker’s Payer, August 2026).

What to expect

Staff sent to buy their own cover face insurers proposing a typical 15% rise for 2027 (Peterson-KFF, July 2026), so expect complaints to come back to employers.

What’s happening

The US has confirmed 3,887 measles cases this year (CDC, October 2026), and the Americas’ health agency reviews its measles-free status in November (PAHO, March 2026).

Losing it would confirm measles is spreading again. Expect more school exclusions, staff absences and travel checks.

Doctors, not AI makers, are being left to answer for AI mistakes

What’s happening

A UK national commission warned that blame for AI errors may shift onto clinicians (UK National Commission, September 2026). EU rules for AI in medical devices start only in August 2028 (White & Case, August 2026).

What to expect

New EU liability rules cover software, including AI, sold after 9 December (European Union, October 2024). Until courts test them, clinicians and their insurers carry the risk.

Low-paid Americans must prove they work to keep health cover

What’s happening

From 1 January, unless a state wins more time, many adults on Medicaid, US health cover for people on low incomes, must show 80 hours a month of work or similar (CMS, June 2026).

What to expect

Five governors asked for a delay in October (Healthcare Dive, October 2026). Expect losses to follow each state’s paperwork rules more than whether people work.

US drug-price deals could slow new medicines elsewhere

What’s happening

Drugmakers covering 90% of the US branded market have signed pricing deals (White House, September 2026), and patented drugs from firms without a deal or US factory plans face a 100% tariff (US Commerce Department, September 2026).

What to expect

With US prices tied to other rich countries, expect slower launches in Europe, where patients already wait 607 days on average (OHE, July 2026). An EU law favouring European-made medicines is expected by early 2027 (Ropes & Gray, September 2026).

Health aid is shrinking and coming with strings as Ebola spreads

What’s happening

Ebola has reached 63 health zones in Congo, with 7,890 confirmed cases (WHO, September 2026), and Zimbabwe and Namibia rejected US aid deals that asked for virus samples and health data (AP, September 2026).

What to expect

Global rules for sharing virus samples are due at the World Health Assembly by May 2027 (WHO, September 2026). Expect outbreaks to run longer before tests and vaccines arrive.

Weight-loss drugs are going cheap and global

What’s happening

Over 50 generic versions went on sale in India after the patent expired in March (Medical Dialogues, July 2026), yet US employers covering them for obesity fell from 72% to 60% in a year (Business Group on Health, August 2026).

What to expect

China’s first generics are expected after mid-2027 (Yicai, June 2026). Expect use to grow far faster than spending, often paid in cash, changing what people eat.

The question 2027 will ask: who carries the cost of care?

Some treatment is getting cheaper, but care and cover are getting scarcer. Congress’s budget office expects the US uninsured to rise from 30 million in 2026 to 37 million in 2036 (CBO, July 2026), and the EU needs about 808,000 more care workers by 2040 to keep today’s ratio (European Labour Authority, April 2026). By the end of 2027, expect the answer in benefit budgets and family diaries as much as in parliaments.
