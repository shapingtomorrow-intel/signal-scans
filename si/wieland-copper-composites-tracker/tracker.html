Copper's next growth market is chip cooling, while aluminium takes the cables

Diamond-copper heat spreaders for AI chips are reaching small-batch supply in China while copper costs almost 4.8 times as much as aluminium. This Change Tracker reads eight copper-composite signals on a five to ten year horizon for an alloy maker's research team.

The consensus treats copper as a commodity squeezed by price. This first cycle finds two movements running in opposite directions. Chinese makers report diamond-copper cooling products at sampling and small-batch supply (Securities Times, September 2026), and Sirui, a switchgear-contact maker, is preparing batch production of low-cost copper-diamond (10jqka iNews, October 2026). Meanwhile copper costs almost 4.8 times as much as aluminium and substitution is accelerating (S&P Global, October 2026). Our reading is that value is moving from tonnes of copper to engineered copper parts, which favours composites an alloy maker can produce on routes it already runs.

Pattern narrative

Heat and switching pull copper into compositesEmerging Opportunity

Diamond-copper, vacuum-switchgear contacts and printed copper heat sinks sit here. CITIC Securities puts diamond-copper at 10% to 20% of the cost of pure diamond and expects it to reach volume first in lids and cold plates (Yicai, July 2026). A Harbin study finds that forming complex shapes, not conductivity, limits its use in water cooling (Rare Metal Materials and Engineering, October 2026). ABB is adding vacuum interrupter capacity in Europe (Evertiq, May 2026), and fusion components pair refractory armour with copper alloy heat sinks (TCT Magazine, October 2026). On this evidence, process routes decide who supplies these markets.

Cheaper metals and faster rivalsEvolving Risk

Aluminium's gains are measurable. Aluminium alloy cables took 45% of State Grid procurement in 2025 (Mysteel, June 2026), and carmakers including Ferrari and BMW are extending aluminium wiring (AL Circle, July 2026). In composites the faster movers are Chinese: Sirui has published a patent application for a diamond-composite heat sink with a 3D-printed flow layer (Gelonghui, September 2026), and Huanghe Whirlwind plans 300 MPCVD machines for diamond heat-sink wafers (Soochow Securities, June 2026). Pure diamond also threatens diamond-copper from above, with Orbray planning volume wafer production from 2028 (Newswitch, June 2026).

Prices and rules that change the recipeTransformation Driver

Copper hit a record in September, 18% above January, pushing users into recycling, substitution and efficiency, each with a penalty (cleanthinking.de, September 2026). The ICSG still forecasts a refined surplus, so the squeeze sits in concentrate, not metal (Canadian Mining Journal, October 2026). Rules add pressure: the EU's vehicle law keeps critical raw materials, copper included, in European circulation (European Commission, August 2026), and one buyer, Panasonic, has told suppliers the RoHS exemption for leaded copper alloys is under review (Panasonic, June 2026). On this evidence, the pressure is real, but every way around it carries a penalty.

Signals gaining momentum

  1. Aluminium replacing copper at record price ratios. A ratio that holds near 4.8 into 2027 would make aluminium the design default for cables and wiring rather than a cost choice.
  2. Diamond-copper heat spreaders for AI chips. A first named volume order for lids or cold plates would turn sampling into a supply contest that Chinese makers enter first.
  3. Chinese copper-alloy makers moving into composites. Batch shipments of low-cost copper-diamond from Sirui would show a contact-alloy maker can cross into chip cooling, setting the bar for European rivals.

Collision 1speeds up +3

Record copper prices push cable and car makers toward aluminium

Copper price → Aluminium substitution

Copper costs almost 4.8 times as much as aluminium, and buyers are acting on it: State Grid already buys 45% of its cables in aluminium. Each substitute carries a size or joining penalty, so the loss is concentrated in commodity wire rather than engineered parts.

Watch: Whether the copper to aluminium price ratio holds near 4.8 into 2027 or falls back toward the 3:1 level of five years ago.

Collision 2slows down −2

Cheaper pure diamond could overtake diamond-copper before it reaches volume

Pure diamond → Diamond-copper

The two pull on the same demand. Diamond-copper wins today on cost, at 10% to 20% of pure diamond, but the capacity being built for diamond wafers is funded by the same AI cooling boom and targets volume from 2028.

Watch: Price quotes for polycrystalline diamond heat sinks falling toward diamond-copper levels, or a hyperscale buyer qualifying pure diamond lids.

Collision 3speeds up +2

Switchgear contact profits fund a Chinese rival's move into chip cooling

Switchgear contacts → Chinese alloy makers

Sirui's contact business holds over 60% of China's market and is the base from which it is preparing low-cost copper-diamond. The pattern is the one an established alloy maker would follow: same powders and pressing, new customer.

Watch: Batch shipments or a named customer for Sirui's copper-diamond, or a similar move by another contact-alloy maker.

Implications

Three markers organise the next cycle: the first named volume order for diamond-copper lids or cold plates, whether copper stays near 4.8 times the price of aluminium, and MOFCOM's decision before 10 January 2027 on synthetic diamond export controls. Diamond-copper made on routes an alloy maker already runs warrants a feasibility review now, since Chinese rivals are at patent and sampling stage. Vacuum-switchgear contacts are worth monitoring as a steady market rather than a new one. Printed copper heat sinks for fusion are a later, smaller opportunity where joining know-how matters more than volume. A decision point arrives if the RoHS review narrows the copper-alloy exemption.
