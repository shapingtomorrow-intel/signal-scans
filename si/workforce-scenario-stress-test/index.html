Scenarios are not forecasts. Their job is to show how differently the next decade could turn out, so that a plan can be built to survive any of them, and then to be watched, so that the plan changes when the evidence does.

This page does both. It takes the nine options in the briefing and runs each one through four plausible futures to 2035, plus a wild card to 2040. A move that works in every future is robust and can be committed to now. A move that works well in some futures and poorly in others is a bet, held ready with a clear trigger. A move that makes things worse in any future is fragile and should not be relied on. It then scores leading indicators on the matrix’s two axes, shows how that reading has moved since the August briefing, and turns the reading into investment priorities.

1. Four futures to 2035, and a wild card to 2040

The two axes are the system’s two biggest uncertainties, the same two the System Map identifies: whether the terms for AI at work are agreed with workers or set by employers alone, which is how strongly the bargaining brake works, and whether careers are built inside firms or across an open market again, which is whether the frozen market keeps turning.

Open market · agreed terms

Sector Passports

People move between employers again, and sector agreements travel with them: training credits, AI rules and pay scales are set across an industry rather than inside one firm.

By 2040: Skills records and training rights are portable across most of a sector, and employers compete on the work they offer rather than on terms.

Open market · employer terms

Free Agents

The job market moves again and each employer sets AI terms alone. Skilled people move often and are paid for it; agents and contractors carry much routine work.

By 2040: Careers are built across many employers, and people without early experience struggle to start one.

Inside firms · agreed terms

Bargained Careers

Few people change employers; careers are built inside organisations under agreements that pair AI with consultation, retraining and job security.

By 2040: Internal academies and agreed AI terms are normal at large employers; change is slower but steadier.

Inside firms · employer terms

Company Ladders

People stay put and employers decide alone how AI and agents change work. Some build strong internal ladders; many cut junior intake and management layers.

By 2040: The thin junior intakes of the mid-2020s show up as shortages of mid-level staff where ladders were not built.

Wild card to 2040

Agents on the books

Large employers count AI agents alongside their staff and rebuild management around supervising them.

Built from two of the briefing’s Potential Surprises to 2040: companies report their AI agents alongside their staff (2029 to 2034, Low-Medium) and middle managers come back to supervise the agents (2029 to 2035, Low-Medium).

What the stress test shows
  1. There is a robust core, and it matches the briefing’s four Implications. Redesigning junior roles while keeping the intake, tying training to internal moves, and agreeing a framework for AI with a named owner for every agent hold up in all four futures.
  2. The fastest moves are the fragile ones. Cutting intake backfires where AI terms are agreed and careers stay inside firms; deploying AI under management rights alone backfires in both futures where terms are agreed. The reading in section 4 sits close to that side.
  3. Each bet turns on one axis. The in-house academy and the internal-route guarantee are strong while careers stay inside firms and weak if the market reopens; sharing the gains is strong only where terms are agreed. The US quits rate and the Quality Jobs Act are the signposts that settle them.

3. What it means for the plan

Commit now

Robust: works in every future.

  • Redesign junior roles around supervised work, and keep the intake (Implication 1).
  • Tie training to internal moves, and report internal fill rates (Implication 2).
  • Agree an AI framework with worker representatives, and give every agent a named owner (Implications 3 and 4).

Prepare, and act on a trigger

Bets: strong in some futures, weak in others.

  • An in-house academy: release it if the reading settles on the inside-firms side.
  • A guaranteed internal route: hold ready on the same trigger.
  • Sharing the gains: keep it on the table if agreed terms gain ground.
  • Keeping training as it is: acceptable only until role-linked training is funded.

Do not rely on

Fragile: backfires in at least one future.

  • Cutting junior intake and letting AI absorb the work.
  • Deploying AI and agents under management rights alone.

4. Which future is gaining momentum, and how it has moved since August

Each future is a corner of the matrix, so the question “which future is gaining momentum” becomes two questions the evidence can answer: are the terms for AI at work being agreed or set by employers, and are careers moving outside firms again or staying inside? Twelve leading indicators answer them, six on each axis. Scored at each reading, they give a reading on each axis and a point on the matrix; the trail of points shows where the evidence is heading.

The pale band marks readings within 15 points of an axis, where no future can lead. Hover over a point for its reading.

How the reading is scored

  1. Each indicator is marked Moving (+1), Pending (+½), Not yet (0) or Against (−1), on the evidence published by the date of the reading.
  2. Each carries a weight: 3 if it would settle its axis on its own, 2 if it is strong evidence, 1 if it supports.
  3. An axis reading is the weighted total for that axis, scaled from −100 (agreed terms, or inside firms) to +100 (employer terms, or the open market).
  4. A future leads when the reading sits in its corner, at least 15 points from both axes, for two readings running.
  5. A shift is a move of 10 points or more on either axis between readings. It is flagged and reviewed in the next quarterly briefing.
  6. A signpost reached (section 6) overrides the score and triggers its decision directly.

7. How the monitoring runs over time

Every weekSignal Scans attach new evidence to each indicator and signpost, including weak signals that do not yet fit any future.
Every monthThe Change Tracker rescores each indicator, the two axis readings are recalculated, and the trail on the matrix gains a point.
Every quarterThe Decision Intelligence briefing reviews any flagged shift, and redraws the scenarios, the System Map and this stress test.
When a signpost is reachedAction Triggers fire, releasing the bet prepared for it and naming the decision it changes.

8. Running it with your own plan

The options above are the briefing’s. The real value comes from replacing them with the organisation’s own workforce priorities and asking the same question of each: does it hold in every future?

BeforeThe organisation shares its workforce plan and people priorities for the next few years; we grade each against the scenarios and the evidence base.
Session, 2 hours20 minutes in the four futures; 40 minutes in which each team lives in one future and answers “it is 2035: what do we wish we had started in 2026?”; 40 minutes testing the plan against the matrix; 20 minutes agreeing the robust core and the trigger for each bet.
AfterThe agreed matrix, indicators and signposts join the programme, and the monitoring cycle above keeps them current.
