Power demand outpaces grid; US policy retreats; insured losses harden

A May 2026 read: clean build-out is accelerating in batteries, renewables and first-of-a-kind nuclear, while data-centre load, the OBBBA-driven US policy reversal and a sixth consecutive USD 100 bn-plus insured-loss year reshape the operating environment.

The 2026 consensus on energy is still the long transition. The cycle's evidence reframes it. Build-out is accelerating, with a record 692 GW of renewables added in 2025 and storage crossing 100 GW of annual additions for the first time (IRENA, March 2026). At the same time AI-driven load, PJM auction signals and the OBBBA's IRA rollback pull capital and policy in different directions, while a third 1.5°C-overshoot year hardens the insured-loss base. The question is whether grids, permitting and insurance can absorb the speed.

Pattern narrative

The cycle's nine signals group into three themes at different tempos. Build-out is the clearest accelerator, load-and-grid the dominant transformer, and the risk side hardening rather than fading.

Clean build-out scales faster than the headlines suggestEmerging Opportunity

Battery storage, the renewables record and the first US advanced-nuclear permit in roughly a decade sit together. Storage took four years to cross 100 GW versus eight for solar, compressing the firming timeline. The next inflection is whether 2026's projected 158 GW of storage lands behind data-centre loads.

Data-centre load and resilience deadlines rewire the operating environmentTransformation Driver

AI-driven demand, the PJM price escalation, the persistent interconnection bottleneck and the EU CER cycle interact. PJM's third capped auction shows load moving faster than firm capacity even as 2.6 TW sits in queue. The EU layer adds a parallel deadline-driven push across 11 sectors.

Physical and policy risk convergeEvolving Risk

The US clean-energy policy reversal and the insured-catastrophe loss line move in the same direction from different systems. OBBBA's phase-out and the offshore-wind stop-work episodes compress US capex into a 2026-2027 window, while a sixth consecutive year above USD 100 bn in insured losses, secondary perils dominant, hardens the underwriting base.

Signals gaining momentum

  1. AI and data-centre electricity demand. A missed hyperscaler go-live or an ISO-ordered large-load curtailment would force tariff and siting reform.
  2. RTO capacity-price escalation. A backstop procurement RFP at PJM would tip the conversation to direct state intervention on data-centre allocations.
  3. Battery storage scaling. A sustained RTO co-location requirement would convert storage into default interconnection economics.
  4. Insured catastrophe losses. A second January-2025-scale wildfire or convective-storm cluster would push FAIR Plan models past carrying capacity.
  5. US clean-energy policy reversal. A Treasury clarification narrowing the 4 July 2026 safe harbour would compress the window further.

Wild Cards to Watch

Compound peak-loss year materialises

Disruptive Risk Potential impact: Very High

Surprise characteristics: A 2026 secondary-peril cluster pushing insured losses toward the modelled peak.

Early warning indicators: Above-normal Atlantic SSTs by July; multi-state convective storms in May-June; Pacific wildfire above 2025 baseline.

Swiss Re's peak-year scenario points to about USD 320 bn in insured losses in 2026 against a base-case USD 148 bn (Swiss Re sigma, March 2026). That would crystallise the property-insurance retreat now visible in California into a broader sun-belt mortgage event.

Hyperscaler-backed firm clean capacity goes commercial

Disruptive Opportunity Potential impact: High

Surprise characteristics: A multi-gigawatt hyperscaler offtake pulling SMRs, geothermal or long-duration storage past first-of-a-kind cost.

Early warning indicators: Second NRC construction permit; long-dated PPAs sub-USD 100/MWh; corporate balance-sheet financing.

The Natrium permit (NRC, March 2026) shows the regulatory machine can move when an application is well prepared. A second and third permit at similar cadence would shift firm clean capacity from speculative to bookable.

EU resilience deadline reveals a sector-wide gap

Disruptive Risk Potential impact: High

Surprise characteristics: A Member-State audit shows critical-entity coverage below 50% by Q4 2026 in energy or digital infrastructure.

Early warning indicators: Late July designations; Commission infringement letters; sector push-back on cross-border resilience requirements.

Strategies were due 17 January 2026 and designation lands in July (CER commentary, February 2026). A patchy roll-out would re-open the H2 2026 climate-resilience framework consultation just as it is meant to land.

Implications

Three reads are worth monitoring this quarter. Capacity is no longer the binding constraint; interconnection, siting and firm-capacity contracting are, which warrants attention to storage co-location terms and the next PJM and MISO auction signals. The OBBBA 4 July 2026 construction-start window is a decision point for US clean-energy capex still on the shelf. The insured-loss base reset matters for capital allocation, directly through property cover and indirectly through mortgage and municipal-bond pricing in exposed regions.
