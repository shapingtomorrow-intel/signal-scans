Scarcity clears at the cap while the demand behind it faces audit

PJM's 2028/29 capacity auction cleared at the $325/MW-day cap with a 6,831 MW reliability shortfall, GE Vernova's gas backlog reached 116 GW, record June heat curtailed European generation, and overstatement critiques cooled the data-centre demand story to Stable.

Edition 3, previous cycle: ‘Delivery overtakes generation as the binding limit; catastrophe momentum cools’ (25 June 2026)

The consensus has not moved: the IEA still projects electricity demand growing 3.6% a year to 2030, and delivery, not generation, remains the binding limit named last cycle (IEA, February 2026). What changed is that scarcity began setting prices: PJM’s 2028/29 auction cleared at the $325/MW-day cap, 6,831 MW short of the reliability requirement (PJM Interconnection, July 2026), and GE Vernova’s gas-equipment backlog and reservations reached 116 GW (GE Vernova, July 2026). AI data-centre demand, Accelerating in both prior cycles, cools to Stable as overstatement critiques land, and record June heat made climate stress an operating constraint. The scarcity is real; how much of the forecast load behind it arrives is not yet proven.

Pattern narrative

Reliability under stressEvolving Risk

France’s hottest day since record-keeping began in 1947 shut reactors on overheated rivers and derated about 2.5 gigawatts of UK gas plant (MIT Technology Review, June 2026); RTE held supply and is pricing 24 billion euros of network climate adaptation to 2040 (RTE, June 2026). Washington met its own summer stress with emergency authority: more than 40 Section 202(c) orders and extensions since May 2025 (POWER Magazine, April 2026). The loss book, by contrast, cooled: USD 46 billion of H1 insured losses was the lowest first half since 2019 (Gallagher Re, July 2026).

Scarcity sets the priceTransformation Driver

The delivery squeeze the last two cycles tracked is now priced. PJM’s auction produced a second consecutive RTO-wide reliability shortfall and a planned September backstop procurement (PJM Interconnection, July 2026), while the IEA counts more than 2,500 GW of projects stalled in queues and puts needed grid investment roughly 50% above today’s USD 400 billion (IEA, February 2026). The counter-current is the audit of demand itself: Grid Strategies argues NERC’s 90 GW data-centre assumption may be overstated (Utility Dive, March 2026), and PJM’s monitor ties $23 billion of price increases to expected data-centre load (Fortune, July 2026). The open question: whether scarcity revenue survives affordability politics.

Resilience obligations and storageEmerging Opportunity

The CER designation deadline arrived on 17 July, turning resilience guidance into obligations for designated critical entities (Deloitte, July 2026); the Commission’s integrated climate-resilience package follows in the fourth quarter (European Parliament, May 2026). Storage keeps compounding, with BNEF forecasting 158 GW in 2026 after a record 112 GW in 2025 (Energy-Storage.news, May 2026), and the IEA reckons flexible connection agreements could enable 750 GW to 900 GW of queued projects.

Signals gaining momentum

  1. Capacity-market scarcity pricing. A September backstop procurement, or a third shortfall in December’s 2029/30 auction, forces the choice between load management and consumer relief.
  2. Gas turbine supply bottleneck. Reservations converting to orders faster than the output ramp expands turns scarcity into allocation: who connects this decade.
  3. Heat as an operating constraint. A second continental episode, or heat-driven load shedding, moves adaptation budgets from plans into procurement.
  4. Emergency-order reliability governance. A challenge to a 202(c) order tests whether emergency authority can keep substituting for market design.

Wild Cards to Watch

Phantom load leaves ratepayers holding the cap

Disruptive RiskPotential impact: Very High

Surprise characteristics: Capacity bought at the cap against demand that never fully arrives.

Early warning indicators: Interconnection-request withdrawals; transformer, chip and turbine lead times; NERC and market-monitor forecast revisions.

The 2028/29 auction’s cleared supply times price totals $16.4 billion (PJM Interconnection, July 2026), procured against forecasts Grid Strategies argues may overstate data-centre load (Utility Dive, March 2026). If that load evaporates after the spend commits, the cost lands on households.

The quiet season breaks late

Disruptive RiskPotential impact: Very High

Surprise characteristics: A single major landfall against a 17% seasonal probability, into a market priced for calm.

Early warning indicators: Late-season Gulf formations; slackening El Nino wind shear; January renewal pricing.

CSU’s nine-storm forecast would make 2026 the quietest season since 2015 (Insurance Journal, July 2026), but it only takes one storm to make for a very bad season (NOAA, May 2026).

Flexible connections jump the queue

Disruptive OpportunityPotential impact: High

Surprise characteristics: Non-firm connection agreements release stranded queue capacity faster than any physical build.

Early warning indicators: PJM’s Connect and Manage filing at FERC; flexible-connection rules spreading to other markets.

The IEA estimates 750 GW to 900 GW of queued projects could be enabled through non-firm connection agreements (IEA, February 2026); PJM plans a Connect and Manage filing at FERC within weeks.

Implications

Worth monitoring to October: PJM’s FERC filings on the Reliability Backstop and Connect and Manage (PJM Interconnection, July 2026), the Atlantic season against its well below normal forecast, and construction-start enforcement (The Tax Adviser, February 2026). Preparation is warranted where capacity-cost pass-through lands in PJM states; the monitor’s findings give affordability intervention a ready evidence base. A decision point arrives if demand-forecast corrections gain official standing: strategies priced against 90 GW of data-centre growth would reprice quickly. On the available evidence the scarcity itself is real; its duration is the open variable.
