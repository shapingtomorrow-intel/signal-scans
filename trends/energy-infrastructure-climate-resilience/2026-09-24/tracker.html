Regulators audit data-centre load; a court curbs emergency orders

Texas and North Carolina now test data-centre load before permitting or building for it, 25 states have approved large-load tariffs, and a federal court found a DOE emergency order unlawful, while US storage set a quarterly record.

Edition 5, previous cycle: ‘Scarcity gets a $555 cap; heat takes a fifth of France’s reactors’ (August 2026)

Consensus holds that data-centre demand outruns supply; this cycle regulators started testing the demand. Texas halted state data-centre permits pending its power and water audit (CBS Texas, September 2026), in a queue where about 90% of new requests are data centres (Utility Dive, August 2026), and North Carolina refused Duke a $584 million turbine justified by load growth it judged unreliable (Utility Dive, September 2026). Ratepayer cost-allocation politics advances to Maturing as 25 states have approved large-load tariffs (EEI, September 2026), and emergency orders, Stable last cycle, warm after a court found one unlawful (Michigan Attorney General, September 2026). Still open: who pays for load that never arrives.

Pattern narrative

Load has to prove itself before anyone paysTransformation Driver

Texas widened its August audit of an estimated 474 GW queue (Utility Dive, August 2026) into a permit halt (CBS Texas, September 2026). California's seven new laws stop data centres shifting costs onto other ratepayers (Governor of California, September 2026), and median minimum tariff terms have risen from 5 to 12 years (LBNL, August 2026). PJM's $555/MW-day backstop still leaves the payer to the states (PJM, July 2026). Forecasts span 9% to 17% of US electricity by 2030 (LBNL, September 2026); on the available evidence, Texas and North Carolina now refuse to underwrite that spread on trust.

Emergency powers and climate costs meet their limitsEvolving Risk

Western Europe had its warmest summer on record (Copernicus, September 2026); French reactors lost more than 10 TWh of output to heat and low rivers, a record, though RTE says the grid held (Révolution Énergétique, September 2026). PJM called demand response in unusually warm weather on 17 September with about 36,000 MW of outages in place (PJM, September 2026). The D.C. Circuit found DOE's first Campbell order unlawful, with $295 million of costs sought from customers (Michigan Attorney General, September 2026). California's wildfire-liability bill died after utilities reported $20 billion in lost market value (CalMatters, September 2026), and Edison warns customers will pay if its rating falls to junk (Claims Journal, September 2026).

Storage outruns the connection rulebookEmerging Opportunity

US storage installations hit a record 20.2 GWh in the second quarter, and the 2030 forecast rose 11.5% to 683 GWh (SEIA, September 2026). FERC's orders proposed a 50 MW large-load threshold but left design to each market, with filings allowed into November (RMI, July 2026); rules letting storage and flexible load substitute for new generation are unwritten.

Signals gaining momentum

  1. Data-centre load verification. An ERCOT audit striking much of the queue would give other commissions a template.
  2. Ratepayer cost-allocation politics. Approval of the tariffs pending in 7 more states would make developer-borne cost the default.
  3. Emergency-order reliability governance. A ruling against later Campbell orders before November's renewals would return retirements to the states.
  4. Battery storage scaling. Storage winning a material share of PJM's backstop would confirm it as a firm substitute that arrives in time.

Collision 1speeds up +3

The bigger the data-centre queue, the harder regulators test it

Data-centre demand → Load verification

Texas faced a queue in which about 90% of new requests were data centres and answered with an audit, then a permit halt. North Carolina refused a turbine justified largely by anticipated data-centre customers. On the available evidence, claimed load now draws scrutiny in proportion to its size, which slows the speculative share of the pipeline rather than the demand itself.

Watch: The ERCOT audit result, and whether other commissions cite it when ruling on generation built for data centres.

Collision 2speeds up +2

Long tariff contracts make data centres prove the load they claim

Cost allocation → Load verification

Large-load tariffs now carry median minimum terms of 12 years and have been approved in 25 states. A developer that pays for capacity whether or not it uses it has reason to request only load it will build, so cost allocation does part of the regulators' verification work.

Watch: Withdrawal rates from utility large-load queues after new tariffs take effect.

Collision 3speeds up +2

Late heat sends grids back to emergency orders as a court limits them

Heat constraint → Emergency orders

PJM called demand response in mid-September with about 36,000 MW of outages in place and asked DOE for an emergency order. Days earlier the D.C. Circuit had found a DOE order unlawful. Heat in the maintenance season raises demand for the tool just as its legal footing narrows.

Watch: DOE's response to the ruling, and whether PJM or other operators request orders during the October outage season.

Wild Cards to Watch

Emergency orders lapse into the winter peak

Disruptive RiskPotential impact: Very High

Surprise characteristics: Courts strip the authority holding retiring plants online as winter demand builds.

Early warning indicators: DOE appeal; rulings on later Campbell orders; renewals due 14 and 30 November.

The D.C. Circuit found the first Campbell order unlawful, yet a sixth runs to 14 November (Michigan Attorney General, September 2026) and the Stanton order to 30 November (US Department of Energy, September 2026). Blocked renewals would leave grid operators weeks to plan.

The quiet season breaks late

Disruptive RiskPotential impact: Very High

Surprise characteristics: One major landfall into a market priced for a record-quiet season.

Early warning indicators: October Gulf formation; El Niño strength; January renewal pricing.

No Atlantic hurricane had formed by 21 September, a first in 112 years, yet about 40% of the season's hurricane activity still remains (Fox Weather, September 2026).

Flexibility clears the backstop before it bids

Disruptive OpportunityPotential impact: High

Surprise characteristics: Bilateral contracts and storage shrink the target before the auction runs.

Early warning indicators: PJM bilateral matches; the published target; third-quarter storage installs.

PJM cuts the target by resources under bilateral contracts (PJM, July 2026); with utility-scale storage up from 88 GWh to 165 GWh in 18 months (SEIA, September 2026), the residual could sit well below 6,831 MW.

Implications

On this reading, load verification is the development most likely to change capital plans this autumn: Texas and North Carolina put the burden of proof on developers, and long tariff terms make it contractual. ERCOT's audit result is the decision point. PJM's backstop warrants preparation for a residual well below 6,831 MW if bilateral deals and storage clear first. November's emergency-order renewals are worth monitoring as a reliability risk, not a formality. A California special session before year end would show whether customers or shareholders carry wildfire cost.
