Stablecoin rules harden as tokenised bank money scales and cash plateaus

This inaugural cycle reads momentum in the future of money as firmly tilted toward tokenisation: United States stablecoin rulemaking and the tokenised settlement stack lead the accelerators, while the retail digital euro holds steady and cash decline has flattened.

The future-of-money debate has split in two: private stablecoins as the disruptive force, and incumbents absorbing their technology. This inaugural cycle finds the second track moving faster. United States GENIUS Act rulemaking is the sharpest accelerator, with Treasury, the OCC and the Council of Economic Advisers all publishing within an April fortnight; the IMF estimates markets have priced the competitive shock at roughly 300 billion dollars off payment incumbents (International Monetary Fund, March 2026). Banks answer with tokenised deposits and on-chain funds; central banks build settlement rails beneath both. The strategic question: does tokenised commercial bank money, not stablecoins, become the dominant on-chain dollar and pound?

Pattern narrative

The deposit-franchise contestEvolving Risk

Three signals converge on one question: where the public will hold money. United States agencies are building the regime that lets stablecoins compete, even as the Council of Economic Advisers finds a yield ban would barely help bank lending (White House Council of Economic Advisers, April 2026) and the Federal Reserve documents the funding strain it creates (Federal Reserve Board, December 2025).

The tokenised settlement stackTransformation Driver

The cycle's structural story is the tokenised settlement stack. Tokenised deposits, tokenised money market funds and wholesale central bank rails are maturing together, with central banks placing settlement in central bank money beneath the private layers (European Central Bank, May 2026). September's Pontes go-live is the next inflection.

New payment surfacesEmerging Opportunity

A third cluster reshapes the payment surface itself. Agent-initiated payments are moving from concept to bank testing while cash use has settled into a plateau rather than a steep decline. The open question: who captures value when an agent, not a consumer, chooses the rail?

Signals gaining momentum

  1. US stablecoin rulemaking machinery. When the OCC finalises its rule and June comment windows close, the GENIUS Act starts a 120-day compliance clock.
  2. Bank deposit disintermediation. Federal Reserve data showing measurable deposit migration would turn a modelled risk into a funding question.
  3. Tokenised bank deposits. Cross-bank fungibility, a clearing layer for deposit tokens, is the threshold that turns pilots into a network.
  4. Agent-initiated payments. A large-scale consumer rollout of agent checkout would force issuers to set authorisation policy quickly.
  5. Wholesale central bank settlement rails. The September go-live of ECB Pontes is when tokenised central bank settlement stops being a pilot.

Wild Cards to Watch

A systemic stablecoin run

Disruptive Risk Potential impact: Very High

Surprise characteristics: A large dollar stablecoin breaks par and redemptions become self-reinforcing.

Early warning indicators: Widening secondary-market discounts; issuer reserves concentrated at a few banks.

The BIS, ECB and New York Fed describe one channel: a mass redemption forces fire sales of Treasury bills and drains the bank deposits backing issuer reserves, so stress transmits to banks within days (Bank for International Settlements, April 2026).

An interoperable tokenised-deposit clearing layer

Disruptive Opportunity Potential impact: High

Surprise characteristics: Banks agree a clearing standard making one bank's deposit token fungible with another's.

Early warning indicators: A live multi-bank clearer; a central bank endorsing deposit-token interoperability.

Tokenised deposits today are mostly single-bank and aimed at large corporates. A neutral clearing layer would let interest-bearing, insured bank money outpace stablecoins as the default on-chain currency (OMFIF, January 2026).

Quantum harvest-now-decrypt-later

Disruptive Risk Potential impact: Very High

Surprise characteristics: Encrypted payment data captured today is decrypted once quantum computers mature.

Early warning indicators: Accelerated quantum hardware milestones; migration deadlines pulled forward.

BIS Project Leap tested post-quantum cryptography inside a live payment system and found significant performance gaps still to close (Bank for International Settlements, December 2025). A faster-than-expected milestone would compress an already tight migration timeline.

Implications

For an institution in payments or deposits, this cycle rewards attention over reaction. The GENIUS Act comment windows closing in June are worth close monitoring, since they convert a fluid debate into fixed compliance dates. Tokenised deposits warrant active preparation, because cross-bank interoperability is the one development that would reset the competitive map. The September Pontes go-live is a natural checkpoint for wholesale settlement in central bank money. Agent-initiated payments are early enough to shape rather than follow. The retail digital euro and the cash question can be tracked at a lower cadence.
