Tokenised settlement clears real money as the stablecoin float shrinks

Project Agora settled real value across 28 institutions while the stablecoin float fell to a six-month low. Ten signals tracked this cycle, from emerging-market dollarisation to the digital euro trilogues.

Edition 4, previous cycle: “US and UK stablecoin rulebooks harden as the digital euro clears Parliament” (16 July 2026).

The consensus read on the future of money runs through stablecoins, a private dollar supervisors are racing to fence in. The evidence points elsewhere. Project Agora settled about CHF 800,000 of real value across 28 institutions and central banks in an average of 80 seconds (BIS, July 2026), while the stablecoin float fell to a six-month low from its May peak (Crypto Briefing, July 2026). The deposit-drain case that moved to Accelerating last cycle now reads Fading, and the sovereignty question has travelled abroad, into economies where dollar tokens pass through capital restrictions that bank deposits do not. The contested ground is which settlement model prevails.

Pattern narrative

The private dollar contracts at home and spreads abroadEvolving Risk

Stablecoin supply contraction, emerging-market dollarisation and the fading deposit-drain case sit together. The float peaked in May 2026 between 300 billion and 316 billion dollars and has fallen since (Crypto Briefing, July 2026), which thins the domestic deposit argument. The BIS moved the question outward: across more than 130 economies, dollar-pegged stablecoin inflows do not respond to capital restrictions the way foreign currency deposits do (BIS, July 2026), a reading the IMF carried to emerging-market policymakers (IMF, August 2026).

Tokenised settlement moves from prototype to productionTransformation Driver

Project Agora settled real value rather than simulated value, across 28 institutions and central banks in 17 transaction scenarios (BIS, July 2026). What that leaves open is which model prevails. Wells Fargo's corporate rollout keeps client money inside the insured deposit franchise (Markets Media, August 2026), in a field where 24 of the 50 largest US banks track it (Tech Times, August 2026), while the shared bank network at The Clearing House targets the first half of 2027 and looks set to replay the earlier split between bank-owned and public payment rails (PYMNTS, June 2026).

Public rulebooks and public rails advance on a clockEmerging Opportunity

Three signals run on dated calendars. The first digital euro trilogue met on 13 July with holding limits, compensation and privacy open (Freshfields, July 2026), and the Bundesbank places first pilot transactions in the second half of 2027 (Deutsche Bundesbank, March 2026). The US joint identity rule ran to a 21 August comment deadline (Federal Register, June 2026); the Bank of England's draft Code sits in a window closing 22 September (Bank of England, June 2026). What decides all three is political.

Signals gaining momentum

  1. Wholesale cross-border tokenised settlement. A published technical specification with named production participants turns a prototype into a procurement decision for correspondent banking.
  2. Bank tokenised deposits reach corporate clients. Once a second and third bank open corporate rails in the same currency pair, treasurers choose a network rather than a bank.
  3. Digital dollarisation in emerging markets. A first national restriction on stablecoin on-ramps converts an IMF policy argument into a country risk line.
  4. Retail digital euro. A trilogue deal on holding limits before year-end fixes the parameter European bank treasuries cannot yet plan around.

Wild Cards to Watch

A systemic stablecoin run before the rulebooks bind

Disruptive RiskPotential impact: Very High

Surprise characteristics: A large issuer breaks its peg while the US and UK rulebooks are still in comment.

Early warning indicators: Redemption spikes; thinner reserve attestations; widening secondary-market discounts.

The GENIUS Act's statutory year for final rules passed with the joint identity rule only reaching its comment deadline on 21 August (Federal Register, June 2026), and the Bank of England will not finalise its Code until the end of 2026 (Bank of England, June 2026). The reporting that would give supervisors early sight of a run is itself still a proposal (OCC, June 2026).

A non-bank consortium owns the shared dollar rail before the banks

Disruptive OpportunityPotential impact: High

Surprise characteristics: The layer banks are building for 2027 arrives first from outside banking.

Early warning indicators: Consortium go-live dates; corporate treasury pilots; merchant acceptance.

The bank-led Clearing House network targets the first half of 2027 (PYMNTS, June 2026), while a stablecoin consortium announced in June is expected live this year (Tech Times, August 2026). That would set the shared rail for on-chain dollars outside bank balance sheets, which the BIS reads as the wrong settlement anchor (BIS, June 2026).

Implications

The cycle's practical work sits in treasury and product rather than policy watching. Corporate treasuries with dollar and sterling flows now choose between individual bank tokenised rails and a shared network at least a year out, and either choice concentrates counterparty exposure worth modelling before the autumn rollouts. European bank treasuries have a narrower window: a trilogue deal on holding limits before year-end sets the deposit-outflow parameter they cannot yet plan against. For anyone with emerging-market exposure, the dollarisation evidence warrants a country-level view of on-ramps rather than one global stablecoin policy.
