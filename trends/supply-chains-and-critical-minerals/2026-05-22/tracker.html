Change Tracker · Supply Chains and Critical Minerals · 22 May 2026

State intervention in critical minerals accelerates as the export-control truce holds

This cycle's momentum sits with the state: Washington's stockpiling, equity and tariff machinery and Beijing's new supply-chain security law are accelerating, while the one fading signal is the easing of export curbs on gallium, germanium and antimony.

Critical mineral supply chains are now treated as contested ground between Washington and Beijing, not a logistics question. This inaugural cycle finds the dominant momentum with the state: governments no longer regulate these markets from outside but operate inside them as stockpilers, equity holders, tariff-setters and licensing authorities. Washington's intervention machinery and Beijing's new supply-chain security law are accelerating together, while the export-control weapons of 2025 sit paused under a fragile truce. The question for boards is whether any sourcing strategy still works without first reading two governments' policy calendars.

Pattern narrative

Beijing institutionalises supply-chain leverageEvolving Risk

The three China signals, rare earth and magnet controls (Stable), the supply-chain security regime (Accelerating) and the gallium, germanium and antimony curbs (Fading), show the risk changing form, not retreating. The October 2025 weapon is holstered under the truce, but the capability is now permanent law: Order No. 834 gives Beijing standing authority to investigate and penalise foreign firms (Squire Patton Boggs, April 2026). The next inflection is the November 2026 expiry.

Washington becomes a market participantTransformation Driver

The three United States signals, stockpiling, equity stakes with price floors, and Section 232 remedies, are accelerating together toward one model: Washington operating inside mineral markets as buyer, shareholder and price-setter. Project Vault adds a USD 12 billion reserve (Supply Chain Dive, February 2026); the January proclamation casts processing dependence as a security threat (the White House, January 2026), which CSIS reads as a turn toward allied cooperation (CSIS, January 2026); in April the remedy track widened to a copper tariff (the White House, April 2026). Wilson Center analysis warns the model leans on a few national champions (Wilson Center, April 2026).

Allied diversification builds, but slowlyEmerging Opportunity

The EU strategic-projects pipeline and the allied buyers' club are both Stable: real, but slower than the threat. The Critical Raw Materials Act drew over 160 second-round applications, yet EU auditors judge the 2030 targets out of reach (European Court of Auditors, February 2026); the IEA's February ministerial urged faster diversification (the IEA, February 2026). Conversion hangs on whether the 2026 G7 settles on one buyers' club or scattered bilateral deals.

Signals gaining momentum

  1. Government equity stakes and price floors. A forced decision arrives when a sustained Chinese price war tests a floor the Treasury must underwrite.
  2. US strategic mineral stockpiling. A first drawdown timed to a supply shock would turn Project Vault into an active market instrument.
  3. US Section 232 processed-minerals trade remedy. Missing the proclamation's 180-day negotiating deadline would trigger tariffs and minimum import prices.
  4. China supply-chain security legal regime. A first extraterritorial enforcement action under Order No. 834 would move this from statute to live risk.

Wild Cards to Watch

The November 2026 control cliff

Disruptive Risk Potential impact: Very High

Surprise characteristics: A calendar-dated shock that markets are pricing as if it will not arrive.

Early warning indicators: Licence approval rates, US-China trade-talk tone, G7 summit signalling.

The mutual suspension of rare earth and minor-metals controls expires in November 2026 (Fastmarkets, January 2026). Non-renewal would snap restrictions back at once, with the foreign direct product rule still on the books (CSIS, October 2025).

Commercial deep-sea mining goes live

Disruptive Opportunity Potential impact: High

Surprise characteristics: A new supply frontier opening outside the China-dominated terrestrial system.

Early warning indicators: NOAA final permit decision, International Seabed Authority response, first offtake deals.

A NOAA full-compliance determination in May 2026 puts a commercial seabed-mining permit within reach before Q1 2027, with at least nine companies in talks (the Associated Press, May 2026). A permit would reset the polymetallic-nodule supply outlook.

First extraterritorial enforcement under Order 834

Disruptive Risk Potential impact: High

Surprise characteristics: Routine compliance with Western export controls reclassified as a Chinese-law offence.

Early warning indicators: First Article 15 investigation, blocking-order use, manager exit restrictions.

Order No. 834's countermeasure articles reach foreign firms that restrict supply to Chinese counterparts (Squire Patton Boggs, April 2026). A first action against a Western trader would create an open conflict-of-laws exposure.

Implications

The cycle's signal for boards is that critical-mineral exposure is now governed by policy calendars, not commodity cycles. Three items warrant monitoring: the November 2026 expiry of the US-China export-control suspension, a hard date on re-escalation risk; the scope of US price-floor and equity instruments, which decide whether Western processing economics survive without permanent state support; and the auditor-flagged gap between EU diversification ambition and delivery. Procurement built on lowest landed cost now carries an unpriced policy exposure, best met by mapping suppliers against the 0.1 percent Chinese-content threshold and stress-testing a non-renewed truce.
